[Codex image-generation docs](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/image-generation), [OpenAI Academy](https://openai.com/academy/image-generation/), [GPT Image prompting cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/multimodal/image-gen-models-prompting-guide)

   OpenAI documents one-or-more reference images, but publishes no tested “best N.” The strongest supported operational default is **one dedicated style tile**, adding a second reference only when it has a distinct job (content/layout/character), not several competing style frames. No credible controlled community comparison of 1 vs. 2 vs. 5 style refs found.

   *So what:* `Image 1: style reference only—copy flat fills, cool palette, outline treatment; do not copy objects/layout.` Then list the new scene and invariants; do not stack redundant style references.

2. **Color fidelity / hex and palette locking**

   **A.** OpenAI says to include colors when material to the outcome, and defines a reference style’s visual language as including its palette. [Codex image-generation docs](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/image-generation), [GPT Image prompting cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/multimodal/image-gen-models-prompting-guide)

   **Nothing found:** OpenAI publishes no claim that `#RRGGBB` values are pixel-exact, no benchmark comparing hex values against hue words, and no documented palette-lock parameter. Credible community evidence establishing a general guarantee was also not found.

   Given the supplied measured result, the practical evidence here is strongest for **style-tile/swatches + explicit hex mapping**, rather than hue words alone. This is an inference from your measurement plus OpenAI’s documented reference-style mechanism, not an OpenAI guarantee.

   *So what:* Include the palette in the style tile and repeat a short mapping: `outline #…; sky #…; wall #…; foliage #…; use no colors outside this 3–4-color palette.`

3. **Uniform outline weight and outline color**

   **A.** The cookbook demonstrates compatible language—“clean, vector-like shapes,” “minimal strokes,” “flat design,” and “no gradients”—but gives no technique or guarantee for uniform stroke weight or a fixed outline hex. [GPT Image prompting cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/multimodal/image-gen-models-prompting-guide)

   **Nothing found:** no official instruction or credible community test that establishes wording capable of reliably locking even line weight across a complex flat illustration.

   *So what:* Make the outline treatment visually prominent in the single style tile, then state it twice: `one even-width continuous outline in #241A12 around every silhouette and interior boundary; no sketch lines, variable ink, hatching, or texture.` Treat it as a scored acceptance check, not a guaranteed prompt property.

4. **Anti-realism / negative constraints**

   **A.** Negative constraints are documented as useful: Codex says to state what the image must not contain, and the cookbook’s GPT Image 2 examples explicitly use “Avoid … gradients, shadows, decorative elements” and “flat design, minimal strokes, no gradients.” [Codex image-generation docs](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/image-generation), [GPT Image prompting cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/multimodal/image-gen-models-prompting-guide)

   The cookbook’s stronger pattern is not negatives alone: specify the desired visual system positively, then add targeted exclusions and preserve lists. It also advises small, one-variable edits to avoid drift. [GPT Image prompting cookbook](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/multimodal/image-gen-models-prompting-guide)

   **Nothing found:** no public A/B evidence that a particular synonym (“vector” vs. “flat cel”) or constraint placement is categorically stronger.

   *So what:* Lead with positive renderer language—`flat 2.5D vector-cartoon, cel fills, cool limited palette, even outline`—then finish with one compact prohibition: `no realism, gradients, cast shadows, ambient shading, texture, warm ink, or painterly rendering.`
