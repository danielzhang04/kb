\### B. Blockers (would make the build wrong)

1. **§ Agent contracts › Campaigner; § Executable hooks; § Reuse plan.**  
   **Defect:** The send-capable agent receives a Gmail adapter, while the scheduler and approval checks are separate components; the spec never states that direct Gmail mutation is technically impossible. The list-builder has the same structural access to vendor adapters outside the budget hook.  
   **Failure:** An agent or injected tool instruction can call Gmail or a provider without passing caps, suppression, WebAuthn, or credit checks—the LLM effectively owns the external action.  
   **Fix:** Expose only one deterministic executor command. Keep raw Gmail-send and vendor credentials in an unexposed local process that accepts a typed request, revalidates every hook, and rejects unsigned/direct calls. Add tests proving the agent tool surface has no raw send/vendor-call operation.

2. **§ Executable hooks › Pre-send recheck; § Cadences; § Campaigner evals; § Threat model.**  
   **Defect:** A SQLite transaction cannot be atomic with Gmail. After the final database check and before Gmail accepts the send, a reply can arrive; hourly reply scanning expands that exposure to nearly 60 minutes unless each send refreshes Gmail.  
   **Failure:** A follow-up can send after the recipient has replied even though the advertised “atomic stop” test passes at an earlier race point.  
   **Fix:** Define the exact linearization point and guarantee. Use one per-thread desktop executor, refresh the Gmail thread immediately before every send, atomically claim the row with compare-and-set, and test replies at every boundary through Gmail acceptance and uncertain-response reconciliation. Do not claim prevention for replies Gmail receives after the defined point.

3. **§ Contract 3 › Approval; § Campaign approval tiers; § P6.**  
   **Defect:** No named component produces human approval records. `scope_hash` omits `policy_hash`; the schema lacks token ID, expiry, nonce, credential/assertion reference, and consumed/replayed state although P6 promises expiry/replay tests. T3 class-level enablement also conflicts with exact-byte approval for replies generated later.  
   **Failure:** A changed cadence/policy can reuse an old approval unless mutable invalidation happens perfectly, while T3 either cannot run or sends model-generated bytes never approved by Daniel.  
   **Fix:** Name the VM-side human WebAuthn issuer and desktop verifier. Bind a signed assertion to `policy_hash`, revision bytes/hash, contact, mailbox, tier, send time/window, expiry, nonce, and permitted action; consume it atomically. Require exact-byte approval for generated replies or restrict auto-replies to deterministic preapproved templates.

4. **§ Personalizer; § P3; § Open questions 2.**  
   **Defect:** `source_snapshots` are a required personalizer input, but no schema, producer, owner, fetcher, lifecycle, or permission contract exists; the spec leaves the producer as an open question.  
   **Failure:** P3 cannot produce evidence from live prospects without either adding undeclared browser authority or accepting an undefined file/input path.  
   **Fix:** Before P3, define a desktop `source_snapshot` contract and deterministic fetcher, its owning agent/script, source allowlist, timestamps, retention, injection treatment, and tests proving the personalizer receives snapshots without browser or VM PII access.

5. **§ Hosts and trust boundary; § Manager; § No-PII hook; § P2/P5 gates.**  
   **Defect:** The VM manager consumes raw natural-language asks, so an ask containing a person’s name reaches the VM before any guard. The pre-commit hook detects only email/phone patterns although the PII law also covers names, notes, excerpts, bodies, and profile details. P2 then requires “zero PII outside desktop SQLite” while LinkedIn’s Chrome profile and `raw_response_ref` necessarily hold PII outside SQLite.  
   **Failure:** PII can enter VM arguments/logs/cards, and the P2 gate is either impossible to pass literally or will be weakened during implementation.  
   **Fix:** Compile and sanitize asks on the desktop; send only an allow-listed typed target policy and opaque IDs to the VM. Add structured VM sink allowlists and runtime redaction tests for every prohibited class. Replace the P2 criterion with “zero PII outside enumerated desktop-local stores; zero PII on VM/git,” and either store raw data inside SQLite or discard it.

6. **§ Contract 2; § Dedupe, cleaning, and attempt rules; § Compact schema; § Threat model.**  
   **Defect:** The prose promises source-value retention, conflict rows, score versions, and employment-role history, but the schema has none of those tables/fields. One scalar `source_lane` cannot preserve merged provenance; `person.company_id/title` cannot represent reviewed job changes.  
   **Failure:** A stale old-company email can remain `valid`, conflicting sources can be silently collapsed, and uncertain title/company data cannot be marked without overwriting the canonical row.  
   **Fix:** Add immutable source observations, employment roles with validity intervals, field-level confidence/timestamps, fit-score version, and a merge/conflict-review table. A role change must transactionally stale employer-dependent contacts. Gate these cases with conflicting-provider and job-change fixtures.

7. **§ Dedupe, cleaning, and attempt rules; § Campaigner tables; § Campaigner eval 8.**  
   **Defect:** The delivery idempotency key includes the scheduled instant. Rescheduling therefore creates a different key for the same logical touch. Gmail itself does not consume this local key.  
   **Failure:** After an uncertain Gmail response, a reschedule can issue the same message again under a new key.  
   **Fix:** Separate a stable logical-send key—campaign, enrollment, step, revision, recipient, mailbox—from approval/schedule scope. Reconcile a deterministic RFC `Message-ID` before any retry or reschedule.

8. **§ Contract 1 › `credit_budget`; § Provider attempts; § Enrichment budget hook; § P2.**  
   **Defect:** Credits are recorded after calls, but no reservation or maximum-call-cost field exists. Two workers can both observe one remaining credit and both make a chargeable call. The named “one-credit-short” test is sequential.  
   **Failure:** A campaign exceeds its hard credit budget and exhausts the vendor allowance despite every individual pre-call check passing.  
   **Fix:** Reserve each adapter’s declared maximum cost through an atomic conditional update, settle actual cost afterward, and record release/overage states. Add a two-worker concurrency test at one remaining credit.

\### C. Majors (should fix before build)

1. **§ Contract 1 › `target_spec`; § Finder lanes; § Manager evals.**  
   **Defect:** There is no typed predicate vocabulary, predicate-to-lane capability map, unsupported-filter behavior, or deterministic eligibility record. `fit_score` has no specified producer or threshold.  
   **Failure:** “People from X school/on X platform” may be silently approximated, and model-assigned fit can become eligibility.  
   **Fix:** Define predicate IDs/types, lane support, exact/approximate/unsupported outcomes, and an `eligibility_decision` containing rule version and failed predicates; require human override for approximations.

2. **§ Goal; § Finder lanes; § P2/P5/P6.**  
   **Defect:** The stated example needs 400 people, but LinkedIn is capped at 20 profile loads/day, PDL at 100/month, PitchBook at 10 rows/day, and no finder-run cursor/checkpoint schema exists. P2 tests only 10 records—2.5% of the contact target.  
   **Failure:** A capped or interrupted run cannot resume deterministically and may take at least 20 LinkedIn days before enrichment.  
   **Fix:** Add persistent lane cursors, restart semantics, per-lane yield estimates, completion/shortfall policy, and a synthetic 200-company/400-person end-to-end gate.

3. **§ Workflows › `reply-triage`; § Campaigner tables; § Campaign approval tiers; § P4.**  
   **Defect:** The email-manager is neither a declared agent nor a deterministic component with an input/output contract. No reply-revision table exists. T3 names `scheduling_logistics`, `thanks_ack`, and `graceful_close`, but none is an allowed `inbound.class`.  
   **Failure:** Reply drafting has nowhere to persist approval-bound bytes, and T3 cannot consume the classifier output its contract requires.  
   **Fix:** Define a reply-draft/revision contract and owner, align classification enums, specify which component sees raw message text, and add one test/gate per enabled reply class.

4. **§ Campaigner tables; § Policy defaults; § Executable hooks.**  
   **Defect:** Required stops include suppression, policy pause, approval/hash mismatch, exhausted touches, and delayed DSN, but `enrollment.stop_reason` cannot represent them.  
   **Failure:** Implementations must overload `cap`, lose the actual reason, or fail schema checks during a mandated stop.  
   **Fix:** Extend the enum or separate transient block reasons from terminal stop reasons; test every policy-default stop path.

5. **§ Policy defaults; § Goal; § P6.**  
   **Defect:** “Optimized sending times” is reduced to a fixed window plus random spacing. The spec correctly labels timing claims unverified, but supplies no experiment or selection component. Networking defaults to 5–10 new contacts/week, making 400 first touches take 40–80 weeks.  
   **Failure:** The system cannot claim optimized timing or practical execution of the example workflow.  
   **Fix:** Rename the initial behavior “bounded scheduling,” then add cohort-based timing optimization only after enough delivered/reply outcomes exist. Require Daniel to choose an explicit throughput target.

6. **§ Phased build; § P1–P6.**  
   **Defect:** “Boss suite is green” never names the command, required test count, fixtures, or timeout. Several gates use broad claims such as “zero PII” without an enumerable artifact set.  
   **Failure:** Two builders can pass different suites, and a gate cannot reliably return one reproducible pass/fail result.  
   **Fix:** Give every phase one exact command, expected test inventory, permitted skips, artifact allowlist, and numeric pass criteria.

7. **§ Goal; § Non-goals; § P3; § Policy defaults.**  
   **Defect:** Sales is described as a goal-facing use case, an intent, a policy family, and a P3 template, while simultaneously being a later separate-domain non-goal.  
   **Failure:** P3 either builds an unvalidated sales path now or ships a visible option that cannot be operated safely.  
   **Fix:** Remove sales templates/options from P1–P6 and reserve the enum, or add a separately gated sales-domain contract.

8. **§ Compact schema › `campaign`; § Contract 1.**  
   **Defect:** The schema makes `policy_hash` globally unique although the contract only defines it as an integrity hash.  
   **Failure:** Two campaigns cannot reuse identical approved policy parameters.  
   **Fix:** Remove the uniqueness constraint; index the hash and bind approvals to `(campaign_id, policy_hash)`.

4. **§ Campaign approval tiers; § P3/P6.** Defer T2/T3 reply automation and `sales-v1.txt` until the T1 live gate passes. Keep reserved enums/schema migration points, not active workflows or prompts.

5. **§ P1; § Open questions 5.** Ship local read-only Datasette first. Defer authenticated Tailscale phone serving until the desktop list workflow passes; it does not affect the first ask-to-draft success condition.
