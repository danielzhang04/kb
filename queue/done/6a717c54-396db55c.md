\## Verdict

Era B should not be reverted wholesale. Its seed-budget enforcement, ≤2 named-cast law, video-local continuity, digest pins, lint gates, and independent review stamping are improvements.

The regressions trace mainly to four changes:

1. Era B exempted first-place frames from the mandatory style seed.
2. The original fix-wave treated humans as an expensive option and converted too many human beats into objects or empty spaces.
3. It added a video-wide palette declaration capable of propagating a neutral register.
4. Its two-step compositor injects scale/perspective prose after the authored prompt.

The best repair is therefore: restore mechanical scene-style anchoring, remove the palette and scale pressure, finish the two-tier population migration, and preserve B’s structural safeguards.

\## 4. Exactly how Era A anchored style

Era A used this scene request order:

`character canonical → expression → pose → interaction template → style anchor/plate → prompt`

For the style anchor itself, the prose selection law was:

`target plate/prior chain frame > refs/env anchor matched by register > approved on-style scene`

Forge preserved the supplied seed order and placed the prompt last. Mechanically, however, it checked only that an environment/style request had at least one seed. It did not verify:

- That the chosen seed was actually a style anchor.
- That its register matched.
- That the preference hierarchy was followed.
- That the caller had not silently removed another seed before Forge saw the list.

\### How the bleed happened

The “style anchors” were full rendered scenes:

- `env-exterior-muted` was literally the Poyais swamp.
- `env-exterior-vivid` was another Poyais plate.
- `env-interior-warm` was bred from the vivid Poyais frame and depicted a period drawing room.
- `env-interior-cool` descended from the muted exterior.
- The README explicitly instructed selection by mood/palette “not the content.”

Consequently, the same pixels supplying outline and cel treatment also supplied room geometry, furniture, atmosphere, and period cues. The model had no mechanical way to use “style but not content.” [Forensic seed trace](/C:/Users/danie/kb/orgs/faceless-youtube/channels/the-second-take/videos/2026-07-28-bricks-fresh/scratchpad/forensic-seed-trace.md:256)

This was environment-content bleed, not proof that an env seed directly transferred a named character identity.

\### Could Era A’s mechanism have been fixed?

Yes—but “register-matched only” would not be enough. It would choose the most appropriate foreign room while still supplying a foreign room.

The bleed-free replacement is:

1. Keep the mandatory non-zero style anchor.
2. Replace full-scene anchors with a composition-free style card/swatches: outline weight/color, shape language, flat fills, cel-shadow treatment, and a broad range of approved channel colors—no room, furniture, characters, lettering, perspective, or narrative props.
3. Mechanically inject it on every first-place/root scene.
4. After the first approved place exists, use that video-local `place_anchor` or chain parent for continuity.
5. Let the prompt choose each scene’s palette freely inside the channel family.

That retains Era A’s strongest mechanism without its Poyais-room contamination.
