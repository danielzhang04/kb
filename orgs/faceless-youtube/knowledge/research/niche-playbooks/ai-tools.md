# Niche playbook — AI tools

*Niche-specific layer. Assumes `universal.md` (craft doctrine) + `knowledge/playbook.md` (policy).
Rewritten 2026-07-01 from deep research pass; sources at bottom.*

## Snapshot

AI tool reviews/demos, LLM news, model comparisons, agent/automation workflows, "how to use X for
Y," AI industry news, model releases, benchmark analyses. The shortest half-life of the 6 niches
(tools change monthly) with high demand. **RPM band $6–14** for generic AI news, **$9–20+** for
enterprise/B2B AI — the sleeper monetization opportunity. Generic tech content sits at $3–6 —
avoid.

**Faceless viability is CONDITIONAL for this niche.** 9 of the top 15 winners run a visible human
host. Two Minute Papers, AI Explained, Fireship prove faceless works with a structural moat
(distinctive voice + investigative brand OR an inimitable format), but generic faceless AI-news
with stock voice is the exact profile the January 2026 policy purge hit hardest.

## Dominant emotional lever (LOCK THIS AT CHANNEL LEVEL)

**Primary: paired awe-at-capability + morbid awe / dread** — the specific-to-AI double lever.
Viewers watch AI content with a **dual-valence arousal state** — thrilled and threatened by the
same demo. MKBHD called Sora "frightening" while Curious Refuge's Caleb Ward called it exciting;
both agreed it was "a massive moment" — that reveal drove the largest single wave of AI YouTube
growth in 2024. Wolfe, Berman, TheAIGRID, Wes Roth all lean on this exact combo. Ryan Doser's 2026
roundup notes AI Explained "with clarity, urgency, and investigative depth" — urgency is the dread
signal.

**Co-dominant: vindication / forbidden-knowledge / "you were lied to."** Emerging strongly 2024-
2026. Carl Brown's "Internet of Bugs" Devin debunk (~27 min) was category-defining precisely because
it framed AI vendor demos as **staged lies**; seeded a permanent skepticism narrative across the
niche. AI Explained's benchmark reality vs. marketing videos work the same lever. Fireship's
deadpan-vindication is the tightest version.

**FOMO is a title-lexicon effect, not a channel-level lever.** "This tool exists and you don't
know it" is a curiosity gap wearing FOMO clothing. Do not build a channel around FOMO.

**Outrage at job automation** exists in one-off videos and titles but no top-10 AI-tools channel
has built its brand around it — it's a *flavor* the awe/dread channels borrow. Bearish for a
whole-channel bet.

**Lock rule:** pick ONE of {**awe-at-capability**, **dread-at-implication**, **vindication-forbidden-
knowledge**} as the channel's dominant lever. Channels trying to be all three read as generic AI
news. Wes Roth = mostly awe/dread. AI Explained = mostly vindication (benchmark reality vs.
marketing). Fireship = deadpan vindication. Two Minute Papers = pure awe.

## Niche-specific title patterns

Ten patterns, each with lever.

1. **Personified vendor + violent verb.** *Lever:* awe/dread + curiosity. *Examples:* "OpenAI
   JUST announced JALAPENO," "Cursor JUST beat EVERYONE," "ChatGPT Images 2.0 Just Killed Every
   Competitor."
2. **All-caps time marker of urgency.** *Lever:* FOMO overlaid on curiosity. *Examples:* "AI News:
   An INSANE Week… Here's What Matters" (Wes Roth-style); "This Week in AI is INSANE"; "Everything
   OpenAI Just Released in 12 Days."
3. **"This changed everything" / superlative-collapse.** *Lever:* awe. *Examples:* "This Changed
   Everything About AI Video"; "The Sora Update That Changes Everything."
4. **Debunk / "the truth about X."** *Lever:* vindication / forbidden knowledge. *Examples:*
   "Debunking Devin: 'First AI Software Engineer' Upwork Lie Exposed" (Internet of Bugs); "The
   Truth About [Tool]'s Benchmark Scores."
5. **Replaced-profession + concrete number.** *Lever:* dread + curiosity. *Examples:* "I Made
   Cursor AI Work Like a $500 Devin AI Engineer"; "I Replaced My Copywriter With Claude for 30
   Days."
6. **Head-to-head shootout with named contenders.** *Lever:* tribal identity + curiosity.
   *Examples:* "Devin vs Cursor: Which AI Coding Agent Actually Ships?"; "GPT-5 vs Claude 4 vs
   Gemini 3.1: The Only Benchmark That Matters."
7. **Countdown / list-based.** *Lever:* curiosity. *Examples:* "AI Tools I Actually Use Every Day
   (2026)" (Matt Wolfe); "10 Underground AI Tools No One Is Talking About." (Note universal
   playbook: numbered lists = ~11% fewer views on average — use when countdown is intrinsic.)
8. **Cost-collapse claim.** *Lever:* awe + FOMO. *Examples:* "I Built [Thing] With AI in 2 Hours
   (Would Have Taken 6 Months)"; "This $20 AI Tool Beats $10K Enterprise Software."
9. **Rhetorical horror / dread punchline.** *Lever:* dread. *Examples:* "AI Just Did Something
   That Shouldn't Be Possible"; "This Is What Happens When You Let an Agent Run for 24 Hours."
10. **Insider-privileged framing.** *Lever:* forbidden knowledge + tribal identity. *Examples:*
    "The Prompt Every OpenAI Employee Uses"; "The Cursor Setup That Actually Works (Nobody Talks
    About This)."

**Cross-cutting title notes:** numbers, all-caps single words, and named entities (labs, tools,
models) dominate; abstract concepts underperform. Cap length at ~55 chars for mobile. Thumbnails:
shocked face + product logo + a single number (face-hosted); single high-contrast object + tool
logo (faceless). Faces showing surprise = +35% CTR, happy = +23%.

## Niche-specific hook flavors

Ten archetypes with templates.

1. **Capability shock / "this shouldn't be possible."** *Mechanism:* open on a demo output *before*
   naming the tool. Visual precedes verbal. *Template:* "This is not a real [artifact]. It was made
   in [time] by [tool]." *Examples:* MKBHD's Sora review opens on the generated clip; Two Minute
   Papers' "Look at this!"; Wes Roth's cold-opens.
2. **Named-antagonist vendor drop.** *Mechanism:* personify a company as a character. *Template:*
   "[Lab] just [aggressive verb] [rival lab] with [thing]."
3. **Debunk / "the demo was fake."** *Mechanism:* frame video as investigation into a hyped claim.
   *Template:* "[Vendor] said [claim]. Here's what actually happened when I ran it." *Example:*
   Internet of Bugs Devin debunk.
4. **Replaced-profession / job-displacement demo.** *Mechanism:* pick a concrete profession, run a
   real end-to-end task, price the AI cost against the human wage. *Template:* "I paid [tool] $X to
   do the job of a [profession] for a day. Here's what happened."
5. **Underground / "you weren't supposed to see this."** *Mechanism:* forbidden-knowledge frame;
   leaked/beta/uncommon technique. *Template:* "The prompt engineers at [lab] don't want you to
   know [X]."
6. **Before/after capability jump.** *Mechanism:* side-by-side of same task done N months apart.
   *Template:* "This is [tool] [month year]. This is [tool] [months later]. What just happened?"
   *Example:* Sora Feb 2024 vs Sora 2 Sept 2025.
7. **Consequence-first / "your competitor is already using this."** *Mechanism:* FOMO wrapped in
   curiosity. *Template:* "Every [role] I know is now using [tool] to do [thing]. If you're not,
   here's why you're falling behind."
8. **Punchline-first deadpan (Fireship-style).** *Mechanism:* deliver the take in the first
   sentence, spend the rest justifying it. *Template:* "[Tool] is dead. Or maybe you already knew
   that. Anyway…"
9. **Micro-benchmark reveal.** *Mechanism:* pose a single hard task, run against N models, reveal
   winner. *Template:* "I gave [task] to [model A], [model B], [model C]. Only one solved it."
   *Examples:* AI Explained benchmarks; Matthew Berman's open-source tests.
10. **Existential frame / "one week away."** *Mechanism:* apocalyptic-adjacent framing of an
    ordinary release. *Template:* "AI is [N] days from doing [X]. Here's the evidence."
    **Warning:** overuse without vindication substance is exactly what the July-2025 sweep flagged.

**Do NOT use as hooks:** "Today I'm going to show you how to…" (feature-tour tell), "In this video
we'll cover…" (agenda dump), "Have you ever wondered…" (dead cliché). All flat-line retention below
40% inside 30s.

## Niche episode beat-template

### Primary: 10–14 min tool-review / news-analysis

1. **0:00–0:05 — Cold-open capability shock.** Show, don't tell. Play the demo output before
   naming the tool.
2. **0:05–0:20 — Named stakes.** State who did it (lab or tool as character) and why it matters.
3. **0:20–0:40 — Curiosity gap plant.** State the question the rest of the video will answer.
   Explicitly withhold the resolution.
4. **0:40–1:30 — Context micro-arc.** How we got here in <60s. One comparison to a prior version
   or rival tool.
5. **1:30–3:30 — Setup-tension-payoff cycle 1: run the tool.** Real screen; real prompt; live
   output. Do NOT narrate every UI click.
6. **3:30–4:00 — Micro-interrupt.** Deadpan aside, meme cut, or contradiction. Prevents the 30–45s
   exit spike.
7. **4:00–6:00 — Setup-tension-payoff cycle 2: edge case / failure mode.** Show one thing the tool
   can't do. This is the *vindication earn* — it purchases the awe you'll spend later.
8. **6:00–8:00 — Cascade reveal / stacking use case.** Chain the tool with a second tool, or apply
   to a second domain. Awe compounds.
9. **8:00–9:30 — Macro re-hook.** Restate stakes with a bigger frame. Mid-video re-arm; ~55–65%
   retention checkpoint.
10. **9:30–11:00 — The withheld peak.** The best output, the wildest example, the strongest
    evidence. Hold for the final 20%.
11. **11:00–12:00 — Deadpan close + one honest limitation.** No "the lesson" sermon. Either (a) a
    next-tool tease, or (b) a one-line jab at the vendor's marketing.

### Structural variants

- **News-analysis variant** (Wes Roth / Matt Wolfe): replace live demo with clip-driven event
  coverage. Hook = the announcement itself; STP cycles are three named news beats.
- **Head-to-head shootout variant:** three tools, one task, revealed one at a time; withhold the
  winner past the mid-hook.
- **Debunk variant** (Internet of Bugs / AI Explained): hook is the claim being audited; each STP
  cycle is a specific frame-by-frame audit; payoff is cumulative distrust.
- **Workflow-build variant** (David Ondrej): hook is the end result; the video is the build;
  withhold the polished output for the final third.

## Length band (2025-2026 retention data)

- **Primary: 10–14 minutes.** Hits mid-roll monetization floor (8:00 minimum) with breathing room;
  sustains a 45–55% AVD if the beat template is honored. Videos 10–30 min generate up to 5× more
  ad revenue per view than Shorts.
- **Secondary: 18–25 minutes** for deep-dive formats (Internet of Bugs Devin debunk = 27 min; AI
  Explained benchmark investigations). Requires 7–9 STP cycles but earns 3–5 mid-roll slots. Use
  only for high-substance investigative or workflow-build content.
- **Weekly news roundup: 12–18 minutes.** Matt Wolfe's dominant. Stack of 6–10 mini-STPs on separate
  announcements — more mid-rolls per minute.
- **Shorts: 25–40s** for tutorial-Shorts; 40–60s for capability-shock demos where payoff is visual.
  Shorts are decoupled from long-form (late 2025); treat as separate top-of-funnel.

**Half-life implications.** Tools change monthly. Two consequences: (1) avoid ultra-long evergreen
tutorials on a specific tool — you're depreciating hours of work on a 3-month asset; prefer 10–14
min "current state" videos you can retire and re-do. (2) ***Format-heavy* videos (workflows, agent
builds, prompt frameworks) age slower than *tool-heavy* videos (specific-model reviews).** "How to
build an X agent" clips outlive "review of GPT-5" clips because the framework is portable, the
model isn't.

**Mid-roll floor:** 8:00 non-negotiable. In AI-tools with $10–15 CPM (top-quintile niche), that's
the difference between a viable channel and a hobby.

## Sub-niches with untapped angles

1. **Vertical AI-vs-jobs, one profession at a time.** Generic "AI is coming for your job" is
   saturated. Undersaturated: a channel that picks *one* profession per video and runs a real
   end-to-end test — paralegal, radiologist, junior copywriter, bookkeeper, tier-1 support agent.
   Real time + cost data. Awe/dread lever, vindication as anchor, uses credibility deficit
   *against* rival slop.
2. **Enterprise / B2B AI workflow coverage.** B2B SaaS content is a $9–14 RPM niche vs $3–6 for
   generic AI news — 2–4× the revenue per view. Underserved because less viral but higher-CLV.
   Sub-topics: agent-ops, procurement of AI tools inside Fortune 500, MLOps, evals-in-production,
   prompt-security. Buyer intent > entertainment intent; algorithm has been forgiving on niche B2B.
3. **AI failure-mode / limitation coverage.** Every AI channel shows what works; almost none run
   repeatable failure-case investigations. Owns the vindication lever cleanly. Long-tail SEO on
   "why does [model] fail at X" is largely uncontested. Aligns perfectly with July-2025 policy —
   you're literally demonstrating original substance.
4. **Model-release autopsy.** Post-launch analysis (3–4 weeks after) of what a release *actually*
   delivered vs. launch hype. E.g., revisiting Devin, GPT-5, Sora 2, Claude Opus 5 six weeks after
   each launched. Vindication lever, timeless-ish, differentiates from day-of reaction crowd.
5. **Workflow-not-tool coverage.** Instead of "here's Cursor," you get "here's the specific
   end-to-end junior-engineer workflow that Cursor + Claude Code + a linter + a CI trigger enables."
   Workflows age slower than tools.
6. **Historical AI retrospectives.** "The year GPT-3 changed everything" / "what OpenAI looked like
   in 2019." Awe + tribal-identity + nostalgia. Very cheap to produce faceless; archive material
   free. Under-touched because "history" feels off-brand in a hype niche — that's the arbitrage.
7. **AI-safety-adjacent investigation, non-doomer.** Doomer AI-safety is culturally exhausted. But
   *specific* safety failures with named vendors — jailbreaks, data-exfiltration bugs, model-
   behavior anomalies — are under-covered by non-safety-professional channels.

**Pick 1–2. More than 2 breaks the one-lever channel rule.**

## Original signature-format opportunities

1. **"The Benchmark."** Standardized recurring head-to-head shootout. Same set of 3–5 real tasks
   run against every new model release. Publish the fixture publicly. Each video is one release;
   the recurring structure builds a comparable time-series audience can quote. Precedent: AI
   Explained's SimpleBench does the DNA; a proprietary version can be lower-brow (build-a-webapp
   / write-a-legal-brief / plan-a-trip / debug-a-repo). CCN-friendly.
2. **"The Replaced Job."** One profession per episode. Real deliverable, real deadline, real budget.
   Pay a human once to establish baseline. Then run the AI. Publish both side by side + judgment
   from a domain expert. Extends into a natural companion Short: 30-sec "the AI got X right and Y
   wrong" clip.
3. **"The Autopsy."** 4–6 weeks after every major AI launch, a "what actually shipped vs what they
   promised" video. Recurring beat: (a) rewatch the launch demo, (b) run the same demo on current
   API, (c) score the deltas, (d) name the marketing choice that overstated. **Unusually protected
   from inauthentic-content policy** because each episode is intrinsically original analysis.
4. **"The Failure Case."** Weekly "one thing this model still can't do." Curiosity + vindication.
   Low production cost (single-take screencap), replicable. Doubles as an evergreen SEO-tail library.
5. **"The Underground Workflow."** Monthly deep-dive on a workflow that's genuinely uncommon — a
   stack of 3–5 tools used together in a non-obvious way. Uses named-antagonist framing against
   standard tutorial channels ("everyone shows you Zapier + ChatGPT; here's what actually works").

**Pick 1 as the flagship recurring show.** Signature format = reason viewers hit subscribe rather
than just watch one video.

## Niche policy quirks & accuracy gates

**The July-15-2025 "inauthentic content" gate.** For an AI-tools channel this is a knife's edge:
your subject matter *is* AI, so any AI-in-production is triple-scrutinized.
- **What passes:** AI as a *tool* under a human editorial spine (original commentary, original
  perspective, original evidence).
- **What fails:** stock TTS narration + stock b-roll + a rewritten Wikipedia article + no POV.

**On-camera-face algorithmic tailwind (2025-2026).** YouTube's algorithm was retuned to favor human
on-camera hosts. On-camera creators surfaced at higher CPM tiers; off-camera + AI-narrated content
deprioritized. **Practical implication:** a fully faceless AI-tools channel competes against a
tuned bias. Budget for that in expected reach.

**Vendor defamation risk (moderate).** Rising but not catastrophic. Walters v. OpenAI (2023-2025) —
first AI-defamation case — resulted in **summary judgment for OpenAI** in May 2025. For an AI-tools
reviewer:
- Never state as fact that a vendor's model "committed" or "was designed for" a crime/fraud.
- Source claims about capabilities to demos you can reproduce on the record.
- Flag "our observation, not vendor claim" language on benchmark videos.
- For negative reviews of paid tools, keep receipts (screencaps, prompts, output logs).

**YMYL adjacencies.** AI in medicine, AI in legal, AI in finance/tax cross into YMYL heightened
scrutiny. "I used ChatGPT to diagnose my rash" formats are demonetized or age-gated at higher
rates. If covering verticals: "not medical/legal/financial advice; verify with a qualified
professional." Cheap insurance.

**Deepfake / cameo / voice-clone traps (Sora 2 era).** Do NOT use likenesses of real people (dead
or alive) in demos without documented consent. Sora 2 rollout drove multiple family-of-deceased
backlash cycles; those videos get purged. Sora 2's "cameo" feature adds a new litigation surface —
don't rely on it in production content.

**Training-data disclosure hygiene.** If you use AI to generate any visual, disclose it. YouTube's
synthetic-content disclosure is enforced; undisclosed AI visuals *in a video about AI* is a
credibility kill and a policy violation.

**Accuracy gate — internal.** Every capability claim in a script gets a "how do we know?" tag before
publish. Options: (a) reproducible demo in this video, (b) vendor documentation link, (c)
third-party benchmark, (d) opinion (labeled). Anything at (d) that reads like (a-c) is the exact
overpromise-underdeliver failure mode that tanks credibility.

## Faceless viability — direct answer

**Short answer: viable, narrow, and conditional.**

**Evidence for viability.** AI Explained (Philip) ~380–420K subs, voice-only. Two Minute Papers
1.6–1.8M, voice-only. Fireship 4.2M, faceless. TheAIGRID 370–395K faceless (but stalling —
growth deceleration coincides with the policy shift).

**Evidence against pure-live-footage AI-gen viability.** YouTube retuned in 2025 to favor
on-camera hosts. Faceless voice reviewing AI tools has to fight two audience biases: (1) is this
an AI reviewing an AI? (2) can this voice back up its claim? The January 2026 purge
disproportionately hit faceless AI-narrated channels; the 16 terminated averaged ~2.2M subs each.

**Minimum viable presence for faceless AI-tools content in 2026:**

1. **Premium human-sounding TTS or a real recorded human voice** with a signature attribute —
   accent, catchphrase, deadpan cadence, or all three. Stock ElevenLabs default = death.
2. **A locked-in dominant lever** (from top of this file).
3. **A visual signature that isn't stock b-roll** — custom animation style (Fireship), distinctive
   on-screen investigative layout (AI Explained), or dense on-screen text/data annotations (Two
   Minute Papers).
4. **Original evidence in every video** — reproducible benchmarks, real screencaps, or original
   analysis you'd defend in a comment thread. This is the July-2025 policy shield.
5. **A named creator identity behind the voice.** Even AI Explained is "Philip." Anonymity +
   faceless is the exact profile the purge targets.
6. **Human-hand cameo option.** Fast-growing hybrid: hands on keyboard, hands on paper, hands on a
   laptop, no face. Cheaply crosses the on-camera-signal bar.

**Recommended tier for this project:** voice-forward faceless with **hand cameos and screencap-
first visuals**, pointed at ONE lever (vindication or awe), producing recurring original evidence
(a Benchmark or Autopsy signature format).

## Anti-patterns

1. **Feature-tour tutorials with no POV.** Zero retention past 90s. Exactly the shape the algorithm
   is now punishing.
2. **Hype-cycle chasing** (publishing "OpenAI JUST announced X" *before* you've verified). Viewers
   can smell copy-paste tweets read aloud.
3. **Overpromising in the title what the demo can't deliver.** "AI Wrote My Entire App in 10
   Minutes" → demo shows a broken todo list. Trust-collapse; retention nosedive at the reveal.
4. **Doomer / "AGI is 6 months away" every video.** Berman has publicly noted negative AI content
   underperforms for him. Once-a-quarter existential frame is fine; every video, audience exhausted
   and algorithm suspicious.
5. **Generic AI-narrated news roundup with stock b-roll.** Signature shape of the January 2026
   purge. If your video could have been generated by a competitor with the same prompt, it will be.
6. **Undisclosed AI-generated visuals in a video *about* AI vendors.** Uniquely damaging: you're
   calling out other people's hype using undisclosed AI. Multiple mid-tier channels have lost
   sponsorships this way.
7. **Reviewing a paid tool with an active affiliate link and no disclosure.** FTC + YouTube-policy.
   Common in AI-tools because affiliate programs are lucrative; also fastest way to a takedown.
8. **Templated series clones.** "10 AI Tools for [X]" repeated across 30 verticals with the same
   script. Textbook July-2025 target.
9. **Fake benchmarks.** Cherry-picked prompts framed as head-to-head. Low-tier channels do it and
   get called out by the comment section.
10. **"The lesson" close.** Ending with "so what this means for you is…" reads like corporate
    LinkedIn copy. Deadpan close beats sermon close.

## Worked example (adapt-safe)

### Video: "Four AI Agents Ran My Business for a Week (Only One Was Worth It)"

**Target length:** 12 minutes. Locks vindication (primary) + dread-flavored awe (secondary).

**Why-original / policy-safe:** Real business, real transactions, real dollar figures, real named
agents, first-person reproducible tests, one net-positive result and three failures. Exact opposite
shape of the templated "top 10 AI tools" video the July-2025 policy targets.

**Opening line (0:00–0:05):** *[Cold cut: a Slack DM from an "agent" politely refunding a customer
$2,400 the human never authorized.]* "This message cost me twenty-four hundred dollars. And I
didn't send it."

**Beat structure:**
- 0:05–0:20 — Named stakes: four agents (Devin-successor, Claude Code, an n8n workflow, a Zapier+GPT
  pipeline), one small e-commerce business, seven days, one human (me) sleeping.
- 0:20–0:45 — Curiosity plant: three of the four broke something; one saved my week.
- 0:45–1:30 — Context: 60s on why "agents can run a business now" became a claim in 2026 + why
  nobody's stress-tested it end-to-end.
- 1:30–3:30 — STP1: Agent A (customer support). Graceful win + the $2,400 refund catastrophe.
  Vindication earn.
- 3:30–3:50 — Micro-interrupt: deadpan aside about how the agent's apology email was better than
  mine would have been.
- 3:50–5:30 — STP2: Agent B (inventory/reorder). Real reorder arrived on time. Small awe beat.
- 5:30–7:00 — STP3: Agent C (marketing/email). Ran a campaign; open rate above baseline; subject
  line the agent chose was on the edge of a compliance rule I hadn't documented.
- 7:00–8:30 — STP4: Agent D (bookkeeping). Categorized 400 transactions in 6 minutes; got 12 wrong;
  one of the wrong ones would have failed an audit.
- 8:30–9:30 — Macro re-hook: score card. One agent net positive, three net negative. "This is what
  'AI can run your business' actually looks like in June 2026."
- 9:30–11:00 — **The withheld peak:** the ONE agent that was worth it (Agent B), plus the exact
  reason (narrow domain + human confirmation loop). Show the confirmation-loop screen.
- 11:00–12:00 — Close: honest limitation ("this doesn't scale to a services business yet") + tease
  next video.

### Shorts bench (7 clip angles)
