# Niche playbook — Engineering disasters / "why it failed"

*Niche-specific layer. Assumes `universal.md` (craft doctrine) + `knowledge/playbook.md` (policy).
Rewritten 2026-07-01 from deep research pass; sources at bottom.*

## Snapshot

Structural failures, industrial accidents, aviation/maritime disasters, catastrophic engineering
decisions, "how X failed," near-misses, root-cause investigations. Natively long-form —
Fascinating Horror, Practical Engineering, Brick Immortar, Plainly Difficult all sustain 15–45+
min at strong retention. Brick Immortar averages ~47 min. **RPM band $5–15** (mid-range with
premium slots for aviation/industrial B2B advertisers).

**Two sizes work — nothing in the middle.** Cinematic single-story channels at 1–5M subs
(Fascinating Horror, Mustard, Green Dot Aviation) or deep-investigative channels at 300K–800K subs
(Brick Immortar, Plainly Difficult, WTYP). **Pick the size band before writing dna.md.**

## Dominant emotional lever (LOCK THIS AT CHANNEL LEVEL)

**Primary: morbid awe / dread — vicarious body-in-extremity at engineered scale.** Fascinating
Horror is the archetype: a calm, non-sensationalized narrator walks you through an entirely mundane-
looking system (a hotel walkway, a scaffold, a cooling tower) until the moment it kills people.
The dread is not gore — it is the audience realizing the everyday object around them is a *pressure
vessel* one bad weld away from disaster. Scrivner's morbid-curiosity framework (dangerous-minds +
bodily-injuries + interpersonal-violence dimensions) predicts why this format retains: it feeds
evolved threat-simulation systems safely. **Strip the gore, keep the dread — retention climbs.**

**Co-dominant: vindication / forbidden-knowledge — "the warning nobody listened to."** Almost every
landmark engineering disaster has a whistleblower-ignored-by-management substructure: Roger Boisjoly
on Challenger, William LeMessurier's late-night Citicorp confession, the Champlain Towers 2018
structural report, the OceanGate engineering director's memos. The lever is *moral clarity*: the
physics was right, the humans failed, the audience gets to feel the vindication of the engineer who
was correct.

**Tertiary — finisher only: awe at the scale of failure.** Not "wonder" (Bright Side lever) —
closer to *sublime dread*. The Chernobyl elephant's foot, the Boeing 737 MAX's global grounding,
the Bhopal death toll. Mustard operates awe-of-engineering; Kyle Hill's Half-Life Histories operates
awe-of-radiological-scale. **This lever is the finisher, not the opener** — it's what earns the
withheld-peak in the final 20%.

**Deprioritized:** righteous anger works when a named villain exists but does not sustain a whole
channel — it burns hot and fatigues. **Schadenfreude is off-brand** — engineering-disasters channels
lose credibility the moment they enjoy the deaths.

## Niche-specific title patterns

Eight patterns, ranked by observed frequency at the top of the niche.

1. **"The [Disaster Name]: [Emotional or Numerical Tag]"** — canonical Fascinating Horror / Brick
   Immortar / Plainly Difficult format. *Lever:* authority/credibility. Best for evergreen search.
   *Examples:* "The Hyatt Regency Walkway Collapse | A Short Documentary"; "The Halifax Explosion
   | A Short Documentary."
2. **"Why the X Y'd"** — Practical Engineering's owned formula. Question-answer framing. *Lever:*
   curiosity gap + smart-feeling puzzle. *Examples:* "Why the Tacoma Narrows Bridge Collapsed";
   "Why Did the Champlain Towers Collapse?"
3. **"How X Ended in Disaster" / "How X Failed"** — Mentour Pilot's dominant. *Lever:* mechanism
   reveal + forbidden knowledge. *Examples:* "Fatal Error! How UPS Flight 1354 Ended in Disaster"
   (941K); "How Oceangate's Titan Ignored 60 Years of Solid Engineering."
4. **Comparison Hijack ("X's Y" / "The Y That Y'd")** — schema-piggyback; huge CTR from mixed
   feeds. *Examples:* "Japan's Chernobyl" (Kyle Hill, multi-million); "The Soviet Concorde: TU-144"
   (Mustard); "The Plane That Couldn't Land" (Green Dot).
5. **Negation / Reverse-Value ("Why You Wouldn't Want to…")** — morbid curiosity framed as
   bragging-rights knowledge. *Example:* "Why You Wouldn't Want to Fly On The Soviet Concorde"
   (Mustard, multi-million evergreen).
6. **Timeframe / Real-Time Tag ("Minute by Minute," "Second by Second," "T-minus")** — emerging
   genre marker for 2024-2026. *Lever:* countdown/dread + retention promise. *Examples:* "The
   Titan Sub Disaster: Minute by Minute" (Brick Immortar); "MH370 Disappearance: Minute by
   Minute" (Nov 2024).
7. **The Named-Villain-Object ("The [Object] That Killed [N]")** — personification (object as
   antagonist). *Examples:* "The Demon Core" (Kyle Hill, 6M+); "The Plane That Was Designed to
   Crash" (Mustard variant).
8. **"What Really Happened To…"** — vindication + forbidden knowledge, retro-correction. Best for
   topics where public media narrative was incomplete. *Examples:* "What Really Happened To The
   Titan Sub?" (Brick Immortar); "The REAL Story of Malaysia Airlines Flight 17" (Mentour, 650K).

**Combine well:** #4 × #7 ("Japan's Demon Core") stacks two curiosity vectors and is under-used.

**Anti-patterns:** ALL-CAPS "SHOCKING" or "You Won't Believe" — kills the niche's trust equity
instantly. Fascinating Horror deliberately does NOT do these.

## Niche-specific hook flavors

Seven archetypes with templates. All ≤7s to first payload.

1. **Timestamp Cold Open.** *Mechanism:* anchors the audience in a specific moment. Instant
   "documentary-ness." Bypasses "why should I care" by pretending we're already inside the story.
   *Template:* "On the [ordinal] of [month] [year], at [time], [mundane scene where nothing appears
   wrong]." Two banal details. Then: "By [time+X hours], [fatal outcome, quantified]."
   *Faceless fit:* perfect.
2. **The Artifact Reveal.** *Mechanism:* show the failed component first, decontextualized.
   Curiosity gap: "what is this thing and why is it terrifying?" *Template:* silent macro-shot of
   the failed part (Hyatt's rod-through-nut, Titan's carbon-fiber hull segment, Challenger's
   O-ring). One-line VO: "This [ordinary object] killed [N] people. This is how." *Faceless fit:*
   excellent.
3. **The Single Doomed Decision.** *Mechanism:* vindication lever. Names the decision that killed
   everyone in the first 5 seconds. *Template:* "If [name/role] had said 'no' at [specific
   meeting/moment], [N] people would still be alive. He didn't."
4. **The Warning Nobody Listened To.** *Mechanism:* forbidden-knowledge frame; establishes
   audience-as-detective. *Template:* "[Engineer name] wrote a memo on [date] that said [quoted
   concern]. [N months later], [disaster]. This is the memo they buried." Show the memo image.
5. **Countdown to Impact.** *Mechanism:* structural retention device. Every scene is timestamped in
   the corner. *Template:* opens with "T-minus [time] to [disaster name]." HUD-style overlay
   throughout. "Minute by Minute" is now a genre keyword.
6. **The Comparison Hook ("X's Y").** *Mechanism:* rides existing audience schema. "Japan's
   Chernobyl," "The Space Shuttle of the Ocean." *Template:* "This is [country/domain]'s [famous
   disaster]. You've never heard of it." Compresses two units of schema into one thumbnail concept.
7. **The Casual Detail That Killed Them.** *Mechanism:* morbid awe — the *disproportion* between
   cause and effect. *Template:* "This [trivial thing: a bolt, a switch, a memo footnote, a 4mm
   crack] killed [N] people." *Examples:* Aloha 737 fatigue crack; Alaska 261's jackscrew grease;
   Hyatt hanger-rod change; Challenger O-ring.

**Bonus pattern (not a hook per se):** the *sponsor-adjacent cold open* where the video opens on
a normal-life scene (someone checking into a hotel, boarding a plane) *before* it says "on the
date this hotel/plane killed them." Fascinating Horror does this. It's the "meanwhile at the same
time…" construction in disaster form.

## Niche episode beat-template

### Primary: Cinematic Single-Story (12–30 min)

- **00:00–00:05 — Hook.** One of the 7 above. Burned-in caption. Must contain death count OR a
  "you've never heard this" promise, but NOT the mechanism.
- **00:05–00:30 — Establishing normalcy.** The ordinary hotel/plane/factory/reactor. Two banal
  images.
- **00:30–01:00 — The ordinary object made sinister.** First reveal of the failed component in its
  *pre-failure* context. Caption: "This [object] would fail in [N] hours."
- **01:00–02:00 — Deep context #1: who built it, when, why.** Doomed-machine biography. Personify
  the institution (Boeing as a character; NASA as a character).
- **02:00–04:00 — Rising action: the warnings.** The memo. The whistleblower. The inspector's
  report. Establish *vindication substrate* early.
- **04:00–06:00 — MID-VIDEO RE-HOOK (mandatory).** Cascading revelation: "But this wasn't the only
  warning." Withheld tease: "The morning of the disaster, one of the engineers made a phone call."
- **06:00–08:00 — Personification of institutions.** The org chart as a character. Who signed off.
  Who overruled whom. Deadpan aside okay here.
- **08:00–11:00 — The disaster itself.** Chronological. Timestamps in-scene. **No casualty-imagery.**
  Focus on the *system* failing (pipes bursting, reactor scramming, wings shedding). The scale-of-
  failure lever pays off HERE, not at the end.
- **11:00–13:00 — Immediate aftermath.** Casualty *count*, not casualty imagery. Rescue effort.
  First-responder witness where quotable.
- **13:00–16:00 — The investigation.** Root cause revealed. *Smart-feeling* payoff — audience
  finally gets the technical mechanism. Extended single analogy (90–120s per doctrine) belongs
  here.
- **16:00–19:00 — MACRO RE-HOOK at 55–65% of runtime.** Withheld peak: "But there was a second
  thing the investigators found. Something they buried in the appendix."
- **19:00–24:00 — The consequences.** Regulatory changes. Trial. Named-antagonist accountability
  moment (or its absence — righteous-anger lever).
- **24:00–26:00 — Ongoing echo.** Where the survivor lives now. What the site looks like today.
  The object in the museum. Withheld peak in final 20% per doctrine.
- **26:00–27:00 — Sign-off.** Fascinating Horror's line ("…and to see what we can learn from the
  mistakes of the past") is canonical. **NEVER a moralistic "the lesson is…"** — the emotion IS
  the payoff.

Micro-interrupt every 30–45s: image cut + text overlay of a new fact. Burned-in captions throughout.

### Structural variants

- **Variant A — Chronological Reconstruction** (Fascinating Horror / Brick Immortar). Straight
  linear from T-minus to aftermath. Best for events with well-documented timelines. Retention =
  countdown tension. Length: 15–30 min.
- **Variant B — Root-Cause Investigation** (Practical Engineering). Opens on failure, then unwinds
  backward. Half the video is the investigation, not the event. Best when mechanism > deaths
  (Tacoma Narrows resonance, water hammer, MCAS logic). Length: 12–20 min. Uses physical-demo
  interstitials.
- **Variant C — The Doomed-Machine Biography** (Mustard, Kyle Hill's Half-Life Histories). The
  disaster is the third act; first two acts are the machine's design and life story. Emotional
  lever: awe + tragedy. Best for iconic objects (Titan, Concorde, Chernobyl RBMK, Demon Core).
  Length: 15–25 min. Very rewatchable, very evergreen.
- **Variant D — The Near-Miss Autopsy** (untapped). The disaster that ALMOST happened. Reader-as-
  detective explicitly framed. Ends on the *coincidence* that saved lives. This is a claimable
  format.

## Length band (2025-2026 retention data)

- **Primary band: 18–28 minutes** — sweet spot for a new AI-gen entrant. Justification:
  - Every video ≥8 min unlocks manual mid-rolls; the algorithm rewards demonstrated ability to hold
    viewers through them.
  - The 30-min inflection: Brick Immortar (47 min avg) and WTYP (90 min+) exist but succeed on
    parasocial trust a new channel does NOT have yet. **Ceiling for cold-start faceless AI-gen:
    ~30 min** before retention curves collapse.
  - The 15-min floor: below 15 min you cannot fit the setup-tension-payoff × 5–7 cycles the doctrine
    requires. Fascinating Horror's ~10–15 min works BECAUSE the calm-voice brand is already trusted.
- **Secondary band: 8–12 min (Shorts-adjacent long-form)** for "explainer" episodes (mechanism-
  focused, no deaths) — dam spillway design, why bridges hum, how a reactor scrams. Build channel
  breadth and cross-recommend into disaster videos.
- **Ad density:** 30+ min disaster docs run 4–6 mid-rolls at natural breakpoints (pre-hook, post-
  context ~2:30, before mid re-hook ~55%, pre-climax ~75%, post-consequences ~90%). Brick Immortar-
  style 47-min docs run 6–8. **Never place a mid-roll during the mid-video re-arm** (55–65%) — that's
  the retention save moment.

Shorts (per doctrine): decoupled from long-form late 2025. In this niche, Shorts serve *discovery*
only, not lift. Use for artifact-reveal micro-clips (H2 hook, 45–60s) driving to long-form.

## Sub-niches with untapped angles

1. **Maritime disasters (still growing).** Brick Immortar owns the investigative lane; the
   *cinematic Green-Dot-Aviation-style* lane for maritime is empty. Green Dot's format (no host,
   in-scene text overlays, cinematic 3D) would print for lost-at-sea stories: MS Estonia, Ocean
   Ranger, Kursk, Sewol, MOL Comfort break-up, 2024 Baltimore Dali collision. The 3D reconstruction
   of a sinking ship is a stronger visual than a crashing plane and has fewer competitors.
2. **Non-Western failures / "the disasters your textbook skipped."** American and European disasters
   saturate the niche. Under-covered: **Banqiao dam failure** (China 1975, ~171K dead — the world's
   worst engineering disaster and it has almost no long-form English YouTube presence); Vajont Dam
   (Italy, undercovered outside Italian YT); the Kyshtym disaster (Soviet nuclear); Bhopal (covered
   but not with modern 3D); Sampoong Department Store (South Korea); Rana Plaza; Beirut port
   explosion (mostly as news, no 30-min investigative).
3. **Near-miss analysis / precursor disasters.** The disaster that almost happened, and the disaster
   it was later a precursor to. Apollo 13; Three Mile Island (near-miss to Chernobyl-scale); Gimli
   Glider; British Airways 5390 (window blowout, pilot survived hanging out of the plane); Millennium
   Bridge wobble; 2003 Northeast blackout. Untapped: pairing a near-miss with the disaster that *did*
   happen when the same warning was ignored (Three Mile Island → Chernobyl; Aloha 243 → Alaska 261).
   S-tier vindication content.
4. **Consumer product recalls at scale.** Almost no engineering-disaster channel covers this. Ford
   Pinto fuel-tank; Firestone/Ford Explorer rollovers; Takata airbags; Samsung Note 7; Cybertruck-
   panel-flying-off. Compact stories, tight mechanisms, righteous-anger built-in. Best for 15–20 min.
5. **Software/systems disasters (the modern lane).** Therac-25 (radiation-therapy software bugs
   that killed patients); Knight Capital's $440M/45-minute algorithmic-trading collapse; Boeing
   737 MAX MCAS as an explicitly software-first story; 2024 CrowdStrike outage; Ariane 5 Flight
   501 (metric-unit rocket loss). Underserved because most disaster-doc channels are staffed by
   non-software-natives.

**Anniversary content:** many disasters have upcoming anniversaries in 2026-2027 (Fukushima 15th
March 2026 fresh; Titanic 115th April 2027; Bhopal 42nd Dec 2026). Anniversary videos have
algorithmic tailwind but are heavily contested — book them 6 months in advance.

## Original signature-format opportunities

1. **"The Autopsy" — 5-vector standardized failure model.** Every episode ends with the same
   rubric: **Design flaw · Manufacturing shortcut · Operational error · Regulatory failure ·
   Cultural pressure.** Each vector gets a red/yellow/green light. Signature graphic gets
   screencapped and shared. Solves the July-2025 "inauthentic content" test easily because the
   *analysis* varies even if the frame is standardized.
2. **"Reading the Report" — line-by-line NTSB/CSB/AAIB walkthrough.** Split-screen: the actual
   report page on the left, animated reconstruction on the right. Narrator reads the report as it
   was written, then translates. Vibe: annotated code review, but for disasters. Trust equity
   signal: "we're not making it up, here's the paragraph."
3. **"The Fix That Wasn't Applied" (the near-miss precursor).** Serialized format. Every episode
   pairs a near-miss (that generated a safety recommendation) with the later disaster that happened
   when the recommendation was ignored. Aloha 243 → Alaska 261. Three Mile Island → Chernobyl. This
   is the *forbidden-knowledge* lever operationalized into a serial.
4. **"The Timeline That Should Not Exist" (the coincidence video).** Focus on the *thin margin* —
   the phone call at 3:47 AM that saved everyone; the shift change that put a competent engineer in
   the seat by luck. Pure morbid-awe lever plus wonder. No named villain. Sensitive to handle but
   very high emotional payoff.

**Bench:** an "Anniversary Vault" format where you rebuild a classic disaster with modern 3D
reconstruction every year on the anniversary. Requires strong production but a small number of
"anniversary" reruns amortize huge amounts of research.

## Niche policy quirks & accuracy gates

**The Analysis-Not-Gore covenant** — load-bearing brand pillar. Fascinating Horror built its
audience explicitly on "in-depth research, respectful storytelling, avoiding sensationalism or
disturbing imagery." Break it and you drop a tier:

- **Never render body-in-extremity imagery**, even in animation.
- **Casualty counts are stated as numbers once**, not repeated for shock.
- **No first-person witness audio of the moment of death.** Air France 447 cockpit voice recorder
  is the sensitive edge case — most winning channels omit or paraphrase; do not use raw.
- **No zoomed-in imagery of human remains**, even at monument sites (elephant's foot photograph
  = maximum acceptable; further edge cases require case-by-case).

**Defamation risk.** Named engineers and companies are trickiest:
- Publicly named in the official investigation report = safe (Boisjoly, LeMessurier, Lochridge,
  Rush).
- Named in credible investigative journalism (WSJ, ProPublica, NYT) = usually safe, attribute the
  claim to the source.
- Speculation that a specific *named-but-not-publicly-identified* engineer caused the disaster =
  defamation risk. Withdraw name if the report used a job title, not a name.
- Corporations: fair game to describe factual actions from public record. Avoid unsourced motive
  claims. "Boeing wanted to kill people to save money" — no. "Boeing chose to avoid a costly
  re-certification, resulting in the MCAS design that killed 346 people" — yes, with source.
- Living whistleblowers: use published quotes only. Don't paraphrase their internal thoughts.

**Anniversary sensitivity.** Anniversary content generates algorithmic tailwind and press pickup,
but survivors' communities are active on those dates and misrepresentations get called out
publicly. **Rule: publish the anniversary video 3–7 days before the anniversary**, never on the day
itself, and never with a "celebration"-adjacent thumbnail. Fascinating Horror times releases around
anniversaries but does not thumbnail-lead with the date.

**AI-content disclosure (July 2025 policy).** Since we're building a faceless AI-gen entrant:
- Premium TTS (ElevenLabs or equivalent), never robot-sounding voice.
- Visual variety per video: no single reusable disaster template.
- YouTube's "altered content" disclosure toggle enabled when using synthetic voice.
- **Do not use AI-generated photorealistic imagery of real deceased people.** Both policy-risky
  and reputation-suicidal.

**Source hygiene.** Trust is built or broken by whether you survive a comments section that
includes actual engineers:
- Every technical claim tied to a named source (NTSB report number; CSB video; peer-reviewed
  paper; primary reporting).
- Prefer *the official investigation report* over Wikipedia. Report bibliography in the
  description.
- Number safety: get the death count right on the first stated. Corrections stick.
- Metric/imperial: state both if the unit was implicated in the failure (Gimli Glider, Mars Climate
  Orbiter).

**Ideological framing.** WTYP operates a leftist-labor frame explicitly and gets away with it
because the podcast structure signals opinion. A single-narrator documentary channel that inserts
ideological framing loses the "objective" trust equity Fascinating Horror has weaponized. **Rule
for our dna.md: the systemic critique lands harder when the narrator refuses to editorialize.**

## Anti-patterns

1. **Casualty gore.** Body imagery, morgue footage, blood, close-ups of injuries. Cheapens the
   channel; also violates YouTube's mid-2020s crackdown on graphic content in monetized long-form.
   Rule: if the shot would upset a survivor's family, cut.
2. **Sensational thumbnail language** ("SHOCKING," "You Won't Believe," ALL CAPS, red arrows,
   screaming faces). The winning channels don't do it because it inverts the brand. The audience is
   here for the *calm, authoritative* frame.
3. **Conspiracy framing when the truth is boring engineering.** MH370 has a temptation to lean into
   "government cover-up"; the winning version is *the engineering and operational analysis*, not
   the conspiracy. Same for 9/11-adjacent structural topics, JFK-adjacent, TWA 800. Boring
   engineering IS the payoff.
4. **"The lesson is…" moralistic closer.** Fascinating Horror's "and to see what we can learn from
   the mistakes of the past" works because it's *implicit-lesson* — it lets the viewer supply the
   moral themselves.
5. **Over-narration without the technical mechanic.** Reciting Wikipedia in a nice voice with stock
   footage. This is the AI-slop failure mode. If your video could be replaced by reading the
   Wikipedia article, delete it. Include the diagram. Include the physics. Include the 90–120s
   extended analogy.
6. **Cross-lever channel.** A channel that does aviation this week, radiological next week,
   financial fraud next week, and cults after that reads as slop. Winning channels pick ONE domain
   (aviation, maritime, nuclear, structural) or ONE lever (dread of ordinary objects).
7. **Naming the wrong perpetrator.** Defamation risk but also credibility risk. If you're not sure
   a person was named in the report, use their role instead. "The night-shift operator" > "Aleksandr
   Akimov" if you can't verify.
8. **Re-uploading the disaster with a different thumbnail every 6 months.** July-2025 policy is
   specifically written to catch this. If Titan-Sub or MH370 keeps drawing views, the winning move
   is a *different angle* (the whistleblower, the near-miss, the retrofit), not a re-tread.
9. **Real-time news chasing.** When Baltimore-Dali happens, news-adjacent creators upload within 48
   hours. The winning long-form disaster channel *waits for the preliminary NTSB report* (~30 days)
   then publishes an investigative piece.
10. **Nihilism.** "Everything is broken, we're all going to die." A subset of engineering-disaster
    content drifts here. Doesn't compound; audience burns out. Keep even the darkest videos
    anchored in *specificity*: this bolt, this decision, this rule that got written.

## Worked example (adapt-safe)

### Video: "The Building That Warned Itself — The Citicorp Center Crisis"

**Format:** Structural Variant D (Near-Miss Autopsy). Length: 22 minutes. Locks morbid awe + vindication.

**Adapt-safe originality:** No large channel has published a modern investigative-cinematic version.
Fascinating Horror covered it in ~2021 (short doc). Practical Engineering has referenced it.
**Nobody has done a 20-minute cinematic single-story treatment with the vindication frame front
and center.**

**One-line pitch:** In 1978, a Princeton undergraduate found a fatal flaw in the newest skyscraper
in New York — and the engineer who designed it agreed. What happened next was a covert, city-scale
race against a hurricane season that nobody told the public about.

**Opening line (Timestamp + Warning-Nobody-Listened-To stack):**
> "On the night of July 30th, 1978, a 51-story skyscraper in Midtown Manhattan was silently being
> welded back together. The people who worked in it had not been told. The people who lived
> downwind of it had not been told. The engineer who had designed the building had made a phone
> call four weeks earlier that changed everything."

**Beat structure (22 min):**

- 00:00–00:05 — Hook.
- 00:05–00:35 — Establishing normalcy: 1977 photos of Citicorp Center opening. Engineering-award
  graphics. Skyline shots. "The building had won awards for its innovative design." (Foreshadow.)
- 00:35–02:00 — Ordinary Object Made Sinister: the cantilevered corner columns. Extended analogy:
  think of an umbrella held by its four ribs instead of its central shaft.
- 02:00–04:30 — Doomed-machine biography: William LeMessurier. Welded joints. Value-engineering
  change to *bolted* joints after his firm handed off the design.
- 04:30–05:30 — MID-VIDEO RE-HOOK: "In June 1978, a Princeton engineering undergraduate named
  Diane Hartley did the math on this building for her thesis. She found something LeMessurier's
  own team had missed."
- 05:30–09:00 — The math: quartering winds vs. perpendicular winds. Cascading revelation: building
  was safe for a 55-year-storm perpendicular, only a 16-year-storm quartering. Hurricane Ella was
  tracking toward New York.
- 09:00–11:00 — The phone call: LeMessurier's own re-analysis in his summer house. His decision to
  tell Citicorp. His decision NOT to tell tenants or city.
- 11:00–14:00 — The covert repair: night-only welding, plywood shielding, secret evacuation plan
  drawn up with the Red Cross. Newspaper strike that accidentally protected the secrecy. **This is
  the disaster-that-didn't-happen** — cinematic reconstruction of welders inside the columns at 3
  AM.
- 14:00–16:00 — MACRO RE-HOOK at 65%: "There is one more thing that was hidden for 40 years.
  Diane Hartley — the student who found the flaw — was never told her thesis had prompted the
  crisis." Show her 1995 interview where she learned the truth from a New Yorker article.
