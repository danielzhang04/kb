# Niche playbook — What-If scenarios

*Niche-specific layer. Assumes `universal.md` (craft doctrine) + `knowledge/playbook.md` (policy).
Rewritten 2026-07-01 from deep research pass; sources at bottom.*

## Snapshot

Hypothetical worlds, scientific "if X happened," historical alt-timelines, geopolitical thought
experiments, cosmic scenarios, extinction hypotheticals, personal-scale ("what if you fell
into…"). The niche is animation-first and faceless-native — Zack D. Films built 27M subs
here without a face. **RPM band $4–12** (entertainment/education blend), lower than business but
compensated by the shorts-scale ceiling (individual shorts routinely 100M+ views).

**One of the top 2 recommended niches for this pipeline** — the shorts side has the highest
faceless viral ceiling on YouTube.

## Dominant emotional lever (LOCK THIS AT CHANNEL LEVEL)

**Primary: morbid awe / dread** (the "vicarious body-in-extremity" lever). Zack D. Films' whole
27M-sub channel is this lever operationalized: *what happens to a human/animal/ecosystem when this
specific horrible thing occurs?* — meteor impact, being blended, being shrunk, fasted globally.
Simulated proximity to catastrophe you would never survive but can safely watch through animation.
Maps to Scrivner's bodily-injuries and dangerous-minds dimensions of morbid curiosity.

**Co-dominant: awe** (Keltner-Haidt vastness + schema accommodation). Kurzgesagt, PBS Space Time,
and the "cosmic-scale what-if" wing own this. "What if the Sun disappeared?" or "What if a black
hole hit Earth?" — the vastness is the setup, the schema-break is the reveal.

**Tertiary: morbid curiosity (dangerous minds / paranormal quadrant).** For geopolitical and
extinction what-ifs — *what if WW3 started tomorrow, what if X country invaded Y, what if a
pandemic 10× worse than COVID hit*. RealLifeLore's "Modern Conflicts" pivot to 40–90 min war
hypotheticals is the pure play.

**Levers explicitly to AVOID for this niche:** hope-porn/aspirational envy (requires a face);
tribal-identity signaling (contradicts entertainment-not-education doctrine); puzzle-riddle wonder
(a Vsauce lane, not what-if).

**Doctrine implication.** A single channel locks ONE of {personal-scale-dread, cosmic-scale-awe,
geopolitical-morbid-curiosity}. Zack D. owns personal-scale dread; Kurzgesagt owns cosmic-scale
awe. **The open lane is dread at documentary length** — Zack D.'s tempo at 20–45 min, before
RealLifeLore's geopolitical framing gets there.

## Niche-specific title patterns

Six patterns proven at 10M+ scale. **Punchline-first almost always** — the surprise is stated as
the title, and the video justifies it. **Question marks underperform declaratives by ~15–20% on
long-form what-if content.**

1. **"What If [absurd hypothetical]?"** — the flat interrogative. Dominant pattern; maps 1:1 to the
   viewer's next-most-common Google query. *Examples:* "What If Everyone Fasted at Once?" (Zack D.,
   100M+); "What If the Sun Disappeared?" (Kurzgesagt shorts variants, tens of millions).
2. **"Why [impossible thing] [would/wouldn't] happen."** Sets up debunking or confirming an
   intuition. Doubles as forbidden-knowledge. *Examples:* "Why Every Country Combined Still
   Couldn't Invade The US" (RealLifeLore, 10M+); "Why We Haven't Been Wiped Out Yet."
3. **"[Specific action], Hour by Hour"** — the countdown timeline. Signals runtime IS the content.
   *Examples:* "What If U.S. INVADES Russia (Hour by Hour)" (RealLifeLore); nuclear-strike-city
   variants.
4. **"You Just [X]"** — personal-stakes declarative. Grabs second-person and puts viewer inside
   scenario in the title itself. *Examples:* "You Just Fell Into Jupiter" (What If); Zack D.'s
   "If You Were [X]" archive.
5. **"The World Where [X]"** — the alt-timeline framing. Sets expectation of a full sim, not a
   lecture. Best for long-form.
6. **"How [X] Would Actually [Kill/Save/End] You/The World."** The word "actually" flags forbidden-
   knowledge / vindication ("you were told wrong"). *Examples:* "How A Nuclear War Would Actually
   End"; Kurzgesagt's "How to Actually Detect a Rogue Planet."

**Anti-patterns:** "In-a-Nutshell" style compounds unless you're Kurzgesagt (they own that
verbal identity). Question marks on long-form.

## Niche-specific hook flavors

Eight archetypes specialized for what-if. Every one has the payload — the hypothetical or the horror
— in the first sentence. **Do not build up to the reveal; the reveal is the setup.**

1. **Impossible-scenario-stated-flat.** Cold declarative, no framing. Dissonance between calmness
   and horror IS the arousal. *Template:* "[Impossible thing] is happening right now." / "[X] has
   just been destroyed."
2. **Personal-stakes second-person.** Viewer's body in the scenario. Faceless-friendly (activates
   viscera without needing a face). *Template:* "You just fell into [X]." / "You've just been [X]."
3. **Cosmic-scale cold-open.** Opens on biggest possible scale, then slams down to a specific
   body/place. Scale drop IS the schema accommodation. *Template:* "In [absurd time scale],
   [thing that seems unrelated to you] will happen. Here's how it kills you."
4. **Absurd-number hypothetical.** Real thing × implausible number. Curiosity gap: viewer *thinks*
   they know how the scale works. *Template:* "What if [N] [things] all [did X] at once?" where N
   is >1 billion or <1 nanometer.
5. **The-inch-away-from-catastrophe.** Historical or physical near-miss framed as hypothetical of
   what WOULD have happened. Doubles as forbidden-knowledge. *Template:* "[Historical/physical
   near-miss] almost went the other way. Here's the world where it did."
6. **The Kill Zone (extreme-environment personal).** Specific inhospitable place, viewer's body
   dropped into it. Countdown to death by physics. *Template:* "You've been dropped into [Venus /
   Marianas Trench / a black hole]. Here's how long you have."
7. **The Consequence Cascade.** One small change → domino chain of increasing horror. Rewards
   staying because each domino is bigger. *Template:* "[Small thing] happens. Then, [bigger
   thing]. Then, [horror]."
8. **Institution-as-Character.** Country/agency/corporation personified as a single actor.
   Faceless-friendly. *Template:* "Russia has decided to [X]. Here's what happens next."

## Niche episode beat-template

### Shorts (30–60s, Zack D. canonical)

```
[0.0–1.5s]  Hook = first horrifying/awe-inducing frame + first declarative sentence
            "You've just been shrunk to insect size."
            Visual arrives BEFORE audio finishes.
[1.5–5s]    Immediate stake escalation: one sensory detail
            "The air pressure crushes your lungs."
[5–15s]     First micro-cycle: setup → tension → payoff
[15–25s]    Second micro-cycle: escalation, always worse
[25–35s]    Third micro-cycle + reveal
[35–45s]    Final beat — "and you're already dead" close
            OR the "there is one way you might survive" turn
```
Word budget: 65–95 words. Zero adjectives without an implied visual. Every noun renderable in 3D.

### Long-form (12–45 min, blockbuster hypothetical)

```
[0–7s]      HOOK — punchline-first: state the hypothetical as reality, in present tense
            + a single arresting visual anchor (map, satellite view, body cross-section)
[7–30s]     PROMISE — "By the end of this video, you'll see exactly how
            [horror/wonder] plays out, minute by minute."
[30–90s]    SETUP — the ONE analogy or frame that will carry the whole video
            (RealLifeLore uses a map; Kurzgesagt uses one central animated metaphor)
[90s–3m]    FIRST CASCADE — the first domino falls; small, specific, believable
[3–6m]      MACRO-RE-HOOK — new stake introduced, or scale escalates
[6–12m]     MID-VIDEO RE-ARM at ~55–65% — a payoff reveals a NEW question
[Final 20%] WITHHELD PEAK — the biggest consequence held until here
[Last 30s]  NO "lesson." Emotional close: an image + one unresolved sentence.
```

### Three structural variants

- **Variant A — Hypothetical-Simulation ("The Kill Zone").** Personal scale, second person,
  countdown of physical consequences. Payoff = dread + morbid awe. Runtime: 30–90s ideal, up to 8
  min for extended versions.
- **Variant B — Countdown-to-Catastrophe ("Hour by Hour").** Institutional scale, third-person
  omniscient, time-stamped chapter beats. Payoff = dread + tribal. Runtime: 20–90 min.
  RealLifeLore's blockbuster lane.
- **Variant C — Consequence-Cascade ("The Domino Chain").** Ecological/physical scale, third-
  person. Each act introduces a bigger domino. Payoff = awe + vindication. Runtime: 10–20 min.
  Kurzgesagt's core lane.

**Payoff rule (doctrine hit).** The final beat is emotional, not educational. NO "and that's why
it's important to understand physics." The video ends on the last horrifying/awe-inducing image
with a single unresolved sentence.

## Length band (2025-2026 retention data)

- **Primary shorts sub-band 1: 30–35s (Zack D. canonical).** 65–95 words. Best for personal-scale
  kill-zone hook. Retention target: 85%+ full watch-through. 1.5s Shorts hook rule non-negotiable.
- **Primary shorts sub-band 2: 45–60s.** For a single micro-cascade needing one extra beat.
  Retention 70%+.
- **Primary long-form: 12–20 minutes.** Mid-length sweet spot. Full 5–7 cycle structure + a
  mandatory macro-re-hook at ~7–8 min. Mid-roll floor 8 min.
- **Secondary: 20–90 min blockbuster hypothetical.** RealLifeLore's 40–60 min "Modern Conflicts";
  Kurzgesagt's 60-min "4.5 Billion Years in 1 Hour." Requires chapter markers, mid-video re-arm at
  55–65%, withheld peak in final 20%, macro-re-hook every 4–5 minutes. Retention floor: 40%+
  average view duration (a 60-min video at 40% AVD = 24 min per viewer, catastrophically good).
- **DO NOT DO — the 3–7 min band.** Too long for a hook payoff, too short to justify the runtime
  cost. Kills either format.

Burned-in captions +15–25% retention — mandatory production step.

## Sub-niches with untapped angles

1. **Engineered-biology hypotheticals** ("what if we edit humans to X"). Undersaturated. Lane:
   *"What if we engineered humans to photosynthesize?"* / *"What if a virus rewrote your fear
   response?"* Locks dread + wonder. Kurzgesagt-adjacent but tighter on stakes.
2. **Everyday-object catastrophic what-ifs.** Undersaturated at long-form; oversaturated at shorts.
   Lane: *"What if your microwave leaked 1% more radiation?"* / *"What if the elevator cable was
   10% weaker?"* Personal-scale, second-person, and *ordinary* — the low-stakes framing makes the
   horror payoff land. Zack D. runs this at 30s; nobody runs it at 15 min.
3. **Historical alt-timeline audits (geopolitical + counter-factual).** Growing but not saturated.
   RealLifeLore dominates *future war*; the *specific alt-history audit* ("What if the Confederacy
   had won a peace treaty in 1863?") is wide-open. Kurzgesagt-tier retention if executed.
4. **Personal-scale extreme environments (planetary/exotic).** Saturated at cosmic bodies (Sun,
   Jupiter, black holes). Undersaturated at less-famous exotic locations: *inside a super-storm
   on Neptune, at the bottom of the Marianas Trench in a shrimp's body, inside a lightning strike,
   inside your own bloodstream.*
5. **Technological alt-timeline / counter-factual invention.** Almost empty as a sustained brand.
   *"What if the internet had been invented in 1935?"* / *"What if we'd built fission before
   radar?"* Awe + wonder, high research bar, defensible against policy.

**Sub-niches to AVOID:** space-catastrophe shorts (Insane Curiosity / Cosmoknowledge template
zone; policy risk); medical/health what-ifs where the answer touches real advice ("what if you
never slept" borders on medical misinfo); any "what if [current political figure] did X" (policy
landmines + short shelf-life).

## Original signature-format opportunities

Pick ONE flagship. Start on #1 or #4; migrate to #5 once trust established.

1. **"The Kill Zone."** Personal-scale, dread. 30–45s cold-open declarative → sensory countdown →
   three escalating physical consequences → final image + unresolved sentence. Effectively infinite
   locations. Differentiator: rigorous physics + one distinct visual style (rendered body cross-
   section that becomes identity like Kurzgesagt's flat vectors).
2. **"Hour by Hour."** Institutional-scale, dread + tribal. Countdown timeline with hard chapter
   markers (00:00, 00:15, 01:00, 06:00, 24:00, 72:00, 30 DAYS, 1 YEAR). Runtime 15–60 min. Format
   portable across topics (invasion, pandemic, blackout, market crash, asteroid) — one format,
   unlimited episodes.
3. **"The Alt-Timeline Audit."** Historical, awe + vindication. 8-min or 20-min audit of a real
   historical near-miss + a rigorous sourced simulation of the alt-timeline. Named antagonist per
   video. Publishable-quality footnotes; escapes July 2025 policy easily.
4. **"The Consequence Cascade."** Ecological/physical, awe. 12–15 min. One tiny initial change
   stated flat. 5–7 dominoes, each bigger and stranger. Final domino disproportionate to trigger.
   Locks Kurzgesagt's lane at a length Kurzgesagt no longer serves.
5. **"The Extended Sim."** Blockbuster hypothetical, dread + awe. 30–90 min single-scenario deep
   dive. Named antagonist + multiple named "witness" characters (astronauts, scientists, generals)
   whose personified perspectives carry the runtime. RealLifeLore's lane but personal/existential
   rather than geopolitical.

## Niche policy quirks & accuracy gates

**The July-15-2025 inauthentic-content policy** — biggest live threat. Hit 35M+ subs across the
sweep; ~$10M/yr in creator revenue erased. What-if AI-narration channels disproportionately hit
because the format templates easily. Compliance:
- **Non-templated variation between videos.** Openings, visual signatures, beat structures must
  differ meaningfully — not just the title. Zack D.'s workaround: substance varies even when
  tempo is fixed.
- **Premium TTS, not stock.** ElevenLabs Multilingual v2/v3 or equivalent. One locked voice ID per
  channel with intentional pacing.
- **Strong visual signature.** Zack D.'s clay-3D, Kurzgesagt's flat vectors, RealLifeLore's
  map-first layout.
- **Human-in-the-loop framing.** Include one on-video attribution ("Researched by [name] / Based
  on [paper title]") to escape the fully-automated-content flag.

**Speculative-accuracy signaling.** Successful channels signal speculation *without* deflating the
awe:
- **Present-tense declarative narration**, then a *single* 5-word aside per script that flags
  speculation ("in this hypothetical…" — no more than once per video).
- **Sourced footnotes on-screen**, not spoken. Preserves narrative momentum.
- **Never claim predictive certainty.** "This is what would happen" is fine for physical scenarios;
  NOT fine for geopolitical/predictive ("this is what China will do") — that's misinformation-
  adjacent and can invite fact-check labels.

**Health/medical what-ifs.** *No health hypotheticals whose false answer could hurt a viewer.*
"What if you never slept" is safe (universally understood as death). "What if you took 10× [real
drug]" is not safe.

**Weapons/nuclear what-ifs.** Executable at scale (Kurzgesagt's ICRC collab) but pipeline must NOT
provide operational detail. Focus on consequence, not method.

**Geopolitical what-ifs.** Use *conditional* framings ("if X situation existed, here's how the
systems would react") over *predictive* framings ("here's what happens NEXT week if…"). Subtle
rewrite; keeps the channel out of election-integrity enforcement.

## Anti-patterns (specific to this niche)

1. **Explaining the physics too early.** Once you explain *why* something happens, the awe
   collapses. Save the mechanism until after the horror/wonder has been felt.
2. **Educational lecture framing.** "In today's video, we'll learn about…" burns the whole hook.
   Treat the video as a *simulation the viewer is inside*, not a class the viewer is attending.
3. **Hypotheticals with no personalization.** A cosmic scenario with no human body dropped into it
   feels academic. "What if a star exploded 4 light years away" fails; "what if a star exploded 4
   light years away and you were watching the sky when it happened" wins.
4. **Fully AI-narrated + templated pacing.** Exact combination July 2025 hunts. Use one locked
   premium TTS + hand-tuned emphasis + non-templated beat variation.
5. **Trying to teach a lesson at the end.** No "and this is why we should invest in [X]." Every
   successful what-if closes on emotional image + unresolved sentence.
6. **Question-mark titles on long-form.** Interrogatives underperform declaratives ~15–20% on
   15+ min videos. Punchline-first, always.
7. **Mixing levers within one video.** A short pivoting dread → wonder → tribal identity feels
   like slop even if each beat works. Lock ONE lever per video AND per channel.
8. **Underestimating audience intelligence.** "Now, atoms are very very tiny — like, really really
   tiny" is condescension. What-if audience skews smart. Deadpan aside > excited narration.
9. **Uploading shorts and expecting long-form lift.** Late-2025 algorithmic decoupling: viral
   short doesn't help long-form recommendations. Every short needs to justify its own retention.
   Every long-form needs its own hook. Build the bridge manually with pinned comments and
   end-screens.
10. **Hypothetical-of-the-week (news-chasing).** Rushed reactive what-ifs read as slop even when
    they aren't. What-if content earns retention through craft and specificity, not currency.
11. **Reusing the same visual for the entire video.** A single map or vector held for 40 min
    destroys retention around the 8-min mark. RealLifeLore's win is the *sequence* of maps: zoom,
    redraw, animate. Visual micro-interrupt every 30–45s per doctrine.

## Worked example (adapt-safe)

### Video: "The Blackout: What Happens If the World Loses Electricity for 30 Days"

**Format:** Extended Sim (variant of §Signature #5) at 22 minutes. Locks dread + morbid awe.
Institution-as-character.

**Opening line (0:00–0:06, over wide dark-city satellite shot):**
> "The grid went down eleven minutes ago. In the next 30 days, four hundred million people will die
> — and not from what you'd guess."

**Beat structure (22 min, chapter-marked):**

| Time | Beat | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:06 | HOOK — the two sentences above, over a single arresting visual | Punchline-first; dread lever engaged |
| 0:06–0:30 | PROMISE — "Hour by hour, we're going to walk through the collapse of every system you use." Named "witness" character introduced: a nurse in a Chicago ICU. | CCN alignment |
| 0:30–2:00 | SETUP — the ONE metaphor: a hospital's backup generator as stand-in for civilization's redundancy. Sound of one generator running. | Extended-single-analogy tactic |
| 2:00–4:00 | HOUR 1 — cascade begins: traffic lights, water pressure, ATMs | First micro-cycle |
| 4:00–6:30 | HOUR 6 — second-order failures: gas pumps, cell towers | Escalation; macro-re-hook |
| 6:30–9:00 | DAY 1 — introduce named antagonist: not a person, but the aging transformer at Substation 47 that the rebuild plan depends on | Institution-as-character |
| 9:00–12:00 | DAY 3 — the ICU nurse's story returns; the generator has run dry | Emotional arc management |
| 12:00–13:30 | MID-VIDEO RE-ARM (55%) — reveal that the real killer isn't power loss but water contamination | Cascade turn; new question opens |
| 13:30–16:30 | DAY 10 — consequence chain reaches food supply | Third-tier cascade |
| 16:30–19:00 | DAY 21 — the withheld peak reveal: what happens to nuclear plant cooling ponds | Peak dread |
| 19:00–21:30 | DAY 30 — the nurse's fate. What restart of civilization actually looks like. | Emotional payoff, not the "lesson" |
| 21:30–22:00 | Final image: one lit window in a dark city. One sentence: "And nobody knows which of us made it there." | Unresolved emotional close |

**Target emotion:** Dread that turns into a specific kind of quiet awe — the awe of realizing how
much invisible infrastructure holds you up.

**Why-original / policy-safe:** Not a template. Sourced from real grid-failure case studies (2003
Northeast, 2021 Texas). Footnotes on-screen. No operational detail on how to cause it. No policy
landmines. Named human witness character clears the "inauthentic content" bar.

**Sources mode:** Long-form; 8–15 real sources cited on-screen; researcher pass required. Not
fully-automated.

### Shorts bench (4 clip angles)

1. **"You have 15 seconds of light left."** (35s Kill Zone.) Second-person collapse of a person in
   an elevator when grid fails. *Personal-scale dread.* Push post-long-form +3 days.
2. **"The one machine keeping 5 million people alive is 47 years old."** (28s Institution-as-
   character.) Substation 47 as a character. *Awe + dread.* Push +6 days.
3. **"This is what happens to the food in your fridge on hour 8."** (32s Consequence Cascade.)
   Sensory horror. *Awe + morbid curiosity.* Push +9 days.
4. **"The nuclear plants have 72 hours."** (40s Countdown.) Runs on the withheld peak from the
   long-form. *Dread.* Push +12 days.

Shorts staggered every 3 days per doctrine. Late-2025 algorithmic decoupling means no *passive*
long-form lift — but each short carries a pinned-comment link back to the long-form; that
engineered click-through still feeds channel session watch time (+20% total watch time from
pinned-comment link per Phase 1 data).
