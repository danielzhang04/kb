# Niche playbook — Horror / internet-lore

*Niche-specific layer. Assumes `universal.md` (craft doctrine) + `knowledge/playbook.md` (policy).
Rewritten 2026-07-01 from deep research pass; sources at bottom.*

## Snapshot

Creepypastas, unsolved mysteries, deep-web lore, iceberg breakdowns, unexplained
deaths/disappearances, cursed objects/games/websites, ARGs, YouTube analog horror, cryptozoology,
urban legends, "the strangest video on the internet"-style deep dives. **RPM band $3–8** —
generally lower than business/tech because horror content is advertiser-friendly-borderline, but
compensated by extraordinary retention and appointment-viewing behavior. The niche has the highest
length ceiling on YouTube outside sports/podcasts (Wendigoon epics 60–120+ min routinely).

**Faceless is native to this niche.** Bedtime Stories, Barely Sociable, Kane Pixels — no faces
required. This is one of the strongest faceless-viable niches in the pipeline.

## Dominant emotional lever (LOCK THIS AT CHANNEL LEVEL)

**Primary: morbid curiosity — the atomic engine.** Scrivner's four-dimension scale maps almost
one-to-one onto horror-lore subgenres: **paranormal danger** drives creepypasta/analog-horror/deep-
web-lore; **minds of dangerous people** drives unsolved-mystery and unexplained-disappearance
content. Scrivner's 2024 *British Journal of Psychology* paper further shows morbid curiosity
predicts interest in conspiracy theories — the exact behavioural profile of the iceberg viewer.

This is not just *appropriate* for the niche; it is the *only* niche whose entire audience self-
identifies through it.

**Secondary: morbid awe / dread** — the vicarious body-in-extremity of Kane Pixels' Backrooms (a
distant sublime of empty vastness), the "you are in that elevator" framing of Cecil Hotel dives,
and the analog-horror aesthetic of Local 58 and The Mandela Catalogue. Awe here is a *dark*
sublime: schema-accommodation against a threat too big or too weird to categorise. Unlike
science-awe channels, **the schema never resolves — the accommodation itself is the payoff.**

**Vindication / forbidden knowledge** — the "you were lied to" framing that carries almost every
iceberg video and every Nexpo-style investigation. Iceberg format is a delivery mechanism for
forbidden knowledge: each tier drops the viewer deeper into things "they" don't want you to know.
Wendigoon's own conspiracy iceberg (16.5M cumulative views, 22 parts) is the paradigm case. **But
this functions more as *packaging* around morbid curiosity than as a separate primary lever.**

**Note on lever purity.** Horror-lore uniquely benefits from being read as *a single lever with
three faces of morbid curiosity* (paranormal / dangerous minds / body). Channels pick a face (Kane
Pixels = paranormal + dread; MrBallen = dangerous minds; Wendigoon = all three but with forbidden-
knowledge packaging) and are still lever-consistent.

## Niche-specific title patterns

Ten patterns with lever + example view range.

1. **"The [Superlative] [Noun] on the Internet"** — curiosity gap + superlative. *Examples:*
   Nexpo *The Darkest Audio on the Internet*; *The Strangest Video on the Internet* — both
   multi-million.
2. **"The [Case/Death/Disappearance] of [Named Person]"** — morbid curiosity (dangerous minds).
   *Examples:* *The Bizarre Death Of Elisa Lam* (29M); countless disappearance breakdowns.
3. **"[Number] [Adjective] [Thing]s that [Verb]"** — countdown + escalation. *Examples:*
   blameitonjorge *Top 20 Disturbing Moments in Kids Shows* (6M+); Mr. Nightmare *3 True [X] Horror
   Stories* (routine 1–3M).
4. **"[X] Iceberg Explained"** — forbidden knowledge + descent contract. *Examples:* *Disturbing
   Movie Iceberg Explained* (~9M); *Conspiracy Iceberg* (16.5M series); *Internet Creepypasta
   Iceberg* (routine 1M+).
5. **"The [Location]'s Strangest Unsolved Mystery"** — morbid curiosity + specificity anchor.
   *Examples:* Nexpo *Siberia's Strangest Unsolved Mystery*; *Japan's Strangest Unsolved Mystery.*
6. **"Who / What is [Entity]?"** — as a genuine question. Curiosity gap + forbidden knowledge.
   *Examples:* Barely Sociable *I Think I Know Who Satoshi Is*; *What is Cicada 3301?* variants.
7. **"[Number] [Objects/People/Places] That Shouldn't Exist"** — paranormal danger. Rampant in the
   niche; typical 500K–3M.
8. **"The [Entity] They Don't Want You to Know About"** — vindication / forbidden knowledge.
   Conspiracy-adjacent; 500K–2M.
9. **"[Cursed/Haunted/Banned] [Object]"** — morbid curiosity + scarcity. *The Banned Video…*; *The
   Cursed Game…*
10. **"I Found Something [Horrifying] on [Platform]"** — first-person witness + platform-specific
    scarcity. Deep-web / dark-web breakdowns; steady 500K–2M.

**Title rule for this niche:** *do not spoil the mystery.* Never put the answer in the title. The
title is the promise of unresolved dread.

## Niche-specific hook flavors

Ten archetypes prioritising the 5-second long-form / 1.5-second Shorts hook window.

1. **Found-Footage Cold Open (no host).** Audience is not told they're watching a YouTube video;
   they're dropped into "discovered" media. *Template:* `[timecode] [degraded footage of a mundane
   place] [sound of camera breathing]`. *Example:* Kane Pixels' Backrooms (78M+); Doctor Nowhere's
   Boiled One (multi-million). **Strongest single hook in the niche.**
2. **The Artifact You Shouldn't Have Seen.** Scarcity + forbidden-knowledge frame. *Template:* "On
   [date], a video appeared on [platform]. It was deleted within [hours]. This is what it showed."
   *Example:* Nexpo's *Disturbing Things From Around The Internet* series.
3. **The Ordinary Detail Turned Wrong.** Pattern-interrupt against a familiar environment.
   *Template:* "This is [very mundane thing]. Look at [normal detail]. Now look at [wrong detail]."
   *Example:* Local 58's PSAs; Mandela Catalogue's "biblical figures on a home video." This is the
   analog-horror grammar.
4. **The Case They Closed Too Quickly.** Righteous-anger sub-note under morbid curiosity;
   investigator activation. *Template:* "[Date, place]. [Person] was found [state]. The police
   called it [official ruling]. It wasn't." *Example:* MrBallen's opening formula.
5. **The Still-Unsolved Mystery.** Unresolved-tension contract; the video does not promise closure.
   *Template:* "To this day, no one knows who [made / did / left] this. But we know when it
   started." *Example:* LEMMiNO Cicada 3301; Barely Sociable's Satoshi.
6. **The Iceberg Descent.** Multi-tier contract. Not a cold-open trick; an *architecture* hook.
   *Template:* "You've seen the tip. But if you go down here, [tier 2]… down here, [tier 3]…"
   *Example:* Wendigoon's Disturbing Movie Iceberg (~9M).
7. **The Second-Person Walk-Through.** Viewer-as-protagonist. *Template:* "You get the email at
   3:07 AM. You know you shouldn't open it. You do." *Example:* creepypasta narration style; Mr.
   Nightmare's high-view "true story" openings.
8. **Named Antagonist Reveal-Then-Withhold.** Introduce the entity, then refuse to describe it.
   *Template:* "They called it [name]. If you're seeing this, that's a problem." *Example:*
   Slenderman canon; Mandela Catalogue's "alternates."
9. **The Video That Keeps Getting Deleted.** Meta-scarcity + urgency. *Template:* "This is my third
   time uploading this. It gets removed every 48 hours." *Example:* Whang!'s "cursed video"
   formats; ReignBot's deep-web deletions.
10. **The Comment Section Discovery.** Community-sourced spine. *Template:* "A viewer left this
    comment on my last video. When I checked, [X] was real." *Example:* Night Mind's ARG
    breakdowns.

## Niche episode beat-template

### Definitive long-form beat template (30–45 min baseline)

```
0:00–0:05   COLD OPEN. The artifact itself (image / audio / footage / document).
            No channel branding. Silence + one line, or wordless.
0:05–0:20   THE HOOK LINE. The impossible claim, spoken flat.
            "This footage was deleted in 48 hours. This is why."
0:20–0:45   MICRO-CONTRACT. What you'll see and what will remain unresolved.
            "By the end of this video, you'll know [X]. You won't know [Y]."
0:45–1:30   ESTABLISHMENT. Time, place, first named person / entity.
            One image on screen the whole time.
1:30–4:30   ACT I — THE DISCOVERY. Setup-tension-payoff #1. First micro-reveal.
4:30–5:00   RE-HOOK #1. Ask a new unanswerable question.
5:00–9:00   ACT II — THE INVESTIGATION DEEPENS. Cycle #2 of setup-tension-payoff.
9:00–9:30   RE-HOOK #2 + AUDIENCE-AS-DETECTIVE beat ("what would you have concluded?")
9:30–15:00  ACT III — THE COMPLICATION. New evidence. Named antagonist emerges.
            Setup-tension-payoff #3.
15:00–20:00 ACT IV — CASCADING REVELATION. Two payoffs stacked; withhold the peak.
20:00–22:00 MID-ROLL RE-ARM (55–65% mark; mandatory).
            Restate what's still unresolved and add a new stakes escalation.
22:00–32:00 ACT V — THE DEEPEST REVEAL you're willing to give.
            Peak dread. DO NOT RESOLVE.
32:00–35:00 THE UNANSWERED QUESTION. The lingering horror.
            Voiceover slows. One long silence.
35:00–36:00 REGISTRATION-FREE OUTRO. No "like and subscribe" energy — a whisper.
            "If you know something, [contact]."
```

### Structural variants

- **A. Iceberg Descent (60–120+ min).** Skip Act I/II framing; the tier list *is* the spine. Each
  tier gets its own 3–7 min STP. Mandatory re-hooks at each tier boundary. Withheld peak = the
  deepest tier — sometimes literally not shown. Wendigoon paradigm.
- **B. Investigative Case (20–45 min).** MrBallen / Nexpo shape. Cold-open on the incident; the
  whole video is one long cycle of new-evidence → contradiction → deeper mystery. No named
  antagonist required.
- **C. Creepypasta-As-If-Real (15–30 min).** Second-person or first-person; no acknowledgment that
  this is fiction. Beat template collapses into a linear escalation with one late peak. Mr.
  Nightmare, Bedtime Stories.
- **D. Analog Horror Serial (5–15 min per episode).** No host; no exposition. Each episode is a
  self-contained "artifact." Payoff is community theorising, not in-video. Local 58 / Mandela
  Catalogue / Kane Pixels.

**Payoff rule (niche-specific).** The reward is *not* closure. It is emotional dread + one
persistent unresolved question the viewer will replay in their head after they close the tab.
**If you resolve everything, retention holds but no one talks about the video, and the algorithm
treats it as complete-but-inert.**

## Length band (2025-2026 retention data)

- **Primary band: 22–35 minutes** (recommended for new AI-gen entrant).
  - Nexpo's core zone is 20–45 min; sweet spot for polished investigative work with 3.4M+ subs.
  - Horror-lore viewers who commit past the 5-minute mark tend to complete at extraordinary rates
    (appointment-viewing behaviour). A 22-min video holding 65% completes for a very strong session
    signal.
  - 22 min gives five STP cycles at ~4 min each + one mandatory mid-roll re-arm.
  - 35 min is upper bound at which a *new* channel can still fill runtime without noticeable
    stretching.
- **Secondary band: 60–90 min "prestige drop" every 4–8 weeks.** Wendigoon (60–120+ min), Barely
  Sociable (75+ min), and LEMMiNO's quarterly deep-dives prove the ceiling is essentially uncapped
  *once trust is established.* **Do not attempt until at least 10K+ subs and demonstrable 50%+
  retention on the primary band.**
- **Where even devoted audience taps out:**
  - Under 15 min: signals "list channel," attracts lower-trust audiences, worse CPM.
  - 45–60 min without prestige credibility: worst zone; viewers expect Wendigoon-tier substance and
    get impatient.
  - 3+ hours before you've earned it: retention collapses at ~40 min.

**Shorts:** per doctrine, horror/lore tolerates the highest shorts-per-long-form ratio (4–8).
**Recommendation: 6 shorts per long-form** for a new AI-gen entrant. Shorts hook window is 1.5s —
analog-horror cold opens are the strongest match.

## Sub-niches with untapped angles

1. **Non-Western folklore + contemporary case files.** English-speaking horror-lore market is
   saturated with Anglosphere / East Asian cases (Elisa Lam, Dyatlov, Japan urban legends). Latin
   American, African, South-East Asian, and Central Asian folk-horror + real-case content is
   drastically under-covered in English. Extremely low competitor density. Compliance risk lower
   (fewer domestic-audience tragedies).
2. **Dead-website / deleted-internet lore.** The archaeology of vanished forums, extinct social
   platforms (Vine, Google+, Yik Yak, MySpace's cursed accounts), and lost media that only exists
   in archive.org. Whang! runs part of this; nobody has claimed the "extinct platform mystery"
   beat as a durable format.
3. **Audio-first lore (numbers stations, mystery broadcasts, cursed audio).** Adjacent to the
   "darkest audio on the internet" format. Fits perfectly with AI-gen premium TTS: the visual is a
   spectrogram; retention carried by audio dread. **Uniquely defensible against video-mimicry AI
   slop.**
4. **Cryptid case-files as investigative journalism (not campfire retelling).** The Why Files
   touches this; MissingEnjoyer / cryptid-focused channels exist but at low quality. A serious
   "cold-case file" treatment of Mothman / Skinwalker Ranch / Loveland Frog with primary-source
   documents and named investigators is under-served.
5. **YouTube-native lore (the platform investigating itself).** Petscop, Sunset Boulevard-style
   unexplained-YouTube-account mysteries, cursed channel discoveries, algorithmic-glitch horror.
   Requires a specific investigative posture ("I found a channel that shouldn't exist"). Low
   saturation because it demands original desk-research rather than script-generation.

## Original signature-format opportunities

1. **"The Case File"** — documents-first investigative format. Cold-open on a scanned document /
   evidence photo. Runtime walks through the file page-by-page. Standing visual: an evidence board
   that fills up as the video progresses. Every video ends with the case file being stamped
   `UNRESOLVED`. Signature ritual = ownable.
2. **"The Iceberg Autopsy"** — Wendigoon's format, but tighter and with a standardised reveal
   ritual. Each tier has: (a) the entity name whispered once, (b) a single image on screen for the
   entire tier, (c) a mandatory "the record shows" evidence beat, (d) a mandatory "and this is
   where it gets worse" transition to the next tier. The ritual itself becomes the retention hook.
3. **"Signal Trace"** — audio-artifact walk. The visual is *always* the spectrogram or the waveform
   of the source audio. Host (voiceover) is the "signals analyst." Each video: (source audio) →
   (waveform anomaly) → (context) → (deeper anomaly) → (unresolved). **Extremely defensible; AI
   slop competitors cannot mimic without owning the source audio.**
4. **"Reading the Comments"** — community-driven mystery. Cold-open on a screenshotted comment on
   the *previous* video. Video spent verifying/deepening the tip. Turns audience into content
   pipeline. Perfect for CCN.
5. **"Field Recording, Reconstructed"** — a fictional-but-plausible found-footage series with the
   aesthetic of Kane Pixels but a smaller, weekly cadence. Uses AI-generated environments as a
   *feature* (uncanny-valley is the point), which sidesteps the July-2025 slop policy — the AI is
   a stylistic choice, not a labor shortcut.

## Niche policy quirks & accuracy gates

**The Elisa-Lam standard.** Real-person tragedy content is heavily scrutinised — both by YouTube's
advertiser-friendly policy and by the audience itself. The 2021 Netflix Cecil Hotel documentary
was widely criticised for exploiting mental illness (Lam had documented bipolar disorder). Rule
for this pipeline:
- For any real-person case, name the mental health / medical context explicitly and respectfully.
- Never speculate about supernatural agency in cases with documented psychiatric factors.
- Name the victim only when it serves the case, not for SEO.
- Do not use victim images without justification.

**The July 15, 2025 inauthentic-content policy.** Notable enforcement: True Crime Case Files (83K
subs) removed after 150+ AI-narrated murder stories. Compliance path for horror-lore AI-gen:
premium TTS + locked single lever + strong visual signature (evidence-board, waveform, tier-map —
not stock B-roll) + genuine original research beat in every video.

**Advertiser-friendly gore rules.** Content where blood/gore/injury is the focal point is not
eligible. Context matters — "educational / documentary framing" of violent events can hold
monetization. **Rule: never linger on gore imagery; describe it, cut away.** Bedtime Stories'
B&W-illustration convention is a solved-problem: stylised depiction sidesteps the graphic-content
demonetization. Adapt it.

**Copyright / IP on creepypasta canon.**
- **Slenderman is NOT public domain** — rights are held by the film production entity and are
  unresolved. Do not build a channel around Slenderman-branded content without licence.
- HP Lovecraft's core mythos IS public domain (Cthulhu, Innsmouth, etc.) but individual
  illustrations are typically not.
- Ted the Caver: explicitly copyrighted by original author.
- General rule: creepypasta *plots* are usually free to reference; specific character names,
  images, and modern extensions (Slenderman, SCP, Backrooms canon post-Kane-Pixels) are IP-loaded
  — reference, don't reproduce.

**YouTube kids-content rule.** Adult-horror themes tagged as attractive to minors get demonetised.
**Never use bright kids-content colour palettes or cartoonish thumbnails on horror content** —
YouTube's classifier reads this as "made for kids" and pulls monetization.

**How successful channels stay monetizable while running dread.** Bedtime Stories: illustration
replaces graphic imagery. Kane Pixels: implied threat, off-screen violence, distant sublime.
MrBallen: language does the work; describe the incident without visualising the body. Wendigoon:
content warnings up front + no on-screen depiction of the deepest tiers. **Common pattern:
language carries the dread; visuals stay abstract or stylised.**

## Anti-patterns

1. **Cinematic-orchestra music underneath dread.** Over-scored horror-lore reads as "content"
   rather than artifact. Dread requires *silence* and *diegetic-adjacent* audio (room tone, static,
   distant hum). If it sounds like a Netflix trailer, retention leaks.
2. **Explaining the mystery too fully.** The niche's contract is unresolved dread. Closure kills
   the second view, kills the community-theorising engine that fuels shares, and neutralises the
   algorithm's session signal. **Never end with "and so we now know that…"**
3. **Disrespectful framing of real victims.** Elisa Lam is the paradigmatic case: content that
   treats a real person's mental illness as an aesthetic prop faces both audience backlash and
   automated demonetization. **The single fastest way to burn a channel's growth mid-flight.**
4. **AI-slop iceberg with no original research.** July-2025 policy is aimed directly at this. If
   your iceberg is a recomposition of the top three Google results for each entity, you're on the
   channel-wide demonetization path. Do original desk-research on at least one tier per video.
5. **Screen-cluttered "explainer" graphics.** Big popup labels, Kurzgesagt-style icons, arrow-
   labelled zooms — these belong in education, not dread. **The niche's visual grammar is one
   image, held long, with silence.**
6. **The "and that's why…" moral outro.** No moralising. No safety takeaway. **Horror-lore
   audiences resent being taught.**
7. **Cheap-jumpscare thumbnails.** Screaming faces, red-eyed silhouettes, high-contrast "WATCH
   BEFORE DELETED" thumbnails work briefly, then get down-weighted. Serious channels (LEMMiNO,
   Kane Pixels, Nexpo) use *quiet* thumbnails: a single object, low saturation, no face.
8. **Content warnings buried in the description.** For gore/mental-health cases, warnings must be
   in-video, before the incident description. Buried warnings both risk demonetization *and*
   invite the "exploitation" backlash pattern.
9. **Mixing levers within one channel.** A channel that does one week of paranormal, one week of
   true-crime-and-victim-sympathy, one week of iceberg trivia reads as slop even when each
   individual video is good. Pick one face of morbid curiosity and defend it.
10. **Naming the entity too early.** In creepypasta / analog-horror grammar, the entity's *name* is
    the last thing you reveal, not the first. Naming it early collapses the tension gradient. Kane
    Pixels doesn't name what's chasing you until deep in the series. Adapt this discipline.

## Worked example (adapt-safe)

### Video: "The [Country]'s Strangest Unsolved Broadcast"

*(placeholder for a specific, low-competitor country; e.g., Kazakhstan / Peru / Nigeria — chosen
from sub-niche #1)*

**Runtime target:** 28 minutes.

**Dominant lever:** Morbid curiosity (paranormal-danger dimension) + forbidden knowledge.

**Format:** Investigative Case variant with a Signal Trace signature.

**Opening line (0:00–0:05 cold-open):**

*[Spectrogram appears. Two seconds of static. One tone bleeds through.]*

> "On [date, placeholder], for eighteen minutes, a shortwave frequency that should have been empty
> started transmitting this."

**Beat structure:**

1. 0:05–0:20 — the hook line: this recording still hasn't been explained.
2. 0:20–0:45 — micro-contract: we'll walk through everything the analysts found, and everything
   they didn't.
3. 0:45–1:30 — establishment: place, date, first named signals-analyst.
4. 1:30–4:30 — Act I: the initial discovery. First anomaly in the waveform. First payoff.
5. 4:30–5:00 — re-hook: what the second recording contained.
6. 5:00–9:00 — Act II: the transcription of the audio (partial). Named phrases the analyst
   recognised.
7. 9:00–9:30 — audience-as-detective beat: "if you're hearing this, what would you conclude?"
8. 9:30–15:00 — Act III: the complication. A pattern is found in adjacent broadcasts. Named entity
   emerges (from folklore / anonymous source).
9. 15:00–20:00 — Act IV: cascading revelation. Two payoffs, third withheld.
10. 20:00–22:00 — mandatory mid-roll re-arm at 60% mark.
11. 22:00–26:00 — Act V: the deepest reveal. A near-decipherable phrase; a single native-language
    word left untranslated. **Peak dread.**
12. 26:00–27:30 — the unanswered question: "The last transmission was [date]. We don't know if it
    stopped, or if we stopped listening."
13. 27:30–28:00 — whisper outro: "If you've heard this frequency, [placeholder contact]."

**Target emotion:** dread with a lingering unresolved question. No closure. No moral.

**Why-original:** English-language horror-lore has almost no coverage of non-Anglosphere signals
mysteries; existing numbers-station content is heavily UK/US-centric.

**Sources mode:** primary-source archived recordings only. Where recordings are not licensable,
use synthesised analog approximations with an on-screen note ("Reconstruction — original recording
not licensed").

**Compliance notes:** No real-person tragedy in the source; if adjacent to a disappearance, follow
Elisa-Lam standard. AI narration used — must include a named human host credit + original research
beat (contacted analyst / archive lookup) to satisfy July-2025 policy.

### Shorts bench (6 clips)

1. **Cold-open (15s).** Just the spectrogram + the one line. Cut hard. Pure hook, zero context.
2. **The Waveform Anomaly (45s).** The specific spike in the waveform + one analyst quote. Ends
   on: *"They still don't know what caused this."*
3. **The Translation Beat (60s).** The near-decipherable phrase. Read it. Cut to black. Do not
   translate.
4. **The Named Entity (30s).** The folklore reference. One sentence: *"[Entity name] appears in
   [region] stories going back to [date]. This is the first recorded audio."*
5. **The Second-Person Walk-Through (45s).** *"You're a shortwave hobbyist. You're tuning through
   the empty bands at 3 AM. This is what you catch."*
6. **The Comment Section Discovery (30s).** *"A viewer left this comment on last week's video. I
   verified it. Here's what it means."* → cliffhanger.
