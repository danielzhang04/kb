# Niche playbook — Micro-health

*Niche-specific layer. Assumes `universal.md` (craft doctrine) + `knowledge/playbook.md` (policy).
Rewritten 2026-07-01 from deep research pass; sources at bottom.*

## Snapshot

Quick health tips, "what happens if X" medical explainers, symptom-decoding, body-mechanic
explainers, condition breakdowns, nutrition/sleep/exercise/mental-health micro-content, medical-
case walkthroughs. **RPM band $6–20+** depending on sub-niche (mental health, addiction, weight
loss can command the top band; general nutrition and body-mechanic content sits in the middle).

**Faceless viability is HIGHLY CONDITIONAL.** The two fully-faceless success stories in this niche
(Zack D. Films 26M, Nucleus Medical 4M, and Kurzgesagt on health topics) are ALL stylized-
animation-first. **No credential-free live-footage faceless health channel appears in the top
ranks of the niche.** This is the single strongest constraint.

**Realistic ceiling estimates:**
- Chubbyemu-style animation-first case-report faceless: **500K–2M subs in 18–24 months** at 1 long +
  3 shorts/week.
- Zack D. Films-style shorts-first animation-heavy faceless: **3–8M subs** in the same window.
- 20M+ tier is likely gated behind real credentials or studio-scale production.

## Dominant emotional lever (LOCK THIS AT CHANNEL LEVEL)

**Primary: morbid curiosity — bodily-injuries dimension.** Chubbyemu's entire channel (3.35–3.69M
subs, ~150 videos) is a controlled experiment proving this. Structured around a single lever:
*what actually goes wrong inside a body.* The bodily-injuries dimension survives faceless treatment
because the payoff is *inside the body*, not on a face. Zack D. Films (26.2M subs) is the shorts-
native validation — 22 shorts over 100M views each, built on stylized 3D animation of bodies
breaking. Institute of Human Anatomy (8.8M subs) is the same lever with real cadavers substituting
for animation.

**Co-dominant: morbid awe / dread — vicarious body-in-extremity.** Distinct from #1: payoff is
*scale of horror + comprehension* rather than clinical unraveling. Kurzgesagt's "Loneliness" (24.1M
views) works this lever — the reveal is not "here's the treatment," it's "you are inside a system
quietly killing you." Any video framed "what happens if you don't sleep / eat sugar / sit all day"
hits this.

**Vindication / forbidden knowledge — "you were lied to."** Highest-EV lever in the wellness end of
the niche and the reason Dr. Eric Berg (14.5M subs) and Dr. Sten Ekberg (5.28M subs) grow despite
fact-checker pushback. **This lever is a compliance minefield** — for a faceless AI-gen operation
with no MD credential, it's the fastest path to a channel-level demonetization strike.

**Levers that reliably underperform:**
- Hope-porn/aspirational envy needs a face and transformation arc — faceless-hostile.
- Righteous anger works only when directed at industry (Big Pharma, food industry) which drags you
  toward vindication territory and its policy risks.
- Tribal identity signaling (keto/carnivore/plant-based tribes) exists but a channel leaning on it
  caps at that tribe's size.

**Default recommendation for this pipeline: Lever 1 (morbid curiosity, bodily injuries) with
occasional Lever 2 (dread) episodes.** This is the Chubbyemu formula. It survives YMYL because
every claim is anchored to a specific case or mechanism, not policy advice.

## Niche-specific title patterns

Seven patterns with lever + real examples.

1. **"[Anonymized patient] [did specific thing]. This is what happened to their [organ]."**
   *Lever:* morbid curiosity. **The single most-replicated viral title format in medical YouTube.**
   *Examples:* "A Scientist Spilled 2 Drops Organic Mercury on Her Hand. This Is What Happened To
   Her Brain" (11M, Chubbyemu); "A Mom Drank 3 Gallons Water In 2 Hours. This is What Happened to
   Her Brain" (7.6M); "A Boy Ate 3 Laundry Pods. This Is What Happened To His Lungs" (multi-million).
2. **"What Happens To Your [Organ] When You [Do Common Thing]"** — *dread + wonder.* *Examples:*
   "What Alcohol Does To Your Body" (IOHA, 20M+); "Do You Know What Coffee Does to Your Brain?"
   (IOHA, multi-M).
3. **"The [Symptom] Your Doctor is Missing"** / **"The [X] Your Body Is Telling You"** —
   *vindication + second-person accusation.* *Examples:* "Your Body's Urgently Trying to Tell You
   Something" (millions, July 2025); many Berg titles.
4. **"[Number] [Foods/Habits/Signs] That [Cause/Reveal X]"** — *list + curiosity.* *Examples:*
   "23 Proven Tips To Improve Your Sleep" (Doctor Mike); "The Top 10 Lies Health Experts Have
   Told You." Note universal doctrine: numbered lists = ~11% fewer views on average — use
   sparingly with real narrative inside each item.
5. **"What if You Held In [Y] / Ate [X] / Didn't [Z] For [Time]?"** — *morbid curiosity, shorts-
   native.* *Examples:* Zack D. Films' entire top-100M shorts catalogue.
6. **"The Truth About [Wellness Topic]"** / **"[Popular Belief] Is a Lie"** — *vindication.*
   *Examples:* Nutrition Made Simple's meta-analysis breakdowns; Doctor Mike's debunk series.
   Compliance-safe when anchored to scientific consensus, dangerous when contrarian.
7. **"[Organ] Autopsy: What Went Wrong"** / **"Reading the Study: [Paper Title]"** — lower CTR but
   higher trust. Good for authority-building videos.

## Niche-specific hook flavors

Eight archetypes.

1. **The Chubbyemu case-open.** Pattern: `[Initials], [age], [sex], presenting to the emergency
   room with [symptom].` Instant in-medias-res + anonymized real-patient = "this happened." Two-
   second establishment of "you are inside a hospital story." *Faceless-adaptable:* yes, but needs
   a fixed narrator persona to carry it.
2. **The normal-thing-turned-lethal.** *Template:* "A [ordinary person] [did ordinary thing to
   extreme]. This is what happened to their [organ]." Schema-violation of an assumption you
   didn't know you had. *Faceless-adaptable:* yes (Zack D. Films uses this).
3. **The what-if-you-held-it hook.** 1–3 second animated visceral payoff up front. *Template:*
   "What happens if you [suppress/do X]?" **This is the native faceless hook.**
4. **The second-person symptom accusation.** *Template:* "You [do this common thing] every [time
   period]. Here's what it's doing to your [organ]." Makes them Google their own symptoms.
   Compliance-sensitive — steer away from "you're dying" toward "here's a mechanism you didn't
   know."
5. **The forbidden-knowledge frame.** *Template:* "The [X] your doctor won't tell you about." /
   "The truth about [wellness topic]." Highest RPM in the niche but highest compliance risk.
   Doctor Mike's version works because he *debunks* the vindication frame — audience gets the
   emotional payoff of "I was lied to" without the misinformation cost. **Dangerous without a
   credentialed anchor.**
6. **The organ-in-front-of-camera / anatomical reveal.** *Template:* "[Show real organ / stylized
   anatomy]. This is what [substance/action] does to it." Visual anchor before context (universal
   doctrine tactic). *Examples:* IOHA's alcohol/caffeine/THC series, all multi-million; Zack D.
   Films' anatomy renders.
7. **The mechanism reveal / how-your-body-works.** *Template:* "[Body process] shouldn't work.
   Here's why it does." Awe + wonder. *Examples:* Kurzgesagt's immune-system videos (10M+ each);
   IOHA tissue close-ups.
8. **DO NOT USE — the "you have X days to live if you keep doing Y" ultimatum.** Compliance-risky,
   high-CTR but the exact shape YouTube's YMYL enforcement targets.

## Niche episode beat-template

### Primary: case-walkthrough (Chubbyemu-derived, 8–12 min)

- **0–5s HOOK.** Anonymized patient identifier + presenting symptom OR a single visceral image ("2
  drops of mercury on skin"). No context. State only the *inciting incident*.
- **5–30s STAKES.** Escalate: symptoms worsen. Withhold cause. Personify the body: "her kidneys
  began to protest." One sentence identifying the mystery: "But her doctors didn't know why."
- **30s–90s FIRST INVESTIGATION.** Introduce the medical setting. Walk through initial workup
  (bloods, imaging, differentials). Each test either rules out a possibility or reveals a new
  symptom.
- **90s–3min NARROW-DOWN.** Second and third cascading reveals. Introduce the antagonist (the
  actual mechanism: a toxin, a genetic condition, a nutrient deficiency). Personify it.
- **3–5min DEEP MECHANISM.** Extended analogy carrying the biology. **Universal doctrine: single
  extended analogy > multiple micro-analogies.** Chubbyemu's mercury video: "mercury binds to
  sulfur atoms in your proteins like a wrench jammed into every gear."
- **5–7min RE-HOOK / MID-BEAT.** New wrinkle: complication, second diagnosis, question about
  consent, or "and this is where it gets worse."
- **7–10min PAYOFF.** Diagnosis confirmed. Treatment attempted. Outcome revealed — emotionally,
  not clinically.
- **10–12min ZOOM-OUT.** One line of "here's what this teaches us about [wider body system]" — but
  not "here's the health advice." Takeaway is *emotional*: awe at the body, dread at the fragility,
  vindication at the misdiagnosis pattern.
- **CTA.** Subscribe framed as "if you like knowing how bodies actually work."

### Structural variants

- **Variant A — Mechanism explainer (Kurzgesagt-style).** Hook = the paradox ("your body's biggest
  killer is a feeling"). Beat: paradox → historical anchor → mechanism build via extended analogy
  → cascading system reveals → dread-payoff. 8–11 min.
- **Variant B — Myth-bust (Doctor Mike/Nutrition Made Simple).** Hook = specific popular claim.
  Beat: claim stated with clip evidence → "let's see what the data says" → walk through 2–3
  studies with real numbers → verdict → nuance section (protects credibility). 10–15 min.
- **Variant C — Warning-signal / symptom-decode.** Hook = second-person accusation. Beat: symptom
  described → misconceptions listed and killed → actual mechanism revealed → escalation to
  worst-case → recovery pattern. 8–12 min. **Highest-CTR, highest compliance risk.**
- **Variant D — Anatomical reveal (IOHA-style).** Hook = the organ or tissue in view. Beat: this
  is X, most people think it does Y, actually it does Z, and here's what breaks it. 6–10 min.

**Never end with:** "so remember to see your doctor about X" — this is a compliance-safe closer
that kills retention and telegraphs the emotional flat-line.

## Length band (2025-2026 retention data)

- **Primary: 8–12 minutes.** RPM sweet spot. YouTube's 8-min floor unlocks 1–2 mid-roll ads; the
  9–12 min range earns 40–60% more RPM than sub-8.
- **Secondary: 12–15 minutes** for case-walkthroughs (Chubbyemu, IOHA deep-dives). Runs 2–3 mid-
  rolls. Only viable with strong retention discipline (macro re-hook every 2–3 min, withheld
  payoff).
- **Where health content breaks:**
  - Under 6 min: insufficient time for cascading-reveal structure; you either hook-and-teach
    (boring) or hook-and-list (templated / flagged post-July 2025).
  - Over 20 min: the trust-vs-length tradeoff. Every minute past 15 = more claims = more surface
    area for a misinformation flag. YMYL retention curves show harder mid-video drops than
    entertainment niches because audiences self-select out as soon as their specific question is
    answered.
  - Podcast lengths (2–3 hrs, Huberman): viable *only* with a credentialed host + clips-channel
    strategy. **Not replicable faceless.**

**Compliance-length tradeoff.** Every claim in a health video needs sourcing (2-source rule). A
14-min video makes ~20–30 claims. A 25-min video makes ~50+, and each is a potential misinformation
flag. Chubbyemu maintains a slow monthly cadence at 12–15 min for this reason.

## Sub-niches with untapped angles

1. **Historical / archival medical cases.** Cases from before 1970 with unusual pathology,
   rediscovered from journals. Historical cases have massive built-in narrative + no living-patient
   consent issues + open the door to pre-antibiotic-era horror. **Faceless-viable because the
   storytelling *is* the value.**
2. **Environmental / occupational disease.** What your job, your building, your commute is doing
   to your body. Radon in basements, PFAS in nonstick, sitting biomechanics, blue light debunked
   and re-explained, indoor air quality. Under-served because most channels chase food/sleep/
   exercise mainstream. Second-person accusation hook lands hard here.
3. **Pharmacology mechanism deep-dives.** How specific drugs work: SSRIs, GLP-1 agonists (Ozempic),
   statins, PPIs. **Ozempic alone would print money in 2026.** Chubbyemu-adjacent but zoomed out
   from the case to the mechanism. Credibility bar is high — but with a fixed persona anchored in
   a fictional-but-consistent narrator identity, the biochemistry is public.
4. **Women's health mechanism** (menstrual cycle science, PCOS mechanism, perimenopause, hormonal
   contraception mechanism). Under-served by big channels because most male hosts avoid it.
   Enormous audience. **Highest compliance sensitivity** — must not stray into treatment advice.
5. **Sleep science micro-mechanisms** beyond the Huberman/Walker overview. Specifically:
   parasomnias, restless-leg mechanism, REM behavior disorder, sleep-related-eating, exploding
   head syndrome. Horror-adjacent + genuine science + under-served + faceless-friendly.

**Honorable mentions:** rare congenital conditions (situs inversus, FOP) — pure morbid awe;
historical epidemic mechanisms — awe + dread; what-your-poop-tells-you and similar body-signal
decodings — huge search volume, low quality of existing coverage, moderate compliance risk.

## Niche policy quirks & accuracy gates

**YMYL is the strictest bucket on YouTube.** Health, medicine, finance content held to a materially
higher bar than entertainment.

1. **Medical misinformation policy (updated 2023, actively enforced through 2026).** YouTube removes
   content contradicting local health authority or WHO guidance on prevention, treatment, or denial
   of specific health conditions. Cancer misinformation added in 2023. Any video contradicting
   CDC/WHO on a covered condition can be removed and struck.
2. **July 15, 2025 inauthentic-content policy** — direct hit on AI-gen health content. Mass-produced
   templated video batches: flagged. Synthetic voices without context/transformation: flagged.
   Recycled scripts, auto-slideshows: flagged. Requires "significantly original and authentic"
   content for YPP. **Sensitive topics (health, news, elections, finance) trigger a more prominent
   AI-disclosure label directly on the video player.**
3. **Advertiser-friendly content guidelines.** Health/wellness content chronically flagged as "not
   suitable for most advertisers" even when correct. Mental health, eating disorders, sexual
   health, body image especially demonetization-prone.
4. **AI disclosure requirements (enforced 2025).** Any realistic altered or synthetic content
   disclosed at upload. For YMYL topics YouTube may **auto-add a more prominent label on the
   player** even if the creator disclosed elsewhere.
5. **Known 2025 enforcement examples.**
   - "Wisdom Spring" / 智慧之泉 (Chinese AI-generated health persona channel) — 16M views before
     triggering platform warnings; subject of academic case studies.
   - Multiple deepfake-doctor channels removed after CBS News coverage of impersonation of real
     physicians.
   - Dr. Eric Berg (credentialed as DC, 14.5M subs) has publicly complained of YouTube censoring
     individual videos in 2024-2025 — even a large credentialed channel gets specific videos struck.

**Practical rulebook for a faceless AI-gen micro-health operation:**
- **Every claim: 2 sources minimum.** One from peer-reviewed journal or WHO/CDC/NIH primary; one
  from a review paper or textbook.
- **Never contradict CDC/WHO** on covered public-health topics (vaccines, cancer, COVID, HIV,
  abortion).
- **Framing must be education, not advice.** "Here's what happens" not "you should do X." "Consult
  a physician" as hard closer on any video mentioning a treatment.
- **AI disclosure on:** TTS voice, AI-generated visuals, AI-scripted narration. Toggle at upload
  every time.
- **Assume the "more prominent label" is showing on the video player** — write hook and title
  assuming viewers see the AI label first.
- **No miracle cure, no "cures cancer," no "your doctor won't tell you"** unless immediately
  followed by the actual mainstream consensus with citation.
- **No unlicensed cadaver imagery.** Use 3D animation exclusively.

## Faceless viability — direct answer

**Cautious yes, with hard constraints.** No credential-free live-footage faceless AI channel
appears in the top ranks of the niche in 2024-2026. The two fully-faceless success stories are
stylized-animation-first (Zack D. Films, Nucleus, Kurzgesagt).

**Winning formula for a faceless-AI-gen micro-health channel in 2026:** a **persona-anchored
channel with a fictional-but-consistent narrator character**, animation-first visuals, and
case-report-style content. Concretely:

1. **Fictional named narrator persona.** Pick a name (e.g., "Case Files with Dr. Simmons" —
   fictional character, disclosed as such in the About page). This buys narrative continuity +
   audience attachment + reduces "who is telling me this" anxiety.
2. **Premium TTS voice, single voice ID, locked across every video.** Consistency = identity even
   without a face.
3. **Animation-first visuals.** No stock footage of doctors, no scraped cadaver imagery. Style
   signature: single distinctive 3D animation style, consistent color palette, custom typography.
4. **Case-report or mechanism-explainer format only.** Not advice, not tips, not "what to eat."
   Payoff is emotional (curiosity satisfied, dread felt, awe at the body).
5. **Full AI disclosure on every upload.**
6. **2-source rule in every description.**
7. **Persona clearly disclosed as fictional in the channel About page.** Prevents the
   "impersonation of real physicians" enforcement risk.
8. **Sponsor-only monetization for the first 6–12 months.** Assume ad monetization is unstable.
   Sponsorships (mattress, apps, supplements-with-caution) and eventually eBook/course sales are
   the durable revenue.

**Bright-line: do not.** Do not use a real doctor's voice cloned. Do not name the persona after a
real living physician. Do not recommend specific treatments. Do not frame any video as advice. Do
not contradict CDC/WHO. Do not produce templated batches. Do not run "your doctor lied to you"
without immediately landing on scientific consensus.

## Anti-patterns

1. **Pseudo-authority claims with no credential.** A faceless AI voice saying "as a doctor, I
   recommend…" is a channel-killer. Triggers both AI disclosure enforcement AND medical
   misinformation policy.
2. **Miracle-cure framing.** "Cures cancer," "reverses diabetes," "eliminates X permanently" is on
   YouTube's enforcement watchlist and immediately advertiser-unfriendly.
3. **Contradicting mainstream medical consensus for engagement.** The Berg / Ekberg / O'Neill
   playbook. Works short-term for RPM, but 2024-2026 enforcement has already caught up with
   individual videos on the biggest channels. Not viable building from zero.
4. **Over-cautious hedging.** Ending every video with "but consult your doctor before making any
   changes" every 40 seconds kills retention. The emotional payoff must be *felt*, not lectured.
5. **Templated inauthentic content.** Batch-uploading 10 near-identical "5 foods that cause X"
   videos is a demonetization trigger under July 2025.
6. **Using AI voice for health "advice."** Even when technically correct, AI-narrated advice-framed
   content is the pattern YouTube is training on for enforcement. Reframe every "advice" moment as
   "here's what happens" or "here's the mechanism."
7. **Symptom-checker anxiety-mining.** "You feel this? You might be dying." Generates viewer
   complaints, which YouTube surfaces to policy enforcement.
8. **Repost-style case walkthroughs.** Retelling other channels' cases without new mechanism
   explanation triggers inauthentic-content policy.
9. **Ambiguous supplement recommendations.** Even "some studies suggest [supplement] may help with
   [condition]" is a demonetization pattern.
10. **Real cadaver footage without licensing.** IOHA operates their own cadaver lab; you cannot
    legally scrape their b-roll or medical journal images without licensing.

## Worked example (adapt-safe)

### Video: "This Woman Ate Licorice Every Day for a Year. This Is What Happened to Her Heart."

**Opening line:** "SR, 42, presenting to the emergency department with irregular heartbeat and
profound weakness. Her potassium level was 1.9 — one of the lowest her cardiologists had ever
seen in a conscious patient."

**Target emotion:** morbid curiosity (bodily injuries) as primary; awe at the endocrine mechanism
as secondary payoff. **No vindication frame, no policy claim.**

**Why-original:** licorice-induced pseudohyperaldosteronism is a real, well-documented pharmacology
mechanism (glycyrrhizin inhibits 11β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 2, causing cortisol to
over-activate mineralocorticoid receptors). Published cases exist. Not a top-covered topic on
YouTube. Fits the "ordinary thing turned lethal" title pattern that has 3 confirmed 7M+ viral
variants.

**Beat structure (12 min target):**
- 0–5s: patient ID + potassium value + irregular rhythm on ECG animation. No context.
- 5–30s: her doctors' initial mystery — normal thyroid, no diuretics, no vomiting.
- 30s–2min: first workup. Rule out cancer, adrenal tumor, Bartter syndrome. Each rule-out is a
  micro-reveal.
- 2–3min: history-taking reveal: she'd been eating black licorice as her stress-relief snack for a
  full year.
- **3–6min: THE MECHANISM.** Extended analogy: cortisol and aldosterone are "twins" that fit the
  same receptor. Your kidneys have a bouncer enzyme that keeps cortisol out. Glycyrrhizin (in
  black licorice) is like a note that makes the bouncer take a coffee break. Cortisol floods the
  receptor. Your kidneys start dumping potassium.
- 6–7min: re-hook. This isn't rare. Case series in the literature.
- 7–9min: outcome. Treatment: IV potassium + stop licorice. Recovery over weeks. But permanent
  hypertension in some documented cases.
- 9–11min: zoom-out. Other foods and supplements that hit real pharmacology (grapefruit + statins,
  St. John's Wort + SSRIs, ginkgo + blood thinners). **Payoff = awe: your kitchen contains real
  drugs.**
- 11–12min: soft closer. "If you liked this, subscribe — we do a case like this every month." No
  "consult your doctor" (it's not a treatment video).

**Sources mode:** 3 sources minimum in description. UpToDate or NEJM case report on licorice-
induced pseudohyperaldosteronism; a review paper on glycyrrhizin pharmacology; FDA advisory on
black licorice (2017, still active).

**Compliance check:** ✅ Not contradicting CDC/WHO. ✅ Not advice ("consult your doctor" not
needed because we're describing what happened, not recommending an action). ✅ AI disclosure
toggle enabled. ✅ Fully faceless (3D animation of heart + kidney tubule + receptor bouncer
analogy).

### Shorts bench (5 clips)

1. **S1** (45s): Just the receptor-bouncer analogy. Hook: "Your kidneys have a bouncer, and
   licorice bribes him."
2. **S2** (30s): Just the potassium-of-1.9 opening. Hook: "This is the lowest potassium her
   doctors had seen in a conscious patient."
3. **S3** (60s): Grapefruit + statins mechanism, teased from the zoom-out section. Hook: "One
   glass of grapefruit juice can 3× your statin dose. Here's why." Payoff: CYP3A4 explainer.
4. **S4** (40s): "What if you ate black licorice every day?" — pure what-if hook. Zack-D-Films-
   style animation.
5. **S5** (55s): "5 foods that are actually real drugs." List-format tease. Hook: "Grapefruit. St.
