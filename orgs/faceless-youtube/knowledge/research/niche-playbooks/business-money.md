# Niche playbook — Business & Money documentaries

*Niche-specific layer. Assumes `universal.md` (craft doctrine) + `knowledge/playbook.md` (policy).
Rewritten 2026-07-01 from deep research pass; sources at bottom.*

## Snapshot

Single companies, founders, fortunes, and frauds turned into narrative "mini-movies" — rise-and-fall
arcs, "how the rich actually do X", scam exposés, mechanic explainers. Structurally faceless &
AI-friendly (VO + stock/archival b-roll + charts + title cards). **RPM top band $10–30+** (business/
finance CPMs $12–25); one of the highest-earning niches on YouTube.

**Niche policy quirk:** highest-RPM lane, so the temptation to churn slideshow retreads is strongest
— and that's exactly what the July-2025 "inauthentic content" policy hunts. Original research + a
stated thesis is mandatory. **No purely-automated AI business doc channel has broken 1M subs since
July 2025.** This niche needs human editorial direction even when the pipeline is AI-assisted.

## Dominant emotional lever (LOCK THIS AT CHANNEL LEVEL)

**Primary: schadenfreude / status-collapse spectatorship** — the mighty falling. Company Man's 1.79M
subs, ColdFusion's 5.2M, and MagnatesMedia's 1.85M are all built on the mismatch between how big/
arrogant an empire felt and how it ended.

**Co-dominant: vindication / forbidden knowledge / "you were lied to"** — MagnatesMedia and
Coffeezilla (4.7M) both lock this. The word "Secret" in MagnatesMedia's "The Secret Chinese
Company That Owns Everything" is the whole hook. Coffeezilla's entire catalog is vindication
energy operationalized through evidence.

**Tertiary/accent: awe at scale** — Modern MBA, Wendover, MagnatesMedia's wealth-decoding lane
(supply chains, PE fund flows, sovereign wealth funds) pull awe when the video makes the audience
feel the *vastness* of a system.

**Override on priors:** outrage rarely lands as *primary* here — political framing splits the
audience. Works as *seasoning* inside a schadenfreude spine (Coffeezilla's Logan Paul series is
~80% vindication, 20% outrage). Pure anti-corporate outrage videos underperform against
schadenfreude collapse videos on the same target.

**Lock rule:** pick ONE spine — **collapse-schadenfreude** *or* **forbidden-knowledge-vindication** —
and let awe-at-scale be the recurring accent. Both dominant options are in the same emotional family
(institutional-failure porn); mixing either with hope-porn / founder-worship signals slop.

## Niche-specific title patterns

Ten patterns, each with the underlying lever.

1. **"The [Rise/Fall/Death] of [Iconic Company]"** — *Schadenfreude.* Only use for household names.
   *Examples:* "The Rise and Fall of Atari" (ColdFusion); "The Rise and Fall of BlackBerry."
2. **"The Secret [Adjective] Company That [Verb-Phrase]"** — *Forbidden-knowledge.* "Secret" is
   one of the highest-CTR words in this niche. Pair with an impossible claim.
   *Example:* "The Secret Chinese Company That Owns Everything" (MagnatesMedia, multi-M).
3. **"How [Company] Actually [Verb]s"** — *Curiosity / forbidden-knowledge.* Specific numbers +
   concrete outcomes beat vague claims. *Examples:* "How WhatsApp Makes Money"; "Why American
   Airlines Stopped Making Money" (Wendover).
4. **"How I Accidentally [Damning-Reveal]"** — *Vindication.* Rare because it requires a real
   event; highest CTR-per-attempt in the niche. *Example:* "I Accidentally Got SBF To Admit to
   Fraud" (Coffeezilla).
5. **"The [Adjective] [Downfall/Scandal] of [Named Antagonist]"** — *Schadenfreude.* One emotive
   adjective max. *Example:* "The Disturbing Downfall of Boeing" (Aug 2024).
6. **"[Impossible Number] → [Nothing]: The Story of [Company]"** — *Awe.* Massive number +
   concrete zero. *Example:* "From $2 Billion to Nothing — The Rise and Fall of Atari."
7. **"You Were Lied To About [Thing Everyone Believes]"** — *Vindication.* The Coffeezilla / How
   Money Works spine. Requires a widely-held belief the video will overturn.
8. **"Inside the [Adjective] World of [Category]"** — *Forbidden-knowledge + awe.* Best for
   wealth-decoding / hidden-tier videos. *Example:* "Levels of Wealth: Inside The Secret Lives of
   The Ultra-Rich" (MagnatesMedia).
9. **"The Day [Person/Company] Lost [Massive Amount]"** — *Schadenfreude.* Specific temporal anchor
   + specific number. Standard across MagnatesMedia + Company Man.
10. **"Why [Everyday Product/Company] Is Actually [Damning Claim]"** — *Forbidden-knowledge +
    vindication.* *Examples:* Fern's Healy expose; How Money Works' payday-loan mechanics.

**Anti-patterns:** neutral, educational-sounding titles ("Understanding X" / "The Economics of X")
now underperform emotive collapse/reveal titles by 3–5× in CTR. **"The Truth About X"** is
over-saturated — treat as burned. Don't stack multiple emotive adjectives ("The Insane Scandalous
Disturbing Fall…") — reads as clickbait.

## Niche-specific hook flavors

Ten archetypes specialized for business-money. Each names the lever.

1. **The impossible-number contrast.** Opens with a number so large or absurd the brain has to
   reconcile it. *Template:* "[Number] more than [famous benchmark]. [Company] is [scale claim].
   And most people have never heard of it." *Lever:* awe (schema-accommodation) inside first 3s.
2. **The peak-before-the-fall cold open.** Opens *at* maximum apparent success — a specific date,
   a boast, a magazine cover. Then rewinds. *Template:* "[Date]. [Person/company] was [absurd
   claim of dominance]. [Beat.] Six months later, [collapse fact]." *Lever:* dramatic irony —
   audience knows the ending, wants to watch the character not know.
3. **The named-antagonist reveal.** Opens by naming the villain before the target audience knows
   they're the villain. *Template:* "You've never heard of [name]. But [name] cost [huge number]
   of people [thing they lost]." *Lever:* forbidden-knowledge.
4. **The everyday-object Trojan horse.** Opens with a mundane consumer touchpoint, reveals the
   empire behind it. *Template:* "Every time you [ordinary action], you're paying [hidden party].
   Here's how they built it." *Lever:* vindication + curiosity gap.
5. **The court-doc / regulator-filing reveal.** Opens with a legal document — SEC filing, court
   transcript, DOJ indictment. *Template:* "On [date], [regulator] filed [document]. Buried on
   page [n] was [damning line]." *Lever:* forbidden-knowledge + authority signal (YMYL protection).
6. **The boast-vs-collapse split-screen.** Juxtaposes an on-record boast against the ending.
   *Template:* "[Quote of arrogant claim from person X, date Y]. This is what happened next."
   *Lever:* schadenfreude activated in first sentence.
7. **The "how much money have they actually made" frame.** Opens by promising to answer the money
   question no one else did. *Template:* "[Company] says they're worth [X]. Their actual profit
   is [Y]." *Lever:* forbidden-knowledge.
8. **The present-tense collapse ("This Is Happening Right Now").** Opens in the middle of an
   unfolding event. *Template:* "As of [today's date], [company] has [current bad number]. Their
   next earnings call is [imminent date]." *Lever:* urgency + unresolved-outcome arousal.
9. **The autopsy cold-open.** Starts at the funeral — bankruptcy filing, delisting, IPO withdrawal
   — and moves backwards. *Template:* "On [date], [company] filed for Chapter 11 / was delisted.
   Here's the [n]-year chain that led to that single day." *Lever:* schadenfreude + puzzle-solving.
10. **The vindication punchline ("They Sold You").** Opens with a marketing message everyone has
    seen, then flips it. *Template:* "You've seen this ad [n] times. It's a lie." *Lever:*
    vindication triggered by pattern-completion.

## Niche episode beat-template

### Primary: "The Autopsy" (schadenfreude/collapse, 13-min baseline)

1. **0:00–0:07 — Peak-of-hubris cold open.** Show maximum apparent success (magazine cover, boast,
   IPO ring, market cap peak). One line of narration.
2. **0:07–0:20 — Punchline-first pivot.** Cut to the ending — bankruptcy, arrest, delisting — in
   one sentence. Audience now knows *where* this ends but not *how*.
3. **0:20–0:55 — Stakes-promise / thesis.** "This isn't a story about [surface cause]. It's about
   [deeper cause]." Set up the named antagonist.
4. **0:55–1:45 — Withheld reveal setup.** Micro-interrupt with a specific mystery: "In [year], one
   decision would guarantee it. We'll come back to that."
5. **1:45–4:00 — Origin (cycle 1).** How the company/person came up. Include one moment of genuine
   skill so the audience respects them before enjoying the fall.
6. **4:00–6:30 — Ascent (cycle 2).** The scaling era. Personify institutions as characters. The
   extended single analogy (§1b/5) lives here — 90–120 seconds of pure metaphor.
7. **6:30–8:30 — First crack (cycle 3).** Audience-as-detective moment — early warning sign visible
   in hindsight. Micro-interrupt at ~7:30.
8. **8:30–10:00 — Mid-video re-arm (55–65% mark, mandatory).** Payoff of the tease from step 4.
9. **10:00–12:00 — The mechanism of failure (cycle 4).** Cascading revelations. Each bigger than
   the last.
10. **12:00–13:30 — The collapse (cycle 5).** Fast pacing. Numbers. Named-antagonist consequences.
11. **13:30–14:30 — Withheld peak.** Final twist or last-revealed detail. Strongest single stat /
    quote lives HERE, not in the opening.
12. **14:30–15:00 — Emotional outro.** NOT "the lesson." A single haunting image or line.

### Structural variants

- **Biography arc** (Newsthink / MagnatesMedia founder stories): swap collapse spine for a life
  arc. Payoff = awe or vindication rather than schadenfreude. **Only works when the person is dead,
  jailed, or fallen** — otherwise CCN test fails for new viewers.
- **Mechanism exposé** (How Money Works / Wendover): open with the everyday touchpoint. Instead of
  a character arc, the "character" is the mechanism itself. Beat: touchpoint → who profits →
  mechanic reveal → who loses → the number. Payoff = vindication.
- **Scam anatomy** (Coffeezilla / Fern Healy): open with victim testimony. Named-antagonist by
  1:30. Court/regulator document by 3:00. Attempted contact with antagonist by 8:00. Consequences
  (arrest, bankruptcy, or ongoing) by 12:00. Payoff = vindication + closure.
- **Present-tense collapse** (Patrick Boyle / Wall Street Millennial): drop the biography arc
  entirely. Open with today's stock chart. Beat: current bad number → why it's bad → chain of
  decisions → missing catalyst → what happens next. Payoff = schadenfreude anticipation.

## Length band (2025-2026 retention data)

- **Primary: 10–15 minutes** for the collapse-schadenfreude spine. MagnatesMedia / ColdFusion /
  Company Man sweet spot. Hits mid-roll floor with breathing room; supports 2–3 mid-rolls.
- **Secondary: 25–45 minutes** for the deep-case / autopsy variant (Modern MBA / Fern territory).
  Requirements to sustain past 15 min: retention holds >50% at the 15-min mark; density of reveals
  stays >1 major reveal per 3 min; mid-video re-arm at 55–65% is mandatory.
- **The 16–24 min "death zone" — avoid.** Too long for the tight-cut audience, too short to justify
  deep-case density. Business docs specifically break in this middle band.

**Mid-roll floor:** 8 min minimum. At 10 min = 2 mid-rolls. At 15 min = 3 mid-rolls. At 30 min =
4–5 at natural scene breaks. **Never place a mid-roll during the 55–65% re-arm** — that's the
retention save moment and an ad break there breaks the recovery.

## Sub-niches with untapped angles

1. **B2B "boring industries" collapse and mechanic docs.** Insurance, logistics, PE roll-ups,
   industrial gases, medical device rollups, dental-chain PE plays. Wendover partially owns
   airlines; almost nobody owns commercial insurance, med-device sales, or the dental-chain PE
   story. High RPM ($10–25 CPM). Freshest lane.
2. **Non-Western collapses.** Chinese property developers (Evergrande, Country Garden), Indian
   conglomerates (post-Hindenburg Adani), Korean chaebol scandals, Nigerian fintech implosions,
   LatAm crypto scams. Cultural distance = Western audiences approach with schadenfreude-friendly
   detachment. Verify sources hard; defamation law varies by jurisdiction.
3. **The "living dead" empire.** Mid-decline companies visibly circling but not collapsed — Peloton,
   WeWork post-BK, Rite Aid, Party City post-BK, zombie mid-caps. Company Man skirts this but only
   after full collapse. Lets you cover a name multiple times as its decline unfolds.
4. **Wealth-tactic exposés / "what the rich actually do."** Family office structures, step-up basis,
   GRATs, foreign asset protection, PE carried-interest, corporate tax inversions. Pure vindication
   lever. Personal-finance RPM. Requires accuracy discipline.
5. **Obscure fraud typologies.** Not the SBFs and Madoffs — mid-tier "you never heard of him"
   frauds. Cattle-Ponzi, art-market fraud, wine-fund frauds, sports-betting arbitrage frauds,
   franchise Ponzis. Every state AG office publishes complaint docs = renewable source material.

## Original signature-format opportunities

Pick ONE as the flagship recurring show. Others become occasional inserts.

1. **"The Autopsy" (5-Failure Model).** Every video dissects EXACTLY five decisions that killed
   the company. Enforced structure: cold-open collapse → "here are the five bullets that killed it,
   in order" → walk through 1–5. *Lever:* schadenfreude + puzzle-solving. Signature graphic
   (numbered chapter cards) = ownable brand device.
2. **"Follow the Money" (Second-Order Losers).** Every video answers: "when [big company] did [big
   thing], who *actually* paid?" Traces cost downstream through suppliers, employees, retirees,
   taxpayers, competitors. *Lever:* vindication + awe-at-scale. Signature visual: a flowchart that
   grows across the video.
3. **"The Playbook They Used" (Reverse-Engineered Tactics).** Reverse-engineer ONE tactic used by
   the ultra-wealthy per episode. Name it (e.g., "The Dell LBO Playbook"). *Lever:* forbidden-
   knowledge. High franchise potential.
4. **"The Kill Shot."** Every video ends with ONE moment — a decision, quote, memo, or number —
   that made the collapse inevitable. Entire video built to earn that one line. *Lever:*
   schadenfreude + awe. Forces research to actually find one; makes each video feel like a puzzle
   you're solving with the narrator.
5. **"Boast vs. Bust."** Pair on-record boast (interview clip, tweet, magazine cover) with actual
   outcome. Two-column visual template. *Lever:* pure schadenfreude. On-record footage acts as
   defamation defense; template is instantly recognizable in thumbnails.

## Niche policy quirks & accuracy gates

**YMYL exposure.** Business-money content is squarely YMYL (Your-Money-Your-Life) under Google's
E-E-A-T guidelines. Practical requirements:
- Cite specific sources on-screen at every major factual claim (court filings, SEC 10-Ks, DOJ press
  releases, court transcripts, published books).
- Include visible "About / Sources" section per video (in-video or description).
- On any personal-finance-adjacent video: "This is entertainment, not financial advice" —
  placed early, not buried.

**Defamation exposure on named targets.** Highest actual legal risk in the niche. Coffeezilla's
ongoing Logan Paul suit (2024 filed, hearing June 2026) is the case study.
- Every damaging claim about a named person must trace to a public court filing, sworn testimony,
  regulator finding, published news report, or the target's own on-record statement.
- Distinguish opinion from fact. "In my view, X looks like a scam" is defensible; "X is a criminal"
  without a conviction is not.
- Prefer targets who are (a) convicted, (b) subject to a regulator finding, (c) already publicly
  accused by mainstream press, or (d) US public figures (actual-malice standard).
- Non-Western targets: UK libel is plaintiff-friendly; certain jurisdictions have criminal
  defamation.
- **Counter-lesson — a disclaimer does NOT immunize you (Coffeezilla, verified 2026-07-02).**
  Coffeezilla's on-page "this video is an opinion and in no way should be construed as statements of
  fact" disclaimer did **not** hold: a federal magistrate ruled his "scam" statements *"reasonably
  capable of defamatory meaning and not unactionable opinions"* — because an **investigator persona**
  makes a reasonable viewer read "scam" as a factual assertion; the Logan Paul suit survived to trial
  (2026). Implication for us (a faceless, authoritative narrator IS exactly that persona): **trace every
  hard claim to a filing** ("the SEC complaint alleges," "the documents show," "on-chain records show X
  moved to Y"), and **phrase the conclusion as the viewer's inference, not the narrator's verdict** —
  Coffeezilla's "pause-as-verdict": sequence quote → document → a beat of silence, never narrate "he's
  guilty." The **"I think X / others would call it Y" hedge** is necessary-not-sufficient. Out-discipline
  Coffeezilla; don't match him.

**Copyright / footage.** Prefer public-domain and official corporate footage (annual reports, IR
decks), licensed news services, transformative editorial use. **Never scrape movie/TV clips as
B-roll** (categorically banned under July 2025). Music: proper library (Musicbed / Artlist /
Epidemic). Photos: license or CC-BY with visible credit.

**Disclosure.** Any AI voice or AI visual disclosed via the "Altered or Synthetic Content" upload
flag. Undisclosed AI = community strike or permanent YPP ineligibility.

## Anti-patterns

1. **Neutral recap = policy risk.** Retelling a Wikipedia-quality summary without added synthesis,
   on-screen sources, or original angle triggers the July-2025 inauthentic-content flag. Discussed
   collapses (Enron, Theranos, WeWork, FTX) require original framing to clear the "materially
   different substance" bar.
2. **Over-narrating balance sheets.** Reading revenue/margin/EBITDA line-by-line tanks retention.
   Convert every financial number into an analog: "$47 billion" alone is nothing; "$47 billion —
   more than Airbnb and Ford combined, wiped out in 6 weeks" activates awe.
3. **Ending on "the lesson."** "And that's why founders need to focus on unit economics" burns the
   entertainment frame. Business-money audiences reject overt didacticism.
4. **Cross-lever slop.** Mixing collapse-schadenfreude with hope-porn founder-worship (both "how X
   died" and "how Y made it" videos) gets flagged as slop.
5. **Unnamed antagonists.** Passive voice ("mistakes were made," "the culture became toxic") tanks
   vs. specific-person accountability. Name the antagonist by 90 seconds; if you can't legally
   name one, the story isn't ready.
6. **Present-tense speculation without sources.** Claims about a company that's "about to collapse"
   without cited data invite defamation risk AND YMYL demotion.
7. **Templated thumbnails.** Red-arrow-down + CEO face + red X = algorithmically flagged post-
   July-2025. Distinctive visual signature is now a monetization prerequisite.
8. **Founder-worship for living, unfallen figures.** Elon-adulation / Musk-genius / Bezos-productivity
   videos age poorly. Only cover living founders as schadenfreude candidates (fallen, ousted,
   indicted).
9. **Copying Company Man's deadpan without the underlying research.** The tone only works because
   the collapse is meticulously reconstructed. Copy the tone without 15 primary sources per video
   and retention collapses in the first 45 seconds.
10. **Aggressive shorts-to-longform funnel promises.** "Watch the full video" CTAs are algorithmically
    punished late 2025 (shorts decoupled from long-form). Shorts must stand alone as a
    schadenfreude/vindication payoff — no "part 1 of N."

## Worked example (adapt-safe)

### Video: "The $30 Billion Boast That Killed WeWork"

**Title options:**
1. "The $30 Billion Boast That Killed WeWork"
2. "The Real Reason WeWork Collapsed (It Wasn't the IPO)"
3. "How Adam Neumann Lost $47 Billion in 6 Weeks"

**Target length:** 13 minutes.

**Target emotion:** Schadenfreude arriving as *satisfying inevitability*. Secondary: vindication
(the audience always suspected the guy was a cult leader).

**Why-original:** The standard WeWork narrative is S-1 filing + Adam-Neumann-was-crazy. This
version reframes around ONE specific boast — Neumann's on-record claim to raise WeWork to $10
trillion — and treats that single sentence as the moment the collapse was locked in. No other
WeWork video anchors on that specific boast as the fulcrum.

**Opening line (0:00–0:07):**
> "In 2018, Adam Neumann told his employees WeWork would be worth ten trillion dollars. Fourteen
> months later, the company was worth almost nothing. This is how one sentence killed a forty-
> seven-billion-dollar company."

Cold-open visual: the actual footage/quote card of the boast + a $10T counter that ticks to nearly zero.

**Beat structure (13-min autopsy):**
- 0:00–0:15 — Boast cold open (above).
- 0:15–0:35 — Punchline-first pivot to Sept 30, 2019 IPO withdrawal + $47B → $8B haircut.
- 0:35–1:00 — Thesis. "This wasn't about coworking. It was about a founder who convinced everyone
  he was a prophet, and the money guys who wanted to believe."
- 1:00–2:00 — Withheld tease. "Before we get to the boast, you need to see what he'd already
  gotten away with by 2017."
- 2:00–4:30 — Origin. Neumann's 2010 pitch. WeGrow, WeLive. Extended analogy: WeWork as "prophet-
  run kibbutz for millennials" — 90–120s.
- 4:30–6:30 — Ascent. SoftBank Vision Fund. Masayoshi Son as enabler-character.
- 6:30–8:00 — First crack. Employees' internal doubts. Tech-company-vs-real-estate ambiguity
  quoted from S-1.
- 8:00–9:00 — Mid-video re-arm. Reveal the $10T boast in full context — payoff of the min-1 tease.
- 9:00–11:00 — Mechanism of failure. S-1 red flags. Governance issues. Private jet. "See" trademark
  buy-back. Numbers escalating.
- 11:00–12:15 — Collapse. IPO withdrawal. Neumann ouster. $1.7B golden parachute. SoftBank writedown.
- 12:15–12:45 — **Withheld peak.** Neumann's next company (Flow) raised $350M in 2022 from a16z —
  the strongest final beat because schadenfreude flips into disbelief-at-scale.
- 12:45–13:00 — Emotional outro. Slow fade over empty WeWork lobbies. Line: "He never really lost."

**Sources mode:** S-1 filing, DealBook coverage, WSJ reporting, court filings around SoftBank
writedown, Bloomberg Originals footage, Neumann's on-record interviews. On-screen source
citations at every major factual claim.

### Shorts bench (7 clip angles, faceless-friendly)

1. **"$10 trillion" boast supercut** (30s) — Neumann's claim + counter animation to zero. Hook:
   "The single sentence that killed a $47B company." *Schadenfreude.*
2. **"$1.7B to lose your job"** (25s) — Neumann golden parachute. Punchline: he then raised $350M
   for Flow. *Vindication + outrage.*
3. **The S-1 red flag hunt** (45s) — Rapid-cut through 5 red flags in the actual S-1 document.
   Faceless-native (document overlays). *Forbidden-knowledge.*
4. **Masayoshi Son gets played** (40s) — SoftBank writedown. Hook: "The world's most powerful
   investor lost $18 billion on one deal." *Schadenfreude at the enabler.*
5. **The Flow paradox** (35s) — Neumann's post-WeWork company. Hook: "Same guy. Same investors.
   New company. $350 million." *Disbelief + vindication.*
6. **"We are a community company"** (25s) — Neumann's tech-not-real-estate framing collapse. On-
   record clip. Reveal: they were a landlord. *Schadenfreude.*
7. **The $5.9M trademark buy** (30s) — Neumann sold WeWork the "We" trademark. Reveal: he owned
   it. *Forbidden-knowledge + outrage.*

## Reference-channel findings (added 2026-07-02)

From a video-analysis + packaging pass on The Second Take's reference board (How Money Works, ColdFusion,
Magnates Media, Wendover, PolyMatter, Patrick Boyle, Coffeezilla, Crayon Capital). **Finance-general
only** — cross-channel *craft* went to `universal.md` (§3d titles, §5a/5b openings + analogy, §8d
thumbnails, §1d-V D/E humor); this-channel *style* went to
`channels/the-second-take/reference-channels.md`. (Method note: the 2026-07-02 pass was transcript +
packaging only; cut-cadence/loudness were directional. Full-video watching works as of 2026-07-04.)

**High-RPM topic selection (what the money mega-hits actually are).**
- The reliable high-view + high-RPM cluster is **white-collar/personal-finance anxiety**: careers (RTO,
  LinkedIn, investment banking, HR), housing/mortgages, banking mechanics, auto finance, AI-job-loss,
  billionaire tax (How Money Works, ColdFusion). Pulls finance/insurance/B2B-SaaS inventory (premium
  CPM) while staying broadly relatable. They deliberately avoid low-RPM viral bait.
- **Newsy tie-in spikes:** the 1M+ outliers are usually news-reactive (a fresh earnings loss, a
  collapse, an AI-bubble moment) shipped the same week (Patrick Boyle ran the FTX story as several
  videos in ~2 weeks off one filing). A real high-CPM lever, but it fights our batch-produce discipline
  — reserve it for genuinely big money events.
- **CPM caution on subject choice:** the mega-hits skew to **legal** corporate empires and finance;
  drug / "most illegal business" angles risk limited-ads (Magnates' Silk Road hit monetizes worse than
  its legal-empire hits). Keep targets legal-and-legible.

**Making finance numbers watchable (finance execution of universal §5a/§5b).**
- **Tangible yardstick, always:** never "$400 billion" — always "= nine Manhattan Projects" / "twice
  Saudi Arabia's GDP."
- **Change one variable at a time** on a visible cost-breakdown (How Money Works' iPhone: "$42 → $263 →
  +$221 to you"). **Two-number contrast** as a payload beat (homeowner median net worth **$255,000** vs
