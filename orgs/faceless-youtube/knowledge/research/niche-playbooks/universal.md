# Universal content playbook (the "manager doc")

The cross-niche **craft** rules for making faceless, AI-generated, dual-format content in this
pipeline. **Everything here is true for EVERY niche**, so it lives here once. Each niche file
(`<niche>.md` in this folder) holds ONLY what's specific to that niche and assumes these rules.

**Skills should read:** `knowledge/playbook.md` (operating rules/policy) + **this file** (craft
doctrine + generative principles) + the active channel's niche file (specifics).
`knowledge/playbook.md` is operating-rules; this is content craft.

*Rewritten 2026-07-01 from a fresh deep research pass across universal virality, AI-gen creator
survival, retention psychology, and 2025-2026 platform data. Sources at bottom.*

---

## 0. What this file is (and how to use it)

This is doctrine first, formulas second. The **why** (emotional lever + psychological mechanism)
is named alongside every **what** (title pattern / hook archetype / opening structure / retention
tactic) — so a scriptwriter can **invent variants** rather than clone examples. Every list below
is a starting library, not a fixed menu.

Rules of the road that permeate everything:
- **Entertainment, not education.** Every piece of content is a story that happens to teach — never
  a lesson that happens to have visuals. See §1.
- **One lever per channel, not per video.** The channels that survived the July 2025 AI-slop
  crackdown all lock a single dominant emotional lever at the channel level. See §1.
- **Arousal, not valence, predicts virality** (Berger & Milkman, *JMR* 2012). Awe, anger, dread,
  amusement spread; low-arousal sadness doesn't. Every lever below is a specific shape of arousal.
- **Emotional payoff, not "the lesson."** No video may end with "and that's why…" or "the takeaway
  is…". The audience takeaway is a *feeling*, not a bullet point. See §7.
- **Craft is generative.** Everything here is named with its psychological mechanism so the
  scriptwriter can compose new variants, not just fill in slot-templates.

## 1. Doctrine: entertainment, not education

Every winning "informative" YouTube channel — from Kurzgesagt to Wendover to Zack D. Films to
Coffeezilla — shares one doctrine: **the video is a story that happens to teach, not a lesson that
happens to have visuals.** A peer-reviewed study of 200+ science YouTube videos (Frontiers in
Communication, 2020) found videos with medium-to-high "story status" were **3.16× more likely to
be popular**, and moderately-to-highly emotionally-activating videos were roughly 3× more likely
to hit high view counts. Every tactic in this playbook is a lever on those two variables.

### 1-P. The payload rule — the primary axis (read before the lever taxonomy)

The lever taxonomy below (§1a) is real, but it is the **second** question, not the first. The channels
that actually win — Explains101, Crayon Capital, Wendover, Zack D. Films — lead with a concrete
**payload** and use emotion as the *frame* around it, never as a substitute for it. Get the order
wrong and you produce mood with nothing inside it: *"you have sixty seconds… it feels like safety but
it isn't… the water is already deciding when it ends."* Four lines, zero information, no reason to keep
watching. **Portent is not payload.**

**The rule:** before a word is scripted, every video AND every short must name — in one sentence — the
**one concrete thing the viewer learns or realizes**: a specific fact, mechanism, number, or genuine
"wait, *what*." If the honest answer is only "a feeling," it fails the gate and does not get made.

- **Information is the spine; emotion is essential but rides on it — and the emotional palette is
  WIDE.** Emotion is not optional and not "secondary" in importance — a fact with no feeling is a
  textbook. But the feeling must be *earned by the payload*, and it is drawn from the **full human
  range: curiosity, sympathy, recognition ("that's so true"), amusement, mild surprise, satisfaction,
  unease** — not only the high-arousal "WOW" band (dread / awe / outrage). **Do not chase the huge
  emotional buy-in.** Big-WOW emotion is hard to earn and, forced, becomes exactly the cliché
  trailer-voice we're killing (§1d-R). A quiet, real "huh, I never thought about it that way" beats a
  strained "you won't BELIEVE what happens next" every time. The channel's locked lever (§1a) sets the
  *dominant* register; within it, use the quiet colors, not just the loud one.
- **Every line earns its place by adding information or advancing the payload.** Test: if a line can be
  deleted with **zero information lost, cut it.** Abstraction and portent ("everything you know is
  wrong," "the fight you're going to lose," bare countdowns) almost always fail this test — that failure
  is what read as "bad movie-trailer" copy.
- **Withholding is a LONG-FORM tactic, not a universal one.** In a 12-minute video you may hold the
  biggest reveal for the final 20% (§7) — because you pay it off *later, in the same video*. In a
  **short there is no "later,"** so withholding *is* emptiness: a short must deliver **and close** its
  payload inside its runtime (§11). Inheriting long-form withholding into shorts is the single most
  common way this pipeline produces vapor.
- **Curiosity gap = a promise you then keep.** Open a specific loop, then close it (in-video). A gap you
  open and never close reads as clickbait, and the 2025-2026 algorithm throttles it as dissatisfaction.

Everything downstream keys off this: `idea-generator` gates and scores on the payload, `scriptwriter`
writes to it, `metadata-writer` promises only what the payload can keep.

### 1a. The emotional lever taxonomy (secondary axis — the register, not the content)

> **Read §1-P first.** The lever is *how* the payload is delivered, not a replacement for it. Lever
> singularity still governs channel identity (below), but a video that pulls a lever with no concrete
> payload underneath is slop with good lighting.

A viable channel picks **ONE dominant lever** and defends it. Cross-lever channels get flagged as
templated slop even when individual videos are strong. Here are the 10 named levers, ranked by
their proven weight in AI-gen-friendly niches:

| # | Lever | Psychological mechanism | Faceless-friendly? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Curiosity gap** (Loewenstein 1994) | Awareness of a gap between what you know and want to know creates a specific discomfort that drives continued watching against intention. **This is the carrier wave** for every other lever — it's how they package for the click. | Yes — universal |
| 2 | **Morbid curiosity** (Scrivner scale, 2021) | Threat-learning adaptation. Four dimensions: **dangerous minds**, **paranormal danger**, **bodily injuries**, **interpersonal violence**. Consuming information about danger without becoming a victim is a survival benefit. | Yes |
| 3 | **Awe** (Keltner & Haidt 2003) | Perceived vastness that dwarfs the self + schema accommodation (existing mental models can't absorb the stimulus, forcing new ones). Top *positive* high-arousal driver of sharing. | Yes |
| 4 | **Morbid awe / dread** — vicarious body-in-extremity | Sustained anxiety about a body enduring abnormal conditions, without gore. Payoff is **duration and survival**, not learning about injury. | Yes |
| 5 | **Righteous anger / moral outrage** (Crockett, Brady & Van Bavel) | Outrage at a perceived moral violation is intrinsically rewarding. Platform algorithms preferentially amplify moral-emotional language. **Post-2024 platforms throttle pure ragebait** — outrage now needs pairing with evidence. | Yes, with named-antagonist discipline |
| 6 | **Vindication / forbidden knowledge / "you were lied to"** | Reward is *epistemic superiority* — the viewer performs the "aha" themselves. Adjacent to outrage but distinct: righteousness of knowing, not of blame. | Yes |
| 7 | **Schadenfreude / status-collapse spectatorship** | Pleasure at deserved misfortune of high-status figures reinforces Just-World belief. Distinct from outrage: outrage wants action; schadenfreude wants spectacle. | Yes |
| 8 | **Hope-porn / aspirational envy** — vicarious reward | Emotional contagion via mirror-neuron response to observed joy + Just-World reinforcement. **Requires a visible face at the moment of the win** — mirror neurons need faces. | **No — hard for faceless.** |
| 9 | **Tribal identity signaling** | Content that lets viewers assert group identity (usually by hating an outgroup) outperforms content that lets them like an ingroup (PNAS 2024). Motivation is identity performance in the comments. | Yes |
| 10 | **Wonder + smart-feeling** (puzzle/riddle) | Lower-arousal, broadly-shareable. Makes viewer feel intelligent. Riddles convert retention structurally (viewers must stay for the answer). | Yes |

**Rules for lever selection:**
- **Faceless channels default to:** morbid curiosity, awe, dread, wonder, outrage,
  forbidden-knowledge, schadenfreude, tribal identity. **Hope-porn is off-limits without a face.**
- **The channel locks the lever, not the video.** Zack D. Films = dread. Bright Side = wonder.
  Infographics Show = safe transgression. Coffeezilla = vindication. Cross-lever channels get
  flagged by the algorithm AND read as slop by humans.
- **Curiosity gap is not the lever — it's the delivery mechanism.** When creators describe a video
  as "curiosity gap-driven," they usually mean "outrage packaged as a gap" or "morbid curiosity
  packaged as a gap." Name the underlying arousal lever; the gap is how you carry it.
- **Sadness doesn't spread — but melancholy packaged as awe does.** Kurzgesagt's cosmically-
  melancholic episodes look like counter-examples until you strip the visuals: dominant arousal is
  awe (vast scale + schema accommodation); melancholy is a garnish that makes the awe feel adult.
  If your video's dominant emotion is sadness at rest without cosmic scale, expect it not to travel.
- **Don't stack too many high-arousal levers in one video.** Dread + outrage + gore reads as
  ragebait and gets throttled post-2024. Kurzgesagt's optimistic-nihilism frame is arousal
  management: dread cued, resolved into awe.

### 1b. The 12 tactics that make teaching feel like entertainment

Every one of these is a lever on story-status or emotional-arousal (the two Frontiers 2020
predictors). Apply to every long-form script.

1. **Withhold the reveal via 5–7 setup-tension-payoff cycles** *(long-form only — see §1-P).* Each
   subtopic is its own micro-story with its own promise and its own punchline, all paid off within the
   same video. Veritasium's electricity video postpones the answer through an entire experiment. **Do
   not carry this into shorts** — a short has no "later" to pay off in, so it must close its own loop
   (§11).
2. **Personify institutions as characters.** Abstract entities (airlines, ports, FDA, an electricity
   grid) become protagonists or antagonists with motives, wins, and losses. Rewrite every "the
   industry did X" line as "a person or company **wanted** X, and **fought/hid/schemed** to get it."
3. **Audience-as-detective.** The viewer is a co-investigator, not a student. Use "I noticed…",
   "here's what's weird…", "so I dug in and found…". Show the work — screenshots, filings, annotated
   maps.
4. **Emotional arc management.** The information is neutral; the *feeling* toward it is designed.
   Every downbeat is followed by an up-beat. Draft the emotional map before the informational map.
5. **Extended single analogy (90–120s mine, not 10-second sprinkle).** Spend 90–120s inside one
   vivid scenario and extract multiple sub-points from it. Kurzgesagt spends 2 minutes on a
   table-tennis rally to cover spatial prediction, temporal prediction, parallel processing.
6. **Visual anchor before context.** Front-load 30–60s with a striking visual whose meaning is
   unexplained. Johnny Harris: "the visuals are telling the story, the script is only there to
   support them." First frame must contain a visual question.
7. **Punchline-first opening.** First 15 seconds must contain either the counterintuitive claim or
   the dramatic question. Kill "let me tell you about the history of…" on sight.
8. **Treat the audience as smart.** Cut all "as you probably know…", "it's really simple…", "don't
   worry if this sounds complicated." Explain jargon **once** via analogy and move on.
9. **Deadpan aside / character-driven narration.** A dry aside every 30–60s is a retention lever
   pure info-narration doesn't have. Half as Interesting, Sam O'Nella, Casually Explained all built
   on this. Consistency across videos makes the *narrator* the brand.
10. **Forbidden-knowledge framing.** "Everyone believes X, but X is wrong." Openly state the
    received wisdom, then dismantle it.
11. **Named antagonist per video.** Every video needs ONE named opposing force — a person, a
    company, a physical law, a misconception. Name by 60 seconds and re-invoke at every beat.
12. **Cascading revelations / stakes escalation.** Start specific and small; systematically expand
    implications until stakes are civilizational. Specific fact → immediate implication → industry
    implication → societal implication → existential. Each 2-minute segment raises stakes one level.

### 1c. Anti-patterns that read as "school"

- **Fact-list with narration** (reading a Wikipedia entry aloud).
- **Slow historical lead-in** ("In 1892, in a small town in Bavaria…") with no cold open.
- **Curriculum coverage** — chasing "the same 10 topics everyone covered."
- **Passive information delivery** — no rhetorical question, no invitation to speculate.
- **Under-explained analogies, over-explained facts.** The reverse of Kurzgesagt.
- **Even, uninflected pacing** — no wow-beats, no stakes raises.
- **"And that's why…" closer.** Payoff must be *felt*, not stated. See §7.

### 1d. Register: general audience vs niche audience

A general audience never asked to be there. A stranger's default state is bounce. The register that
earns a stranger is systematically different from the register that keeps a subscriber. **Paddy
Galloway's CCN rule** captures it:

> Every video should serve **C**ore fans, **C**asual returners, AND **N**ew viewers. Ship only if
> all three can click and enjoy.

Concrete register shifts for a general audience (apply to every script):

| Axis | Niche register (avoid) | General-audience register (use) |
|---|---|---|
| **Opening** | Callback to prior video / format continuity | Front-load spectacle, stakes, one "wow" the scroller grasps in half a second |
| **Jargon** | Free use of specialist terms | Every specialist term translated into a functional metaphor. Rule: 15–25 words per sentence max, active present tense |
| **Analogies** | Rapid-fire references rewarding pattern recognition | One analogy, developed deeply, 90–120s per metaphor |
| **Stakes** | Internal to the discipline ("this could 3× your MRR") | Universal: health, family, survival, humiliation, freedom, money |
| **Assumed knowledge** | References to past videos, community memes | Zero — establish who/what/why inside 15 seconds |
| **Character POV** | Creator's own authority carries the video | Single named human whose face + voice carry the stakes (or, faceless: one named person whose story carries the video) |
| **Packaging** | Insider signals ("Part 4: The Sourdough Hydration Deep-Dive") | Comparisons, time-brackets, before/after, extreme conditions |

**Named tactics for the general-audience register:**
1. **CCN pass.** Ship only if Core + Casual + New can all click and enjoy.
2. **Curse-of-knowledge audit.** Read your own script as if you've never heard of the topic. Every
   unexplained noun is a suspect.
3. **Universal stakes swap.** Replace niche-internal KPIs with survival/money/family/status/freedom.
4. **Named-human POV.** Anchor on one person (ideally not the creator) articulating stakes in their
   own words.
5. **Single-analogy depth.** Pick one metaphor, mine it for 90–120s.
6. **Specific-to-significant arc.** Specific surprise gets the click; widening implication earns
   retention.
7. **Reframe > dilute.** Don't remove the niche — repackage it. JESSER's basketball ideas went from
   300–400K views to 5–10M once reframed as universally-shaped stories without abandoning basketball.

### 1d-R. Default voice register: plain, concrete, specific (the anti-trailer-voice rule)

The **house default is plain-spoken, concrete, and specific** — the register of Explains101 ("your
parents bought houses easily; you can't — here's the mechanism"), Crayon Capital's documentary-story
narration, Casually Explained's deadpan specificity. It leads with a real fact and trusts the fact to
carry the interest. High-arousal "epic narration" is **not** the default — it's an **opt-in register**
for the one or two niches that earn it (horror-internet-lore's dread; a business *collapse* story's
gravity), and even there it must ride on concrete substance, never replace it. Each channel's register
is a **locked `dna.md` field**, not a per-video choice.

**Positive register rules (apply to every script):**
- **A concrete noun or number in every line.** "Three known Italian foods," "banks create money by
  making loans," "the side windows are tempered, the windshield is laminated" — name *what*, *how much*,
  *when*. Specifics land; abstractions evaporate.
- **Open plain, not loud.** A relatable question or a flat surprising statement beats a threat. Kill the
  trailer-voice cold open.
- **Lower the arousal so the facts can land, and use the quiet emotions.** Deadpan/calm delivery makes
  a specific detail hit harder than cranked intensity, which buries it. Aim for the achievable feelings
  — curiosity, sympathy, recognition, amusement, mild unease — not a strained "WOW." Real quiet emotion
  is both more honest and far more reproducible at scale than forced high-arousal.
- **Ground stakes in the viewer's real life,** not cosmic doom. "Your parents bought houses easily and
  you can't" beats "the fight you're going to lose."

**Banned "trailer-voice / empty-portent" patterns (a script fails review if it uses these):**
- Vague menace with no referent: *"the water is already deciding when it ends," "it has already won an
  argument you didn't know you were having."* Say the actual mechanism instead.
- Portent clichés: *"everything you think you know is wrong," "that's the fight you're going to lose,"
  "nobody tells you this," "what happens next will…," "the part nobody talks about."*
- Bare countdown/threat with no information: *"you have 60 seconds"* as a whole beat, carrying no fact.
- Sentences that survive with **zero information removed** — if deleting the line loses no fact, it was
  portent; cut it.

Why this is load-bearing: our own first fixture failed exactly here — a dread short that delivered a
*feeling* and no information, in movie-trailer cadence. The lever was fine; the missing payload and the
overwrought register were the bug. This block is the fix; `scriptwriter` §5 and the `humanizer` pass
enforce it.

### 1d-V. Voice craft: narrator persona, cadence, and humor

Payload + register (§1-P, §1d-R) decide *what* is said and in what mood. This section is *how a person
says it* — the layer that separates the channels the audience loves (Casually Explained, Half as
Interesting, Sam O'Nella, Wendover) from competent-but-lifeless AI narration.

**A. Narrator persona — the channel is a person.** Every channel that wins has a recognizable narrator
*personality*, and that personality is the moat (the §13 Simon-Whistler point: the voice is the brand).
Each channel locks a **narrator persona** in `dna.md` — a few traits (e.g. "dry, curious, unimpressed
by hype, explains like a smart friend at a bar"). The scriptwriter writes *toward* that person on every
line. Consistency across videos makes the narrator the brand; a voiceless script is the tell of a
content farm.

**B. Cadence — kill the metronome.** The #1 reason narration sounds robotic is **sentence-length
uniformity** (Gary Provost's "stuck record": "This sentence has five words. Here are five more words…
several together become monotonous"). The cure is **variance, not brevity** — collide short and long.
A mechanical **cadence self-check** every script must pass:
- No **3 consecutive sentences within ~3 words of the same length** — break the run.
- **Every paragraph has at least one ≤5-word sentence AND one ≥20-word sentence.**
- **No grammatical template repeated 3× in a row** ("X. Then Y. Then Z."; "Hit the window. Hit the
  window!"). Emphasis is built by escalation and specificity, **never by repeating a clause or adding
  "!".** Fix "Hit the window. Hit the window!" → *"She hits the window. Nothing. She hits it again."*
  (the beat "Nothing." does what the "!" was faking).
- **Punctuation is the breath score** (and it's what carries prosody through TTS): comma = catch,
  period = full stop, **a new sentence or a colon for a sharp cut-in/reveal**, **ellipsis … = a held,
  lingering beat**, colon = lean-in. Author cadence in the punctuation; reserve explicit `[PAUSE]` cues
  for genuine dramatic gaps only (a wall of identical pause cues is its own metronome).
  **HARD RULE: NO em dashes or en dashes in a script, EVER (the `—` and `–` characters).** They read as
  an AI tell and add nothing the ear can hear (the viewer only ever hears the audio); every cut-in becomes
  a period, comma, or colon instead. This is a hard formatting gate, enforced in the humanize pass.
- Compress sentence length as tension rises; isolate a reveal on its own short line. Add ~one "human
  move" per beat (a mid-thought correction, a short wry aside) — but vary them so they don't become their
  own tic. *(Second-person "you" is a per-channel dial — §1d-V F; channels that lock third-person/one-narrator,
  e.g. The Second Take, do NOT use it.)*
- **The short-punch rule — comedic or payload, never portent.** A standalone ultra-short line is earned
  ONLY when it lands **dry wit or a concrete payload**: a deadpan reveal, an absurd undercut, the
  punchline of an understatement ("There was one small problem with Poyais. It didn't exist."). The
  brevity is the *timing of a joke or the snap of a fact.* A clipped fragment that manufactures **drama
  or gravitas with no joke and no fact** ("Everything changed." · "The rules were gone." · "For good.")
  is trailer-voice portent (§1d-R) and reads as AI — cut it or write the real sentence. And **never
  stack** short fragments in a run to fake momentum: one earned beat after a long build, never three in
  a row ("A loan. For a government. Of a country that wasn't there." is the run to avoid). The reference
  channels that use short punches heavily (Crayon Capital, Patrick Boyle — both dry, *not* comedy
  channels) always use them as single witty/factual landings, never as portent staccato.

**C. Humor & cultural texture — fact-carrying, gravity-scaled (niche-tuned).** The comedic rate is **not a
constant** — it scales with the subject's gravity (the DIAL, §1d-V F); whatever the density, the rule that
keeps it from being cringe is absolute: **the joke must carry a
fact** — if deleting the line loses information (an example, a correction, a real drawback), keep it;
if it only loses a laugh, cut it. Mechanics that travel: *deadpan understatement · anticlimax list ·
hyper-specific example ("the only person who says that is my mum") · the teaching-joke (the punchline
delivers the correction) · mock-enthusiasm reframe · register-mismatch (calm voice, absurd subject) ·
parenthetical throwaway.* **Cultural/historical references** are texture, not memes: use **evergreen
"cultural furniture"** (well-known history, common life experience, legible deep-cut shows) that
rewards the smart viewer without punishing the one who misses it — **never trend/meme-chasing** (dates
fast, reads as try-hard). **Humor is set by a gravity DIAL, banded per channel in `dna.md`** (§1d-V F):
the *density* scales with the subject's gravity *within* a channel — it is **not a fixed per-niche rate.**
Finance/YMYL is not humor-off (**refined 2026-07-02, see §1d-V D: dry humor works even in serious money
content under four locks; only fact-less or viewer-aimed jokes backfire**); **dread/morbid** keeps the hard
rule that levity attaches to the *mundane detail*, never the danger or the death — the payload stays 100%
sincere, and humor goes fully OFF on the life-or-death line.

**D. Humor works in finance/YMYL — correction (2026-07-02; Patrick Boyle + Casually Explained + How
Money Works).** The earlier "earnest for finance/YMYL; forcing humor there backfires" was **too strict.**
Dry humor is *credibility-positive* even in serious money content, under four locks: (1) **fact-rider
test** — after any quip, delete it; if information vanishes (an example, a real drawback, a sourced
detail), keep it; if only a laugh is lost, cut it. No joke is load-bearing for information. (2) **Target
discipline** — the butt is always the fraudster / institution / the fool, **never the viewer's money or
the concept being taught.** (3) **Deadpan, plain delivery, with the citation immediately *before* the
quip** to license it (Boyle: quote the court filing, *then* the dry aside). (4) **density set by topic gravity (the
DIAL, §1d-V F), and humor OFF entirely while teaching the actual mechanism** (Boyle's "how short interest exceeds 100%"
explainer is 100% straight; Crayon cuts jokes to zero for the human-cost payoff). So **dry humor belongs
in finance** — the "earnest only" stance was wrong; how *much* is the gravity DIAL's call (§1d-V F), not a
fixed rate. What actually backfires is a joke that carries no fact, or one aimed at the viewer.

**E. More cadence + humor mechanics (2026-07-02 reference research).**
- **Accurate-runway → terminal-turn** (Casually Explained): a long, genuinely-*correct* setup sentence
  (20–30 words) detonated by a short flat drop at the *end* of the sentence/paragraph. The more accurate
  the runway sounds, the harder the turn hits — the delete-test made mechanical. Encode as a rule: after
  any 20–30-word data sentence, land a **≤5-word deadpan undercut** (Half as Interesting's signature).
- **"By X I mean Y" redefinition** (Half as Interesting): expose a euphemism/lie by restating it plainly
  — "by creating a network, I mean tricking a company into building one and then stealing it."
  Finance-perfect for spin.
- **Undercut-your-own-authority** (Half as Interesting): run your own mini-analysis, then dismiss it ("a
  p-value of 0.035… to the extent it does anything, which it doesn't") — models honesty and comedy at once.
- **The pause is a held beat, not silence** (Internet Historian): the "pause before the punchline" is
  almost never dead air — it's a *held shot + sustained music* (a 13-second wordless beat after "$570
  million to build. And you could tell. You could *really* tell."). For our TTS pipeline: carry the beat
  with a held visual + music bed, not just a bare `[PAUSE]`.
- **Deflate-don't-resolve close** (Casually Explained, HAI, Wendover, Crayon, RealLifeLore): do NOT
  summarize or CTA-inspire. End on the smallest stake, a shrug, a moral mirror, or a historical rhyme,
  then hard-cut. "And that's why…" is banned (§7); this is its positive replacement.
- **Tonal modulation by act** (Crayon): humor budget scales *inversely* with stakes — front-load
  fact-jokes in the setup, cut humor to near-zero for the payoff / human-cost beat. Density is
  act-dependent, not constant. **Calibrate the dial to the audience's personal STAKE (2026-07-07):** for a
  distant curiosity story (a 200-year-old con), don't cut to zero or go solemn/literary — stop joking *at* the
  deaths but keep the light register; reserve full comedy-off for genuinely resonant, personally-felt suffering.
  The back half + close stay in the same curiosity voice; **no paper-conclusion / no timeless-lesson wrap-up**
  (a channel's `storytelling-grammar.md` §1.11 exit menu).
- **Measured joke density in the admired channels (evidence for the DIAL, not a target to force):**
  ~2.5–3.5 gags/min for the comedy-*primary* explainers (Casually Explained, HAI, Sam O'Nella); the
  storytelling explainers (Crayon, HeyHistorically) **swing 4–5× within their own catalog** — dense on
  money-absurdity, near-zero on human cost. So density is **set by topic gravity, not a channel constant**
  (§1d-V F): a machine that forces a fixed rate reads as vapor. Don't chase the comedy-primary channels'
  baseline on a payload-primary topic — that restraint is a human-writer moat.

**F. Register is a DIAL set by TOPIC GRAVITY; the narrator is a per-channel lock (updated 2026-07-07).** The
comedic *density* is **not a per-channel constant and not a fixed rate** — the writer sets it by the
subject's gravity *before writing*, and each channel's `dna.md` sets only the **band** it swings inside:
- **money-absurdity / systemic farce → hot** (dense wit).
- **human-villainy / ruin → wry + sparse.**
- **human cost (death, cruelty, personal ruin) → comedy OFF entirely** — the earned tonal drop is where the
  emotion lands.
The reference storytelling channels swing **4–5× between these modes within one catalog** (Crayon runs dense
on a bubble, near-silent on a robber-baron's victims); a channel that fixes one rate reads as tone-deaf.
`dna.md` positions the band — a dry-sprinkle explainer sits low; a storytelling-first channel (a finance-
*narrative* rather than a pure explainer) sits higher and more comedic — but the dial still moves with gravity
inside that band. The comedic toolbox is the same at any setting: anachronistic-analogy-that-*teaches* ["jazz
became the Wi-Fi of the 20s"], deadpan undercut, ironic re-label, absurd escalation, bathos, bait-and-switch.
The **fact-rider delete-test** and **evergreen-only** (no dating memes/slang) are absolute at every setting.
  - **The humor bar — clearing the toolbox ≠ funny.** A comedic beat ships only if it ALSO: maps its analogy
    onto a modern thing the viewer *instantly & universally* pictures (PASS "a startup with no product";
    **BANNED** dead/literary metaphors like "the load-bearing wall of the con" and coined repurposings nobody's
    heard); uses **plain words with zero narrator self-editorializing** (a transition may label the *fact's*
    quality, never the narrator's mood); and **gloss-or-cuts** every unfamiliar name/term in one beat. Channel
    version with worked PASS/FAIL: a channel's `storytelling-grammar.md §2.5`.
- **Narrator voice is a per-channel lock.** A channel may lock **one narrator, third-person, no second
  person** — all dramatized beats delivered as the narrator's **reported speech** (never voiced character
  exchanges; where this is locked, **no quotes in the script at all** — everything is characterized, §5c/§5d),
  never casting the viewer as a character. Where a channel sets this, the §1b tactics that use "you" /
  audience-as-detective / character-voices are recast into the narrator's own third-person voice or dropped.
- **Illustrative devices** (a transparently-hypothetical "stock barber," narrator-described) are allowed and
  distinct from sourced facts — they don't touch the leash, as long as real named people stay sourced.
- The full per-channel register + macro-architecture grammar lives in the channel's
  `storytelling-grammar.md` (see **§5d**).

### 1e. Why this doctrine matters (survival)

YouTube's **July 15, 2025 "inauthentic content" policy** demonetizes templated, mass-produced,
insight-free video at the **whole-channel** level. AI-assisted is explicitly fine; templated AI slop
is not. Enforcement through January 2026: **16 major channels terminated, ~35M subscribers wiped,
~4.7B lifetime views erased, ~$9.8M/yr in ad revenue killed.** A Kapwing sweep flagged 278 AI-slop
channels totaling ~63B views and estimated ~21% of Shorts served to new users are pure AI slop.

**The best defense is emotional-lever singularity, not visual variety.** YouTube's classifier sees
templated formats. Human viewers see templated *feelings*. A channel that varies its aesthetic
template but locks its emotional lever reads as consistent-to-humans and non-templated-to-classifier.
A channel that varies its emotional lever but keeps a rigid visual template reads as slop to both.

## 2. The originality moat (July 2025 policy — non-negotiable)

Verbatim official language from YouTube's July 15, 2025 policy update (Ritchie/Creator Liaison):

> "Mass-produced or repetitive content … that looks like it's made with a template with little to
> no variation across videos, or content that's easily replicable at scale."

Enumerated prohibited examples:
- Songs modified to change pitch or speed but otherwise identical.
- **AI-generated content made with generic templates giving the impression of mass production without
  adding the creator's original, authentic insights.**
- Mass-produced content using a similar or unoriginal template across multiple videos.

Ritchie's specific examples: channels that upload narrative stories with only superficial differences
between them; channels that upload slideshows that all have the same narration.

**Positive carve-out:** "YouTube welcomes creators using AI tools to enhance storytelling, and
channels that use AI in their content remain eligible for monetization" — provided synthetic realism
is disclosed. Escape hatch on reused content: monetization survives "if you've added significant
original commentary, modifications, or educational or entertainment value."

**Scope is channel-wide, not per-video.** One templated pattern can demonetize everything.

### 2a. The compliant AI-gen playbook (all six required)

Every faceless AI-gen channel that survived the crackdown does all six:

1. **Original research/substance.** Synthesize ≥2–3 sources per video; add facts the top search
   result doesn't have. A "reading Wikipedia aloud" script is uploading to a ban.
2. **A stated POV/thesis.** Take a position, name a winner/lesson, editorialize. Neutral recap = slop.
3. **A repeatable proprietary framework / signature format.** Your own recurring structure ("The
   Autopsy" 5-failure model, "Two Timelines", "Reading the Report", "Case File"). This is the
   single strongest differentiator and the thing skills template around.
4. **A consistent authored identity.** One voice, one visual system, one worldview; any frame is
   "yours."
5. **Premium TTS or a real recorded human voice.** ElevenLabs prosody-model tier is the current
   minimum viable; default TTS (Google/OpenAI stock) is documented as "a direct ticket to a yellow
   icon." (Prosody accuracy: ElevenLabs ~64.6% vs Google/OpenAI default ~45.8%.)
6. **Named creator identity behind the voice.** Even AI Explained is "Philip." Anonymous + faceless
   + AI-narrated is the exact profile the classifier hunts.

### 2b. The 90-second AI-fatigue wall

Retention Rabbit (2025 State of YouTube Audience Retention, ~10K videos): **"Monotonous AI
narration leads to an average 35% viewer drop-off within the first 45 seconds vs. human narration."**
Companion figure: heavy AI-generated content shows ~70% lower retention vs. human-fronted
comparables. Directional, not law (single-vendor sample), but the mechanism is real:

- **Flat pitch contour** — no F0 rise/fall for contrast.
- **Missing prosodic hierarchy** — no pause after key claims, no accelerando into a beat.
- **Uniform loudness / RMS** — no whisper-to-emphasis dynamic range.
- **Wrong register for content intent** — a horror line in newsreader-neutral is an uncanny valley.

Rule: write for **cadence and specificity** (short sentences, hard nouns, varied stress) even more
than for hook type. Voice is a channel asset, not a per-video choice — one recognizable voice
register across every upload.

### 2c. Named refusals as identity

Every surviving faceless channel has a **named refusal** — a category of content the format
explicitly does not handle. Zack refuses non-visualizable topics. Bright Side refuses non-evergreen.
Infographics refuses on-camera hosts. These refusals hold the DNA legible.

Hard rule for the idea-generator: **study a format, never clone a specific rival.** Synthesize 3+
sources, change the lens, add the framework.

## 3. Titles

### 3a. Universal principles

Every winning title = **one concrete anchor (proper noun / number / "you") + one curiosity or
emotion trigger** — never both slots generic.

- Front-load the keyword/subject (search + mobile truncation); keep long-form ~40–55 chars.
- One idea per title. **Never answer the question in the title** — it opens the loop the video closes.
- Prefer second person ("you/your") when the topic allows.
- **Session watch time now outweighs raw CTR** (2025 satisfaction-weighted algorithm). A high-CTR
  click with low retention costs the channel more than a lower-CTR click with high retention.
  Mismatched title/thumbnail actively hurts.
- **Familiarity anchor (cold-start rule).** The concrete anchor should be something the audience
  *already knows* — a famous name, era, product, place, or a universally graspable premise — with the
  obscure specifics as the *payoff*, not the hook. An unknown proper noun (a person/company nobody's
  heard of) gives cold browse traffic no foothold. Lead instead with the biggest familiar thing the
  story touches: Warren Buffett, the Backstreet Boys, "the lottery," "the biggest gold discovery in
  history," "the richest country on Earth." **Obscure *stories* are a differentiation moat; obscure
  *anchors* kill CTR** — the fix for an obscure topic is packaging, not avoidance. Weight this hardest on
  a new/small channel with no format-trust yet (its early videos live or die on cold traffic); an
  established channel can lead with pure novelty because its audience already trusts the format. Even the
  channels that specialize in obscure stories title against a famous umbrella (Crayon Capital's biggest
  hits are *Wolf of Wall Street* and *2008*; HeyHistorically leans on Rome / WW2 / pirates).

### 3b. Anti-patterns (2025-2026 data)

- **Numbered lists in titles = ~11% fewer views on average** (300K-title 2025 study). Use only when
  the countdown structure is intrinsic to the content, never as decoration.
- Question-mark titles underperform declarative titles ~15–20% on long-form documentary content —
  punchline-first wins.
- "The Truth About X" is over-saturated — treat as burned.
- Neutral educational titles ("Understanding X," "The Economics of X") lose 3–5× on CTR to emotive
  collapse/reveal titles in most niches.

### 3c. Title pattern library (15, with psychological levers)

Each pattern names the lever so the writer can invent variants. Niche files add niche-flavored
formulas on top of these.

1. **Impossible-number frame.** *Lever:* cognitive dissonance from oddly specific big numbers.
   *Template:* "Why are [oddly specific number] [surprising nouns] in [mundane place]?" *Example:*
   Veritasium "Why Are 96,000,000 Black Balls on This Reservoir?" (100M+ views).
2. **Superlative extreme ($1 vs $1B).** *Lever:* contrast + escalation curiosity. *Template:*
   "$[tiny] vs $[absurd] [subject]." *Example:* MrBeast "$1 vs $1,000,000,000 Yacht" (200M+).
3. **Three-tier escalation.** *Lever:* rule-of-three completion instinct. *Template:* "[Small] vs
   [Medium] vs [Huge] [subject]." *Example:* Johnny Harris "$25K vs $25M vs $25B."
4. **Hidden-mechanism reveal.** *Lever:* knowledge-gap principle (Loewenstein). *Template:* "How
   [everyday thing] actually [surprising verb]." *Example:* Veritasium "How Electricity Actually
   Works."
5. **False-belief flip.** *Lever:* ego-defensive click. *Template:* "[Popular belief] is wrong" or
   "Everything you know about [X] is [false]."
6. **Second-person accusation.** *Lever:* self-referential processing (medial prefrontal cortex
   activation). *Template:* "You're [doing X wrong / missing Y / losing Z]."
7. **Forbidden explanation / suppressed info.** *Lever:* reactance + FOMO. *Template:* "The [thing]
   [authority] doesn't want you to know." *Caution:* verges on YouTube "misleading metadata" if
   unsupported.
8. **Withheld payoff / curiosity gap.** *Lever:* Zeigarnik effect. *Template:* "The [X] that
   [surprising result]." *Example:* "The Habit That Made Me a Millionaire" (many 5–20M).
9. **Time-boxed experiment / personal stakes.** *Lever:* pre-loaded story arc. *Template:* "I spent
   [N days] [doing X]." *Example:* MrBeast "I Spent 50 Hours in Solitary" (100M+).
10. **Stakes-first reveal.** *Lever:* loss aversion, morbid curiosity. *Template:* "[Terrible
    outcome] and here's why." *Example:* Chubbyemu "A Man Ate 100 Pounds of Rice. This Is What
    Happened to His [Organ]" (10M+).
11. **Still-unsolved / ongoing mystery.** *Lever:* cliffhanger-in-reality. *Template:* "The [thing]
    we still can't [solve/explain]." *Example:* LEMMiNO "Cicada 3301" (30M+).
12. **Authority-decoded / expert judgment.** *Lever:* vicarious credentialing. *Template:* "A
    [profession] rates [things]" or "A [expert] explains [phenomenon]."
13. **The real reason / hidden cause.** *Lever:* ownership of "the truth." *Template:* "The real
    reason [X happens]" or "Why [X] is actually about [Y]."
14. **Collapse frame / downfall.** *Lever:* morbid arc + schadenfreude. *Template:* "How [thing/
    person/company] [fell/collapsed/lost everything]." *Example:* "How Nokia Died" (5–15M variants).
15. **Named-antagonist reveal.** *Lever:* forbidden-knowledge signal. *Template:* "You've never heard
    of [name]. But [name] cost [huge number] of people [thing they lost]."

**Generative principle:** every one of these works because it pairs a **concrete anchor** with an
**emotional lever**. If a proposed title has one without the other, it's undercooked. If it stacks
two anchors and no lever, it reads as documentary; two levers and no anchor, it reads as clickbait.

### 3d. New title patterns from the reference channels (added 2026-07-02)

From a cross-channel scan of 10+ top videos each (How Money Works, Wendover, PolyMatter, Internet
Historian, Johnny Harris, Magnates Media, RealLifeLore). Each still obeys the anchor+lever rule; these
are new *shapes*, verified verbatim:

1. **Mocking-strawman title** *(vindication).* The title IS the dumb objection the video demolishes,
   often typed in sarcastic mixed-case. *Ex (How Money Works):* `bUt ThAt'S mY NeSt EgG!`,
   `jUsT BuIlD MoRe HoUsEs!!`. The viewer clicks to watch the strawman get dismantled. The single
   best-fitting pattern for a forbidden-knowledge/vindication channel.
2. **Definitive-version claim** *(curiosity + authority).* Claim the last word on a question the
   audience already half-knows: "[Topic], Explained Finally" / "The REAL Reason [X]" (Johnny Harris).
3. **Definite-article outcome-noun** *(verdict pre-load).* "The [Fall / Cost / Failure / Downfall] of
   [Proper Noun]" (Internet Historian, Wendover, Magnates). One abstract outcome noun pre-loads the
   verdict; the named subject supplies the anchor. Cleaner than a full sentence.
4. **Negative-adjective + trusted brand** *(betrayal).* "The [Disturbing/Dark] [History/Truth] of
   [Household Brand]" (Magnates "The Disturbing History of Coca-Cola") — weaponizes a trusted brand.
5. **Decline-verb + intensifier** *(schadenfreude/vindication).* "How [X] Got So [Terrible/Bad]" /
   "Why [X] is Suddenly [Failing]" / "The Slow, Quiet Death of [X]" (Wendover). The verb + intensifier
   signals a system rotting. Beats a neutral "Why X does Y" — prefer a moral/decline frame ("How Car
   Dealerships *Scam* America").
6. **Colon two-parter (hook : payoff-scope escalation).** "[Local story]: The [Global] [Crisis]"
   (PolyMatter "Why Sri Lanka is Collapsing: the Coming Global Food Crisis"). The colon escalates
   stakes from specific to civilizational.
7. **Rigid brand-prefix + deliberately flat topic label** *(register signal).* "Casually Explained:
   [bare noun]" — the *flatness* is the deadpan promise; the title is a straight man, the comedy lives
   in the video. A repeatable franchise tag ("[Topic] Explained Like You're 5" — Crayon) doubles as
   brand + permission-to-simplify and is NOT a burned numbered-list.

## 4. Hooks (0–5s long-form / 0–1.5s Shorts)

### 4a. The 55% first-minute cliff

**55% of viewers exit within the first 60 seconds** on the average YouTube video (Retention Rabbit
2025 benchmark). Average retention across all videos: **23.7%**. Only 1 in 6 videos (16.8%) clears
50%. **Establish value within 7 seconds** (Creator Insider guidance): if you're holding 70%+ at 30s,
you're strong; 75%+ is elite; below 60% means the hook is broken.

Shorts: the swipe-decision window is **1.3–1.8 seconds** (Paddy Galloway 3.3B-view study via VidIQ).
50–60% of drop-off happens in the first 3 seconds. Shorts with an immediate hook in the first 2
seconds retain 19% more viewers than slow-start shorts.

### 4b. Hook mechanics (generative principles)

The hook line has one job: **open the loop before the answer is conceivable**. Every archetype
below is a specific mechanism for opening that loop.

- **Front-load the payload.** MrBeast's leaked doc: front-load information in the first minute; do
  not build up to the payoff. Cut every word that isn't earning its place.
- **Visual arrives before verbal.** First frame contains the visual question. Narration completes
  the frame's tension.
- **No greeting, no logo splash, no self-introduction, no "in today's video."** Every one of these
  is measurably dead — 33%+ of viewers drop off in the first 30s when the intro is a generic
  greeting rather than an engaging hook. Faceless channels especially: the viewer didn't come for
  you, they came for the topic.

### 4c. Long-form hook archetype library (20)

Each names the psychological lever so scriptwriter can invent new instances.

1. **Timestamp-anchored artifact.** *Lever:* specificity as credibility. *Template:* "On the [day]
   of [month] [year], [ordinary agents] arrived at [ordinary location]. [Ordinary detail, then
   anomaly]." *Example:* Fascinating Horror (~5–7M per video).
2. **Counter-consensus challenge.** *Lever:* belief-defense reflex. *Template:* "Everything you've
   been told about [X] is [wrong / a lie / half the story]. [Concrete reason]." *Example:* Johnny
   Harris "Moon Landing Conspiracy" ("22% of Americans…").
3. **Impossible-contrast anchor.** *Lever:* brain freezes trying to resolve the gap. *Template:*
   "[Tiny number / cheap thing] vs [enormous number / expensive thing]." *Example:* MrBeast $1 vs
   $1B Yacht.
4. **Startling-statistic dropper.** *Lever:* specificity heuristic. *Template:* "[Precise %] of
   [group the viewer belongs to] [does bad/surprising thing] — [without knowing / and it's [X-ing]
   them]."
5. **Forbidden question.** *Lever:* curiosity + mild conspiracy. *Template:* "Why does nobody ask
   [obvious but taboo question about X]?" Measured at 15% viral rate + 78% completion in Opus.pro's
   8,254-clip corpus.
6. **False-belief flip.** *Lever:* self-image at stake. *Template:* "If you [common behavior],
   here's what's actually happening — and it's the opposite of what you think." Measured 69%
   completion rate in Reelforge's viral-formula analysis.
7. **In-media-res artifact / cold-open climax.** *Lever:* Zeigarnik effect. *Template:* "[Peak
   moment mid-sentence]. [Cut.] Six months earlier…" *Example:* Kurzgesagt cold open of "The world
   you see is not real."
8. **Second-person immersion.** *Lever:* bypasses narrator distrust. *Template:* "You [wake up /
   arrive at / open the door to] [ordinary setup]. [Wrong detail]. [Anomaly]." *Example:* horror/
   lore native; Zack D. Films' "if you were…"
9. **Time-bomb frame.** *Lever:* urgency without justification. *Template:* "By [near-future year],
   [large scary event] will [verb]. Here's what almost nobody is saying about why."
10. **Superlative anchor.** *Lever:* collapses evaluation cost. *Template:* "[World's / History's /
    The most / The only] [superlative noun] — and it's [twist]." *Example:* Veritasium "Why Are
    96M Black Balls on This Reservoir?" (~90M+).
11. **Small-detail-that-changed-everything.** *Lever:* disproportion ratio (small cause / big
    effect). *Template:* "[Big outcome] happened because of a [tiny thing]. Buried in [large
    document/system] was a single [detail]." *Native for:* engineering disasters, business
    collapses.
12. **Witness-account cold open.** *Lever:* testimony trust. *Template:* "[Ordinary person] was
    [doing mundane thing]. Then they saw / heard / found [anomaly]."
13. **Personal-stakes confession.** *Lever:* parasocial cost — vulnerability locks empathy.
    *Template:* "[N] months ago, I [was failing / was ready to quit]. Then [event]."
14. **Data-drop authority.** *Lever:* authority heuristic + specificity. *Template:* "I / we / a
    team analyzed [large N] [things]. Here's the [surprising pattern]." Opus.pro finding:
    Authority/Credibility hooks average highest views (6,706) of any bucket in their 8,254-clip
    study.
15. **Zoom-out reveal.** *Lever:* scale escalation. *Template:* "This [tiny object] doesn't look
    like much. But it's [what makes / caused / prevents] [enormous consequence]." *Native for:*
    Kurzgesagt, Veritasium.
16. **Paradox opener.** *Lever:* apparent contradiction demands resolution. *Template:* "[Year] was
    [superlative safe/good]. It was also [superlative disastrous for specific thing]."
17. **Rhetorical trap-question.** *Lever:* viewer's brain auto-answers wrong, then video is the
    correction. *Template:* "[Simple-sounding question]. Most people say [obvious answer]. They're
    [wrong / missing the interesting part]."
18. **Explicit promise / value-contract.** *Lever:* removes uncertainty. *Template:* "In the next
    [N] minutes, you'll [specific outcome]." Retention Rabbit: scripts with a specific value claim
    in the first 15s retain 52% vs 44% without.
19. **Insider-authority opener.** *Lever:* credentialed voice via role framing. *Template:* "As a
    [profession/insider], I've seen [claim]. Here's what almost nobody outside [industry] knows."
20. **Prophecy / stakes-of-inaction.** *Lever:* loss aversion is stronger than gain drive.
    *Template:* "If you don't [action] in the next [timeframe], [bad specific consequence]."

**Generative principle:** every archetype above is (1) a specific psychological lever + (2) a
sentence pattern that opens a loop the video will close. A writer who knows *why* the timestamp
works can invent variants ("At mile marker 47 on I-95…") without cloning Fascinating Horror.

### 4d. Short-form hook archetype library (10)

The Shorts environment collapses the hook window to ~1.3 seconds. Some long-form archetypes cross
over; these are short-form-native.

1. **Pattern-interrupt visual (frame-one action).** Frame one is *already mid-action* — a pour, a
   smash, a rip, a fall. Pattern interrupt in first 5s = +23% retention (Ventress data).
2. **On-frame text promise (3–7 words).** 85% of Shorts watchers start silent. Bold caption on
   frame 1 wins or loses the swipe. On-screen text during hook: +18% watch time.
3. **"Imagine if…" hypothetical.** Zack D. Films built 27M subs on this pattern; 22+ shorts have
   crossed 100M each.
4. **POV framing.** "POV:" as first two characters primes the viewer that they're inside the frame.
   High stitch/remix rate. Ugccopilot: "POV: You just discovered your coworker makes 2× your salary"
   — 23% viral rate, 67% completion.
5. **Confession / mid-thought voice.** "Wait — [claim]." / "Hear me out — [contrarian premise]."
   HookMafia 2026: "Overly polished intros are losing to hooks that feel like you caught someone
   mid-thought."
6. **Number-anchored first frame.** Big single number on screen ("$47,000," "3,200%"). Numbers
   precede words in visual salience.
7. **"This shouldn't exist" impossible-object reveal.** No motion needed — the impossibility is
   the interrupt.
8. **Direct second-person accusation.** "You" as first on-screen word. Higher stopping-power than
   "I" or "We."
9. **Micro-story cold open (10-word narrative).** A single sentence that is a whole story: "She
   opened the door. Her father was already dead."
10. **Comparison-frame (split-screen from frame 1).** Brain forced to compare. Comparison shorts
    pull 28M+ views on trivial premises (VidIQ corpus).

## 5. Opening structures (5–30s "second gate")

The hook line handles 0–5s. These structures handle **5–30s** — the window where the remaining 55%
of viewers still leave. They are how the writer *builds a corridor* from the hook to the video
body. Hook and second-gate are **separate craft problems**: a great hook with no second-gate
structure loses at 15 seconds. Require both per script.

**The hook-line jargon rule (load-bearing for money/technical niches).** Never lead the first line with a
domain term or the mechanism itself (bonds, yield, CDO, securitization, leverage). Jargon in second one
signals "lecture" and gates out everyone who isn't already fluent — the exact opposite of the familiarity
anchor (§3a). Lead with the universal, concrete **human / absurd / visual** image; the mechanism is the
*payload the hook pays off into*, a beat later — never the hook itself. (E.g. open on "a few hundred
families sailed to a country that didn't exist," not "you could buy sovereign bonds for a fictional
state." Applies to any jargon-heavy niche, not just finance.)

10 structures, each with mechanism:

1. **Cold-open-then-rewind.** Show the climax (or a fragment) first, cut, then rewind to "how we got
   here." Viewer stays because they now have a specific unresolved image.
2. **Three-question ladder.** Three escalating questions before the topic is named. Each is
   progressively bigger. Viewer commits because they now taste the payoff scope.
3. **False-premise flip.** State the conventional belief plainly. Pause. Contradict it. The pause
   is doing structural work.
4. **Ticking-clock frame.** Announce an explicit timer inside the opening ("This next 90 seconds
   decides whether the whole building comes down"). Retention benefits from every clock reference.
5. **Artifact-then-context reveal.** Open on a physical object (a scan, a document, a bolt). Hold.
   Only then reveal what it is. Visual before narration.
6. **Historical zoom-out.** Start with the immediate specific event, then explicitly widen to a
   century-scale pattern, then return. Signals *this is not just news, it's history*.
7. **Stakes escalation ladder.** Three consecutive escalations in the first 30s. Something bad →
   something worse → catastrophe. Brain finishes the pattern and stays for the fourth rung.
8. **Question-answer-bigger-question spiral.** Each answer immediately opens a strictly larger
   question. Kurzgesagt/Veritasium signature.
9. **Second-person walk-through.** "You wake up in [scenario]. You look around." Viewer becomes the
   protagonist of the opening 30 seconds. Heavy lift in horror/lore.
10. **Preview-of-payoff (result-first).** Show the finished outcome before any process. MrBeast
    leaked doc mandates front-loading the visual payoff.

### 5a. More opening structures (added 2026-07-02 reference research)

Verified in the openings of top finance/explainer videos (video-vision transcripts). Add to the 10 above:

11. **Protagonist-walkthrough cold open.** Teach the entire mechanism through one hyper-specific
    narrative *day/character* before any thesis; the numbers arrive as lived events, not a lecture.
    *Wendover trucking:* "It's 5:00 a.m. in upstate New York… Walmart Distribution Center 6096…
    $0.55/mile… nets $43.25 day one," THEN zooms out to the thesis. Turns exposition into story.
12. **Dream-then-turn.** Sell the spectacle straight for ~30–120s (spec sheet, the marvel), then ONE
    pivot sentence flips it: RealLifeLore — "…while in reality it is rapidly becoming one of humanity's
    greatest unmitigated disasters." The straight sell is the setup; the pivot is the hook.
13. **Name-withholding loop.** Front-load superlative stats, delay the subject's *name* ~15s. Crayon:
    "This company was worth 7.9 trillion dollars — bigger than Apple and Microsoft combined… and today
    almost nobody can tell you its name." The withheld name is the loop.
14. **Dated-artifact open.** Quote a named publication + exact date in the first 10s as the authority
    anchor. ColdFusion: "This is Forbes magazine on the 14th of February 2023. The title reads:
    America's Best Banks." Instant credibility, then the ironic turn.
15. **Cold testimonial montage.** Open on real (or dramatized) first-person voices *before any
    narration*, then the narrator enters (ColdFusion ran ~32s of Gen-Z testimonials first). Good for
    macro/relatable topics.
16. **Mock-sincere thesis.** State the video's real premise as an earnest-sounding value claim that is
    quietly the first joke (Casually Explained: "…the core of what I think life is all about: making as
    much money as humanly possible with complete disregard to everything else"). A deadpan-persona
    replacement for the hook.

**The tangible-yardstick rule (universal, cross-channel):** never speak a big number bare. Pair every
large figure with a physical/comparative unit *in the same sentence.* "$400 billion = nine Manhattan
Projects or two Apollo programs" (How Money Works); "$2 trillion — roughly twice Saudi Arabia's entire
GDP" (RealLifeLore); "7.9 trillion — bigger than Apple and Microsoft combined" (Crayon). Median
time-to-first-number in top finance openings ≈ **6s**. Comparison-stacking (3–5 equivalents, incl. one
"% of world total") makes scale visceral (RealLifeLore).

### 5b. Explanation & analogy craft — making a hard idea click (added 2026-07-02)

§1b tactic 5 (extended single analogy) named the *duration*; these name the *mechanisms*, from
3Blue1Brown / Veritasium / Kurzgesagt / Casually Explained / Crayon / How Money Works:

1. **Weighable physical prop.** Turn an abstract quantity into an object with position/weight you
   *watch move.* 3B1B renders an integral as a dot whose center of mass "wobbles." Pick ONE prop per
   mechanism and reuse it for every sub-point (money supply = a bathtub: tap = the Fed, drain = QT,
   overflow = inflation — track the water level all video).
2. **Tune-a-dial-until-it-jumps.** The click is a single tracked variable doing something dramatic at
   exactly the right setting. Sweep a parameter, watch one number lurch; the lurch *is* the concept.
3. **Prediction-then-violation.** Commit the viewer to a wrong answer *first* (a concrete rig + a
   multiple-choice bet — Veritasium's bulb-and-two-wires), then break it. Commitment makes the
   correction stick. Strong opener in its own right.
4. **Exhaustive cast-list mapping.** Map *every* jargon noun into ONE consistent world and let the
   world's own physics smuggle in the real mechanism. Kurzgesagt's immune system = a kingdom's army;
   "neutrophils fight so furiously they kill healthy cells" teaches real collateral tissue damage
   inside the metaphor.
5. **Reader-as-counterparty.** Put the viewer *inside* the transaction in second person: "You own a
   3% bond. A 5% bond just launched. What's yours worth now?" The mechanism self-derives.
6. **Recursive concrete number.** For anything compounding/multiplying, run the *real* dollar chain,
   never the formula: "$100 deposit → keep $10, lend $90 → that $90 is a new deposit → keep $9, lend
   $81 … supply grows to $271." The multiplier is *felt.*
7. **One-sentence recompression (the designated "click" beat).** After the build, restate the whole
   mechanism as ONE everyday action — 3B1B lands the Fourier transform as "essentially unmixing a mixed
   bucket of paint." The recompression is the receipt the analogy paid off.
8. **Change-one-variable-at-a-time.** On a visible cost-breakdown, move ONE number while holding all
   else constant, so the viewer tracks a single moving figure (How Money Works: iPhone labor "$42 in
   China → $263 in the US → +$221 to you").
9. **Dialogue-reenactment micro-scene.** Dramatize a concept as a 2–4 line character exchange instead
   of narration (Crayon's NINJA loan: "—Should I worry about paying this back? —Not your problem. If
   you default we sell the house for more."). A default exposition mode for casual watchability.
10. **Analogy-as-indictment.** The analogy teaches the mechanism AND exposes its flaw in the *same*
    sentence — fusing payload and the vindication lever. Crayon on naked CDS: "buying life insurance on
    your neighbor's grandma, and rooting for her to slip"; on issuer-pays ratings: "hiring a food
    critic, then telling them they won't get paid unless they call your raw chicken Michelin-starred."
    The single most powerful device for a "here's how it actually works" channel.

**The three click-generators** (across all three explainer channels): (a) a *visible state-change* you
watch happen; (b) *prediction violation* (you were made to answer, reality broke your answer); (c)
*one-sentence recompression* at the end. Design at least one into every explainer.

**Steelman-then-kill** (cross-channel: Wendover, How Money Works, ColdFusion, Crayon, Casually
Explained): explicitly voice the obvious/comforting explanation or the skeptic's objection *verbatim*,
then bury it with data. Doubles as a credibility signal (reads as fair) AND a retention device (reopens
the mystery). Wendover raises "COVID" as the cause of budget-airline failure, then kills it: "passenger
volumes hit an all-time record summer 2023." How Money Works opens "for all the hate banks get, they do
a lot of good…" before the teardown.

### 5c. Story staging — tell it, don't recite it (added 2026-07-06)

From an A/B study (our researched scripts vs Crayon / HeyHistorically / OverSimplified / Coffeezilla /
MagnatesMedia on the *same topics*; full write-up in a channel's `storytelling-grammar.md` §4 staging). The finding
that governs this section: **compelling money-story scripts are 70–85% payload — the craft is a thin
*staging* layer over dense facts, not narrative replacing information.** A straight-line fact-recital of a
sourced ledger is the default failure; these are the moves that turn the same facts into a story. All are
generative (kinds, not lines); transfer the *framing*, not the comedy channels' joke frequency.

1. **The scene is the default beat unit — not the fact.** Across every reference the load-bearing beats
   are *performed* (a personified interaction, a character action, a two-line exchange), not summarized —
   Crayon plays the petrodollar deal as Kissinger's monologue; OverSimplified plays nations as characters
   in a souring relationship. Narrate only the connective tissue. Stage a **verbatim** quote when you have
   one; otherwise use **hedged paraphrase / reported speech**, never an invented quote. *(A channel that
   locks one-narrator / no-second-person / no-quotes — §1d-V F — launders **every** dramatized beat into the
   narrator's reported speech, characterizing what was said with attitude and quoting nothing.)*
2. **The mechanism-ASSEMBLY reveal.** For a how-it-was-engineered payload, build the machine one *nameable
   lever per beat* with the signpost said out loud ("here's the crucial part…", "this ended up being
   genius because…") so the viewer *watches the trick get built* — MagnatesMedia's spine, the purest
   long-form execution of the vindication lever, and sharper than a smooth one-pass "cascading revelations."
3. **Verification-as-content (vindication lever).** The "aha" fires when the viewer *watches the bar get
   cleared*, not when told a verdict. Coffeezilla *stages the proving* ("I asked for a selfie and
   cross-referenced it"), **counts corroboration out loud** ("two independent insiders, both in the room"),
   **exonerates the obvious suspect** where evidence is thin (to earn the accusations that land), and lands
   the payoff on the **system**, not the person (also the defamation-safe posture).
4. **Character-first villainy + human stakes (depth calibrated to register).** Front-load the villain's
   *documented* psychology so the con reads as one person's obsession, not weather. Register the human cost
   concretely and comedy-off, but calibrate the depth to the channel's register: a documentary/true-crime
   register can land a *named, rendered* victim; an entertainment-story register keeps the cost *light and
   aggregate* (the wreckage as a fast beat, not a named biography). The channel's grammar doc governs which
   (e.g. The Second Take keeps it light). This is the difference between "a lucid explainer" and "a story."
5. **Irony cross-cut for the pre-spoiled ending.** Money stories usually end in a known collapse, so
   outcome-suspense is dead — carry tension by *cross-cutting dramatic irony* (the mark feeling clever as
   the trap closes) and withholding the *motive/mechanism* reveal, not "what happens next."
6. **Universality bridge to close.** Name the rhyme (tulips → dot-com → NFTs → this), so the viewer leaves
   able to spot the next one — not just told a past story. Second person does this most directly ("you'll
   see it again"); a channel that bans "you" (§1d-V F) recasts it in the third person / as the narrator's
   own observation ("the pattern outlives the players").

**What already works and must not be eroded to chase this:** payload density, a front-loaded
forbidden-knowledge thesis, analogy-as-indictment (§5b), the person→system landing, and sourcing +
defamation discipline (don't import a reference's *unsourced* color to match its vividness — add richness
only from sourced character/victim material).

### 5d. Narrative architecture — how to frame & sequence the WHOLE video (updated 2026-07-07)

The macro layer *above* §5c's beat-staging: how the compelling channels **architect** a story instead of
telling it start-to-finish. From a multi-video study (Crayon Capital / HeyHistorically / OverSimplified); the
channel-specific version + the comedic register that pairs with it live in a channel's `storytelling-grammar.md`
(§1). **The reference channels are *mostly chronological* yet never read linear** — because they impose a
non-chronological shape, weave parallel threads, and stage scenes instead of summarizing. Our pipeline's
default failure is a flat mechanism-essay in date order. Design the architecture in the OUTLINE, before
drafting prose. Generative — pick the shape the material wants, don't clone.

1. **Organizing FRAME that isn't chronology.** Force the material into a shape that does double duty
   (structure + a running motif): twelve chaotic years told as "four presidents of the pirate republic"; a
   fraud as "the five promises." **Refuse plain chronology** — nobody opens at "the beginning."
2. **Paradox-hook, then rewind.** Cold-open on the single sharpest ironic contradiction stated as settled
   fact with the *mechanism withheld* (never the chronological start); drop the title after the hook; then an
   overt **rewind + dateline reset** ("July 8th, 1839…") is the audible gear-shift into the story — visible
   seams are part of the voice. (Alternatives: present-day mystery → hard rewind; a **tone-lock** that
   establishes the register/contract before naming the subject.)
3. **Name the reversal (arc shape).** In one sentence name the reversal the hook promised ("the thing that
   should have destroyed them made them stronger") and write toward it. No clean reversal → hand off to an
   **analytical framework** at the turn (question → rubric → evidence → vindication — front-load the framework,
   then deploy facts as *ammunition*). Shape menu: disaster arc · rise-and-fall empire ·
   question→framework→evidence · one-souring-relationship (multi-actor sprawl) · escalation-list-as-arc.
4. **Cross-cutting toolkit — the linearity fix.** Weave parallel threads instead of finishing one before
   starting the next:
   - **Board-state cross-cut** — before the protagonist acts, lay out every faction's *simultaneous* position,
     then drop him in so the move lands with weight ("while X was in London taking out loans, across the world
     the settlers were stepping off the boat into a swamp").
   - **Park-and-cut** — interrupt a rising thread to jump to another, naming the move ("meanwhile, back
     with…"); the parked tension holds while the other advances.
   - **Ensemble reaction montage** — one triggering event → snap-cut between several actors reacting to it.
   - **Mirror cross-cut** — place two parallel figures so one reframes the other (the antagonist who is
     secretly living the same story as the hero).
   - **Irony cross-cut** — for a pre-spoiled ending, intercut one side's obliviousness against the gathering
     disaster.
5. **Plant-and-detonate + a controlling spine.** Plant a loaded detail in act one, let it disappear, detonate
   it at the turn. Bind the whole piece with ONE controlling motif — a recurring **epithet** + an **obsessive
   object** — that tracks the character across time; plant → payoff → invert ("the party" → "the music
   stopped"); **bookend the cold-open line at its payoff.**
6. **Scene-as-default unit + mechanism-assembly.** Stage the 4–6 highest-charge moments as scenes (§5c),
   summarizing connective tissue in a line. For a "how it worked" payload, install ONE nameable lever per beat
   with the causal signpost said aloud ("here's the crucial part…") so the viewer *watches the machine get
   built*; for a fork, stage the **costed decision space** (Option A/B/C with real tradeoffs — time, money,
   risk), let it hang, then collapse to what actually happened (a natural fit for finance).
7. **Emotional CONTOUR (tone is a designed dial).** Sequence the tone deliberately (rollercoaster / sawtooth /
   slow-boil). **Front-load the silliness so the video earns its serious ending; let the jokes recede in the
   last act;** comedy density is inverse to stakes and goes fully OFF for the grave beats (the DIAL, §1d-V F).
8. **Transitions — the seam kit. Leave zero flat "and then" joins:**
   - **Turn-word grammar** — one clause that closes the prior beat and opens the next ("Then things got
     worse." / "Now, back to…").
   - **Exit every beat on an open loop / forward-promise** the viewer must stay to collect.
   - **Honest ranked escalation** — "it gets worse" must be followed by something that is, in fact, worse.
   - **Dates as scene headers; value-loaded re-hooks** ("the *real* genius of it…"); a **fake-out ending** (a
     deliberate wrong ending, then a retract) as a mid-video reset.
   - **Deliver the moment; never announce it.** Cut the category-label opener whose only job is to flag the
     *kind* of beat coming ("here's the strange part," "the uncomfortable question") and mechanical scaffolding
     ("Piece one / two," "Reason one / two"). Open on the content — the real question the section answers, or
     the next action stated plainly. Audit *every* section opener.
9. **Known-outcome tension engines.** Our stories are usually pre-spoiled (the title gives away the collapse),
   so outcome-suspense is dead — carry tension on: dramatic irony · comic dread (make them *wait* for a doom
   we can see coming) · how-badly / how-exactly assembly · micro-suspense inside individual vignettes.
10. **Exit menu (never a tidy moral or CTA).** Settled story → return to the hook-paradox and seal the irony.
    Ongoing story → a suspense triad that refuses to resolve. Also: **where-are-they-now** capsule fates (one
    wry line each) · the dry deflate button · bathos · a sequel/loop hook. **Never "and that's why…"** (§7).

This is the layer that turns a lucid *explainer* into a *video people watch to the end.*

## 6. Retention tactics library

### 6a. The 15 named tactics

1. **Nested open loops (macro / secondary / micro).** 10-min video = 1 macro loop (opened 0:20,
   resolved final 20%) + 3–5 secondary loops (2–3 min each) + micro loops (incomplete sentences
   bridged across cuts, MrBeast's signature).
2. **Verbal-into-cut pattern interrupt (micro-loop).** Cut before a verb completes. Brain waits
   across the cut. MrBeast's most heavily-used technique. Every 5–15s through the first 3 minutes.
3. **Visual pattern interrupt (zoom / angle / insert).** Every 30–45s in the first 3 minutes; every
   15–30s optimal for AI-gen faceless (Zack D. edit density).
4. **Audio pattern interrupt (SFX / music drop / silence).** Auditory novelty gates attention the
   same way visual does. Half a second of silence right before a reveal makes the reveal land
   twice as hard. SFX every 20–30s; music track change at every act boundary.
5. **Progress anchors (counter / timer / chapter marker).** Visible progress activates goal-gradient
   motivation. Counter visible ≥20% of runtime; increment non-linear (steeper near end).
6. **Withheld peak (best fact in final third).** Rank your 3 best facts. Put #3 in first 90s, #2
   around the 40–50% mark, #1 in the final 20%. Tease #1 in the hook. **The single most consistent
   structural recommendation across all sources — treat as a hard rule.**
7. **Contradiction hook ("But actually, that's wrong").** Injects a second false-belief flip
   mid-video, re-opening curiosity right when the first loop closes. Once per act max.
8. **Callback loops (verbal + visual match cut).** Late-video echo of an early phrase/image
   satisfies completion drive. Callback + visual match cut is the closest thing to a mechanical
   retention hack that works.
9. **Stakes escalation ("Here's where it gets worse").** At each 25% mark, escalate stakes
   numerically or narratively.
10. **Narrative re-arm (promise refresh mid-video).** Direct restatement of what viewers are still
    going to get if they stay. Once around 40–50% for 10+ min videos; ~65% is the second optimal
    spot (right before the documented 55–65% exodus zone).
11. **Sensory shift (voice / music / silence change).** Category-level reset — modality novelty.
    New track / new genre at each act boundary; VO tonal shift at the mid-video re-arm; deliberate
    0.5–1s silence before every big reveal.
12. **Character / new entity injection at re-engagement.** MrBeast doc: introduce new person,
    object, or environment at 3-min and 6-min marks.
13. **Direct-address "this next part" bridge.** Second-person shifts frame from *watching* to
    *being told*. One clear "in the next minute you'll see…" bridge per act.
14. **Cascading payoff ("Yes, but…").** Instead of closing loops flat, close them into a new loop.
    Every mid-video payoff immediately followed by "but that's not the whole picture."
15. **Chapter/act reset (framed break).** Overt structural marker resets viewer expectations for a
    new mini-video inside the long form. Every 2–3 min in 10–15 min videos; every 3–4 min in 20+
    min videos.

**Bonus:** rewatchable detail injection (easter eggs that pay off in the ending). Especially
valuable for shorts — Zack D. Films' rewatch rate is a major driver.

### 6b. Re-hook cadence (updated 2025-2026)

The old "60–90s" rule is **directionally correct but too coarse** for 2025-2026. Actual cadence has
three tiers:

- **Tier 1: 55% first-minute cliff (universal).** Establish value in 7 seconds. Hold 70%+ at 30s
  or the algorithm never gives you a second chance.
- **Tier 2: micro-cadence within the video (30–45s beats).** New stimulus every 30–45s — a new
  visual, a new claim, a tone shift, or a stat. **Faster than the old 60–90s rule.** Audiences
  trained on Shorts expect faster cadence *inside* long-form now. SQ Magazine 2026: 52% skip videos
  >60s "even if the topic interests them."
- **Tier 3: macro re-hook every 2–3 minutes + mandatory mid-video re-arm at 55–65% runtime.**
  MrBeast: re-engagement points at 3-min and 6-min marks. For videos >10 min expect a **~15%
  secondary drop-off around the 55–65% runtime mark** without deliberate re-engagement. **This is
  the "danger zone" the old playbook missed.**

**Retention benchmarks by length** (Retention Rabbit 2025):
- <5 min: 65–75% strong
- 5–10 min: 50–60% strong (best-retaining bracket, 31.5% platform average)
- 10–15 min: 40–50% strong
- 15–30 min: 35–45% strong
- 30–60 min: 25–35% strong
- 60+ min: 20–30% strong

Improving retention by 10 percentage points correlates with a **25%+ increase in algorithmic
impressions**.

## 7. Payoff structures

Six named structures. Match to title pattern + lever.

1. **The Reveal (answer to the named question).** Closes the macro loop opened by a curiosity-gap
   title. *Best for:* hidden-mechanism / impossible-number / hidden-cause titles. *Example:*
   Veritasium 96M Black Balls — plain answer delivered late.
2. **The Twist (subverted expectation).** Payoff inverts the viewer's assumed answer. Highest-share
   structure. *Best for:* false-belief flip, contrarian, "the real reason" titles.
3. **The Vindication ("as I said at the start…").** Late-video echo of the cold-open image or phrase
   — "I get it now" satisfaction. Callback loop resolution. *Best for:* long-form docs, engineering
   disasters, business collapses.
4. **The Escalating Tally (biggest at end).** Withheld-peak structure. Series of comparisons builds
   toward a final entry dramatically larger than the rest. *Best for:* ranked-superlative lists,
   three-tier escalation, $1-vs-$1B titles.
5. **The Connect-the-Dots (assembled explanation).** Video plants apparently unrelated clues; the
   ending assembles them. Highest cognitive-satisfaction payoff. *Best for:* engineering disasters
   (causal chain), unsolved-mystery reveals, business collapse stories.
6. **The Punchline (comedic or absurd sting).** Fast unexpected payoff on emotional release, not
   intellectual closure. Optimized for share/rewatch. *Best for:* AI-gen shorts (Zack D.), what-if
   with absurd endings, horror/lore with morbid humor.

**Hard rule (doctrine hit):** the final beat is emotional, not educational. NO "and that's why it's
important to understand physics." The video ends on the last emotionally-charged image with a
single unresolved sentence — the viewer should feel it, not learn it.

## 8. Thumbnails (2026 rules)

### 8a. Alignment rule

Paddy Galloway's rule: **plan title + thumbnail before recording**, and treat them as one asset.
The thumbnail should visually deliver the promise the title makes in words, but neither should
fully resolve the question. Since 2025 YouTube A/B-tests up to three titles and three thumbnails
per video — the recommended pattern is **one safe variant + two hypothesis variants**, not three
similar variants.

### 8b. What's working (2025-2026)

- **"Proof of human" thumbnails.** Hybrid real-photo subject + AI background outperforms fully
  AI-generated by 18–22% (Banana Thumbnail A/B data). **47.3% of creators stopped using fully AI
  thumbnails** (Social Blade December 2025 Creator Survey). Fully AI-generated plastic-skin
  portraits are algorithmically penalized.
- **Closed-mouth determined expression** beats open-mouth shock face by 15–20% CTR on 2026 mobile
  feeds.
- **Neo-minimalism:** one dominant subject, ≥50% negative space, ≤2 primary colors. **Thumbnails
  with zero text often outperform text-bearing variants;** when text helps, cap at 3 words. The
  "five-word thumbnail" era ended in 2024.
- **Single-artifact focus + red circle on the anomaly.** Works for engineering disasters, horror/
  lore ("what's wrong here?"), micro-health ("look at this scan").
- **Before/after split** for transformation content — but only when the delta is *visually obvious*.
- **Numbers rendered as visual objects**, not text (a stack of money, dozens of pills). Veritasium's
  black balls worked because the number was *seen*, not read.

### 8c. What's dead

- Rainbow arrows and neon circles bracketing everything.
- Open-mouth shock face.
- All-caps thumbnail text (mobile CTR negative in most niches).
- Fully AI-generated portraits.
- Cluttered five-element thumbnails.
- Clickbait titles with mismatched thumbnails (costs more than it earns under session-time
  weighting).

**Faceless-channel default:** single dominant subject + brand-consistent palette + zero or ≤3
words of text. This transfers Zack D. Films' advantage.

**Shorts:** design the **first frame** as the thumbnail.

### 8d. Thumbnail executions from the reference channels (added 2026-07-02)

Scanned across 10+ thumbnails each. New cross-channel rules:

- **Thumbnail text = a reframe, not the title.** The single most consistent finding. The thumbnail's
  2–4 words carry a *different* curiosity angle or a sarcastic verdict — never a restatement. How Money
  Works: title states the topic, thumbnail says "WELL…WE TRIED…". Wendover: "WHAT WENT WRONG?" /
  "100% CHANCE OF RIPPING YOU OFF." PolyMatter splits title vs thumbnail across two angles. HAI:
  "BOWLERS HATE THESE," never "string pinsetters." Two curiosity gaps for the price of one.
- **Persistent corner wordmark** (ColdFusion, PolyMatter, HAI, Wendover): the channel logo locked in
  the *same corner of every thumbnail* = instant in-feed brand recognition. Cheap and compounding.
- **The chart IS the image** (Wendover, PolyMatter): a labeled line-cross / candlestick drawn over a
  photo, not a face ("LEGACY vs LOW-COST AIRLINES" curves crossing a "0% OPERATING MARGIN" line).
  Native to finance and fully faceless.
- **Number in a single accent color** (RealLifeLore yellow "$2 TRILLION," ColdFusion gold money figures,
  Crayon red alarm): one reusable signal color across thumbnails + in-video charts.
- **Open-mouth-shock caveat.** The blanket "no open-mouth face" rule (§8c) is register-dependent: Crayon
  Capital uses open-mouth panic *cartoon* faces freely and it works *for the crisis/collapse register.*
  The rule holds for calm-authority registers; a crisis-explainer may use a shocked *illustrated* face
  (never a photoreal one).
- **Thumbnail-as-joke** (Half as Interesting): a fabricated satirical mockup (a fake storefront/spec
  sheet) where the thumbnail itself is the punchline — an option for a comedic register only. For a
  locked-visual-register channel (§13), pick ONE thumbnail system and hold it.

## 9. Episode skeleton (universal — variant per niche)

Niche files give the niche-specific beat template. All are variants of:

1. **Hook (0–7s)** — open the loop + stakes. See §4.
2. **Second gate (7–30s)** — the structure that carries the viewer from the hook to the body.
   See §5.
3. **Premise/promise** — state the question the video answers; plant the payoff.
4. **Body = nested open loops** — each segment answers one question while opening the next (the
   retention engine); one visual per beat. See §6.
5. **Mid-video re-arm (55–65%)** — restate what's still unresolved; add a stakes escalation.
   Mandatory for anything >10 min.
6. **The turn/payoff** — the withheld peak the hook promised. Best fact in the final 20%.
7. **Emotional close** — an image + a single unresolved sentence. NO "the lesson." Loop-out to a
   related video to chain the session.

## 10. Pacing & length

- **New cut every 3–8s; new stimulus every 30–45s.** Faster than the old rule.
- **Micro-interrupt every 30–45s** (visual, audio, or verbal).
- **Macro re-hook every 2–3 min.**
- **Mandatory mid-video re-arm at 55–65% runtime** for any video >10 min.

**Measured cut cadence in top explainers (2026-07-02 reference scan).** "Cut every 3–8s" is a *ceiling*,
not a floor — several top finance/explainer channels run **slower, narration-led** cadences and hold
retention on the script alone: PolyMatter ~4–9s/visual (crossfades, not hard cuts; zero hard cuts in one
120s intro), Crayon ~6–7s/scene (with fast intra-scene puppet animation), Wendover ~10s/visual but with
deliberate **40–60s holds on a single data graphic** built line-by-line during a number-walk, ColdFusion
~150 wpm with a **compressed loudness range (~3 LU, no vocal spikes)** as the actual texture of "calm
authority." Takeaway: **a new concrete referent every 3–6s in the *narration*** matters more than raw cut
rate; visuals can breathe if the script keeps delivering. (Cadence figures here are scene-detection
estimates from the 2026-07-02 transcript-only pass — treat as directional; full-video watching works as
of 2026-07-04 and can now measure these exactly.)

Length bands vary sharply by niche. General rule: **retention beats length** — go longer only while
holding retention above the benchmark for the length band (see §6b). Specific bands per niche live
in the niche files. The 15-min-for-money rule is outdated: mid-rolls unlock at 8+ min; after that,
session time and retention rule.

### The Long ↔ Short interaction (updated late-2025 — algorithmic decoupling)

**What changed late 2025:** Shorts and long-form got **algorithmically decoupled at the discovery
layer**. Shorts performance no longer *passively* lifts long-form recommendations, and vice versa.
**What did NOT change:** an engineered Short → long-form click-through is still a high-value signal
— the click feeds long-form session watch time, which the algorithm still weights heavily. What
died is the automatic spillover, not the funnel itself. You have to earn the click, not receive
it. Funnel mechanics that still work (Phase 1 research data):

- Pinned-comment link on Shorts → **+20% total channel watch time**.
- Description link on Shorts → **+12% conversions among non-subscribers**.
- Shorts with a link element → **~4.5% CTR** industry average.
- Reverse-repurpose (viral Short → long-form expansion) → **+40% long-form watch time** vs.
  cold-launching on the same topic.
- Running both formats → **~2.5× total channel watch time** in year one vs. single-format.

The funnel must be engineered explicitly (pinned comment, description link, end-screen).

The successful pattern isn't "shorts as clip mine" — it's **shorts as their own format**, with
occasional borrowing from long-form (Paddy Galloway synthesis of a 3.3B-view study). Winning
creators fall into three archetypes:

1. **Shorts-native** (Zack D. Films) — shorts are the product; long-form is optional glue.
2. **Two-format portfolio** (Wendover/HAI, MrBeast, Doctor Mike) — each format is its own show
   with its own audience.
3. **Long-form primary + light shorts** (Wendigoon, Practical Engineering, Modern MBA) — shorts,
   if used at all, are separate.

**Publishing order is fixed:** long-form first (so shorts can point to it), then stagger shorts
every 2–3 days with pinned-comment link. Bursting shorts on release day cannibalizes the seed
audience.

**Reverse-repurpose (Short → Long):** a short exceeding 5× your channel baseline is a candidate for
long-form expansion. Only expand when the long-form has 12+ min of *actual substance* — padded
expansion tanks retention. Reverse-repurpose data: +40% long-form watch time vs. cold-launching a
new long-form on the same topic.

**Per-niche shorts cadence** (from AIR Media-Tech 18K-channel study + per-niche analysis; each
niche file confirms its band):
- Business docs: 2–4 shorts per long-form
- What-if: 3–6
- AI tools: 2–3
- Engineering disasters: 1–3
- Horror/internet lore: 4–8
- Micro-health: 3–5

The universal "2–3 per long-form" cap is retired. Idea-generator and scriptwriter should scale the
short bench to the niche band, cutting weak ones rather than padding to a number.

## 11. Shorts structure (2025-2026)

### 11-0. A short is a closed loop, not a trailer (the self-contained rule)

The load-bearing 2025-2026 finding (confirmed across creator breakdowns + Paddy Galloway's 3.3B-view
Shorts study): the algorithm now optimizes for **satisfaction** — viewed-vs-swiped, completion, and
replays. A short that opens a curiosity gap and points off-video for the answer *spikes early drop-off*,
which the system reads as dissatisfaction and throttles. So:

- **Deliver a complete, self-contained payload INSIDE the runtime.** The spine is
  **Hook → Context → Payoff → Loop**, all within the short. State a genuinely surprising, concrete
  thing and *pay it off here.* (Per §1-P: withholding is a long-form tactic; in a short it's the bug.)
- **"If the idea needs more time to pay off, it's a long-form idea in disguise."** Don't clip it into a
  teaser — either it lands in <40s or it isn't a short.
- **Front-load the payload; treat the first second as a thumbnail.** Viewers swipe in ~1 second; top
  shorts hold **70–90% viewed-vs-swiped.** Put the fact (or a 0.3s flash of the end state) in second one.
- **One surprising thing, stated densely across ~3–5 escalating beats** — not one vague mood, not ten
  forgettable facts. Each beat adds a concrete detail or raises the stakes toward the single payoff.
- **Close the loop, then seam it.** Answer the question you opened; write the last line to flow back
  into the first so a replay is seamless. **Since March 31 2025 each loop/replay counts as a view** — a
  tight loop can push AVD past 100% and is a strong distribution signal.
- **The long-form link is a bonus in the pinned comment, never the payoff.** Never end a short on "watch
  the full video to find out" — that *is* the withholding failure. The short must fully satisfy on its
  own; the pinned link is for the minority who want depth.
- **Burned hooks that now read as "ad" and get swiped:** "Did you know…," "You won't believe…," generic
  question openers, "Hey guys, welcome back," any slow build or buried lede. See §12.

### 11a. Length + hook

- **Sweet spot: 15–30 seconds** for max retention (80%+ possible).
- 25–40s for tutorial/explainer; 30–45s for storytelling. Retention drops sharply after 45s.
- 3-minute Shorts (unlocked October 2024) are almost never optimal — they compete with long-form
  retention thresholds without the RPM benefit.
- **Hook window: 1.3–1.8 seconds** — the actual swipe-decision window.
- 50–60% of drop-off happens in the first 3 seconds.

### 11b. Retention thresholds

- "Good" view-through: **~70% average view duration**.
- Viral shorts (>1M views) average **~76% retention**.
- Shorts above **~75% retention get 3× more push** to new audiences.
- Under 30% 3-second retention effectively kills distribution.

### 11c. Craft rules

- **Burned-in captions: +15–25% retention.** The single highest-leverage formatting choice.
- One visual cut every 2–4 seconds keeps attention.
- 9:16 aspect ratio mandatory (95%+ mobile viewing).
- **Replay rate is a distinct signal** — a 10% replay rate materially boosts distribution.
- **CTA in caption:** +22% likes and comments.
- **CTA to long-form works best in the pinned comment, not on-screen** — CTR to another video is
  NOT a Shorts ranking signal (unlike long-form CTR).

### 11d. Algorithm signals (what YouTube weights)

**For Shorts:** replay rate, swipe-through rate, like-to-view ratio, share rate, watch-through,
user satisfaction survey. **Not raw watch time.** A 10-sec video with high engagement outranks a
60-sec video with low engagement.

**For long-form:** AVD, session watch time, CTR, subscriber gain, satisfaction surveys.

**One-line rule of thumb for shorts 2025-2026:** design for a 1.5s stop, 70%+ view-through, and a
replay hook — everything else is optimization on top.

## 12. Anti-patterns (measurably killing retention 2025-2026)

Each supported by 2024-2026 data:

1. **"Hey guys, welcome back to my channel."** 33%+ of viewers drop off in the first 30 seconds
   when the intro is a generic greeting.
2. **Logo splash / channel intro sting before value.** Every second of animated logo is a second
   the algorithm sees as pre-content abandonment.
3. **Self-introduction ("My name is X, and today…").** 15+ seconds on self-intro lands the video
   in the sub-45% first-minute retention bucket.
4. **"In today's video…" / meta-signalling.** Filler by definition — references the video from
   inside the video.
5. **Slow narrative buildup ("Before we begin, some context…").** Casual viewers show 60% initial
   drop-off vs. 35% for engaged learners.
6. **Over-produced intro music / branded sting.** Musical intros are legacy TV logic and correlate
   with the AI-slop retention penalty.
7. **Robotic AI narration with no cadence variation.** ~35% drop-off in the first 45 seconds
   vs. human narration (directional, single-vendor). See §2b.
8. **Ambient B-roll with no motion for the first 3–5 seconds.** 72% of viral Shorts over 1M views
   use fast-paced editing with minimal dead air.
9. **Storytelling hooks that take 8–10 seconds to pay off.** Pattern-interrupt hooks now dominate;
   even a 4-second setup competes with 1-second competitors.
10. **"Subscribe" / channel-ask in the first 30 seconds.** Viewers exit on any conversion-ask that
    predates value delivery.

## 13. Production universals (AI pipeline)

- **Voice: expressive, inflected AI voice or human VO — never flat monotone TTS.** The #1 slop tell
  and the largest single retention lever the pipeline controls. Premium TTS (ElevenLabs prosody
  tier) minimum. One recognizable voice register per channel, locked.
- **Visual identity: lock one house style** (palette, type, motion, sound) per channel. **The
  channel's visual signature is a monetization prerequisite in 2026, not a style choice** —
  templated stock B-roll is a July-2025 policy trigger.
- **Visual register — stylized vs. real-footage — is a per-niche `dna.md` decision, not a global
  default.** The winning variable is *distinctive + coherent + matched to the content*, while avoiding
  the uncanny middle:
  - **Stylized / illustrated wins when the subject is abstract** (finance, what-if, mechanisms,
    explainers, most history). There's nothing real to film; a **single locked style-token in every
    image prompt** buys instant brand coherence AND hides AI-artifact tells — *nobody expects realism
    from a doodle.* It's cheaper, more automatable, and dodges the "AI slop" look. (Crayon Capital's
    hand-drawn finance; Explains101's whiteboard; a flat-vector what-if.)
  - **Real footage / photoreal wins only when the value IS the realism** — a real place, archival, a
    product UI, actual screen-captures (ai-tools demos, internet-lore evidence à la Visual Venture).
    There, authenticity is the register.
  - **Ban the uncanny middle: generic semi-photoreal AI B-roll** (the "cinematic dread stock-AI" look).
    It is uncanny enough to read as slop AND carries no brand identity — the worst quadrant. If a niche
    wants realism, use real stock/screencap/archival (blend per §6a/tools.md); if it wants a signature,
    commit fully to a stylized locked look. Don't split the difference.
- **A cheap, locked, distinctive style beats expensive photoreal AI B-roll** for the abstract niches —
  but the aesthetic alone is clone-bait (already being mass-cloned), so defensibility must come from the
  **editorial voice / payload**, not the look.
- **Where AI is weak:** high-end bespoke animation (Zack-D 3D, Kurzgesagt) is hard to fully
  generate — plan a hybrid (reusable asset kits + Blender / image→video tools + assembly), keep
  the *substance* human. Flat 2D / motion-graphics + charts + a locked illustrated style is the most
  AI-friendly lane and often the *better* one, not merely the cheaper one.
- **Disclosure:** any realistic altered or synthetic content must be disclosed via YouTube's
  Altered Content toggle at upload. YMYL topics (health, news, finance, elections) trigger a more
  prominent player-level label — write assuming viewers see it first.
- **Cadence discipline:** batch-produce, don't react. Researched-queued-packaged pipelines
  distinguish surviving AI-gen channels from slop even more than aesthetic. Zero trend-chasing.

### 13a. Visual-narration grammar — what to SHOW over what NARRATION

§13 locks the *look*; this locks *what appears*. Derived from a frame-by-frame study of illustrated
story channels against their audio (HeyHistorically, Crayon Capital, OverSimplified + animation
craft from Odd1sOut/Jaiden/Domics/SomethingElse). It is the fix for the most common visual failure:
a shot that literally depicts the sentence. A channel with a `visual-kit/visual-grammar.md` holds its
own staging application (staging conventions, composition menu, lever translation; the live asset
vocabulary is `visual-kit/registry/registry.json`); this is the shared, **binding** craft
every channel inherits. **Everything below is LAW, not reference** — the shot-writer authors to it.

**The governing rule — a grammar, not a phrasebook.** We map *kinds* of narration to *kinds* of
shot, never fixed "when the script says X, draw Y" pairs. Cited images are illustrations of a class,
never rules. The procedure: **read the line → identify its narration type → pick the shot class →
then INVENT a fresh, on-style shot.** Same-typed lines in different videos must yield visibly
different images. Reusing one depiction as a reflex rebuilds the phrasebook and re-enters slop
(templated near-duplicates = the July-2025 trigger). This is the same "name the principle so the
writer invents variants" rule used for hooks (§4b) and titles (§3a).

**Core doctrine (every studied channel converged on these):**
1. **Non-literal is the DEFAULT; literal depiction is reserved for concrete physical action/objects.**
   If the line names a physical thing/action, show it. If it names a **concept, force, relationship,
   quantity, or claim**, do NOT show the words — show a stand-in.
2. **One idea per FRAME.** Standalone shots swap whole on the beat (~every 4–8s); shots on a **held
   stage** instead *chain* — the set persists and ONE element is added/moved per frame — with a
   whole-swap only at a stage change (full cadence + staging law in §13a-ii).
3. **Personify institutions/forces as characters; stage relationships as physical interactions**
   between them (not diagrams).
4. **Glue every number/date to a tangible object or a diegetic surface** (a label on the thing, a
   sign, a screenshot, a torn calendar) — never a floating narrator caption.
5. **Motion is cheap and cut-driven:** an idle micro-motion baseline so no frame is dead + ONE
   meaningful transform per shot (the coin that shrinks, the scale that tips, the chart that draws) +
   hold-then-hard-cut on the payload word. Every shot is a composed slate — the full binding grammar
   is §13a-i (within-shot motion) + §13a-ii (cut cadence).
6. **Register-switches carry meaning** — shifting to a cleaner "infographic" register signals "this
   is the real machinery"; the switch itself is a reading cue.
7. **Humor usually lives in the image-vs-words CONTRAST (ironic counterpoint), not a funnier drawing.**
8. **Palette codes tone/time** — warm = day/triumph, cool = night/defeat, high-contrast = danger, a
   monochrome flip for a horror/tragedy turn. The scene palette is a cheap emotional register.
9. **Escalate by MULTIPLYING the cast/prop, not redrawing** — a bigger pile, a growing crowd, an
   on-screen counter ticking up; keep a small reusable cast + prop kit and reuse it.

**The narration-type → shot-class table** (pick the class, invent the shot):

| When the narration is… | Show a shot of class… |
| --- | --- |
| an abstract force/property/state (trust, inflation, dominance, "cut off") | **symbolic stand-in object** or **personification** — one concrete object/creature that *embodies* it |
| a relationship/deal/conflict between parties | **staged interaction** between personified parties (handshake, linked arms, tug-of-war, argument) |
| an institution/nation/company as an actor | **personified character with one identity tag** (flag-tie, hat, uniform) or its **iconic landmark** |
| a bare number/stat/date/quantity | **number glued to its referent object**, a **diegetic dateline**, or a **countable mass** |
| a comparison or a trend | **physicalized imbalance** (tipping scale, relative size) or a **deliberately-crude in-world chart** |
| a historical event/announcement/shock | **diegetic media** (period TV/radio/newspaper) or a **dialogue reenactment** |
| a mechanism / "how it really works" | **register-shift to a clean infographic + animate the one transform** |
| a plan/spatial move/territory | **top-down map or plan-view with tokens/arrows/color-fills** |
| a claim/boast/euphemism/spin | **ironic counterpoint** (image contradicts the words) or **literal-unmasking** |
| a dry aside/punchline | **reaction shot on the payload word** or a **deadpan cutaway to a mundane/absurd object** |
| a line with a vivid verb/idiom | **draw the phrase literally** (a visual pun on the idiom, not the fact) |
| a grim/violent/tragic beat | **aftermath / witness / stylized-safe + palette shift** — never gore |
| scale/magnitude | **scale as argument** (relative size) or **crowd multiplication** |
| a real physical action/object | **literal depiction** (the one place it's correct) |

**Cross-channel caution:** the studied channels lean on **real-archival-footage registers** and
**meme/wojak/anachronism cutaways** — a channel that bans the uncanny middle (§13) and runs an
evergreen-references-only humor dial (§1d-V) takes their *timing and contrast*, not those layers; its
"gravity register" is a restrained, desaturated version of its OWN style + a real dated document, not
real footage.

#### 13a-i. Within-shot motion — a shot is a COMPOSED SLATE, never a static hold (BINDING)

The single biggest pacing fix. A shot is **not** a still image parked on screen for N seconds; it is a
**composed slate with internal choreography synced to the VO.** The shot-writer AUTHORS this now (as the
`within_shot_motion` field per shot, and by composing `still_prompt` *to move*), even though full motion
*rendering* is a later Remotion phase — the authoring is binding regardless. Every shot carries all four:

- **(a) Idle micro-motion baseline** — every held frame *breathes*: a globe spins, cash flutters, a flame
  flickers, a character bobs/blinks, rain streaks. No frame is ever dead.
- **(b) ONE meaningful transform that IS the beat** — spend the motion budget on the single move that
  carries the payload: a coin shrinks (debasement), a scale tips (over-printing), a chart draws itself, a
  ship sails toward frame, a plug pulls out, a crowd multiplies. Everything else stays near-still.
- **(c) Pop-in / type-on synced to the narrated word** — captions, list items, badges, icon callouts
  *appear* on the word. **For enumerations/lists, elements appear one at a time ON the word.** Canonical
  example: *"no church, no paved roads, no rivers of gold"* → hold **ONE** image and drop an X /
  strike-through / bomb on each promised thing *as it is named*. This is **one shot with internal
  choreography** — not three cuts, and not a static hold.
- **(d) Hold, then hard-cut on the payload word** — hold a neutral/composed pose ~0.6–0.8s, then snap
  (1–2 frames, no easing) to the reaction/reveal on the exact punch word. Never ease into a punchline.

Gesture/lean/recoil is the acting (body language carries emotion; simple-handed rigs lean on posture);
the camera stays mostly locked, with an occasional slow push-in on a character intro or an establishing
landmark as a cheap substitute for animation.

#### 13a-ii. Cut cadence — and the HARD RULE that kills stretch-to-fill (BINDING)

- **~4–8s per frame-swap**, one distinct visual idea per sentence/clause. The eye is rewarded
  continuously; there is never a "narrator over one B-roll" stretch.
- **Vary cadence by role:** fast (**1.5–3s**) for jokes/enumerations; slow (**5–12s**) for
  dialogue/mechanism beats/emotional turns. Place a deliberate **breath-beat** (a longer, quieter,
  atmospheric shot) at emotional turns.
- **HARD RULE: a shot may exceed ~8s ONLY if it carries a progressive within-shot reveal** (a
  type-on enumeration, a drawing chart, a multiplying crowd). Otherwise, **cut.**
- **Failure mode to avoid — stretch-to-fill:** an under-produced shot list gets re-timed to the VO and
  each shot stretched to fill the gap, leaving one visual on screen for a dead 15–25s. The fix is
  authored here: produce enough shots (roughly `runtime ÷ 8s` minimum) and give any long shot a
  progressive reveal so it earns its length.
- **Black title/chapter cards** every ~30–90s serve as breath + reset + payload-first callouts — the
  cheapest retention/rhythm tool.

**Held stages + the continuity hierarchy (executor-agnostic).** Continuity between shots comes from
three tools, cheapest first: **(1) move a layer** (a motion engine composites element cutouts — zero
drift), **(2) a seeded delta-chain** (a held STAGE: a `base` frame + **≤3 `delta` frames**, each
adding/moving ONE element on the SAME set — the still-era realization of §13a-i's progressive reveal),
and **(3) a hard cut** to a new stage. **No fades, ever** — a flat-vector cartoon dissolving reads as
photo-documentary B-roll; continuity is the held stage, never a blend. A stage's shots carry only the
*intent* (which set they share, and what changed), never the generation mechanism — the executor
(the seeded delta-chain, OR — since 2026-07-12 — the LIVE layer-move compositor: a `plate` + animated
cutout `layers`, planned by `motion-planner`, rendered by the engine `LayerView`) realizes it. Delta frames run fast (1.5–3s); base/hold frames
4–12s; each frame stays one timing unit with its own `vo_ref`.

#### 13a-iii. Motion direction — the MEASURED grammar (the motion-layer sibling of §13a-i)

Where §13a-i says a shot must move and §13a-ii says when to cut, this section says **how the
reference grade actually moves** — measured 2026-07-08 by frame-burst teardown (3–4fps windows,
easing read from frame spacing, cut lists from scene-detection, audio from loudness traces + model
listening) of Crayon Capital ×3 (the base; every video extracted twice, independently, convergent),
HeyHistorically, OverSimplified, and Kurzgesagt (entrances/type only, aspirational). Full evidence:
the channel's `visual-kit/research/motion-logs/`. Learn the mechanic, never clone the execution.

**The one-line summary: the camera is furniture; the ELEMENT layer is the life; the cut is the verb.**

1. **Camera law — locked by default, one motivated crawl when it moves.** Explainer mode: ~10–15%
   of holds carry any camera motion; story mode: ~20% overt + a micro-drift floor (0.3–1.5%/s,
   pixel-diff-visible only) on pictorial holds. **Text/diagram/chapter cards are DEAD STATIC,
   always.** A camera move is ONE slow single-direction crawl spanning the whole hold (push-in
   1–3%/s typical, reveal-by-widening ~3%/s, tracking ≤8%/s) — motivated by intro/vista/gravity.
   The fast register (whip-pan/whip-zoom, one blur frame) exists ONLY inside list montages and
   dialogue ping-pong. No mid-hold re-frames; re-frame = punch-in CUT.
2. **Entrance vocabulary (measured counts, most→least):** pop/snap-in with a 0.3–0.5s spring settle
   (one element per spoken NOUN) > type-on/handwrite/draw-on > slide/swing/toss-in with real motion
   blur (~0.5s, ease-out, no overshoot) > grow > walk-on > drop. **Fade-in is near-banned** (≈1 per
   video, mood only). In-world characters/props arrive staged-at-the-cut or slide in — never fade.
   Every entrance lands ON its spoken word.
3. **Text always animates on, at speech pace, and it IS an event:** typewriter ~12–15 chars/s
   (story cards, matching VO), ~25 chars/s (explainer pivot cards); labels/stat lines handwrite
   word-by-word paced to the VO. Text entrances get an SFX tick; the words completing on the spoken
   word is the sync payoff. Pre-set text exists only on chapter titles and in-prop print revealed
   by an insert cut.
4. **Transition law:** hard cut ≈ 99–100% of scene seams (zero scene crossfades across 50+ minutes
   of Crayon). The exceptions are RESERVED-MEANING devices, ≤2 per video: fade-through-black =
   death/era-jump (paired with a music drop + palette shift), white-flash = flashback, iris/wipe =
   once, at an act seam or montage list (wipes land on list items). Two black-card families:
   **chapter card** (title pre-set or arrives whole, holds 1.5–3.5s in a VO gap, LF boom/drone or
   music drop; may fade-up out ~0.5s) and **write-on pivot card** (the VO's exact sentence types on
   at speech pace, hard cut out ON the next scene's first word — doubles as transition + attention
   reset). Era-jump cuts land in VO pauses with NO hit; gag/seam cuts take the sting.
5. **Number-selling recipe (emphasis grammar):** the number is NEVER floating overlay text — it is
   made in-world (painted, typed into a ledger, on a tossed newspaper, on a prop). The full
   sequence: mix dips (~0.5–2s, to ≈−40dB) → riser → the carrier prop arrives (~0.75s, ease-out/
   spring) → hit lands ON the spoken number → hold ~1.5s → reaction cut or hard CONTRAST cut
   (juxtaposition, not motion, delivers the weight). Variant: the scene BECOMES the statistic
   (bars grow with characters riding them; buildings rise while the stat handwrites at VO pace).
6. **Held-set evolution — the unanimous verdict:** reference-grade channels evolve a held set LIVE
   (one element pops/draws per spoken noun; an in-world agent erases and redraws; panels accrete
   while earlier panels stay alive) and **never cut to a changed state of the same set.**
   Enumeration WITHIN a concept = live adds on the held set; enumeration ACROSS concepts = hard
   cuts. Charts/maps: structure arrives first, then populates in waves timed to nouns, emphasis
   red LAST as the punch; draw-ons ride a riser; UI/dashboard panels pop pre-populated one-per-noun.
7. **Cut cadence (measured, refines §13a-ii):** story mode median hold 3–5s (~12–13 cuts/min),
   explainer ~3s (~13–17/min); the cold open runs a full second faster than the back half. Longest
   holds (16–38s) are ALWAYS progressive builds — confirming the hard rule. Highest-energy cuts
   flip background VALUE (dark↔light); a punch-in cut re-frames the same scene for emphasis.
8. **Audio grammar (co-equal layer) — MEASURED audio-only 2026-07-10** (Demucs vocal/residual stems +
   ffmpeg/librosa over 8 references; supersedes the earlier model-listening estimates that hallucinated
   the SFX inventory; per-video reports + `synthesis.md` in `visual-kit/research/audio-logs/`):
   - **The bed is PLACED, not wall-to-wall.** Music presence ~**79%** (61–85%; Kurzgesagt's 94% is the
     restrained *floor*, not the target) — real music-off passages exist. Hyper-compressed (LRA ~3.5–4.5),
     integrated ~**−18 LUFS**. References CLIP (true-peak often >0 dBFS); **our** master holds true-peak
     **≤ −1**, target ~−15/−16 LUFS.
   - **Two ducking regimes (register-dependent).** Explainer/story keeps the bed PRESENT, ducking only
     ~**2–3 dB** under VO (not buried). The comedic register (OverSimplified) does the opposite on marked
     beats — **music AND SFX AND VO drop together** in a synchronized full-stop (the silence-cut gag). Both
     are wanted; the full-stop is the same device as our number-reveal dip, and should be **extended so SFX
     drop in that gap too**, not just the bed.
   - **Silence is a scalpel (measured).** Ordinary music dips ~**19 dB** (16–26) on gravity/reveal; the
     deliberate full-silence drop (to −40 dB) is reserved for the number/reveal + chapter card.
   - **Breath is SELECTIVE.** A sustained/dramatic hit earns its own VO silence ~**0.55s** (0.3–0.8) — but
     only ~**20%** of events get one; most ride under continuous VO. → breathe on specific beats only (a
     number reveal, a chapter boundary), never on every event.
   - **SFX couple to structural events.** In the references, structural SFX track scene structure
     (scene-change→whoosh, chapter boundary→boom, an escalation capper→thud, text→tick); content-nuanced
     hits (the number-reveal punch, an aside→sting) land where the content warrants. In OUR stack ALL of
     these are **authored** by the `audio-director` and placed selectively — a whoosh/boom is NOT fired on
     *every* instance (the director's `grammar-guidance.md` sets the restraint: whoosh is rare, pop is
     additive-only). Per-element pop/pluck/riser stay overlay-driven (dormant until device-cards ship).
     Camera crawls + idle loops stay silent; a boom under a frozen frame is the one audio-only emphasis.
     Density (directional — SFX+music not separable in the residual) ~**20–40 transients/min** combined →
     SFX-only is lower; the story cap ~20/min holds.
   - **Dips land on ~⅓ of punchlines** (~36%, directional) — dip the music on SOME emphasis beats, never all
     (predictability kills it).
   - VO pace ~**200 wpm** (story/comedic; explainer ~165). Concrete dials live in `audio-tokens.json`
     (breath lengths, `dip_db`, `master_target`, the music-lane knobs `music_present_db`/`music_fade_s`/
     `track_switch_gap_s`).
9. **Typography law:** ONE handwritten/marker family carries everything (cards, bubbles, labels);
   sentence case for spoken lines, ALL-CAPS for labels/warnings; **red is the only emphasis ink**
   (semantic: alarm/prohibition/ownership); numbers/dates live on diegetic surfaces (§13 rule 4).
   **No burned word-captions exist anywhere in the studied 16:9 grade** — on-screen text is always
   diegetic or a deliberate card. One contrast face per video max, as a device.
10. **Executor reconciliation (how OUR stack realizes each rule):** camera law + cadence + cards →
    the motion engine (tokens + derivation); live held-set evolution → TODAY approximated by the
    seeded delta-chain (§13a-ii: one element per delta, landing on its noun) and by engine-drawn
    `progressive-reveal`/device overlays (true live adds, use them whenever the added element can be
    code-drawn); FULL live layer-adds on painted sets → the LIVE layer-move compositor (built 2026-07-12: `plate` + animated cutout `layers` — slide/path/bob/appear — planned by `motion-planner`, rendered by the engine `LayerView`; this
    teardown is its evidence base); limb animation / fluid morphs / Kurzgesagt's continuous-travel
    camera → observed, NOT adoptable. A grammar row with no executor in the current stack is
    authored as intent and realized at the cut (never faked with an off-grammar mechanism).
    **Music (Phase 3B):** the measured "placed bed, ~2–3 dB present duck" is realized as a **placed
    music lane** (`build_music_lane` → engine `MusicLane`): per-section mood tracks (authored by the
    `audio-director`) at a **CONSTANT present level** (`music_present_db`, ear-gated to 7), silent on
    authored `dry` spans, switching tracks via a fade→silence→fade gap. Two
    measured behaviors are DELIBERATELY not realized (owner taste, spec 2026-07-12-phase3b): the per-phrase
    ~2–3 dB VO duck (we hold the level constant — calm over breathing) and the references' frequent ~19 dB
    on-beat dips (music drops ONLY on the full-stop, dry spans, and track switches). The −40 full-stop dip
    is inherited by the lane, not re-authored.

**How this grammar is realized (2026-07-12) — no per-shot treatment enum.** Treatment is NOT a per-shot enum
the author picks; the old beat-type field is retired. The camera is **always locked** and every shot **hard-cuts** (the
engine; §13a-iii.1 — idle-bob + element deltas + the element-layer compositor carry the life), and the sound
is **authored** by the `audio-director` as one `audio-plan.json` (SFX · pause · music · dry), guided by this
measured grammar and its `grammar-guidance.md` distillation and placed **selectively** (not on every scene/
boundary). Deliberate **breaths** are authored `pause` cues — a render-inserted silence *before* the anchor
word, into which the dip lands while the outgoing frame holds; distinct from the writer's `[PAUSE]`/`[BEAT]`
prosody (baked in the VO). Breath lengths + all audio dials are data in `audio-tokens.json`, not restated here.

### The Simon Whistler test

Craig Billings, host of Doctor NOS (1.7M subs, part of Simon Whistler's compliant faceless
network): *"The people who do the same content as me without their face in it, most of them are
getting demonetized."* Same script, same production, same b-roll — the presence of a real narrated
voice is the monetization pivot. Where a human narrator isn't viable, hand cameos + screencap-first
visuals + one signature format is the compromise that clears the credibility bar.
