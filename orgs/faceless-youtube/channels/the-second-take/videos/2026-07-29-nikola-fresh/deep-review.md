## I. Diagnosis — why it reads competent, not amazing

Ranked. Each is a root cause; most of the 30+ line defects below hang off one of these five.

**1. Nobody in this script is a person.** Milton gets five words ("like a timeshare salesman" — generic,
and the only characterization he ever receives). No reaction when he texts his engineer "idiot," none at
the "Nikola is in my blood" letter, none at the pardon. GM puts in $2B and walks in twelve weeks; the
narrator has no opinion. The bar spends a full block building Q.T. Wiles (Gordon Ramsay setup-then-apply)
before Wiles does anything; silver's heat is Bunker the cheap billionaire who flew coach. This script's
narrator has sharp attitude about *mechanisms* (SPAC, safe harbor, order book — the three best blocks) and
none about *people*. That's why it reads like excellent Wikipedia. **Doctrine gap: nothing says build the
person before the machine.**

**2. There is no rise.** Nikola is never a phenomenon anyone was excited about — the only "big" the viewer
gets is a stock quote. §2.7 says the collapse only hurts if the viewer watched the climb; the writer treated
the June squeeze as the climb. No "And they were HOT" moment exists anywhere.

**3. The back half is a court reporter.** P13–P15: 287 words, zero pulls, zero reactions, average sentence
length climbing to 23 words. Grammar §1.3 and taste critic #13 name this exact decay zone — and it shipped
anyway. **Pipeline cause found:** taste findings are subtractive by design and the editor is forbidden to
free-write, so a #13 "flat stretch" finding has *no additive repair path* — nobody in the pipeline is
allowed to add the missing bit. Coherence is the only additive lane. Structural fix in §III.

**4. The caper is never staged.** The title's own scene — towing the truck up the hill — is a 30-word
subordinate clause inside the Hindenburg paragraph. The hook opens on the 2016 unveiling, then abandons it:
"This is not a pusher" is the best plant in the script and is never cashed. Ledger F-03 hands over the
pressure beat (chief engineer warned Milton weeks out, asked to postpone; prosecutors' characterization,
attribute cleanly) and it sits unused. §3.2 exists; blind transmission of it is still ~50%.

**5. Flat chronology, tension spent at 47%.** Fourteen of eighteen paragraphs turn on a date, eight as
spelled-out ordinals ("the thirtieth of November"). The one question the script arms — was the truck faked? —
is fully answered by P9, and nothing re-arms. P10–P12 run as a three-item list (Badger / hydrogen / orders)
joined by "After that," "Then there was," "And."

Plus one selection error: the ending picks Ford. **Lucid is this story's Colorado Brick Company** — bought
the factory for a thousandth of the peak (F-43), planted in P16, never paid off.

## II. Script edit directives (r2 sheet)

Numbered, zoned. Rewrites are proposals in-voice; leash-verify every new line at apply time (F-refs given).
Global guard on ALL rewrites: compression comes out of clauses and beats, never function words — keep the
subjects, the "that"s, the "and"s a speaker says.

## III. Pipeline deltas (slim — logic changes in place, no appended sections)

Ranked by leverage. Each names the exact file + section and the edit shape.

**P1. critics.md orchestration — give the flat-stretch finding an additive repair path.** Root cause of
§I.3: taste is subtractive, the editor may not free-write, so a #13 finding is un-fixable by the pipeline
that detects it. Edit the routing rule: a taste **#13 flat-stretch / quiet-script finding routes to the
writer pass alongside [STRUCTURAL] coherence findings** (the writer adds the missing short bit per flagged
block, leashed); the editor stays subtractive. One clause in the orchestration block + one clause in #13.

**P2. grammar §2.7 — build the person before the machine.** Add two sentences to the existing rise-before-
fall law: the protagonist is built as a character (one block: who he is, how he talks, one named pull)
before the scheme runs — the bar gives Wiles a full Gordon Ramsay block before he touches a number — and
the narrator keeps a point of view about the *people*, with reactions attaching to the man, not only the
mechanism. A script whose only sharp opinions are about mechanisms reads as a well-written summary.

**P3. grammar §1.1 — the compression budget.** Extend the spoken-grammar sentence: cuts come out of clauses
and beats, never the small words a speaker says — dropped subjects, dropped "that," dropped connectives are
a written tic ("Son of a Texas oil billionaire, and..." → "He was the son of..."). Five hand-fixes in one
Daniel pass, all caused by doctrine's own terseness pressure with no counterweight.

**P4. grammar §1.4 — the extended-conceit line.** Insert the distinction the breath-measure can't see: a
pull may *contrast to add a fact* ("except Madoff sold securities and MacGregor sold a fake country"),
never *extend into a second frame the viewer must construct* ("It's Ticketmaster, except they owned every
seat in the building" died for the built-out scenario, not the length). The tell: an "except"/"imagine"
that opens a hypothetical instead of adding a fact. Four hand-cuts across two videos, one of them a locked
line.

**P5. critics.md verdict overlay — locks expire.** Edit in place: a lock binds the regen its verdict sheet
drives; a later Daniel hand-edit outranks an earlier verdict; a line locked two rounds ago is ordinary
text. (4 of 7 silver locks are dead in the accepted script — current wording would have defended them.)

**P6. grammar §1.1 — still life in any tense.** Rescope from "present-tense still life" to agency in any
tense: "The guards were cowboys." → "To guard their silver, the Hunts hired cowboys."; a scale comparison
gets an agent and a verb ("They could've built an Eiffel Tower with all that silver."), never an appositive
fragment ("Basically an Eiffel Tower, in silver."). Three hand-fixes in one pass, all past-tense and
grammatical, invisible to the current rule.

**P7. grammar §2.3 — the cut sets the length.** Replace the falsified clause ("Landing well under means
beats are starved of color"): a script that lands under the band because every dead beat is gone ships
short (accepted silver: 7:56 against an 8–10 header); landing under because beats are *starved* is the
check, and length is never added back as padding.

**P8. grammar §1.6 — condition the identity aside.** "Yeah, that's his real name." fires only when the name
is itself the joke (Bunker, MacGregor), never as a reflex on an ordinary name. (Daniel deleted it off
"Bunker Hunt" context r2→accepted after r1 recommended it; currently the grammar's own exemplar invites it
unconditionally.)

**P9. grammar §1.6 — mid-block interest-assertion joins the ban.** The banned list covers credibility
padding; add its cousin: the narrator grading a fact's interest mid-delivery ("which is its own kind of
insane," "and absolutely nobody cared"). State the fact; the viewer supplies the amazement.

**P10. grammar §1.4 — the norm-then-violation frame.** One sentence: between the named pull and the prose
fallback sits a third reach — state the normal behavior, then the violation ("Now normally, people who
trade futures sell the contract for money. But the Hunts? They demanded payment in silver."). It's what
Daniel reaches for when no pull exists; doctrine currently offers only pull-or-lecture.

**P11. SKILL.md verdict-regen step — post-hand-edit sweep.** One line: after any human hand-edit pass, run
coherence + leash once over the edited script (relative-time phrases, referents, superlative bounds; a new
human-added fact gets targeted verification, never a hedge or a cut). Every Daniel hand-pass to date has
left 2–3 continuity breaks that the boss caught ad hoc; make it a step. (Exact placement at apply time.)

Net size impact on grammar: ≈ +230 words across seven in-place edits, one clause removed. critics.md: two
surgical edits. No new files, no new sections.
