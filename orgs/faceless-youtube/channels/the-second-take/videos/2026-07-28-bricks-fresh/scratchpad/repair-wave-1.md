## 5. Payload-last reorder assessment (SEPARATE DELIVERABLE — nothing applied)

**Question:** does a deterministic, meaning-preserving reorder exist that moves the `Palette:` sentence off
the final position so the payload lands last, across the 133–149 unlettered non-delta shots that close on it?

**Rule tested (R):** *where a non-delta prompt's final sentence begins with `Palette`, and the sentence
immediately before it begins with `Framing:`, move the `Palette…` sentence to sit immediately BEFORE the
`Framing:` sentence; change nothing else.* This is the only rule a script can apply without knowing which
sentence is the payload — `Framing:` and `Palette:` are both order-independent scene data, so the swap is
provably meaning-preserving. Its effect is that **whatever sentence currently sits before `Framing:` becomes
the closer.**

**Sample: 12 shots (≥10 as briefed), 4 per fifth across fifths 3–5.** "After" = the sentence R promotes to
the final position.

**Failure cases where no safe reorder exists at all:**

1. **Identity IS the payload** (`L107`, `L171`, and every `personified-character` / `reaction-shot` frame
   whose subject is the named figure). The ordering law pins identity FIRST; the payload-last law would pin
   it LAST. No permutation satisfies both — only re-authoring does, by giving the shot a separate closing
   payload object.
2. **Whole-frame payload** (`L124`, `L161`, `L203`, `L233`). The payload is the opening scene sentence, so
   the only "reorder" is moving sentence [0] to the end, which strips the identity/subject zone and makes
   the prompt open on furniture. Strictly worse.
3. **Absence closers** (`L136` "the field … stands plain and unlettered", `L161` "every surface of the shell
   is bare block and unlettered"). These sentences exist to *suppress* lettering. Promoting one into the
   most-literally-read slot makes "unlettered" the loudest instruction in the prompt — the L-2 class of
   defect, where a production rule gets drawn as content.
4. **The `Palette:` sentence is not always inert.** `L136` closes "the loop the one red accent"; `L107`
   closes "one warm pool of aisle light"; `L120`/`L241` name their single semantic red there. Moving it
   earlier is still meaning-preserving, but the shot loses its only closing statement about its accent —
   which on this channel is a semantic decision, not decoration.

**VERDICT: needs-per-shot-authoring.** The mechanical half is safe — a script *can* move `Palette:` off the
final position without changing meaning — but it does not fix M-2, it relocates it: the payload lands last
only ~40 % of the time and gets demoted behind an incidental fact in half the sample, and two whole classes
of shot (identity-payload, whole-frame-payload) have no legal permutation at all. **Do not script it
file-wide.** The proportionate response is the pair the review already identifies: the doctrine sentence in
`visual-grammar.md §2` making the *unlettered* payload an explicit authoring requirement (so the next file
is authored right rather than reordered after the fact), plus per-shot authoring on the frames where the
payload is a small object in a big frame — which is exactly the six shots repaired under M-2(a) above.
A cheap middle option if the caller wants a signal without a rule: a lint **HEADS-UP** (never HARD) on any
non-delta unlettered prompt of ≥4 sentences closing on `Palette:` — a nudge at authoring time, decidable,
and it costs nothing when the payload genuinely is the whole frame.
