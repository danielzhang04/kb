## Per-critic-question results

### Q1 — Scene logic and facts: FAIL

Most geography, orientation, multi-figure geometry, and factual staging are coherent. Failures:

- L03's final arcade-cabinet change illustrates the preceding “Pac-Man” list item while its own VO is the corporate-scam payload; the opening never visually lands the scam claim.
- L09 reduces the iPhone-release craze analogy to one phone in an almost empty showroom; the image carries the noun, not the demand phenomenon being compared.
- L35–L40 and L43 have stale `changed_elements` and, in L35–L40, the revenue payloads land one shot after the words that name them.
- L118–L120 never show a brick actually inside a box; the base freezes the foreman carrying it “toward” the carton and the deltas move on to paper tabs.
- L132 does not establish the specific customer pallet that L133 claims merely to wrap.
- L203 calls the object a “signed audit ledger” while also requiring every glyph field to be blank and unlettered.
- L207 explicitly holds “the instant before reversal” on the words “the judge threw the whole thing out”; the consequence arrives late at L208.

### Q2 — Literal check against the depiction bar: FAIL

- L03 and L09 are competent object illustrations but do not depict the meaning of their lines.
- L76 begins on “What a dick” but cuts directly to a figureless factory-capacity tableau. Re-anchor it to “Now, the targets…” and let the prior Wiles frame or a reaction cut carry the aside.
- The file otherwise uses non-literal classes productively; no class quota is imposed. The high `ironic-counterpoint` count is less concerning than the repeated physical staging used to realize it.

### Q3 — Prompt construction and forced payload ordering: FAIL

- Registry vocabulary: every backticked name resolves except `packing-executive` and `family-packer` (four uses each, L160–L163). Their L160 note explicitly surfaces them as new story-bearing cast requiring Pass-1 minting, so this is an intentional pre-gen asset gate, not a typo; they must exist before scene generation.
- L23–L25 declare `figures.crowd: true` but describe no people. Forge would still inject the crowd exemplar and crowd-rig clause into a figureless brick-hook chain.
- Positive absence passes: no prompt uses a “no X” negation list.
- Two-cast construction passes: no shot exceeds two named cast; every two-cast shot pins eye-line/head scale and a coherent shared plane or shared work island; all five `handoff` uses are fresh two-cast bases.
- Payload-order forced row: every sampled shot and every base was checked. Clean shots either close on the payload/literal or use the grammar's legal atmosphere/depth tail with the payload already isolated. The substantive misses are L03, L09, L76, L118, and L207, where the correct payload is not actually in the frame. L235 also leaks workflow mechanism into scene prose: “through the layered path.”

### Q4 — Renderability and generator risk: FAIL

- L118 is a freeze of incomplete continuous action (“carrying one rust-red brick … toward the fixed carton”); the promised completed act never appears in the chain.
- L133 introduces an implicitly new “customer pallet” at the same time it adds wrap, so the delta asks for two semantic changes unless the pallet is established in L132.
- L235 contains nonvisual mechanism prose (`through the layered path`) in `still_prompt`.
- Figure caps, interaction placement, and countable n≥3 staging otherwise pass. Counts are generally given as rows, tiers, bays, courts, or other drawable arrangements rather than bare numbers.

### Q5 — Disclosure order: PASS

No premature identity/twist disclosure remains. MiniScribe, Terry Johnson, Wiles, the rented warehouse, Colorado Brick Company, customers, the judge, and HR enter on or after their narration disclosures. The L112 and L132 assembly concerns are resolved as detailed below. The early generic warehouse at L83 is not a deliberately withheld twist; the rented offsite warehouse remains absent until L113.

### Q6 — Two-figure plane/scale coherence: PASS

All 22 two-cast shots preserve a coherent shared plane/topology. L163 lacks the literal word `plane`, but “share one distant work island,” matching eye-line, and equal head scale pin the topology. L132 correctly keeps foreman and customer on the same middle-distance plane with equal head scale. No second-named figure is accidentally pushed into crowd scale.

### Q7 — Action-chain cause-to-effect readability: FAIL

- L36/L37 metadata describes the other shot's change; L39/L40 repeats the same one-shot shift, and L43's date-card metadata does not describe its rewind mechanism.
- L35–L40 therefore read as delayed payload chains: `125 MILLION` appears on “year,” and `600 MILLION` appears on the following “year,” instead of on the amount words.
- L118–L120 skips the boxing consequence.
- L132–L134 lacks the specific pallet in the base that the first delta wraps.
- L207–L208 stages setup after the VO has already stated the reversal.
- Other declared chains read causally and deliver their final consequences.

### Q8 — Semantic-cast justification: PASS, with asset gate

Named cast belongs to the beats it carries: `brick-foreman` performs the fraud's management/warehouse acts; `auditor-rep`, `hq-banker`, `bond-investor`, and `return-customer` stay role-specific; `pc-boxy` is not reused as a general mascot; and `drive-maker`'s later Maxtor use is explicitly authorized in the current registry. `packing-executive` and `family-packer` are semantically justified but must be minted before their first scene.

### Taste-ground-truth ruling

**Fail.** Many shots state the desired ratios, but the file does not reliably stage a fresh scale gestalt. “One-fifth frame height,” clear upper volume, and aisle/lane/row recession have become reusable verbal assertions. With only 22/243 shots making an explicit camera/vantage choice, most prompts still leave the engine at its eye-level frontal rest position. The world is often deep, but it is repeatedly the same corridor/table/plinth world. This guards against the exact standing failure in `taste-ground-truth.md`: ratios are named more often than a distinct world/camera composition is invented.
