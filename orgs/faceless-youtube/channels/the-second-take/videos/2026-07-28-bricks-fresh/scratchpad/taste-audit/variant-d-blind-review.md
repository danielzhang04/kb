## 4. Weaknesses, comparative verdict, and human decision

**Weakest D frames first**

1. **L04:** The pixels stage a clean skeptical reaction, but `pc-boxy` is not the causal subject of the narrated scam and occupies more visual weight than "middle distance" suggests.
2. **L10:** The ramp and doorway communicate release/exit, but the carton is too generic to make the Apple/iPhone comparison immediate, and the sparse room loses the richer retail path visible in B.
3. **L09:** The shelf depletion is clear and the chain hold is strong, but the crowd remains after the narrated causal subject has shifted back to the computers; it is well bounded yet unnecessary to make the sentence true.
4. **L02:** The two references are unusually complete, but the overhead wig display looks like a surreal decoration rather than hair worn in the period world.
5. **L03:** The exposed drive improves specificity, but the clay-red rectangle projects like a detached block, only partly reading as a rigid corner forcing open the seam.

**D versus A:** D improves story-state continuity at L05-L06 and L07-L09, gives the buyer crowd actual bounding geometry, and makes the hard-drive explanation at L11-L12 much more specific. A improves on D's immediate period atmosphere at L01 and offers a richer, less repetitive stocked-computer world at L07. A's L05 is visibly weakened by the residual red siren recorded in its parked manifest row.

**D versus B:** D improves the combined big-hair/arcade beat, the compromised hardware clue, the held stage sequences, and the exposed/nested drive explanation. B improves spatial depth and world scale at L04, gives L05 a more relaxed retail tableau, and gives L10 a clearer path from product ramp to open shop door. D is often more causally explicit; B is often more environmentally persuasive.

**D versus C:** D improves causal specificity across the hardware/scam opening, supplies coherent held stages, fixes the buyer/depletion sequence, and explains hard-drive identity and placement. C supplies broader compositional variety and, at L09, keeps a thing-led beat free of a non-causal crowd; it also produces several airy standalone scenes. C's L01, L07, and L08 manifests are parked, matching visible crowd prominence or physical-argument defects in those rows.

**Neutral human-decision menu**

- Keep D as one coherent sequence if held cameras, causal state changes, crowd geometry, and hardware legibility matter most, accepting the noted occupancy and symbolic-literalness weaknesses.
- Choose a stage-aware hybrid: retain D's L05-L06 and L07-L09 blocks intact, while considering A for L01, B for L04 or L10, and the preferred standalone D rows elsewhere.
- Choose row-by-row on still-image taste using Section 3, accepting that taking B at L05 or A at L07 would surrender D's corresponding held-stage continuity.
- Request fresh human review or new evidence only for the weak D rows before deciding what to retain.
- Prefer a full A, B, or C comparator set if atmosphere, spacious retail depth, or compositional variety outweigh D's chain and explanatory advantages.

These options describe different tradeoffs in the observed frames. They do not establish a doctrine winner.
