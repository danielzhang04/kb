# Composition + People/Crowd Taste Audit — bricks-fresh regen vs liked baseline

Scope: composition + people/crowd factors only. Rig/script/lettering and pixel-color metrics owned elsewhere.

Liked baseline grammar (from L05-09,11,19-21,23-25 in `_archive-pre-reset/scenes/`):
- Deep diagonals / single-point vanishing corridors (L21-storefront-recession, L20-river, L22-warehouse-row)
- Small-to-mid figure scale in environments that carry the story, NOT tight adult-headshot crops
- Foreground anchors (crates, boxes, doorframes, barrels) flanking or grounding the frame
- Generous negative space/breathing room even in busy scenes
- Crowds, when present, live as a small-scale, distant BACKGROUND layer (through a window, down a receding line) — never a close co-planar wall pressed to camera
- Great non-character shots use spacious staging (CRT shelf, hard drive on pillow, warehouse exterior) — object/environment given room to read, not frame-filling

## Findings

### 1. Systemic wins vs the liked grammar
- **Deep-diagonal / vanishing-point corridors** reproduce the baseline grammar cleanly: L09 (queue), L15 (convention hall), L18 (loading dock), L22 (storage row). All four keep the figure small-to-mid scale, stage genuine fore/mid/background separation, and leave large negative space — this is the strongest, most consistent win in the set.
- **Single-character "packing/warehouse" vignettes** (L05, L18, L23, L24, L25) reliably include a foreground object anchor (box, cart, table) plus a background depth layer (shelving, wall, brick), echoing baseline L19/L21's "simple readable staging."
- **Crowd-as-distant-background-layer, when used, works**: L08 (chaos shelf), L09 (queue), L15 (convention hall) all keep crowd figures small and staged behind a genuine depth cue (diagonal shelf line, receding queue, converging display tables) rather than pressed to camera.

### 2. Systemic misses (pattern + shots)
- **Crowd-wall-at-camera is the dominant, repeated failure mode**: L02 (roller rink), L06 (storefront window), L07 (money-reaching) all render the crowd as a co-planar mass filling 55-65% of frame height, at essentially zero recession, with adult head-to-body proportions instead of the squat ~2.7-face-only crowd-rig standard. This directly inverts the baseline grammar, where the same story beats (crowd outside a shop window, L06-07 in the archive) kept the crowd small and physically distant through the glass. This is the single biggest compositional regression in the set — 3 of 6 MISS shots are this exact failure.
- **Same-beat wide shots got re-cropped tight**: L20/L21 recreate the liked baseline L20 gold-rush market scene, but instead of the baseline's sweeping wide shot with tiny distant panning figures (the thing the human specifically praised as a "great non-character/environment shot"), the new pair crops in close on a 7-figure cluster at the stall — losing the scale contrast that made the original land.
- **Frame-filling prop inserts lose breathing room**: L13 (overhead drawer shot) fills the frame edge-to-edge with the object and gives it almost no negative space or background depth, unlike every liked non-character shot (CRT shelf, hard drive-on-pillow, warehouse exterior), which all gave the subject room to sit in a lit, legible environment.

### 3. Worst individual shots and why
1. **L02** (roller rink) — worst offender: 14+ adult-proportioned heads packed edge-to-edge with ~10% negative space and zero depth staggering.
2. **L06** (storefront crowd at glass) — repeats the same wall-of-heads problem on the exact beat the baseline nailed by keeping the crowd small/distant; this version brings them uncomfortably close and large.
3. **L07** (money-reaching crowd) — same wall-of-heads issue, worsened by outstretched arms eating even more of the frame.
4. **L13** (drawer overhead insert) — no negative space or depth at all; breaks the spacious-staging rule every liked non-character shot follows.
5. **L20/L21** (market stall pair) — abandon the wide/tiny-figures grammar the baseline used for this same story beat, losing the intended scale contrast.

### 4. Crowd-specific verdicts
- **Depth-staggered / background-layer crowds — MATCH**: L08, L09, L15. These should be the template: keep crowd scale small (≤20% frame height), stage them behind a genuine depth cue, and let the environment do most of the storytelling.
- **Co-planar wall-of-heads crowds — MISS**: L02, L06, L07. Systemic rework needed: push the crowd back into mid/background, shrink scale, and apply the squat ~2.7-face-only crowd-rig proportion (per crowd-rig-poyais-standard memory) instead of adult body proportions.
- **Two-character foreground vignette with background crowd — PARTIAL**: L16/L17 (boxing ring). The crowd itself is handled correctly (small, background, along the ropes), but the two dominant foreground characters are large/frontal (~45% height each), cutting into the negative space that the liked grammar reserves — so these register PARTIAL rather than a clean match, for reasons unrelated to the crowd itself.
