Scope: FRESH = `assets/scenes/L01-L25` (L09 skipped — parked, individuated-crowd defect). V2 = `scratchpad/fresh-gen/v2-frames/`. LIKED = `assets/_archive-pre-reset/scenes/{L05-09,L11,L19-25}`. All 24 FRESH, all 25 V2, all 13 LIKED frames viewed directly.

Structural note before the table: shots.json groups consecutive FRESH ids into `base`/`delta` **stages** — one composed background reused across 2-3 short consecutive VO lines (e.g. L01-L03 = one mall stage across 3 VO beats; L04-L06 = one repair-shop stage; L13-L15 = one product-pedestal stage; L18-L20 = one colosseum stage; L23-L25 = one warehouse stage). This is by design, not a duplication defect — but it means the 24 FRESH frames represent only **~9 distinct compositions**, each seen 2-3 times with minor variation. V2's 25 frames are 25 distinct compositions. Noted because it affects perceived variety even where any single composition scores well.

## Set-level verdicts

**1. Improved vs V2, per dimension**
- **Composition / scale gestalt: IMPROVED.** FRESH lands several genuinely deep, structured-world compositions that V2 didn't reach — the two-story mall (L01-03), the nested-archway showroom (L16-17), and especially the colosseum (L18-20), which is the single most architecturally ambitious shot across either generated set. V2's strongest scale attempt (L12, tiny figure at a receding storage-unit row) is matched or exceeded by several FRESH shots. Object-hero/non-character shots (L13-15 product pedestal, L23-25 warehouse) are also more spacious and better-lit than V2's flatter equivalents (V2 L22-25 box/brick close-ups).
- **Crowd: MIXED, arguably regressed on rig discipline.** V2's crowd shots (mall/auction/wrestling-ring audiences) hold a more uniformly simplified rig throughout. FRESH's crowds are *better staged* (deeper queues, better architecture around them) but let individuation creep back in on near-camera figures in three separate shot-groups (L07/08, L16/17, L22) — see verdict 4.
- **Color: parity.** Both sets stay balanced-warm, neither oversaturated; no meaningful difference.
- Chains-read: not scored (out of scope per brief).

**2. Honest headline — does FRESH close the "major gap" vs LIKED?**
No. Composition/scale-gestalt has genuinely closed: L18, L01-03, and L23-25 in particular could sit inside the LIKED grammar on ratio and structure alone — small-to-moderate figures, deep multi-plane architecture, generous open air, non-character world shots doing real work. That axis is no longer the dominant problem.

What remains, and it shows up in **every single one of the 13 LIKED frames and none of the 24 FRESH frames**, is render register: LIKED images carry visible paper/canvas grain, volumetric dust-beam light, painterly gradient shading on terrain and materials, moody desaturated color grading, and real material sheen (metal, glass, gold). FRESH — like V2 before it — stays flat-cel throughout: uniform color fills, uniformly thin linework, and only a handful of soft glow/vignette touches (L10-12's lamp wash, L18-20's edge vignette, L23-25's window sunbeams) that gesture at atmosphere without approaching LIKED's richness. This is a texture/render-technique gap, not a composition gap — confirming ground-truth axis 2 as the dominant unclosed cause, not axis 4 (scale execution), which this audit found substantially delivered in the better FRESH shots.

**3. Best 3 / worst 3 FRESH frames**
- Best: **L18** (colosseum — best depth, best crowd solution via distant silhouette, only real mood-lighting vignette in the set), **L25** (warehouse boxes — deepest structured racking, best light-beam execution, punchline reveal reads clean), **L14** (product pedestal — symmetric non-character world shot, glow accent, added story-readable detail).
- Worst: **L21** (prospector porch — flattest depth, least open air, reads as figure-in-front-of-backdrop rather than world-with-people-in-it), **L04** (computer shop — cluttered shelving eats the open-air ratio, figures run past the 30% band), **L07** (auction hall — good architecture undercut by the crowd-individuation creep).

**4. Does the L09-parked crowd defect appear subclinically elsewhere?**
Yes, in three places: **L07/L08** (auction-hall near-counter crowd — visible hair-color variety and smiling/eyebrow detail on ~15 foreground figures), **L16/L17** (archway-showroom queue — same pattern, ~15 near-camera figures with individuated faces), and **L22** (market-stall — 2-3 near-camera customers with full facial detail). None are severe enough to have been flagged/parked the way L09 was, but the mechanism is identical to the parked diagnosis: dense foreground-scale crowd placement erodes the simplified dot-eye rig back toward individuated faces. This is a live, recurring, unaddressed pattern — worth a mechanism-level fix (rig-collapse reinforcement at close/foreground crowd distances specifically) rather than continued per-shot retries.
