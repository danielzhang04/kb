### ST-006 — The Company That Shipped Bricks and Called Them Hard Drives
- **Format:** long-form (~10–13 min). *(Shorts skipped for this production batch.)*
- **Provisional angle:** MiniScribe, a hot 1980s disk-drive maker and IBM supplier, hid an inventory shortfall by literally packing ~26,000 bricks into hard-drive boxes, serial-numbering them, and shipping them overseas to fake inventory — then counting the same fakes twice by shipping and returning them. Lead with the human-revenge exposure (laid-off employees tipped the press) and the auditor failure (auditors *weighed* the "drives" and signed off).
- **Payload promise:** The viewer learns that inventory — the thing auditors physically weigh and count — was itself the lie, that senior officers personally packed bricks, and that the system never caught it; a fired employee did.
- **Lever:** vindication / forbidden-knowledge (how the check itself was gamed).
- **Key questions the video must answer (→ researcher's seed):**
  1. The brick operation specifics: the secret warehouse, who packed them, the $4.3M booked, the ship-and-return double count.
  2. How did auditors weigh boxes and sign off — what exactly did they check vs. assume?
  3. The role of the pressure culture / CEO Q.T. Wiles' "turn-around" theatrics in driving it.
  4. How the laid-off employees exposed it, and to whom.
  5. Consequences: the lawsuits, who paid, what changed in inventory auditing.
- **Title options:** 1) The Company That Shipped Bricks and Called Them Hard Drives 2) To Hit Their Numbers, the Executives Packed Bricks at Midnight 3) The $4 Million of Hard Drives That Were Literally Bricks
- **Why original:** Darkly funny + intensely visual (a stylized channel can *show* the brick warehouse); fresh at the big-documentary tier; the revenge-exposure + auditor-failure angle beats the "funny brick fact" listicles.
