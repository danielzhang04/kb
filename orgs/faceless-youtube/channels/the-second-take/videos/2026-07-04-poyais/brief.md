### ST-009 — The Prince Who Sold a Country That Didn't Exist
- **Format:** long-form (~12–15 min) + shorts bench (provisional; shorts-writer finalizes from the researched long-form)
- **Provisional angle:** Gregor MacGregor (1822) invented a lush Central American nation, "Poyais," crowned himself its prince, and sold London investors government bonds, land grants, and printed Poyais banknotes — for a country that was empty jungle. ~250 settlers sailed to claim their land; more than half died. Tell the finance-mania mechanics AND the settler tragedy together (listicles do one or the other).
- **Payload promise:** The viewer learns *why* it worked — post-Napoleonic London was so hungry for Latin American bonds that a confident man with letterhead and a title could manufacture sovereign credit from nothing — plus the human cost the "wildest scam" clips omit.
- **Lever:** vindication / forbidden-knowledge.
- **Title options:** 1) The Prince Who Sold a Country That Didn't Exist 2) You Could Buy Government Bonds for a Nation That Was Never Real 3) The Fake Country That London Bought
