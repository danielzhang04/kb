**Weaknesses, worst first.** The shard's dominant failure is **sparseness**: a large majority of these frames are one object on dead air (L71, L72, L86, L90) or a subject against one or two flat colour planes with an empty wedge occupying a third of canvas (L61, L68, L70, L73, L77–L80, L87, L88). The §6 "built, rich, filled edge-to-edge, fore/mid/background" bar is met in only about six frames (L64, L74, L81, L82, L85, and partially L67/L69).

Second is **render drift off flat cel**, in three distinct directions: soft-gradient realistic hands recurring across L62, L67, L68, L82 and L85; radial glow and airbrush on L65, L84 and L74's sky; and outright painterly rendering on L75, L76 and L86 — L86 in particular is watercolour, not cel.

Third, the **climax chain L77–L80 is broken**. The fines were supposed to accrete: L79 loses the OCC slab established in L78, and L80 discards all three slabs for a single unrelated total card, so the sequence has no build. The same held-set failure hits L87→L88 (red bolt clamps become one silver G-clamp) and L68→L69 (two different ethics-line posters). Location continuity is worse still — the bank appears as a modern facade (L64), a grey temple in a swamp (L76), a stucco arched building (L77–L80) and a cream columned temple (L87).

Fourth, **palette and accent discipline are loose**. The red drifts off `#d7402b` to a bright salmon across the whole L77–L80 chain and to a brick tone in L87; it is used decoratively as a poster border (L68), a bench stripe (L81), a full-width ribbon (L73) and a full-width bar (L88). Several scenes collapse to a single monochrome hue family (L63, L83) while others run five-plus hues (L66, L74).

Fifth, **expression lands wrong at both ends**: caricature scowls on ordinary beats (L67, L76, and the mascot in L73), a startled face on an explicitly unsurprised beat (L63), and — worse — two leads with **no facial features at all** (L70, L74), where the beat's emotion is carried by nothing.

Finally, three frames carry defects that would be visible to any viewer: the "COMEDY OFF" production note baked onto a cash register (L69), caption plaques covering the lead's head (L74), and the missing "ADMITTED" stamp that is the whole subject of L90. The strongest frames in the shard are L69 (palette turn, restrained expression), L81 (staging, though the lead is drawn in a clashing register) and L82 (a genuinely well-judged deadpan gag) — none of them clean.
