[B-ROLL: a courtroom docket with a single name on it, everything else blank]
Out of the entire Wells Fargo fake accounts scandal, exactly one person was ever criminally charged. [BEAT]

[B-ROLL: a charge sheet that reads "obstruction of a bank examination," not "fraud"]
And it was not for the fraud. In 2023, the executive who had run the bank's retail arm pleaded guilty to a single count of obstructing a bank examination, getting in the way of the regulators looking into it.

[B-ROLL: a sentence card — probation, home confinement, no prison — then an empty cell]
A judge sentenced her to three years of probation, six months of home confinement, and no prison. The judge turned down the prosecutors' request for a year behind bars, and said she had been singled out. Nobody was ever convicted of opening a single fake account, because in the end, nobody had exactly ordered it.
