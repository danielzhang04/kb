[B-ROLL: the word GREAT and the number 8 snapping together like a slogan on a scorecard]
The biggest retail bank fraud of the decade started with a rhyme. [BEAT]

[B-ROLL: a household checklist filling in — checking, savings, card, login]
Wells Fargo wanted eight of its products in every household. Checking, savings, a credit card, online banking, the works. And the reason the target was eight, and not seven or ten, was partly that eight rhymed with great.

[B-ROLL: a 1999 campaign poster reading "Going for Gr-eight"]
There was even an internal campaign, back in 1999, called Going for Gr-eight. Once a bonus rode on that number, thousands of employees started opening accounts customers never asked for, just to hit it. No mastermind dreamed it up. There was just a goal nobody could reach honestly, and a rhyme holding the whole thing together.
