# Short 01 — The number that didn't double

- **Archetype:** Shocking stat (with a myth-bust payoff)
- **Parent long-form:** 2026-07-19-wells-fargo
- **Status:** publish
- **Target length:** ~35s (visual — the count animates)
- **First frame / on-frame text (3–5 words):** "3.5 MILLION. NOBODY ASKED."
- **Caption:** burned-in throughout; emphasis words punched ("doubling," "it didn't," "found more of it").

---

[B-ROLL: a counter ticking up from 2.1 to 3.5 million, fast, over an ordinary bank counter]
A major American bank opened as many as three and a half million accounts and credit cards that customers never asked for. [BEAT]

[B-ROLL: the 2016 number and the 2017 number sitting side by side]
When that number first came out in 2016, it was smaller. About two point one million. A year later it climbed to three and a half, and that got reported as the fraud doubling. It didn't.

[B-ROLL: a review window stretching wider on a timeline, sweeping up accounts that were already there]
The bank had gone back and reviewed more years, and counted more of what was already there. Same fraud. They'd just found more of it. And even that number is the bank's own estimate of accounts that might have been unauthorized, not a hard count of confirmed fakes. The bank was Wells Fargo. And nobody in a back room ordered any of it.
