### ST-012 — How Two Brothers Bought Up Half the World's Silver
- **Format:** long-form (~12–16 min). *(Shorts skipped for this production batch.)*
- **Provisional angle:** In 1979–80, Texas oil heirs Nelson Bunker Hunt and William Herbert Hunt tried to corner the global silver market, quietly buying up a huge share of the world's deliverable silver on borrowed money and driving the price from around $6 to nearly $50 an ounce, until the exchanges changed the rules, the price crashed on "Silver Thursday," and their empire imploded. Do the forensic on *how you buy up a market* and how the system fought back, not just the "rich guys lost money" beat.
- **Payload promise:** The viewer learns what buying up / cornering a market actually means, how the Hunts used leverage and physical delivery to squeeze it, how much of the world's silver they controlled, and how the COMEX rule change ("Silver Rule 7") deliberately broke them in a single day.
- **Lever:** vindication / forbidden-knowledge — "how two men nearly owned a metal, and how the house changed the rules." Register runs HOT (money-absurdity, hubris).
- **Title (locked):** How Two Brothers Bought Up Half the World's Silver.
  - **ACCURACY FLAG (must resolve in research, feeds metadata):** verify "**half**" vs "**a third**." The Hunts are commonly described as holding roughly *a third of the world's total* silver but closer to *half of the deliverable / privately-held* supply. Keep the title's "half" ONLY if it's defensible as half of the *deliverable/privately-held* supply (state that framing in the script); if the honest figure is a third of total with no clean "half" framing, flag it so the primary title is revised to "a third." The script must state whatever the sourced, honest figure is, hedged, and the title must not overstate it.
- **Key questions the video must answer (→ researcher's seed):**
  1. Mechanically, how did the Hunts buy up silver (physical hoarding + futures + leverage), and roughly what share of world/deliverable/privately-held silver did they end up controlling (the half-vs-third question)?
  2. The price path: the run from ~$6 to ~$50 (Jan 1980) and the collapse on Silver Thursday (27 March 1980) with real dates and figures.
  3. What exactly did the exchanges/COMEX do (margin hikes, "Silver Rule 7" limiting long positions) and was it fair or a rigged rescue?
  4. The margin call and bailout: the ~$1B+ loan arranged to prevent a cascade; who was exposed?
  5. The aftermath: the Hunts' bankruptcy, the CFTC/market-manipulation findings, and what changed in commodity rules.
