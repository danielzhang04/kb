## Fact ledger  ← the scriptwriter may state ONLY what appears here

**The boom**
- **[F-01]** Nauru is a Pacific island of about 8 square miles (~21 km²), one of the world's smallest states. — *Src:* S1,S2 · *Conf:* high.
- **[F-02]** Its wealth came from phosphate rock, formed over millennia from fossilized seabird droppings (guano) that hardened into some of the purest phosphate on Earth. — *Src:* S2 · *Conf:* high.
- **[F-03]** Nauru became independent in 1968, and in the years after independence it had among the highest GDP per capita in the world, frequently cited as the highest, on phosphate alone. — *Src:* S1,S2 · *Conf:* high · *Note:* "highest in the world" is widely repeated; some accounts say second (behind a Gulf oil state). Hedge to "one of the richest places on Earth per person."
- **[F-04]** Landowners and citizens received phosphate royalties without having to work the mines; the money simply arrived. — *Src:* S2 · *Conf:* med.

**The self-destruction (the wealth and the wasteland are the same act)**
- **[F-05]** Strip-mining the phosphate meant tearing the island apart: by the boom's later decades about 80% of Nauru's surface had been mined out, leaving a moonscape of jagged limestone pinnacles. — *Src:* S2,S4 · *Conf:* high.
- **[F-06]** The mined-out interior became effectively uninhabitable, pushing the population onto a narrow coastal strip. — *Src:* S2 · *Conf:* high.
- **[F-07]** The very resource that made Nauru rich was finite and visibly running out, which is why the country set money aside for the day the phosphate was gone (see F-08). — *Src:* S1,S4 · *Conf:* high · *Note:* connective — the trust existed *because* everyone knew the guano would end.

**The trust fund (the payload: where the money went)**
- **[F-08]** Nauru put much of its phosphate income into a sovereign trust, the Nauru Phosphate Royalties Trust (NPRT), meant to fund the island after the phosphate ran out and to rehabilitate the land. — *Src:* S4,S5 · *Conf:* high.
- **[F-09]** Across the boom, Nauru earned enormous phosphate royalties; one common figure is that roughly $1.7 billion in royalties was ultimately squandered on bad decisions. — *Src:* S2 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* "$1.7B squandered" is a summary figure; the trust's own valuation is tracked separately in F-10.
- **[F-10]** By 1991 the NPRT was valued at about A$1.3 billion; by 2004 it was estimated at only about A$300 million, a near-total collapse blamed on mismanagement and bad investments. — *Src:* S5 · *Conf:* high · *Note:* this is the cleanest documented peak-to-crash pair; use A$1.3B → A$300M.
- **[F-11]** The trust invested in overseas real estate, hotels, and businesses; its flagship asset was Nauru House, an office tower in Melbourne, Australia, called the jewel in the crown of its holdings. — *Src:* S5 · *Conf:* high.
- **[F-12]** Nauru House, its most valuable property, was sold in 2004 to help pay off Nauru's debts, including debt owed to GE Capital. — *Src:* S5 · *Conf:* high · *Note:* the flagship asset going to a creditor is the concrete image of the collapse.
- **[F-13]** Nauru ran a national airline, Air Nauru, whose fleet could reportedly carry roughly 10% of the country's population at once and which ran at a large ongoing loss. — *Src:* S2 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* the "10% of the population" line is widely repeated; hedge as "by some accounts."

**The musical (the icon of the waste)**
- **[F-14]** Nauru's leadership backed a London West End musical about Leonardo da Vinci, "Leonardo the Musical: A Portrait of Love." — *Src:* S3,S4 · *Conf:* high.
- **[F-15]** It opened at the Strand Theatre in London on 3 June 1993 and closed about a month later after only 37 performances. — *Src:* S3 · *Conf:* high.
- **[F-16]** The project was brought to Nauru by Duke Minks, an adviser to Nauru and a former road manager for the 1960s pop group Unit 4 + 2, working with the group's former singer Tommy Moeller. — *Src:* S3 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* named, documented; present as reported.
- **[F-17]** Nauru sank roughly £2 million into the production (some accounts put the total loss nearer $5 million), and recovered almost none of it. — *Src:* S3 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* figures conflict (£1.5–2M vs ~$5M); hedge. Nauru's president Bernard Dowiyogo reportedly attended the premiere (S4, Conf: low).

**The survival pivots (comedy off here — this is the tragedy)**
- **[F-18]** As the money vanished, phosphate income cratered: by 2004 Nauru's phosphate exports brought in about US$640,000, down from the billions the island had earned in its prime. — *Src:* S1 · *Conf:* high.
- **[F-19]** In 2001 Nauru began hosting an Australian offshore immigration-detention centre under the Howard government's "Pacific Solution," starting with pledges of around A$20 million plus a further A$10 million in aid. — *Src:* S6 · *Conf:* high.
- **[F-20]** The centre closed in 2007 (under PM Kevin Rudd) and reopened in 2012 (under PM Julia Gillard); it became central to Nauru's economy — by 2019–20 the processing facility made up about 58% of total Nauruan government revenue. — *Src:* S6 · *Conf:* high.
- **[F-21]** Since about 2013 Australia has provided on the order of two-thirds of Nauru's GDP, largely through the detention arrangement. — *Src:* S4 · *Conf:* med.
- **[F-22]** In the 1990s–2000s Nauru also ran an offshore-banking/shell-bank haven and sold passports and citizenship. — *Src:* S7 · *Conf:* high.
- **[F-23]** According to a former deputy chairman of Russia's central bank (Viktor Melnikov), around US$70 billion of Russian criminal money was laundered through Nauru in 1998. — *Src:* S7 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* attribute to Melnikov's estimate, not as flat fact.
- **[F-24]** Nauru sold passports to foreign nationals, and it was reported that some ended up with al-Qaeda members; under pressure the US Treasury designated Nauru a money-laundering state (2002) and the FATF blacklisted it, before removing it in 2005 after Nauru passed anti-laundering laws. — *Src:* S7 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* the al-Qaeda passport claim is reported; present as reported, hedge.
- **[F-25]** More recently Nauru has run a citizenship-by-investment ("climate resilience") program offering a passport for a minimum investment (around $105,000). — *Src:* S6 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* "as of the mid-2020s."

**The human cost**
- **[F-26]** With its farmland mined away, Nauru shifted almost entirely to imported processed food and developed among the highest rates of obesity and type-2 diabetes in the world. — *Src:* S2 · *Conf:* high · *Note:* exact rankings vary by year; keep it general ("among the highest on Earth").
- **[F-27]** By 2013 about 24% of Nauruans were living below the poverty line. — *Src:* S2 · *Conf:* med.

**Connective / relational facts (ownership · cause · identity · sequence)**
- **[F-28]** The trust fund and the strip-mining were two halves of one bargain: the island was consumed to build a fortune that was supposed to outlive it, so squandering the fortune meant Nauru had destroyed its land AND lost the money it was destroyed for. — *Src:* S4,S8 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* causal/identity link the writer needs for coherence; grounded in F-05/F-08/F-10.
- **[F-29]** Before independence the phosphate was mined by a foreign (British/Australian/NZ) operation with most profits going abroad; Nauru took over the mining itself around independence in 1968–1970, which is when the island's own royalties, and its own responsibility for the aftermath, began. — *Src:* S1,S4 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* general historical record; verify the exact buyout year at lock.
- **[F-30]** The bitter loop: Australia, which profited from Nauru's phosphate in the colonial era, later became the country paying Nauru to stay afloat by hosting its unwanted asylum seekers. — *Src:* S4,S6 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* the irony is real and sourced in its parts (F-29 + F-19/F-21); present as the documented sequence, not editorializing.

## Cast, motive & human-cost
- **Protagonist = the whole nation.** There's no single villain; the "character" is a tiny country handed an impossible fortune with no institutions to manage it, surrounded by advisers and salesmen who steered it into vanity bets (F-08–F-17). The interiority the writer can open on: a place that got rich so fast it never learned to say no, and trusted the wrong people to invest a fortune it didn't understand.
- **The advisers/salesmen** (the nearest thing to antagonists): the outside operators like Duke Minks who pitched a bird-poop republic on a West End musical (F-16). Documented, named, present as reported. Not a milked biography.
- **Human cost (light, aggregate, comedy OFF):** the island itself was consumed, 80% a limestone moonscape (F-05); farmland gone, the population pushed to the coast (F-06); a health collapse (among the world's worst obesity/diabetes, F-26); a quarter in poverty by 2013 (F-27); and a country that ended up renting out its sovereignty, hosting a detention camp and selling passports, to survive (F-19–F-25). State it plainly and move; do not lecture.
- **Witnessed-absurd / telling details:** (a) a national airline that could fly a tenth of the whole country at once (F-13); (b) the government of an 8-square-mile island owning a skyscraper in Melbourne (F-11), then handing it to a creditor (F-12); (c) a bird-poop fortune bet on a Leonardo da Vinci musical that ran 37 nights (F-14–F-15).

## Verification chain (vindication lever — how the case is PROVEN)
- **What is proven / uncontested:** the boom, the ~80% strip-mining, the NPRT peak-to-collapse (A$1.3B→A$300M), the Nauru House sale, the musical's dates and 37-performance run, and the detention-centre timeline and revenue share are all documented across independent sources [F-03,F-05,F-10,F-12,F-15,F-19,F-20]. **What stays soft:** the exact musical loss, Air Nauru's loss figures, and the money-laundering totals (attributed, not proven) [F-13,F-17,F-23].
- **Corroboration:** the arc appears consistently across the encyclopedic record (S1), engineering/culture press (S2), the musical's own documented history (S3), and finance/development writeups (S4,S5); the detention economics come from independent policy analysis (S6).
- **Exoneration / nuance:** this is mismanagement and bad luck (a genuinely finite one-resource economy), NOT a single embezzler looting a vault — the honest frame is resource curse plus specific vanity bets, not one thief. Surface this so the writer doesn't invent a villain the record doesn't support.

## Myths / misconceptions to bust
- **Myth:** "A bad musical bankrupted Nauru." → **Actually:** the musical (~£2M, F-17) was a rounding error against a A$1.3-billion trust; it's the *emblem* of the mismanagement, not the cause. The real story is a whole portfolio quietly bled dry (F-10,F-11) plus a finite resource running out (F-05,F-18). Bust the single-cause myth; keep the musical as the icon.
- **Myth:** "They just spent it all on luxuries." → **Actually:** much went into ostensibly serious investments (a Melbourne skyscraper, overseas property, an airline) that were simply mismanaged or ran at a loss (F-11,F-13) — the tragedy is that it looked responsible.

## Why this matters (universality): OPTIONAL body texture, never the close
- **The rhyme:** the resource curse — a windfall from one finite thing (oil, a mineral, a boom stock) arriving faster than the institutions to manage it, and being gone before anyone builds anything that lasts. **The spottable tell:** a fortune spent as income rather than treated as a one-time inheritance, and a small body of decision-makers trusting outside salesmen with money they don't understand. Weave only where it lands; do NOT tee up a "this happens every generation" moral. End on the story's own irony (the island consumed for a fortune it then lost), never a lesson.
