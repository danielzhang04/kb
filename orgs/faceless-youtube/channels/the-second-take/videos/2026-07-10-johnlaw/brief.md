### ST-028 — The Gambler Who Invented Paper Money and Broke France
- **Format:** long-form (~13–17 min). *(Shorts skipped for this production batch.)*
- **Provisional angle:** John Law was a Scottish gambler and convicted killer (he fled Britain after a fatal duel) who turned his knack for probability into a theory of money, then talked post-Louis-XIV France into the most radical experiment in financial history: replace scarce gold coin with paper money issued by a national bank he ran. He bundled it with the Mississippi Company, a monopoly on French Louisiana he hyped into the first great stock bubble, briefly became the most powerful man in France, and then the whole thing collapsed, taking the French economy with it. Do the forensic on how paper money and central banking were born, and why the experiment blew up.
- **Payload promise:** The viewer learns that modern paper money, central banking, and the joint-stock bubble were essentially invented/mainstreamed in one 5-year run by one gambler; how his bank-plus-trading-company machine pumped shares roughly tenfold (and coined the word "millionaire"); and how printing money to prop up the share price detonated into collapse and set French finance back a century.
- **Lever:** vindication / forbidden-knowledge — "the money in your wallet was invented by a gambler, and it blew up a country the first time." Register HOT (audacity + hubris).
- **Key questions the video must answer (→ researcher's seed):**
  1. The mechanism: how Law's Banque Générale/Royale issued paper notes, and how he tied the bank to the Mississippi Company so the two propped each other up.
  2. The bubble: the share-price run (~500 to ~10,000 livres), the rue Quincampoix frenzy, the coining of "millionaire," and how much of it was real.
  3. The doom loop: how printing money to support the share price turned into inflation and a run, and the specific trigger of the collapse (1720).
  4. Who Law actually was: the duel/killing, the gambling years, and his genuine economic ideas (visionary vs. charlatan).
  5. The fallout: the ruin of investors, the damage to French royal finances (a line worth checking toward 1789), Law's flight, and his death poor in Venice (1729).
- **Title options:** 1) The Gambler Who Invented Paper Money and Broke France 2) A Convicted Killer Talked France Into Printing Money 3) The Man Who Invented Paper Money, Then Destroyed a Country With It
