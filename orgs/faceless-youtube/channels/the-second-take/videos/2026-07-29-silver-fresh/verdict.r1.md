## Directives (endorsed boss findings, r1 review)

1. **Intro.** Everything before the first turn ("So why silver?" / "So who was this guy?") shrinks
   to one-third to one-half its current ~250 words. Concise hook, make it great: the identity beat
   is sitting unused (a man literally named Bunker, hoarding metal against the government — the
   "Yeah, that's his real name" move), and the first named pull belongs in the door.
2. **Pull density up throughout.** First named pull currently lands at the Ticketmaster line, past
   halfway. Scale figures land as comparisons, never bare: 195M oz and the $7B peak each get a pull
   (195M oz ≈ 6,000 tons — roughly an Eiffel Tower's weight in silver is derivable arithmetic;
   any comparison stays inside the leash). Kill textbook-y stretches; split any sentence that fails
   the one-breath test.
3. **Peak block purity (§3.1).** Strip origin material ($2/oz, 1973, first tens of millions of oz)
   from the climb block; the rewind owns the start. Currently both facts are told twice. Keep the
   "On paper." capper on the peak.
4. **Move the 2%-vs-half reframe to the corner beat**, where the smallness of the deliverable pool
   is what makes the corner work (§2.2). The hook keeps only its woven one-clause qualifier. The
   grandmother's-drawer color and the NONE caps may move with it.
5. **Gloss "corner" at first use** — it is the story's core maneuver and never gets its same-breath
   gloss (§1.1).
6. **IMIC block:** kill the paired parallel aphorism ("The paperwork lived in Bermuda. The silver
   never did.") — one punch max out of the three stacked fragments; the block also earns a joke
   (a shell-company gag is available on the facts).
7. **Rule-change block:** "You could not buy" → "You couldn't buy"; at most one spelled-out date,
   neighbors ride relative; fix the "if that sounds vaguely familiar" antecedent so it points at
   the move, not the rule's name.
8. **Rigged block:** trim the re-explanation of the shorts (already established at the corner
   beat); lean on it instead.
9. **Margin-call block:** opening sentence fails first-listen parse ("multiplied it exactly as
   hard on the way down" — multiplied what?). Plain version: on the way down the same borrowed
   money multiplied the losses.
10. **Tiffany block:** re-aim the irony. Not "ran a day too late to matter" (too late for what?) —
    the ad demanded somebody stop the Hunts, and the market did it the next morning for reasons
    that had nothing to do with the ad (F-42). Keep the tea-sets plant→payoff and the
    of-course-Tiffany-sells-silver beat.
11. **Silver Thursday block:** "five different Wall Street firms" → "half a dozen" (ledger names
    six, "at least five"). Watch the calendar cadence: spelled-out dates are stacking block after
    block in the back half.
12. **Bailout block exits on the punch:** the Fed nuance moves before "Hard money, my ass." — never
    trailing after it (§1.6).
13. **Keeneland:** gloss plainly (the "a sale ring in Kentucky" register); the name may ride along
    or drop.
14. **Ending:** the quote close loses its timestamp walk-back ("And years before the bankruptcy," →
    "Somebody once asked Bunker…"). If it can be told in the Hunts'-version attributed voice the
    script already uses, land a minor-party last laugh (the shorts on the exchange board voted the
    rule and walked away); invent nothing beyond F-15. The silver hoard's ultimate fate stays
    unraised — genuine ledger gap, do not close it.
15. **Keep in substance** (rewording allowed, beat must survive): the GameStop/Robinhood pull at
    the rule change; the margin gloss ("like a house") and Chapter 11 gloss; the Zurich →
    Roman-coins payoff; the quote close as the ending; "None of this is investment advice" aside
