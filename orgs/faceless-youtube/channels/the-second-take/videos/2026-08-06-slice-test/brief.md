## PICKED — The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower — Twice

Chosen for the slice: cleanest single-mechanism story, an A-tier familiarity anchor (the Eiffel
Tower), one named character, a crisp con to reveal, and a built-in twist — everything the pipeline
needs to exercise, with zero dependence on a long-form to land.

- **Format:** standalone short (~60–90s VO, single topic, self-closing payload).
- **Working title:** The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower — Twice
- **Title options:** 1) The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower — Twice · 2) He Sold a Landmark He Didn't
  Own. Then He Did It Again. · 3) The Con Man Who "Auctioned" the Eiffel Tower for Scrap
- **Hook (first ~1.5s, curiosity gap the short pays off):** "In 1925, a man sold the Eiffel Tower.
  He didn't own it — and he got away with it *twice*."
- **Angle / POV:** the vindication cut — *here's the actual mechanism.* Victor Lustig didn't forge a
  deed; he weaponized a real, plausible problem (the tower was aging and expensive to maintain, and
  papers had floated scrapping it), posed as a government official, and ran a **rigged scrap-metal
  auction** for scrap dealers — the "sale" was really a setup to solicit a bribe. The forbidden-
  knowledge payoff is *why it worked twice*: the mark's own shame was the getaway car.
- **Payload (the one thing the viewer walks away with):** a con doesn't need a fake object — Lustig
  sold nothing but *authority + plausibility*, and the reason he escaped is that a defrauded mark who
  feels like a fool won't go to the police. Shame is the con's best security system.
- **Why this one (vs. the shortlist):** highest-scoring, most self-contained, strongest familiar
  anchor, and it stresses the whole idea→short path with a real named character, a clean money
  mechanism, and a twist — ideal for a functionality proof. Not present anywhere in ST-001…ST-028.
- **Why original:** backlog has cons (dos Reis, Kreuger, Pearlman) but none is the Lustig/Eiffel
  authority-and-shame con; this is a different mechanism (no fake asset at all) and a different era/
  character. Not cloning any rival's format.
- **~60–90s scope note:** one narrator, third-person, no character voices/quotes. Money-locked (a
  fraud). Humor dial = **hot-to-medium** (money-absurdity of "selling a landmark," roast the con and
  the greedy scrap dealers — never the audience). Close on a realization, not a lesson. One clean
  payload, closed in-short; the long-form channel link is a bonus in the pinned comment, never the
  payoff.
- **Key beats (≈5, tight):**
  1. **Cold-open claim (~0–8s):** 1925, Paris — a man sold the Eiffel Tower for scrap. He didn't own it.
  2. **The plausible setup (~8–30s):** the tower was aging and pricey to maintain; newspapers mused
     about tearing it down. Lustig, posing as a deputy government official, secretly gathered the
     city's scrap-metal dealers and "auctioned" 7,000 tons of iron — official-looking, hush-hush.
  3. **The real trick (~30–55s):** the auction was cover. He singled out the eager winner and
     solicited a **bribe** to "secure the deal," took the cash, and vanished to Austria.
  4. **The twist / re-arm (~55–75s):** the victim was too humiliated to go to police — so weeks later
     Lustig came **back** and ran the identical con on a new set of dealers.
  5. **Dry close (~75–90s):** he sold nothing but authority and everyone's own greed — and the
     silence of a mark too proud to admit he'd been played.
- **Verify-before-script (facts to confirm; no live lookup this slice):** year (1925) and second
  attempt; Lustig's posed title ("Deputy Director-General of the Ministry of Posts/Telegraphs" per
  common accounts); tonnage/valuation figures if stated on-screen; that the first mark did not report
  it. Two reputable sources required per channel YMYL gate before scripting.

- **Candidate long-form tie-in (bonus, not required):** a future "great impostors / authority cons"
