# Self-diagnosis — does this research.md scaffold a STORY or just ledger facts?

**Verdict: a strong, well-sourced fact-ledger with a *skeletal* narrative layer bolted on. It gives the
writer an accurate spine and a payload, but under-provisions the connective tissue — cast/motive, human
stakes, the second-person "why this matters," and telling ironic detail — that turns a mechanism into a
story. It would produce a *correct* video that risks feeling like an explainer, which is exactly what the
dna forbids ("we tell the STORY and teach through it, NOT a how-X-works explainer").**

## What it does well (real scaffolding present)

- **Story SPINE exists and is causal.** The `Narrative spine` isn't a topic list — it's an 8-beat arc with
  a turn (beat 5: "it was never the bear — it was the next buyer") and a withheld final-20% payload
  (beats 7–8). Cold-open / mid-re-arm / withheld-fine-print are all explicitly nominated. This matches the
  dna's recurring structure and the niche "Autopsy" template.
- **Turning points are marked, not buried:** the 1995 first "retirement," the eBay/price-guide reframe, the
  1998 peak, the Sept-1999 "retire everything" stunt, the collapse. The writer can find the pivots.
- **Ironic/telling details are seeded** — the 1996 Forbes "empty shelves" quote (the con documented in
  real time), the 50¢-to-vote stunt, and the kicker (billionaire, hid $80M, pleaded guilty, *no jail*).
  These carry the vindication lever.
- **Analogy-as-indictment candidates** (musical chairs, scoreboard-with-no-game) teach and indict in one
  line — the §5b job done right.
- **Myths-to-bust section** directly loads the vindication payload ("they were never rare").
- **Sourcing discipline is genuine:** atomic facts, IDs, dated, two-source on hard numbers, the tax case
  traced to DOJ/7th-Circuit and phrased as *what the record alleges/states* (defamation-safe), and honest
  low-confidence flags on the two thin colour numbers instead of laundering them.

## Where it reads as ledger, not story (the honest gaps)

1. **Cast + motive is missing.** There is *no protagonist psychology* in the ledger. Ty Warner appears only
   as an agent of tactics (he "retired," he "limited," he "deposited"). *Why* — the obsessive
   perfectionist, the ego, the control freak who removed his designers' names — is absent. I actively
   *ignored* his biography as "mood, not payload." That was a cost-discipline call that removed the exact
   thing that makes a rise-and-fall a *character* story. A ledger can't dramatize a boardroom lie or a
   2-line exchange (the dna's default beat device) if it never captured the man.

2. **Human stakes are absent.** The ledger has crime-in-aggregate ([F-14]) but no *named victim*, no "the
   mother behind on her mortgage buying wheelbarrows of bears," no lost college fund. Those are the
   second-person gut-punch beats — and I cut them under "ignore: nostalgia/tangents." Result: the stakes
   are institutional and abstract (sales figures), not human. The reference leaned entirely on these; our
   dossier can't write them because it didn't collect them.

3. **No second-person "why this matters."** The dna wants second person "where it lands." The dossier is
   pure third-person mechanism; it never seeds a "you'd have done this too / here's the tell you can spot in
   the next bubble (NFTs, tulips)" bridge that makes the viewer implicated. The universal-bubble callout
   (tulips/NFTs) — an obvious, cheap "why this matters to you" beat — isn't even in the ledger.

4. **Telling detail is thin outside the money beats.** The genuinely *story* details — the designer whose
   name Warner erased, his own copycat-paranoia warning note, collectors following delivery trucks — are
   the texture that makes it feel witnessed rather than summarized. The ledger has the skeleton of the con
   but little of its lived absurdity.

5. **The scaffolding is *asserted*, not *stocked*.** The spine says beat 2 = "the product was real," but the
   ledger under-supplies the sensory/character facts a writer needs to *dramatize* it; the writer will
   either invent (violating the leash) or narrate the mechanism flatly (the explainer failure mode).

## Root cause (a process finding, not just this run)

The two disciplines that make the ledger *trustworthy* — "ignore the saturated nostalgia," "cut mood, keep
payload," cost-bounded LIGHT tier — are in **direct tension** with narrative richness. They prune exactly
the human/character connective tissue that separates The Second Take's "story, told" doctrine from a How-X-
Works explainer. The researcher optimized for *defensible payload density* and got it; it did **not** have
an explicit instruction to harvest **cast, motive, human-cost stakes, and 2–3 telling anecdotes as
first-class ledger items**. The template's `Narrative spine` section invites a spine but the `Fact ledger`
schema rewards atomic checkable *numbers*, so story-fuel facts (a quote, a scene, a victim) get deprioritized
or ignored.

## Concrete fix (for the skill, not just this dossier)

- Add a required ledger sub-class: **Character & scene facts** — protagonist motive/trait, ≥1 named human
  victim, and 2–3 telling/ironic anecdotes — sourced like any other fact, so the writer can dramatize
  without inventing. (Here: Warner erasing his designers' names; a named collector who lost a college fund;
  collectors chasing delivery trucks.)
- Add a **"Why this matters (2nd person / universality)"** line to the spine (the tulip/NFT bridge; the
  spottable tell) so the dossier seeds the implicating beat the dna wants.
- Loosen the "ignore mood" rule to "ignore mood that carries **no** payload" — human cost and character
  *are* payload for a story channel; they're the emotional half of vindication.
