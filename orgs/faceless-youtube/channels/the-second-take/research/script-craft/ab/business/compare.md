## B. Where OURS is genuinely GOOD (be fair)

1. **The forbidden-knowledge thesis is sharper and better front-loaded than theirs.** Ours names the
   whole engine in the first 30s: *"The con was the shortage, and the shortage is the whole engine…
   Sell the sense of not-enough instead of an actual short supply, and a five-dollar plush animal
   starts trading like a stock."* Magnates buries its thesis in a soft mid-video aside (*"there was a
   slight Ponzi scheme element"*). For our locked **vindication** lever, ours states the payoff-idea
   cleaner.

2. **Analogy-as-indictment is a strength — arguably better than the reference.** Three teach-and-indict
   analogies, all §5b-grade: *"a bakery swearing it will never bake your favorite loaf again, standing
   in front of an oven that never shuts off"*; *"a scoreboard with no game underneath it"*; *"musical
   chairs, where the music is new buyers."* Magnates' analogies (*"like a lottery ticket,"* *"diamonds
   on the box"*) are good but looser. This is our clearest craft win.

3. **The make-vs-flip money yardstick — the reference's #5 lesson — ours already does.** *"pennies to
   make. It sold for five dollars… more than five thousand"*; *"around thirty dollars on average, six
   times the register price"*; *"That's just Ty selling toys to stores. None of the resale."* Internal
   cost-to-price ratio, no bare big numbers. Fully matches char-magnates technique #10.

4. **The turn is crisp.** *"The toy was never the asset. The next person's belief was the asset… He'd
   dealt himself the only guaranteed seat at the table."* Clean mid-video re-arm.

5. **Deflate-don't-resolve close is excellent** and matches doctrine §1d-V E: *"The man who sold the
   shortage walked. The collectors, the ones who believed the bears were rare, got to keep the bears."*
   Dry button, no "and that's why," hard cut. As strong as Magnates' close.

6. **Sourcing rigor is HIGHER than theirs, and that is not a flaw to fix.** Magnates asserts *"25 and
   65 cents each,"* *"10% of all eBay sales,"* *"$22,000,"* *"$11,000"* for the Spook bear — numbers our
   researcher deliberately *declined* to state as unsourced ([F-03][F-09][F-10] flagged low). Some of
   their extra vividness is borrowed from figures we honestly couldn't verify. **The fix is never to
   adopt their unsourced numbers** — it's to source-and-add the *character/victim* material that IS
   documentable.

## C. The 3–5 highest-leverage fixes (split researcher vs writer)

**The core finding:** ours is a *lucid essay about a machine*; theirs is a *character story with human
wreckage*. Ours wins on epistemic clarity, analogy, and rigor; it loses on PEOPLE. The dominant gaps
(A1, A3, A6, A7) are all the same root cause — the ledger pruned cast, victims, and lived detail as
"mood, not payload" — which is a **researcher** problem, and it aligns exactly with the researcher's own
diagnosis.

1. **[RESEARCHER — highest leverage] Add a `Cast, motive & human-cost` ledger sub-class.** Three
   required, all sourceable: (a) Warner's documented psychology (perfectionism, copycat-paranoia, the
   erased-designer-names control move); (b) ≥1 **named/sourced victim** (the cerebral-palsy mother; the
   actor's lost college fund); (c) 2–3 **witnessed-absurd details** (following delivery trucks; McDonald's
   disguises; "never let a child touch a beanie"). Fixes A1, A3, A6 at once. This is the biggest single
   uplift and closes the "reads like an explainer, not a story" hole the dna forbids.

2. **[RESEARCHER] Add a `Why this matters (universality)` spine line** — the tulip-mania + NFT rhyme +
   the one spottable tell of the next bubble. Cheap, high-value, currently absent from the ledger. Fixes A7.

3. **[WRITER] Re-shape the middle as an explicit assemble-the-machine spine.** The levers are already in
   the ledger; stop narrating them smoothly in one pass — isolate each on its own beat with a causal
   signpost ("here's the lever… and here's why it's genius"), so the viewer watches the trick get built
   part by part. Fixes A4. (No new research needed.)

4. **[WRITER] End 2–3 sections on a withheld loop, and dramatize one beat as a micro-scene.** Convert
   summary section-ends into forward-pulling cliffhangers (A5); once the researcher supplies cast/quotes,
   stage one beat (the "retirement" con landing, or a collector's line) as a 2–4 line exchange instead of
   narration (A8, rubric dims 3/6).

5. **[WRITER, do-now-from-current-material] Seed one Warner-character line + one second-person bridge**
   even before new research lands — the close can add "you've watched this before: tulips, dot-com, NFTs"
   from general knowledge to stop-gap A7.

**Transferable craft (portable, weight heavily):** villain-psychology front-load · mechanism-as-parts-list
assembly with per-lever signposts · cold testimonial open · named human-cost victim beat · witnessed-absurd
telling detail · chapter-gate withheld loops · universality/rhyme bridge · dialogue-reenactment micro-scene.

**Non-portable (do NOT import):** the 31-min runtime and its two sponsor/channel-system reads (Magnates
monetization, not our format or band); the explicit next-video Ponzi teaser (series-retention move we don't
run yet); and their unsourced color numbers (25–65¢, "10% of eBay," $22,000, $11,000) — our researcher was
*right* to omit these; matching their vividness must come from sourced character/victim material, not from
laundering their thin numbers.
