## Research plan (what was and wasn't chased)

**Questions sharpened:**
1. Exactly how much was in / taken from each bank, and how much was recovered? (the payload rests here)
2. Was the "10-minute time lock" a real invented stall, by whom, and did the gang wait? (the turning point)
3. What did the gang net from prior train robberies vs. the heat ($ reward) they drew? (the incentive math)
4. Named motive: fame (Bob's Jesse James line) and/or funding an exit from the territory (Heck Thomas)?
5. Precise casualty roster on both sides; Emmett's wounds, sentence, pardon, later life.

**Chase list:** dollar figures per bank + recovery; the Ball time-lock ruse; prior-robbery haul + reward amounts; Emmett's own stated reason; the four dead defenders by name/trade; the "hometown, recognized instantly" mechanism.

**Ignore list:** minute-by-minute gunfight choreography (who shot whom from which window — mood, not payload); the "Death Alley" tourism angle; disputed fake-beard theatrics beyond one line; broader Old-West outlawry survey. This is ONE money story, not a Dalton-family biography.

**Source bar (LIGHT/history):** town/museum primary-adjacent records (Coffeyville, Dalton Defenders Museum), U.S. National Park Service teaching materials, established history outlets (HISTORY, True West, Legends of America), encyclopedic (Wikipedia, Encyclopedia of the Great Plains). Numbers cross-checked across ≥2 independent surfaces before `high`.

## Fact ledger  ← the scriptwriter may state ONLY what appears here

- **[F-01]** On October 5, 1892, five members of the Dalton Gang attempted to rob two banks in Coffeyville, Kansas, simultaneously. — *Src:* S1, S3, S4 · *Conf:* high · *Note:* the raid's defining fact.
- **[F-02]** The two targets sat across the street from each other: the C.M. Condon & Co. bank and the First National Bank. — *Src:* S1, S2 · *Conf:* high
- **[F-03]** Bob and Emmett Dalton entered the First National Bank; Grat Dalton, Bill Powers, and Dick Broadwell entered the Condon bank. — *Src:* S1, S5 · *Conf:* high
- **[F-04]** Coffeyville was the Daltons' hometown; townspeople recognized the men despite disguises. — *Src:* S2, S6 · *Conf:* high · *Note:* the fake-beard "disguise" detail is widely repeated; treat the theatrics as med, the recognition as high.
- **[F-05]** Before Coffeyville the gang were train robbers across Oklahoma Territory in 1891–92 (Perry, Orlando, Red Rock, Adair, Pryor Creek). — *Src:* S4, S7 · *Conf:* high
- **[F-06]** Prior train jobs netted roughly $10,000 from Missouri-Kansas-Texas trains and about $17,000 from a July 1892 robbery at Pryor Creek. — *Src:* S7 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* figures single-threaded in this pass; directionally consistent but spot-check exact amounts at lock.
- **[F-07]** A reward of about $5,000 was placed on each Dalton's head after the train robberies. — *Src:* S7 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* verify exact figure/issuer at lock.
- **[F-08]** Bob Dalton, the leader, reportedly wanted to "beat anything Jesse James ever did — rob two banks at once, in broad daylight." — *Src:* S4, S7 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* a quotation attributed to Bob in secondary sources; phrase as "reportedly said," not verbatim gospel.
- **[F-09]** Emmett Dalton said years later that relentless pursuit by Deputy U.S. Marshal Heck Thomas was a key reason for the raid — a big haul was meant to fund the gang's exit from the territory. — *Src:* S7 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* Emmett's own later account; self-serving-source caveat (he was building a redemption narrative).
- **[F-10]** At the Condon bank, cashier Charley (C.M.) Ball told the robbers the safe was on a time lock that could not open until about 9:45–10:00, i.e. roughly 10 minutes out. — *Src:* S1, S8 · *Conf:* high
- **[F-11]** The time lock was a lie — the safe had no such lock and was openable; the actual time was about 9:40. Grat reportedly said "I'll wait." — *Src:* S1, S8 · *Conf:* high · *Note:* the load-bearing irony; consistent across the town record and secondary sources.
- **[F-12]** Ball's stall gave townspeople the minutes they needed to arm themselves at the Isham (hardware) store beside the banks. — *Src:* S1, S2 · *Conf:* high · *Note:* the hardware store's adjacency is the mechanism the whole reversal turns on.
- **[F-13]** At the First National, Bob and Emmett filled a sack with roughly $20,000; at the Condon the robbers grabbed about $1,500 in loose cash. — *Src:* S1, S8 · *Conf:* med · *Note:* ~$20k First National is repeated across sources; the $1,500 loose-cash Condon figure is single-threaded — spot-check.
- **[F-14]** The banks were robbed of roughly $25,000 total; after the books were balanced the Condon was $20 short and the First National $1.98 over — i.e. essentially all the money was recovered. — *Src:* S1, S8 · *Conf:* high · *Note:* the payload figure — the gang died for a net take of ~$20.
- **[F-15]** The gunfight lasted about 12 minutes (some sources say under 15). — *Src:* S1, S6 · *Conf:* high
- **[F-16]** Four gang members were killed: Bob Dalton, Grat Dalton, Bill Powers, and Dick Broadwell. — *Src:* S1, S3, S5 · *Conf:* high
- **[F-17]** Four Coffeyville defenders were killed: Marshal Charles Connelly (46, also a schoolteacher), George Cubine (36, shoemaker), Charles Brown (59, shoemaker), and Lucius Baldwin (23, store clerk). — *Src:* S1, S9 · *Conf:* high
- **[F-18]** Three defenders (Cubine, Brown, Baldwin) were killed near the hardware store; Marshal Connelly was killed in what became known as "Death Alley." — *Src:* S9 · *Conf:* high
- **[F-19]** Emmett Dalton was the sole raider to survive, with about 23 gunshot wounds. — *Src:* S1, S3, S5 · *Conf:* high
- **[F-20]** In March 1893 Emmett pleaded guilty to second-degree murder and was sentenced to life at the Kansas penitentiary in Lansing. — *Src:* S5 · *Conf:* high
- **[F-21]** Emmett was paroled in June 1907 for treatment of his Coffeyville-injured arm and pardoned in November 1907 by Kansas Governor Edward W. Hoch — roughly 14 years served. — *Src:* S5 · *Conf:* high
- **[F-22]** After release Emmett capitalized on his notoriety as an author and actor, playing himself in the 1918 silent film "Beyond the Law," and later worked in construction/real estate near Los Angeles. — *Src:* S5 · *Conf:* high · *Note:* the ironic "outlaw becomes his own brand" coda.
- **[F-23]** Emmett later claimed he never fired a shot during the raid. — *Src:* S7 · *Conf:* low · *Note:* self-serving; uncorroborated; present as his claim only, do not assert as fact.

## Narrative spine (recommended section outline — the writer's outline seed)

1. **Cold open — the ~$20 payout** — the whole raid, priced: four robbers dead, four townsmen dead, for a net take that balanced to about twenty dollars. Draws on [F-14], [F-16], [F-17].
2. **Who they were / the incentive setup** — ex-lawmen turned train robbers, $5,000 on each head, prior jobs that paid ($10k, $17k) but drew unsurvivable heat. Draws on [F-05], [F-06], [F-07].
3. **The bet** — Bob's "beat Jesse James" fame play PLUS Emmett's later "we needed to fund an exit from Heck Thomas" — the reputation/cash-flow logic behind two banks at once. Draws on [F-08], [F-09], [F-01].
4. **The home-field problem** — robbing your own hometown means zero anonymity; recognized on arrival. Draws on [F-04], [F-03].
5. **The stall that killed them** — a cashier invents a time lock; the gang *waits*; the town arms up next door. Draws on [F-10], [F-11], [F-12].
6. **The 12 minutes** — the reversal and the toll, kept tight. Draws on [F-15], [F-16], [F-17], [F-18].
7. **The withheld fine print** — the money was all recovered; and the one survivor turned the disaster into a paid personal brand. Draws on [F-14], [F-19]–[F-22].

- **Cold-open candidate:** the ~$20 net take against eight dead — [F-14] + [F-16]/[F-17].
- **Mid-video re-arm (the turn):** the time-lock was a lie the gang chose to believe, and the belief cost them the escape window — [F-11]/[F-12].
- **Withheld "fine print" (final 20%):** every dollar was recovered ([F-14]); the sole survivor monetized the failure into books and film ([F-22]).

## Analogies that teach AND indict (§5b candidates)

- The double-bank plan is like doubling your position size to make back losses faster: the "upside" scales, but so does the thing that kills you — here, the certainty of being recognized. Grounds [F-01]/[F-04].
- Believing the teller's time-lock is like trusting a counterparty's word on a number you could check yourself in five seconds — they waited on an unverified claim and it cost them everything. Grounds [F-11].
- A robbery with no way to *keep* the cash is a trade with a great entry and no exit — the take was never the problem, the extraction was. Grounds [F-13]/[F-14].

## Myths / misconceptions to bust

- **Myth:** the Daltons were unlucky — a good plan spoiled by a mob. → **Actually:** the plan was negative-expected-value from the start (hometown = guaranteed recognition; a fake time lock they never checked; no way to hold the cash they grabbed). They lost on the math, not the luck. ([F-04], [F-11], [F-14]) — strong vindication payload.
- **Myth:** they got away with ~$25,000. → **Actually:** essentially all of it was recovered — the net take balanced to about $20. ([F-14]) — the "the part they don't tell you" beat.

## Open questions, gaps & hedges

- **Insurance / why the town fought to the death.** The reference framing ("no insurance, stolen gold is just gone, so the town fought") is intuitive but NOT sourced here. Federal deposit insurance (FDIC) did not exist until 1933, but whether these specific 1892 banks/depositors were privately bonded is unverified. Treat the "town had every incentive because the loss was final" line as reasoned inference, not sourced fact — hedge or drop.
- **Exact prior-robbery dollar figures [F-06] and the $5,000 reward [F-07]** are med-confidence / single-threaded in this light pass — spot-check against a primary or a second reputable source before the script states them as hard numbers.
- **The Condon $1,500 loose-cash figure [F-13]** is single-threaded; the ~$20,000 First National figure is well-repeated.
- **Bob's Jesse James quote [F-08] and Emmett's Heck-Thomas motive [F-09]** are attributed/recollected, not documentary. Phrase as "reportedly"/"Emmett later said," never as flat fact — Emmett is a self-interested narrator ([F-23] shows him minimizing his own role).
- **Frank Dalton / the brothers' lawman past.** The reference says an older brother (Frank), a deputy marshal, was killed, pushing the younger Daltons outlaw. Plausible and commonly told, but NOT verified in this pass — research before using.
- **A sixth brother, Bill Dalton,** reportedly waited with relief horses, gave up, went home, and was later killed as the last Dalton outlaw (1894). Commonly told; unverified here — flag before use.
