## B. What OURS did well (be fair — our dry register is a deliberate, different instrument)

1. **Payload density crushes theirs.** Nearly every one of our lines carries a fact, name, number, or
   mechanism (rubric dim 1 = 2). Theirs has whole zero-fact performance stretches (the entire
   Victorian-politeness retreat exchange teaches nothing). For a YMYL channel judged on payload, ours is
   the correct trade.
2. **The MONEY thesis is genuinely ours and superior for the channel.** "It is a money story wearing a
   gunfight's clothes… they died because they were bad at math." The §5b analogies that teach AND indict —
   "doubling your position to win back your losses faster," "trusting a counterparty's word on a number you
   could check yourself in five seconds," "a trade with a perfect entry and no exit" — execute the
   vindication lever. HeyHistorically has none of this; it's a funny retelling, not an argument.
3. **Human cost handled with humor OFF (§1d-V E tonal modulation).** We name the four dead defenders with
   ages and trades — "A teacher, two shoemakers, and a clerk, dead in their own street on a Wednesday
   morning." Theirs plays deaths for laughs ("received judgment," "mowed down," the headshot gag). Ours is
   the correct, more resonant call for YMYL and a real craft win.
4. **Withheld fine-print reveal, tightly sequenced.** "They had grabbed roughly twenty-five thousand
   dollars… Then they lost all of it… About twenty dollars, for eight lives." The payload sits in the final
   20% (rubric dim 8) — cleaner than theirs.
5. **Short-punch discipline is rubric-clean.** "It was a lie." lands a payload after a long setup — a
   witty/factual landing, never portent staccato (§1d-V short-punch rule).
6. **Deflate-don't-resolve close.** "The only thing they ever really stole was twelve minutes. And most of
   those, they gave away." Dry button, no CTA — matches their "Bloody children." for quality.

## C. The 3–5 highest-leverage fixes

**1. [RESEARCHER · TRANSFERABLE] Source the "why the town fought" / finality-of-loss motive.**
Absent from research → writer forced to drop → the town's ferocity has no engine ("defending their own
homes" is generic filler). Go one search deeper: 1892 had no federal deposit insurance (FDIC = 1933); were
these specific banks/depositors bonded? Was there a contemporaneous town account of *why* they armed so
fast? This is simultaneously the missing emotional core AND a native money beat (irreversible loss) — the
single most on-brand thing HeyHistorically has that we don't. Researcher's own diagnosis already concedes
this hole.

**2. [WRITER · TRANSFERABLE] Scene the hinge instead of narrating it.**
The Charley Ball time-lock bluff is the story's turning point and we deliver it as flat narration ("He told
them the safe was on a time lock… he said"). Play it as a 2–4 line insider-vs-mark micro-scene (rubric dim
6). GUARDRAIL: Ball's exact words are unsourced ([F-10] is a mechanism, not a quote) — so paraphrase/hedge
the dialogue, do NOT invent verbatim period quotes on a YMYL channel. Keep the one sourced line, "I'll
wait," as the button. Transfer the *framing* (dialogue-scene the hinge), never the joke density.

**3. [WRITER · TRANSFERABLE] Turn the single irony paragraph into a genuine cross-cut.**
We state the "waiting on a lie while the town arms next door" irony exactly once. On a pre-spoiled ending
(our cold open gives away the outcome), this cross-cut IS the tension engine — cut between the gang's
stillness and the street quietly filling, the way char-heist F3 marks as explicitly transferable. Low cost,
high retention payoff; no new facts required.

**4. [RESEARCHER · TRANSFERABLE] Develop two characters the event hands us for free.**
(a) Charley Ball — the cashier who bluffs an armed gang is the story's hero and cleanest irony, currently
one clause; source his name/words/fate. (b) The ex-lawmen origin (Frank Dalton killed → the brothers turn
outlaw), currently dropped as unverified — source or kill it. Both give the writer a *person* and a real
"why," which is what separates our competent ledger from a landed story.

**5. [WRITER · DO-NOT-ADOPT marker — protect the register].**
Explicitly REFUSE, on every future pass: the comedian-narrator persona ("I swear to God… how do you keep
doing this?"), anachronism gags ("omega brain," the Victorian-politeness firefight), deaths played for
laughs, and the ~2.5–3.5 joke/min density. Our dry-understatement register + humor-OFF-for-the-human-cost
is the CORRECT YMYL instrument (§1d-V D four locks; char-heist "the transferable craft is the FRAMING, not
the FREQUENCY"). The lift we want from HeyHistorically is scening, cross-cut, and character — not comedy.
