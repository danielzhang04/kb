# Picked-idea brief — The Coffeyville Raid (stands in for idea-generator output)

- **Channel:** The Second Take · **Niche:** business-money · **Slug:** 2026-07-06-coffeyville-double-heist
- **Lever:** Vindication / forbidden-knowledge · **Register:** plain-concrete-specific, dry-smart insider

## Subject (independently identified from the reference only for scope)
The **Dalton Gang's simultaneous double bank robbery in Coffeyville, Kansas, on October 5, 1892** — five
men tried to rob the C.M. Condon & Co. bank and the First National Bank at the same time, in their own
hometown, in broad daylight, and were shot to pieces by armed townspeople within ~12 minutes.

## Angle (The Second Take frame — money story, not a Wild-West war story)
This is a **money story dressed as a gunfight.** The gang didn't die because they were bad shots — they
died because of a **cash-flow problem and a bad ROI bet.** Frame it as the 19th-century version of a
doomed "double or nothing" play: they were already America's most-wanted after a string of train
robberies that paid poorly relative to the heat they drew, so the leader bet everything on one
reputation-making score that would out-do Jesse James AND fund an exit from the territory. The "hook"
isn't the beards — it's the **incentive math** that made robbing two banks at once look rational to them.

## Hook promise (cold-open, <5s, that the video pays off)
A number or a stark claim: five men walked into two banks with a plan to steal more in ten minutes than
they'd made in years of train robbery — and it took the town **twelve minutes** to kill four of them. Or:
"The most famous bank robbery in the Old West failed because the robbers believed a lie a bank teller made
up on the spot." Pick the sourced one.

## Payload promise (the "forbidden-knowledge" the system never spells out)
The part nobody tells you: **the money never worked.** Even the bag they did grab from First National
($20k+) they never kept; the raid was a **negative-expected-value bet the whole time** — high fixed cost
(certain recognition in their hometown), a fake "time-lock" that cost them their escape window, and no
insurance meant the town had every incentive to fight to the death because *stolen cash was simply gone.*
The forbidden-knowledge payoff: the Daltons weren't undone by bad luck, they were undone by **doing the
math wrong on risk vs. reward** — the same error that blows up modern over-leveraged bets.

## Key questions the video must answer (research seed)
1. **Why two banks at once?** What was the actual strategic/financial reasoning (fame, or funding an exit
   from the territory to escape Marshal Heck Thomas)? What did Emmett say later?
2. **The money numbers:** How much was actually in each bank / taken / recovered? How much had their prior
   train robberies netted vs. the heat they generated? Were the banks insured (why did the town fight)?
3. **The fake time-lock:** Did a bank employee really invent a "the safe is on a ten-minute timer" story,
   and did the gang wait? Source it or flag it as legend.
4. **Who died / survived, and the toll:** gang deaths, townspeople/marshal deaths, Emmett's 23 wounds,
   his prison term + pardon + later life.
5. **The reputation economics:** why did being a "wanted-poster celebrity" push them toward a bigger,
   dumber score? (the vindication/incentive spine)
6. **What's myth vs. documented** — fake beards, "recognized instantly," the timer, exact quotes.

## Familiarity anchor (universal §3a)
Lead against **Jesse James / the Wild West bank robbery** (the thing everyone already "knows"), and let the
Coffeyville specifics + the money math be the payoff.
