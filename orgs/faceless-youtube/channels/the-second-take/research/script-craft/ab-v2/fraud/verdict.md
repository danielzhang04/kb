## 2. Did the gaps CLOSE? (gap-by-gap, quoted)

### (a) Verification-as-content — **CLOSED (biggest single win).**
- **v1 (flat):** "An on-chain analysis firm called Bubblemaps traced the wallets behind LIBRA and found
  they were linked to the wallets that had launched MELANIA." → a stated finding, one firm, no proving.
- **v2 (staged):** "the proof didn't come from one place — it came from three, separately. Bubblemaps
  traced roughly eighty-seven million… Chainalysis, cited by Reuters, put it around ninety-nine
  million… Nansen ran the whole order book and reached the same place from the other direction…
  **Different firms. Different methods. Same finding.**" And the match is staged *before* the
  accusation: "The same wallet stood behind both launches… **The match lands before the accusation
  does. That's the tell that this was a factory, not an accident.**"
- **Verdict: genuinely improved, not overclaimed.** This is the exact Coffeezilla move ("two
  independent pieces of evidence") ported to a faceless format via forensic firms instead of an
  interview. The corroboration-counted-out-loud + proof-before-accusation are both present.

### (b) Mechanism-assembly — **CLOSED.**
- **v1 (one-pass):** "That move has a name. It is called sniping." … "LIBRA ran on something called
  single-sided liquidity. Picture a cash till that only opens from one side." — levers present but
  narrated smoothly.
- **v2 (built lever-by-lever):** "So how does the machine actually work? **It's built one piece at a
  time, and the first piece is a word: sniping. Here's the crucial part.**" … "**Second lever — and
  this is the one that makes it look clean.** It's called single-sided liquidity." Plus the "one tell
  gives it away" fee beat.
- **Verdict: improved.** Explicit signposts; the viewer feels the trick assemble. Textbook.

### (c) Character / motive — **CLOSED (villain), PARTIAL (see §5).**
- **v1:** "Kelsier Ventures, and its CEO Hayden Davis, were the mechanics in the back." — an org-chart
  role.
- **v2:** "**Now meet the man who built the machine.** Hayden Davis… **Davis is not a man who hides.**
  … he sat down for an interview and, instead of denying the mechanics, he walked through them. His
  worldview, roughly in his own words: **the people who get mad are the people who aren't insiders.**"
- **Verdict: improved.** A rendered villain with a documented worldview/motive, front-loaded, and the
  worldview line is set up to pay off at the close ("that line comes back at the end") — good craft.

### (d) Named human stakes — **PARTIALLY closed / honestly SOFT.**
- **v1:** "Investors, by the most common estimate, lost around two hundred and fifty-one million
  dollars between them." — abstract aggregate.
- **v2:** names **Dave Portnoy** ("His paper loss ran somewhere between five and six million dollars")
  and humanises the aggregate: "**Around seventy-four thousand people put real money into LIBRA and
  lost it… Rent money, savings, a first crypto trade that somebody's brother-in-law swore was safe.**"
- **Verdict: improved but the rubric's actual target is only half-met.** Dim 15 wants "≥1 *named,
  sourced* human-cost victim landed straight, humor off." Portnoy is named+sourced but he is an
  **insider who got made whole** — used for the irony cross-cut, *not* as straight human cost. The
  straight human cost (74k) is landed humor-off but is an **unnamed aggregate**. So the "named + straight
  human-cost victim" is split across two people and neither fully satisfies. **The writer's self-flag on
  aggregate human-cost being soft is correct.** I still score dim 15 = 2 (a rendered villain + *a* named
  stake exists, per the rubric's "2 =" wording), but this is the one genuine remaining hole.

### (e) Scene-as-default — **IMPROVED.**
- **v1:** the closest thing to a scene is the POV "Say you are one of them. You see the tweet, you buy
  in early, you feel clever." — otherwise pure narration + analogy.
- **v2:** adds sourced staged beats — "**Novelli's mother and sister were filmed in May of 2025

- **Verdict: improved** via hedged/paraphrased scene (per grammar §5, no invented quotes). Not yet a
  *voiced 2–4 line exchange* — it's staged imagery + reported action, not dialogue. Real gain, not full
  reach.

### (f) Universality / 2nd-person bridge — **CLOSED.**
- **v1:** no historical-rhyme callout; closes on the casino image only.
- **v2:** "**Go back far enough and it's tulips in Holland, dot-com in the nineties, NFTs in 2021 —

## 5. Honest verdict

**The upgrade worked for this topic.** +4 points (25 → 29), and — the part that matters for validating
the fix — **all four points landed on the exact weighted dimensions the grammar targeted** (6 scene,
13 mechanism-assembly, 14 verification-staged, 15 character/named-stakes). The gaps the original A/B
diagnosed (verification-as-content, mechanism-assembly, character/motive, scene-as-default,
universality) closed with quotable, non-overclaimed improvements, and the fix did it **without eroding
a single protected strength** — payload, forbidden-knowledge thesis, analogy-as-indictment,
person→system landing, and defamation discipline all held. Staging added ~50s and one slightly sweeping
close line, but no dilution, no weakened hedging, no invented factual color.

**What's still weak (verifying the writer's two self-flags):**
- **Aggregate human-cost / named victim (SOFT — confirmed).** The one genuine remaining hole. The
  rubric wants a *named, sourced, straight* human-cost victim; v2 delivers a named *insider* (Portnoy,
  used for irony) plus an *unnamed* straight aggregate (74k). Real people with faces are still missing.
  If the ledger has a sourceable named ordinary loser, the writer should land one straight, humor off.
- **Irony cross-cut (SOFT — confirmed, but adequate).** Portnoy-made-whole vs 74k-with-a-class-action
  ("Two completely different casinos, depending on which side of the rope") is a strong *single
  juxtaposition*, not an interleaved cross-cut that carries tension across the act. It works; it isn't a
  standout. Reasonable to leave, but it's the weakest of the new beats.
- Minor: verbatim villain-trap and self-skepticism beat are still un-ported (both partly by faceless/
  defamation necessity).

**Bottom line: PASS.** v2 is a real, targeted, non-cosmetic improvement that validates the researcher-
extraction + writer-staging fix end-to-end on the fraud topic. It clears the publishable bar with room
(29/30, no 0s, no 0s on 13/14). The two soft spots the writer flagged are real but narrow, and both are
"reach further," not "regression." The pipeline change is proven for this lever.
