## Crayon Capital — Petrodollar Explained

**Art register:** WallStreetBets "crayon" / MS-Paint-doodle aesthetic. Thick wobbly black
outlines, flat fills, deliberately crude. Humans are round bald stick-figures (dot eyes, small
mouths); nations are personified two ways — **Uncle Sam** (stars-&-stripes top hat) for the USA,
and **countryballs** (flag-textured spheres with tiny arms) for everyone else. A tight recurring
prop vocabulary carries the whole video: an **oil barrel with a flame/oil-drop logo**, **green
cash piles/mountains**, **gold $ coins** (and ¥/€/£/₽/₿ coin variants for rivals), a
**stars-&-stripes top hat**. Humor is almost entirely in the **ironic on-screen captions and dry
speech bubbles**, not slapstick. Notably, the channel switches to a **cleaner "corporate
infographic" register** (2.5D, soft shadows, real logos) for the 2–3 shots that show the actual
*mechanism* (the recycling loop, the reserve-share network) — a deliberate contrast that signals
"this is the real machinery."

### B. Recurring narration→visual patterns (NON-literal moves called out)

- **Nations are never maps — they're personified.** USA = Uncle Sam (top hat) *everywhere*; other
  nations = flag-**countryballs** or a single caricatured leader (Kissinger, the Saudi King,
  Saddam, Gaddafi, Maduro, Putin, Trump). A treaty between states becomes **two flag-balls shaking
  hands** (2:56, 9:47). [NON-literal, the workhorse move]
- **Abstract systems → a single labeled physical object.** "Trust" → an award **badge** (0:49);
  "SWIFT" → a labeled **globe** (9:03); the whole petrodollar mechanism → a rotating **3-node loop
  diagram** (4:15). [NON-literal]
- **Being cut off / sanctioned → physical disconnection.** "Financial internet / the grid" → a
  **plug yanked from a socket** (6:50); sanction → a **"DO NOT ENTER" sign** + empty bowl (7:00);
  frozen savings → **cash at the bottom of a pit** (9:13). [NON-literal — the strongest cluster]
- **Money quantities → absurd physical scale.** "$3T of oil" → a barrel-**shopping-cart** (0:06);
  "piles of dollars" → a **cash mountain** dwarfing a person (3:45). [NON-literal]
- **Ideas/euphemisms literalized for the joke.** "Legalized robbery" → a **magnifying glass**
  revealing the phrase (6:40) and a **masked bandit** on the cash (4:00); "the plan died with him"
  → a coin **rolling away into rubble** (7:44). [NON-literal, joke-carrying]
- **Two visual registers, switched on purpose.** Crude doodle register for story/emotion; a
  **clean 2.5D infographic** register (4:15, 4:20, 10:06) reserved for the actual mechanism/data —
  the shift itself signals "pay attention, this is how it really works."
- **Captions do the dateline/citation work.** Place-and-year stamps ("Saudi Arabia, 1974", "Iraq,
  2003", "January 2026", "143 tons of Gold") are burned into the frame, freeing the VO to stay
  conversational. [labeled-prop]
- **Escalation via crowd-growth.** The same shot re-populates to show scale: angry-mob nations
  grow (1:01→1:19); challenger coin-figures multiply into a "sea" (10:52→11:01).
- **Literal depiction is reserved for real-world tech**, not economics: EVs, solar/wind, oil
  derricks (10:24–10:34) are the only straight depictions — everything monetary is a metaphor.

### B2. CLASS of money-narration → CLASS of visual move (the priority output)

**How to read this:** the left column is the *kind* of line the narration is doing; the middle is
the *kind* of shot Crayon Capital reaches for; the right column is only an **illustration** of that
class from this one video — NOT a rule. The actual image content should vary wildly; it's the
narration-type → shot-type pairing that transfers. A prompt-writer should use these classes to
*invent* an on-style shot, never to look up a fixed depiction.

| Class of money-narration | Class of visual move | Example seen here (illustrative only) |
|---|---|---|
| An abstract force / power / property ("trust", "a system", "demand", "inflation") | **Bind it to one tangible object or afflicted body** — give the intangible a physical carrier | "Most trusted currency" → an award badge; inflation → an Earth with sickly streaks; SWIFT → a labeled globe |
| A country / institution acting or deciding | **Personify as a recurring character** (fixed home-country avatar; flag-balls or caricatured leaders for others) | USA = Uncle Sam; Saudi Arabia = a king on a throne; Kissinger/Saddam/Gaddafi/Maduro as caricatures |
| A deal, alliance, or trade *between* parties | **Stage it as an interaction between two characters** — handshake, linking arms, one handing something to another | Saudi-ball × USA-ball handshake; India × Putin handshake; a coalition linking arms |
| A bare number / statistic / quantity | **Glue the number to a physical object at absurd scale** (or burn it in as a caption on the thing) | "$3T of oil" → a barrel-shopping-cart; "143 tons of gold" → a full warehouse; surplus → a cash mountain |
| A self-reinforcing mechanism / how-it-actually-works | **Switch to a clean diagram register and animate the flow** (arrows, gears, numbered nodes) | The recycling loop as a rotating 3-node cycle; reserve-share as a circuit-board globe |
| A villain's / defiant party's claim or announcement | **Put the character on a stage** (podium, conference table, press room) with a speech bubble | Each leader who ditches the dollar declares it "at the long table"; Trump at a rainy podium |
| A euphemism, spin, or ironic truth | **Ironic-counterpoint cutaway that literalizes the subtext** | "Legalized robbery" → magnifying glass unmasking the phrase + a masked bandit on the cash pile |
| Coercion / being shut out / a penalty | **Physical disconnection or denial** — unplug, block, bury, wall off | Cut from the system → a plug yanked from a socket; sanction → a DO-NOT-ENTER sign; frozen reserves → cash in a pit |
| A collective mood shift (fear, greed, solidarity) | **Show it across a crowd** — repeated reactions, or a group multiplying | Everyone fears the same → identical thought-bubbles; challengers grow into a "sea" of figures |
| A turn / escalation in the story | **Re-populate or transform the same tableau** to show change over time | Angry-mob nations grow; the champion-belt USA gets swarmed by more rivals |
| A real-world physical thing (tech, place, event) | **Straight literal depiction** — reserved almost exclusively for non-monetary nouns | EVs at a charger, solar/wind farm, oil derrick; a period CRT TV for a broadcast |
| A conflict / act of force | **Cartoon-violence beat with a comic burst + dateline caption** | "Iraq, 2003" invasion → soldiers + "BANG!!!" burst |
| A payoff / thesis landing | **Emotional set-piece or in-joke tableau** | The WSB ape roaring "Apes together… Strong!"; Uncle Sam's swagger power-pose |

### C. Within-shot motion patterns

- **Idle micro-motion is the baseline** — even "still" shots breathe (globe spin, cash flutter,
  rain streaks, sign-bob). Nothing is a dead still.
- **Pop-in / type-on for information** — captions, list items, badges, and icon callouts *appear*
  in sync with the narrated beat (0:49 badge, 0:55 list, 4:05 labels).
- **Gesture as emotion** — arms-spread swagger (3:35), angry stomp (7:10), ape roar (11:06),
  mob sign-bob (1:01). Character body-language carries the emotional payload.
- **Arrows draw / wheels rotate** in the infographic register (4:15 loop, 10:06 gears) — motion
  *is* the explanation of the mechanism.
- **Crowd multiplication** as an animated escalation device (1:01→1:19; 10:52→11:01).
- **Small directed actions** land jokes/beats: coin rolls away (7:44), plug pulls out (6:50),
  cart rolls (0:06), coin handed between palms (10:43).
- Camera is mostly locked; occasional **slow push-in** on a character intro (2:26).

### D. Cut cadence feel

Brisk but not frantic — a **new full-frame image roughly every 4–8 seconds**, essentially one
distinct visual idea per narrated sentence/clause. Most shots are single-purpose (one concept =
one image) and swapped wholesale rather than edited within. The pace tightens for list/enumeration
beats ("Japan? Dollars. Germany? Dollars." → rapid ball queue) and for the challenger montage
(Iraq→Libya→Venezuela, each ~10–15s with its own dateline). It slows slightly for the mechanism
infographics (4:15–4:30) where a single diagram holds while it animates. The rhythm is
**caption-and-image-per-beat**, keeping the eye rewarded continuously so the ~11-min runtime never
feels static.

### E. Generative principles for a money-topic-story visual-prompt-writer

These are *principles*, not templates. Each says "classify the line, then apply the move, then
invent fresh imagery." The examples are there to show the move, not to be copied.

1. **Classify the narration before you prompt an image.** Ask what *kind* of line this is — an
   abstract force, a party acting, a deal, a bare number, a claim, a euphemism, a coercion, a mood,
   a turn — then apply that class's visual move (see B2). Two different scripts hitting the same
   *class* should produce completely different pictures. The relationship is fixed; the content is
   free.
2. **For any abstraction, give it a body or a carrier — never a chart of the word.** An intangible
   force gets bound to one tangible object or an afflicted character. The prompt should *choose a
   fresh embodiment each time* (a force could be a weather system, a creature, a machine, a
   possessed object) rather than reuse "trust = a badge." The habit is "make it physical," not
   "use this prop."
3. **Personify the players and let the drama happen between them.** Institutions and nations become
   recurring characters; any relationship *between* parties (a deal, a threat, a betrayal, a
   dependency) is best staged as an **interaction between two characters** — but the interaction can
   be a handshake, a shove, a leash, a gift, a standoff. Pick the interaction that fits the *emotion*
   of the beat, then invent the staging.
4. **Match the register to the payload.** Story/emotion beats ride the expressive character style;
   the actual mechanism or a hard statistic earns a **cleaner diagram register with animated flow**
   (arrows, gears, numbered nodes). The deliberate *shift* between registers is itself a signal to
   the viewer — reserve the clean look so it keeps meaning "this is how the machine really works."
5. **Let captions carry the facts so the picture can carry the feeling.** Burn numbers, dates, and
   place-names into the frame as labels; that frees each image to do something expressive or funny
   rather than merely illustrate. Land humor **visually** through ironic-counterpoint cutaways, and
   keep the eye rewarded — roughly one distinct visual idea per narrated beat (~every 4–8s), with
   everything in idle motion so no frame is dead. The prompt-writer's job is to invent the specific
   gag/embodiment on-style, not to retrieve a stored one.
