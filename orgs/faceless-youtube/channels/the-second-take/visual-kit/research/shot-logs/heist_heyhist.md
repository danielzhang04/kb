### B. Recurring narration→visual patterns (the kinds of narration and the shot each gets)

**Named place / date / geography → labeled diegetic sign or map-with-token.** "Coffeyville, Kansas,
1892" never appears as a narrator caption; it's a wooden town SIGN (0:00) and a US map with a
treasure-chest pin (0:05). The world announces itself.

**A plan / spatial tactic → top-down map with moving dots.** The hardest abstraction here (two teams,
two banks, across the street) becomes an overhead street diagram with tokens sliding along paths
(2:21). The same overhead grammar is reused for the bank interior (5:03) and the grim aftermath
(10:36). *This is the channel's #1 tool for making an invisible plan/space concrete.*

**A quantity / number → a mass of bodies or a stamped graphic.** "100,000 people" = a crowd of
identical figures (2:58); "angry armed mob" = an overhead swarm of heads (4:54); "A+ rating" = a giant
"A+" stamped on the screenshot (3:07). Numbers are never spoken-and-forgotten — they get a countable
object.

**An abstract state (debt, time, a lie) → a visual pun or metaphor object.** THE signature move.
"Debt traps you" → a shackled figure in a cell (2:48). "10-minute timer" → a Big Ben clock balanced on
a wrist (5:18). "The manager lied" → his face becomes a troll-face meme (5:22). Each intangible gets a
single concrete/punny image.

**Dialogue / invented banter → characters acting it out (often anachronistically formal).** The script
invents posh back-and-forth ("You make an excellent suggestion, little bro"); the visuals reenact it as
staged two-shots (6:13). Historical off-screen events (Frank's death, 1:35) are dramatized as quick
tableaux.

**A named institution / role → costume + prop shorthand.** "Deputies" = badge + dark suit (1:26);
"the bank" = a labeled storefront; "hardware store" = a wall of rifles with a shop sign (4:36).

**A joke or absurd beat → meme-reference, anachronism, or fourth-wall gag.** Troll-face (5:22),
full anime "Nani?!" speed-line cutaway (9:09), crying wojak (9:13), a baby villain with a lollipop
(1:53), a random rocking chair (8:00), and a "Does anyone read this?" speech bubble on a background sign
(4:36). Humor is carried by *pop-culture visual iconography*, not by the drawing being funnier.

**Violence / death → stylized, censored, ad-safe.** Bullets are bright yellow beams (6:13, 8:20);
fatal headshots become X-eyes (7:40) or a black-void head with one blood drip (9:41). Gore is abstracted
so the tone stays comedic and monetizable.

**Shootout initiations → POV down the gun barrel.** A repeated framing (0:15, 6:04, 7:30) that puts the
viewer inside the action and unifies the many gunfight beats.

**Emotional / aftermath narration → wide empty establishing shots.** After the noise, the video breathes
with a silhouette-against-sky (10:08) and a lone horse in an empty sunset desert (10:17) — mood over
information. The video knows when to STOP being a gag machine.

**Non-literal moves flagged explicitly:** map-with-dots for a plan; crowd-as-number; A+ stamp;
debt-as-shackles; timer-as-Big-Ben; liar-as-troll-face; mob-as-looming-shadows; headshot-as-black-void;
anime-Nani cutaway; baby-villain; laser-beam bullets. Roughly a third of all shots are non-literal.

### C. Within-shot motion patterns

These are animated panels, not stills. Typical motion is small and looping:
- **Idle micro-motion** on nearly every held shot: character sway, blink, breathing, dust motes, drifting
  clouds, a swaying prop (rocking chair, hanging clock). Nothing is ever fully frozen.
- **Gesture-as-beat:** facepalm slide (0:59), shrug (5:09), hand-to-chin lean (0:34), pointing (3:25),
  arms-crossed tap (8:59) — the key pose animates in to land the line.
- **Run/walk cycles** for "spread"/chase/escape beats (4:17 wildfire runner, 6:50 crouch-run).
- **Recoil + muzzle-flash pulse + laser-streak** for all gunfire (6:04, 8:20).
- **Token slides across a map** for plans/movement (2:21), and slow **camera push/zoom toward a pin**
  on locator maps (0:05).
- **Element pop-on** for callouts: "A+" sparkles in (3:07), title text pops (1:04), speech bubbles appear.
- **Reaction deformation:** jaw-drops, comic tears wobbling (9:13/9:19), anime speed-lines zoom (9:09).
- **Slow parallax / dust drift** on establishing/atmosphere shots (0:10, 10:17) for a calmer pace.

### D. Cut cadence feel

Very fast, near-continuous. In sampled 1-fps windows the image is different essentially every frame,
implying **~1.5–3 seconds per shot on average** through the body — roughly one distinct illustrated panel
per sentence or clause. It cuts FASTEST during jokes and action (meme swaps, reaction shots, gunfire
beats can be sub-second stingers, e.g. the troll-face and Nani cutaways are quick punches). It LINGERS
noticeably only in two places: the atmospheric establishers (the dusty-town wide, the sunset desert,
the silhouette-sky ~3-5s) and the one sincere emotional beat (Emmett over his dead brother). The rhythm
is "constant new information/gag, with rare deliberate breaths" — the holds are earned and used for tone.

### E. Top 5 transferable lessons for an illustrated explainer's visual-prompt-writer

1. **Give every abstraction a concrete OBJECT or PUN — never just illustrate the sentence.** A plan → an
   overhead map with labeled tokens. A number → a countable crowd or a stamped graphic. A state (debt,
   time, a lie) → one metaphor object (shackles, a Big Ben wristwatch, a troll-face). When a cue is
   abstract, the prompt-writer's job is to *pick the object*, not to depict the words. This is exactly
   the fix for a writer that "defaults to boring literal depictions."

2. **Reuse a small set of signature framings as a visual grammar.** This channel leans on ~4 repeatable
   devices — the top-down tactical map, the gun-barrel POV, the crowd-as-mass, and the diegetic
   sign/UI/screenshot. A prompt-writer should keep a house kit of 4–6 such framings and assign each
   narration-type to one, so the video feels authored, not random.

3. **Make labels and numbers DIEGETIC.** Place names, prices, ratings, and stats live inside the world
   as signs, shop banners, screenshots, and stamped callouts — not as narrator subtitles. Prompt for
   "a wooden storefront sign reading X," "a website screenshot with a giant A+ stamped on it."

4. **Encode tone and humor through pop-culture iconography and anachronism, and keep violence stylized.**
   Meme faces (troll, wojak, anime "Nani?!"), out-of-period props, and fourth-wall asides do the comedic
   lifting cheaply. Render harm as X-eyes / beams / a black-void head so the piece stays comedic AND
   ad-safe. Bake these as optional "gag layer" prompts on beats flagged as jokes.

5. **Vary cadence deliberately: default to one fresh panel per clause, but PRESCRIBE the breath-beats.**
   Cut fast through info and gags; then explicitly call a slow, near-empty establishing/atmosphere shot
   (wide landscape, silhouette-against-sky) at emotional turns and section ends. The prompt-writer should
   tag a few shots as "hold/atmosphere" so the render isn't a relentless strobe — the pauses are what make
   the density readable.
