### B. Narration-TYPE → shot-TYPE patterns (how this money-topic / historical STORY video visualizes each kind of line)

This is a comedic historical STORY (Depression-era money crisis → the Emu War), not a lesson/explainer. The grammar below is a mapping from **what KIND of line the narration is** to **what KIND of shot answers it** — with the specific Emu-War gags cited ONLY as illustrations of each class. The image content is free to be anything on-style; it's the class-to-class relationship and the comedic timing that transfer.

1. **Dry fact / number / place → route it through a recurring "data object," not a literal scene.** Any quantitative or geographic beat gets glued to one canonical prop (here: the satellite Australia map — "20,000 emus" as a swarm of map icons, "Campion" as a labeled dot + arrow, "1,000 killed" as one emu with a big ✗ and "X 1000"; the currency peg as a labeled rope to a gold pile). The class: *a number/location becomes an object or a count-on-an-icon.* The specific object should vary by story; the move is "don't stage the fact, label it."

2. **Abstraction / institution / trend / emotion → personify it and give it a one-line skit.** Markets, policies, nations, moods are drawn as characters with faces and made to TALK (crash = panicking traders; "the Earth got depressed" = a sad planet; nations = map-blobs boasting; the gold standard = a nation-character towing another). The class: *abstract force → a character (or a physical object) that acts out the beat in miniature.* Non-literal is the DEFAULT here; the exact personification is open.

3. **A boast / claim / stated intention → set up an image the FACTS can later contradict (or contradict it immediately).** When narration asserts something ("declaring victory," "quick and painless," a nation bragging about its "protected" national bird), the shot leans INTO the claim so the counter-fact stings — a "MISSION ACCOMPLISHED" banner over a loss, majestic real photos before the dumpy-emu reveal. The class: *a claim invites an ironic-counterpoint image;* the picture asserts what the words will undercut. This is the workhorse joke engine and the single most transferable one.

4. **A euphemism / weasel-word / understatement → quote it as giant on-screen TEXT or as a deliberately mundane object.** "A number of birds" slams up as huge text; the war ends on a small chunk of chain-link fence. The class: *deflationary language gets literalized flat and un-epic* so the emphasis itself is the joke.

5. **A character's plan / dialogue / argument → hold ONE staged set and let speech bubbles carry it.** Debates and wordplay (the ~20s feather-hat slip; the emu "war-council"; "learn your vowels") play over a single unchanging frame with swapping bubbles and type-on. The class: *when the writing is the joke, the visual just tags WHO speaks and gets out of the way* — spend no animation there.

6. **The punch WORD / a fact landing → cut to a reaction or a pop-in ON the beat.** Reaction bubbles ("What? / What?"), a threat popping into frame, the propaganda camera crew appearing to yell "CUT!" exactly at each failure. The class: *time a reaction shot or element pop-in to the punch word,* not before or after. Comedic timing is in the pop, not the drawing.

7. **A physical event / action → THIS is where literal depiction is allowed.** Emus scattering, the gun-truck crashing, the food-chain chain. Literal is reserved for concrete motion and used sparingly — plus at the very top to orient ("This is Australia") and for real archival photos that sell "this actually happened." The class: *literal = seasoning for physical action and authenticity beats, never the default for ideas.*

8. **Register-switches are themselves a narrative voice.** Flat cartoon = comedy; real photo/archival footage = sincerity / "real history" (often dropped UNDER a mock-solemn beat so authenticity sharpens the joke); real head-cutouts on doodle bodies = "a real person, absurdly"; giant text = emphasis/irony. The class: *switching visual register tells the viewer how to read the beat* — establish a few registers and move between them deliberately.

9. **Runners compound — re-cash an established image later.** A device introduced once (nation-blobs, the anthropomorphized enemy, the propaganda crew, a repeated punch-text like "MORE economic downturn") pays off again on callback. The class: *reward memory;* the second appearance is funnier and cheaper than a new gag.

### C. Within-shot motion patterns

- **Pop-in / reveal** is the dominant micro-motion: props, icons, speech bubbles, text cards, extra threats all POP or slide into a held frame rather than the whole scene animating. Cheap, reads as "joke landing."
- **Speech-bubble swap + type-on** carries dialogue-heavy beats on static sets (Parliament, farm). The bubble appearing IS the edit.
- **Full-screen text punch-in** ("THAT'S RIGHT," "A NUMBER," "MISSION ACCOMPLISHED," "X 1000") — bold word slams on for emphasis/irony, often the punch itself.
- **Idle micro-jitter** on crowds (traders, MPs) gives life without real animation — a subtle wobble loop.
- **Walk / run / drive cycles** reserved for genuine physical action (emus fleeing, tow-car, gun-truck). Parallax side-scroll for the desert food-chain gag.
- **Ken-Burns slow pan/zoom** exclusively on the REAL archival photos — signals "sincere doc footage," and the slow move is what makes the mock-elegy read as heartfelt.
- **Map draw-ons**: labels type on, red arrows draw across, icons multiply — the "infographic" motion vocabulary.
- **One-shot spectacle**: the truck fireball / car-explosion-at-dusk gets a fuller particle+slow-mo treatment BECAUSE it's parodying cinematic drama — motion budget spikes exactly at the parody.

### D. Cut cadence feel

Fast and gag-timed, not metronomic. Roughly a new SHOT or a new on-frame element every 2–5 seconds — but the unit isn't the camera cut, it's the JOKE beat: many "cuts" are actually a new speech bubble or a prop pop-in on a held background. Dialogue skits sit on one set for 10–20s while bubbles swap (low visual churn, high verbal churn); action/transition beats cut faster (map → field → reaction in quick succession). Rhythm = SETUP shot held slightly longer, then a rapid pop/text-card on the PUNCH word. Establishing map beats and archival-photo beats are the longest holds (3–6s), giving the ear a rest between comedic salvos. The escalation runner ("MORE economic downturn") deliberately quickens the cut rate to build absurd momentum, then breaks it with "Hey!"

### E. Generative principles for a money-topic / historical-STORY visual-prompt-writer

These are generative RULES for INVENTING on-style shots, not a lookup table. Each says "classify the narration line, then pick a shot CLASS" — the actual image is free to vary wildly. The Emu-War examples are only there to show the class, never to be copied.

1. **First classify the line, THEN choose a shot class — never depict the noun literally by reflex.** Before prompting, ask what KIND of beat this is: dry fact/number, abstraction, boast/claim, euphemism, character plan/dialogue, punch word, physical action, or authenticity beat. The narration-type dictates the shot-type (per §B). Literal depiction of the subject is the correct answer ONLY for physical action and archival/authenticity beats; for everything else, reflex-literal is the failure mode to avoid. This single classify-first habit is the whole game.

2. **For every abstraction, generate a fresh personification or object-metaphor — vary it deliberately.** Markets, policies, institutions, moods, trends should become a character-with-a-face running a one-line skit, OR a physical object/prop that acts out the beat. Deliberately invent a DIFFERENT vehicle each time (don't let every institution become the same blob) so the channel reads as inventive, not templated. The rule is "abstraction → embodiment"; the embodiment is yours to design.

3. **Scan each beat for a claim the story later undercuts, and prompt the ironic-counterpoint image.** Wherever narration states a boast, an intention, or a spin that the facts contradict, generate an image that sincerely ASSERTS the claim so the counter-fact lands harder (triumphant banner over a loss; grand footage before a deflating reveal; a weasel-phrase blown up as text). Flag these beats explicitly in the shot list as "ironic-counterpoint." The specific contradiction is discovered per-story; only the technique is fixed.

4. **Budget motion and art by joke location, not per line.** Decide where the joke lives: if it's in the WRITING (dialogue, wordplay, a long verbal gag), hold ONE invented staged set and carry it with swapping bubbles / type-on text — no new art. If it's in the MOTION or spectacle (or you're parodying a cinematic cliché), spend the animation budget there (cycles, particles, slow-mo). Time reaction shots and element pop-ins to land ON the punch word. This keeps a dense video producible.

5. **Establish a small register system and a reusable data-object, then improvise WITHIN them.** Define a few visual registers for the channel (e.g. stylized-cartoon = story/comedy, real archival/photo = "this actually happened", bold text = emphasis/irony) and one recurring data-device (a map, ledger, chart, or desk) that ALL numbers/places flow through — then invent freely inside that frame. Switching register is how you signal "read this beat differently"; the shared data-object gives dry facts instant legibility and a house style. Also: when a payoff is deflationary understatement, prompt the most mundane, least-epic object you can invent — the anticlimax itself is the joke. The system is fixed; the contents are invented every time.
