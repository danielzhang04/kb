## OverSimplified — The Cold War (Part 1)

Reference: `coldwar_oversimp.mp4` (16:03, 854×480). Analysis from ~100 sampled frames across 8 windows. Art style = flat 2D vector cartoon: thick dark outlines, "bean people" (featureless white ovals, dot eyes, stick limbs) for crowds/generic actors, and chibi caricatures for named leaders (Stalin = black quiff + mustache + green tunic; Lenin = bald goatee; Truman = round glasses; Khrushchev = bald, jowly; MacArthur = peaked cap, pipe, aviators; Nixon = jowls). Blank-white-void staging is the default "set."

### B. Narration-TYPE → shot-TYPE grammar (NON-literal moves flagged)

*Framing note:* OverSimplified is a **historical STORY video** (the client's analog: money-topic / economic-history STORY videos), not a lesson/"explainer." The craft below is about telling a story with a cast, not illustrating a syllabus.

**READ THIS AS CLASSES, NOT A LOOKUP TABLE.** Each item below pairs a *kind of narration* with a *kind of shot*. The specific images cited (a rainbow hug, a "COFFER" bin, a nightclub rope) are **illustrations of the class only — never fixed rules.** "USSR" does not "= a bear"; a nation-as-actor could be a leader, a shape-with-a-face, a mascot, a shopkeeper, anything on-style. The transferable asset is the narration-type→shot-type *relationship*, so the prompt-writer INVENTS a fresh on-style image each time rather than cloning these.

1. **Narration type: a NATION / IDEOLOGY / INSTITUTION acting or feeling → Shot type: PERSONIFY it as a character** (NON-LITERAL, the signature move). Two registers seen here, both just illustrations of "give the abstract actor a body":
   - **Leader-as-nation:** the country's history is voiced/acted by a chibi caricature of its leader (Stalin, Truman, Khrushchev) standing in a blank void or a minimal set. Diplomacy = these characters talking to each other (Big Three table, 2:24 / 4:16; Kitchen Debate, 12:59).
   - **Country-shape-as-creature:** the literal map silhouette of a nation is given googly eyes + stick limbs and made to walk, hug, snarl, flee, or converse (USA/UK/USSR hug under a rainbow at 2:14; snarl at 4:36; USA-blob recruits Czechoslovakia-blob at 5:55; USA-blob shocked eyes at 8:04; USSR-blob retreats at 9:24). **This is how abstract state-to-state relations become physical, emotional, and funny.** Emotion is carried by swapping the blob's face (hug→snarl) — same asset, new feeling.

2. **Narration type: a spatial/territorial claim, a "side," or a shift in who-controls-what → Shot type: a MAP carrying meaning through a fixed color-code + tokens.** Here: red/blue/neutral fills, animated territory spread, a drawn border for the Iron Curtain, flag pins (1:56, 2:05, 4:26, 8:34). The *convention* transfers (pick a stable color/token language, let it read pre-verbally); the specific palette and map are free to change per video.

3. **Narration type: a direction, flow, or measured distance → Shot type: a MAP with a labeled arrow / token.** Here: MARSHALL AID sweeping the Atlantic (5:25), "100 MILES" to Berlin (6:25), region word-labels "COMMUNIST"/"ANTI-COMMUNIST" (8:14). The class is "spatial relationships get arrows + labels, not prose"; the illustrations are not templates.

4. **Narration type: a bare NUMBER or quantity → Shot type: glue it to a TANGIBLE object or a count-up, never leave it as voiceover.** Here (illustrations only): a budget = gold coins poured between a "COFFER" chest and a "MILITARY" bin (13:27); an airlift = "277,804 flights / 2.3 million tonnes" ticking up (7:14); defectors = people-icons piling on one side of a map (13:45). The generative rule: *find the object/container/pile that makes THIS number physical* — the object should differ every time.

5. **Narration type: a dry abstraction, a policy, a treaty, a systemic condition → Shot type: a concrete STAND-IN scene or object (NON-LITERAL).** Here: an economic bloc → a velvet-rope nightclub with a bouncer (6:15); West-vs-East prosperity → a split-screen (13:36); a client-state's dependence → "let me check with my mom" (5:55); occupying Berlin → a domestic argument with the city as a trapped spouse (6:45); the atomic secret → a labeled picture-book prop (2:42). These are wildly different inventions serving ONE class ("make the abstraction a tangible scene") — that variety is the point, not a menu to copy.

6. **Narration type: a TREATY / DEAL / negotiation / summit, OR any state-to-state exchange → Shot type: stage it as a face-to-face INTERACTION between the personified actors.** Here: the Big Three at a round table (2:24, 4:16), the Kitchen Debate showroom (12:59), Checkpoint Charlie phone-call insets (15:29), one blob recruiting another (5:55). The class is "diplomacy = characters doing something to each other in a shared space."

7. **Narration type: a serious/somber real event, OR a punchline that needs a hard tonal snap → Shot type: a deliberate REGISTER-BREAK out of the house style.** Two sub-classes: (a) real archival footage/photos for gravity — airlift, nuclear test, Sputnik, the real Wall (7:14–7:24, 12:50, 14:47); (b) an absurd real-photo/collage cutaway for a joke — the drill-laden man for "COMMUNIST!" (12:04), the face-on-the-U-2 (12:13). The tonal whiplash is the tool; the specific footage is incidental.

8. **Narration type: a chapter turn, a date, a new faction, or a recurring structural beat → Shot type: a TITLE/CHAPTER card, optionally as a running-gag callback.** Here: "COMMUNIST", "SPUTNIK! 1957", and the twin "1952/1960 ELECTION" cards that rhyme deliberately. Class = "segment the story and reward the viewer with callbacks"; the card content varies.

9. **Narration type: a punchy emotional reaction or a landed joke → Shot type: a tight REACTION single (push-in on a face, or the personified actor performing the emotion).** Here: Stalin "This is great!" arms-up (5:35), Churchill "How?!" push-in (2:51), a country-blob's shocked googly eyes (8:04).

10. **Narration type: a dry aside, a sponsor pivot, or a tonal wink → Shot type: an IRONIC-COUNTERPOINT or ANACHRONISM cutaway.** Here: the VPN read entered through the plot (3:01), the Trump "big beautiful wall" line on the Berlin Wall build (14:39). Class = "let the image contradict or time-warp against the line for comedy"; the specific anachronism is invented per beat.

*(Craft aside, not a narration→shot class: sets and cast assets are REUSED across the video — the round table, the Party auditorium, the Berlin split-map recur — which buys continuity and cuts cost. Worth mirroring at the pipeline level via a persistent asset library.)*

### C. Within-shot motion patterns

- **Map territory color-fill / spread** — the workhorse: red or blue floods across regions to show conquest, revolution, influence (1:09 red burst, 8:34 southward sweep).
- **Arrow-draw across the map** — directional lines animate on for aid, invasion, distance (5:25, 6:25).
- **Count-up number ticks** — stats climb on screen (7:14).
- **Country-blob walk-cycle / gesture** — the personified nations bounce, walk on stick legs, throw arms, swap to angry/shocked faces (2:14, 4:36, 5:55, 8:04, 9:24).
- **Speech-text type-on / caption swap** — dialogue appears as floating text that swaps line-by-line during a held two-shot (nearly every dialogue scene).
- **Character lean / recoil / arms-up / topple** — big single-pose emotional beats (5:35 arms up; 9:51 Stalin topples over dead).
- **Push-in / zoom** on faces and on map labels for emphasis.
- **Parallax** on side-scroll street/battlefield scenes (1:16 rowboat, 6:35 West Berlin street, 9:24 snowy retreat).
- **Prop / element pop-in** — bombs, ballots, picture-books, coins, flags appear with a pop (2:42, 8:24, 13:27).
- **Bubble / inset expand** — thought bubbles (airlift preview, 7:04) and phone-call circular insets (Checkpoint Charlie, 15:29).
- **Idle micro-motion** — a constant subtle bob/breathe on otherwise static characters keeps frames alive during dialogue.
- Archival footage segments simply play as video (the only "real" motion).

### D. Cut cadence feel

Fast and rhythmic, but VARIABLE by function:
- **Joke / list beats:** very fast — a new image every ~1.5–3s. Rapid-fire disaster lists (1:16–1:25) and buzzword gags (11:55–12:13) fire a fresh cutaway per punchline.
- **Dialogue scenes:** the shot HOLDS on a two- or three-shot for ~5–12s while the joke plays out through swapping text captions and small gestures rather than cutting. The camera stays; the words change.
- **Map / info beats:** ~3–5s holds while the fill/arrow animates, then cut.
- **Register-break cutaways** (real footage / real photos) are short punches: 2–4s, then back to cartoon.
- Overall the video never sits still — even a "held" dialogue frame has type-on text, idle bob, and pop-ins providing motion. Roughly ~250–350 distinct shots across 16 min (est. ~3s average), with the density spiking on comedic runs and relaxing into the acted dialogue scenes.

### E. Generative principles for a money-topic / historical-STORY visual-prompt-writer

*These are GENERATIVE rules — a way to reason from a narration line to an on-style shot the writer INVENTS. They are deliberately not a lookup table; no principle says "depict X as Y." The goal is that two writers following them produce equally on-style but visually different shots.*

1. **First, classify the line; then choose a shot-TYPE; only then invent the image.** Before writing any prompt, tag the narration beat by KIND — is it an actor (nation/institution/company/market), a deal/interaction, a bare number, a dry abstraction/policy, a spatial claim, a threat, a reaction, a dry aside, or a story turn? Map that kind to a shot-TYPE (personify / stage-as-interaction / glue-to-object / concrete-stand-in / map-with-tokens / register-break / title-card / reaction-single / ironic-cutaway). The concrete picture is the LAST decision and should vary freely. This ordering is what stops the writer defaulting to literal depiction.

2. **Personify the abstract actors, and let relationships play as physical action.** Any nation/institution/currency/company that "does" or "feels" something in the story should get a body — but WHICH body is an open creative choice (a caricatured figurehead, a shape-with-a-face, a mascot, a shopkeeper, an anthropomorphized object). Render the relationship as those characters doing something to each other (hugging, recruiting, arguing, fleeing, out-bidding). Carry emotion by re-posing/re-facing the SAME actor. Vary the personification device across beats so the channel never reads as "the one where everything is a bear."

3. **Never let a number or an abstraction sit as bare narration — make it tangible, and make the object different each time.** For a stat, ask "what container, pile, object, or count-up makes THIS quantity physical?" For a policy/treaty/condition, ask "what everyday scene or prop is this abstraction secretly LIKE?" The answer should be freshly invented per line; the discipline is the tangibility, not any particular object. (Guard against a house cliché forming — rotate the metaphors.)

4. **Reach for spatial grammar only when the line is spatial or comparative, and keep a consistent token language within a video.** When narration is about where, flow, sides, or share-of-something, build a map/diagram with a stable color-code and simple tokens (arrows for flow, fills for change, labels/pins for identity) so it reads pre-verbally. The token language should be consistent WITHIN a video for legibility but reinvented per video so the format stays fresh.

5. **Deliberately choose a REGISTER per beat, and commit fully — no uncanny middle.** Decide each beat's register on purpose: full house-style illustration for the story spine; a hard break to real footage/photo for a genuinely somber or credibility-critical moment; an absurd real-photo/collage cutaway for a punchline; an ironic-counterpoint or anachronism when the image should wink against the line. Tag the register in the shot so nothing lands in the half-real, half-cartoon zone the channel bans. Use title/chapter cards to segment the story and to set up callback gags.

6. **Design each shot as still + prescribed motion, and pace the cut by story function.** Specify what MOVES inside every frame (type-on text, a pop-in prop, a token sliding on a map, a micro-bob, a face-swap, a push-in) so a stills-plus-motion pipeline never feels dead. Let cadence follow function: hold longer on staged dialogue/interaction (let it play through text and small gestures rather than cutting), cut fast on joke and reaction beats, give map/info beats enough time to animate their change. Build a reusable cast + set library so recurring actors and locations stay consistent and cheap across a video and across the channel.
