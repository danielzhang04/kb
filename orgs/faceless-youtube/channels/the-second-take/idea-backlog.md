### ST-001 — The Man Who Almost Bought the Bank He Was Robbing
- **Format:** long-form (~12–16 min) + shorts bench (provisional; shorts-writer finalizes from the researched long-form)
- **Provisional angle:** Artur Alves dos Reis (Portugal, 1925) didn't counterfeit — he forged an authorization that tricked the Bank of Portugal's *own* London printer (Waterlow & Sons) into running off millions in **real** 500-escudo notes, then used the fortune to secretly buy shares of the Bank of Portugal itself — nearly coming to *legally own* the institution he was defrauding. Tell it as "the trick was that there was no trick," climaxing on the buy-the-central-bank move most retellings rush past.
- **Payload promise:** The viewer leaves understanding that money's value is a *trust chain*, not the paper — dos Reis attacked the chain, not the notes — and that a single con nearly toppled a democracy (it helped trigger the 1926 coup → Salazar).
- **Key questions the video must answer (→ researcher's seed):**
  1. What EXACTLY did the forged contract say, and why did Waterlow & Sons accept it without contacting the Bank of Portugal?
  2. How many notes / what escudo value entered circulation, and what share of currency-in-circulation and GDP was that? (verify the LSE working-paper figures)
  3. Mechanically, how did he convert the notes into a controlling stake in the Bank of Portugal, and how close did he get?
  4. What single detail exposed it (duplicate serial numbers) — who noticed, and how?
  5. Direct line from the scandal to the 1926 military coup: what's documented vs. inferred?
  6. What happened to Waterlow & Sons (the civil suit) and to dos Reis afterward?
- **Title options:** 1) The Man Who Made the Bank Print His Own Money 2) He Didn't Fake the Money. He Faked the Order to Print It. 3) The Con That Nearly Bought a Country's Central Bank
- **Why original:** Cross-validated by two independent scouts as under-told on video; inverts every counterfeiting-story expectation (the notes were real); the buy-the-central-bank climax + democracy-ending stakes are skipped by the listicle takes.
- **Score:** 91/100 (payload 19, hook 14, lever 10, demand 10, monetization 14, diff 14, fit 5, feasibility 5)
