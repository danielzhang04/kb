## Long-form VO

[B-ROLL: the Nikola One promo — a sleek semi gliding down an empty highway at dusk] The most important electric truck video ever made shows a truck that could not move under its own power. [PAUSE] It's not driving here. It's rolling. Downhill. With nothing switched on.

[B-ROLL: freeze-frame, arrow overlay on the flat-looking road revealing a slight grade] The company was called Nikola. At its peak it was worth thirty-four billion dollars — briefly more than Ford. It had never sold a single truck.

This is the autopsy of a company that died from one decision: the day it decided that *looking* real was easier than *being* real.

[B-ROLL: founder Trevor Milton on stage, arms wide, huge screen behind him] Meet Trevor Milton. In 2016 he walks on stage and unveils the Nikola One — a hydrogen-electric semi he says will end diesel. The room believes him. [PAUSE] For a while, everyone does.

[B-ROLL: montage — magazine covers, "next Tesla" headlines, GM logo] By 2020 the pitch is irresistible. A partnership with General Motors. A stock ticker, NKLA, that retail investors pile into. On paper, Milton is a billionaire several times over.

[B-ROLL: the promo video again, "Nikola One in motion" title card] The centerpiece of the hype was that video. "Nikola One in motion." It looks like proof. A working truck, on a real road, moving.

[PAUSE] Here's what the camera didn't show. The truck had no working powertrain. So the team towed it to the top of a shallow hill, pointed the camera to hide the slope, and let gravity do the rest. The "in motion" truck was a two-ton paperweight coasting downhill.

[B-ROLL: physics diagram — mass on an incline, gravity vector] That's the whole trick. Not software. Not a battery breakthrough. A hill.

--- MID-VIDEO RE-ARM ---

[B-ROLL: Hindenburg Research report on screen, headline "Nikola: How to Parlay an Ocean of Lies"] In September 2020, a short-seller named Hindenburg Research publishes a report calling Nikola an "intricate fraud." The rolling-truck detail is in there. [PAUSE] Milton had tweeted that the truck was "in motion." Technically true. Technically.

[B-ROLL: stock chart NKLA falling off a cliff] The stock collapses. GM quietly shrinks the deal. Milton resigns within weeks.

[B-ROLL: courthouse exterior, slow push-in] Then the part that hype men never plan for. The SEC. The Department of Justice. In 2022 a jury convicts Trevor Milton of securities and wire fraud.

[B-ROLL: document close-up — sentencing order] In 2023 he's sentenced to four years in federal prison and later ordered to pay hundreds of millions in restitution. The company files for bankruptcy in 2025.

[PAUSE] And here's the detail I saved for last. [B-ROLL: the original promo, replayed slowly] Nikola never had to fake it. The engineers were actually building real hydrogen trucks — they just weren't ready. Milton could have waited. Instead he rolled a truck down a hill for a camera, and that single shortcut is the thing that put him in prison and erased thirty-four billion dollars.

[B-ROLL: empty stage, lights off] The truck was always going to move eventually. Milton just couldn't stand to wait for gravity's slower cousin: the truth.
