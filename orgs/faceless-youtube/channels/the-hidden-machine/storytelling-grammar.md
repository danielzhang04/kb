# The Hidden Machine — Storytelling Grammar

**What this is:** the channel's single source of truth for *how a video is told* — the voice, the story
shape, the staging. It is a **generative grammar**: kinds of move to invent from, never a template to
clone. Read it every run; where it conflicts with generic doctrine, this doc wins for The Hidden Machine.

**The North Star:** a smart, funny friend who just found out what actually happens behind something you
do every day, and is walking you down into it while still a little stunned by it themselves. Not a
writer crafting prose, not an engineering lecturer. **Raw and a little messy beats polished and bland.**

**The narrator stance is discovering WITH the viewer.** The vertigo is shared, not smirked at from
above. Dry irony is available on the line that earns it, and a story that genuinely earns a villain or a
character gets one — but a narrator standing above the machine, or above the people who use it without
knowing, breaks the channel's contract (`dna.md`: the arc runs vertigo → wonder, and comedic deflation
is a named refusal).

**The voice reference:** `channels/the-hidden-machine/example-scripts.md` does not exist yet — it gets
seeded from the first Daniel-approved script for this channel. Until then every example slot below reads
*[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*, and **every rule here is written to teach without
its example.** The prose is the law; the slot is future calibration.

## 1. The voice

**1.1 — Casual friend, plain words.** No literary phrasing, and no term a normal person wouldn't say
out loud ("packet switching," "interchange fee," "hydraulic head") — say the plain version or cut it.
**When a term or mechanism the viewer may not know has to stay, contextualize it in the same breath:** a
plain gloss or an analogy pitched at someone who has never thought about it, never an assumption of
knowledge. This is a systems *story*, not an engineering course: a piece of mechanism earns its place by
making the scale land, and the parts that don't do that get cut. The dry-explainer trap — mechanism
recited with no emotional payload — is this niche's easiest death
(`knowledge/research/niche-playbooks/hidden-systems.md` §Pitfalls). **Spoken grammar beats written
grammar every time:** "none of them are" is what a person says out loud, so it is what gets written, and
a copy editor's correction that nobody would say is a defect. *Test: would you actually say this line to
a friend?* (Formatting: no em/en dashes, ever — an AI tell the viewer can't hear. Breath is periods,
commas, and colons. `script.md` is pure voiceover prose: no cues, no brackets, no stage directions.)
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

**Every line parses on the first listen, and names the thing instead of the idea of it.** There is no
rewind on a voiceover, so a sentence whose meaning depends on the viewer working out what a pronoun
points at ("That's it doing that") is already gone by the time they get there. Worse is **concept-prose**:
the writer naming the *shape* of what is happening instead of what is happening. Say who or what does
what, to what, and what comes of it, in the order it happens. On this channel the actor is often not a
person — a signal, a valve, a pallet, a pump — and it still has to be named and still has to *do*
something.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

**Paragraphs are idea blocks, four to five sentences on average.** A block carries one idea to its end
and stops; the next block starts the next idea. Short sentences are how a speaker lands a hit, and they
land *inside* a block, powered by the longer sentences around them.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

A sentence standing alone as its own paragraph is the defect, and a page of them is the staccato
monotone this pipeline falls into by default: every line gets identical weight, so nothing lands and
the whole script reads as one long flat drop. Block length varies because thoughts vary. A run of short
sentences *inside* a block, carrying real content, is just how people talk when they pick up speed.

**Tense: present is this channel's default, because the machine is running right now.** The viewer's
action just happened and the system it triggered is still moving, so tell it as it happens. Past tense
is for the history of the system — how it came to be built this way — and that material is licensed only
where the history genuinely *is* the story (§1.4). What is banned is the still life: a camera hovering
over infrastructure with nothing happening in it. Put an actor in the sentence and have it do something.

**1.2 — Land the point inside the telling; no grandeur buttons.** The recurring failure is ending a
beat on a summary, a thesis, or a profound line about what the system Means. Land the point where it
happens and keep going. **The mechanical check:** read the last sentence of every block. If it sums up
or moralizes what just happened instead of landing on a fact, an action, or the push into what comes
next, fold it forward or cut it. A dry *factual* aside is the good kind; keep it. Chopping a button down
to a fragment on its own line fixes nothing: the defect was the summarizing, not the length, and a
clipped drop only adds the monotone on top. The block still ends on something that happened.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

**1.3 — The wit is abundant, warm, and rides the facts.** Jokes, metaphors, and asides are not
seasoning for a few beats; they run **throughout** — the recurring draft failure is a script that goes
deadpan-flat for stretches, and on a mechanism channel that flatness *is* the dry-explainer trap.
A couple of beats in a row with no joke, analogy, aside, or reaction is a defect, not restraint.
Sincerity is not solemnity: the humor is what keeps the register a friend talking and not a lecture.
**The register is warm, irreverent, and loaded with pop culture:** a narrator who is plainly enjoying
this. Dry irony is welcome on the lines where it actually lands, but smirking distance as the house
register is out, because it reads as somebody who thinks the story is beneath him — and here it costs
double, since comedic deflation (the punchline shrinking the scale instead of landing it) is a named
refusal in `dna.md`. **The test on any laugh line: does the magnitude survive it?** If the joke makes
the machine feel smaller, it is the wrong joke. Not everything is a joke either: plain speech carrying
the story is plain speech, and the flat sentence between two funny ones is what makes them funny.
**Profanity is in bounds at the "ass" and "shit" grade and stops there**; the f-word is not the channel.
Every piece of wit still rides a fact: delete the joke and the fact survives; delete the fact and the
joke collapses.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

**1.4 — Analogies are the explanatory engine.** The strongest tool this channel has. A modern comparison
the viewer instantly pictures IS the explanation, not decoration on top of it — reach for one wherever a
mechanism, a scale, or an absurdity would otherwise need a lecture. Runs of comparisons are welcome, and
a good one can recur as a callback. The working kinds:
> Scale: a magnitude set against something the viewer already has a feel for (§2.8 is the full toolbox).
> Mechanism: a familiar everyday process that works the same way as the one being explained.
> Speed / time: a duration or a rate translated into something the body measures.
> Situation: naming what this moment is *like*, in one image the viewer already owns.

**Era comparisons are off by default.** This is a present-tense channel about machinery running right
now; anchoring a beat in a decade the way a period story does is not our texture and reads as borrowed
from a different kind of video. The one license: when the system's *history* is genuinely the story
(why it was built this shape, what it replaced, why nobody has replaced it since), period material is on
the table and gets named as history out loud. Outside that case, cut it.

Cut a comparison only when it repeats a point already landed, derails the story, or makes the fact
harder to understand. **The universality bar governs every pull, not just the analogies:** a joke, a
metaphor, a turn of phrase, a franchise reference, each has to be something a general viewer understands
and pictures instantly, and will still understand in five years. Long-lived franchises, films, and
shared cultural touchstones are exactly the right material. A pull fails the bar when it fades fast, or
when a general viewer would need it explained, never because it is popular. A private literary metaphor
fails; so does slang with a shelf life. Once `example-scripts.md` is seeded, its approved excerpts
become the calibration for where that bar sits.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

**1.5 — The narrator is present and reacting.** The energy comes from a narrator who is IN the telling:
direct questions answered casually, conversational joints ("So anyway," "you know," "See?"), honest
reactions, casual repetition when momentum wants it, and first-person asides. Generic "you" is normal
speech — and on this channel it is close to literal, because the video opened inside the viewer's own
action. The reactions are the shared-vertigo kind: the narrator is finding the size of this thing
startling in the same beat the viewer does, not performing amazement at the viewer. Where the research
supports a genuinely chained descent, the narrator may number the drops out loud as an ordered set — it
exposes the chain's mechanism, never bare sequence, and runs as a complete set once started.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

**1.6 — State the amazing thing flat; never pad credibility.** Banned: "that part is real," "he
actually did," "he really did," "seriously," "genuinely audacious," "let that sink in," "mind-blowing."
State the insane fact plainly and let the *viewer* be the one amazed. This is the voice-level half of
`dna.md`'s earned-magnitude rule: a number that needs an adjective bolted on to feel big is a number
that isn't big enough, and the fix is a better number or a better comparison (§2.8), never a louder
word. **Heat is carried by the delivery, not by insistence:** capitals on a word the voice would lean
on, and the knowing stock phrase a friend uses to build something up before revealing it, are both
correct, because they are how the line is *said*. What stays dead is trailer drama, the clipped fragment
that manufactures a mood while carrying no joke and no new fact ("Everything changed." / "For good.").
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

## 2. The story

**2.1 — The hook gets them in the door; it does not tell the story.** Its one job is intrigue: leave the
viewer holding exactly one question the story now owes them. **The wedge is not optional: open inside
the viewer's own ordinary action, in the present, before any system is named** — the tap, the flush, the
send, the flip. Opening on the structure makes us Practical Engineering; opening on the object sitting
there makes us Branch Education (`reference-channels.md` §Wedge defense; `dna.md` names both as
refusals). Put the viewer's own body in the first sentence.

The **shape inside the wedge is open**, and inventing a new one is fair game. Three that work: the
**scale-fact turn**, where the action is followed by one startling sourced number and then the
how-question that licenses the whole runtime; the **myth-bust open**, which states the received belief
about what just happened and complicates it in the very next sentence; and the **impossible-fact
open**, which states the settled, impossible-sounding consequence of the action and withholds the
mechanism. Concrete stakes (a count, a distance, a mass) go wherever they land hardest.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

What stays dead is outlining the story's beats ("first this happens, then that"), "welcome back," and
the still life: a scene with nothing happening in it, a system left unnamed to manufacture mystery, a
fact stated so abstractly that nothing is moving in the sentence. Naming the action, the object, and the
number in the first breath costs nothing and buys the viewer something to hold.

**Object- or number-led packaging → the open still starts in the action:** when the title or thumbnail
promises a specific object or a specific number, that promise gets paid inside the first two sentences —
but the first sentence is still the viewer doing the ordinary thing, not the object sitting on a table.
The wedge is an entry point, not a withholding device.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

**2.2 — Select by story value; develop what you keep.** `research.md` is a pool, not a checklist — and
on this channel it is a deliberately capped pool (`dna.md`: `research_scope: capped-to-descent-chain`),
which makes the selection job sharper, not looser. Cut the beats that don't carry the descent; give the
ones that do their real color — the physical object, the concrete detail, the telling number. Three
inert research paragraphs collapse to one beat, but a beat that carries the story earns more than a
hurried sentence. The pipeline's most stubborn miss is stuffing in facts because they're sourced; the
opposite miss — racing through a layer that deserved room — is just as real.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

**A fact enters when the story needs it, and not one beat earlier.** Sourced material sitting in
`research.md` has no claim on a position; put each fact where it does work. **Scale is the exception
worth planning for: it lands early, and it lands as a comparison**, because a raw figure means nothing
on its own and a comparison the viewer already has a feel for means everything (§2.8).

**2.3 — Let the story set the length inside the 8–10 minute band, natural center near the top of it.**
Word target still follows the **channel voice's measured wpm** — recorded in `dna.md` once the voice is
locked — as a center of gravity within the band, not a universal constant. That measured rate is gross,
so it already carries the narrator's natural pausing: runtime is words divided by it, and
`lint_script.py` computes the number. Landing well under usually means layers are under-developed
(starved of color), not that filler is missing; running long usually means dwell (§2.5). Add length only
as a genuine further drop or a genuine second branch, never padding — branching or extending to fill the
clock breaks `dna.md`'s content-density test and the earned-magnitude rule with it.

**2.4 — Motifs and callbacks carry the telling.** Plant a phrase, an image, or a number and reuse it so
it pays off: the figure named in the opening action can return at the bottom of the descent with no new
setup, meaning something completely different now. A recurring analogy is a callback too. **The plant is
invisible.** The line does its whole job the first time it appears and simply comes back later; the
moment the script tells the viewer to hold onto something because it matters later, the payoff is spent
before it arrives and the machinery is showing.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

**2.5 — Color, not dwell.** *Color* is a new, concrete, specific detail that makes a beat vivid: what a
piece of the system physically is, what it weighs, where it sits, how many there are. *Dwell* is
restating a point after it has landed. **The test: does the line add a new fact or image, or
re-emphasize one already said?** Add the first, cut the second. A line that survives with zero
information removed is filler. Lean toward color — the miss we correct most is a draft gone too terse,
and on a mechanism channel a terse draft is indistinguishable from a recitation.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

**2.6 — Design for retention before drafting.** These are outline decisions, not post-hoc patches: open
on the action in the very first lines (no logo, no throat-clearing); give a real reason to stay inside
the first ~15 seconds; keep new stimulus arriving beat to beat; re-arm the tension at the mandatory
55–65% mark, which on this channel means a new drop — one more layer, a second branch, or a number that
resets a scale the viewer thought they had already grasped; and hold the single biggest scale beat for
the final stretch instead of front-loading everything.

**2.7 — A descent establishes each layer before dropping through it, and motivates every drop out
loud.** The bottom of the chain only lands if the viewer stood on every floor above it: what this layer
physically is, what it looks like, and how big it already is, before anything goes underneath it.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

From there, every drop says out loud what causes it. The chain is spoken, not implied: the action needs
this, this only works because of that, that exists only because of the thing under it. The question the
previous layer left open is the reason we are going deeper, and the script says so.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

A layer that merely comes next, with no because, is where the viewer quietly stops caring — that is the
chapter grab-bag the niche playbook warns about, a parts tour wearing a descent's clothes.

**2.8 — The scale-comparison toolbox.** Magnitude has to be **felt, not stated**. A raw figure is a
fact; a comparison is an experience, and this channel's entire lever is the gap between a tiny human
action and a colossal response being *experienced*. Reach for these:

- **Stacked comparison** — one comparison lands the size, and a second reframes the first, so the
  magnitude arrives in two moves instead of one. Two is a build; four is a lecture.
- **Humanizing units** — convert a rate or a total into a unit the body already owns: per second becomes
  per heartbeat, per blink, per breath; a distance becomes a travel time; a mass becomes things the
  viewer has actually lifted; a count becomes people in a room, a stadium, a city.
- **The one-more-zoom** — the viewer thinks they have grasped the number, and the line pulls the frame
  out once more to show that the number they were holding was the *small* one. Costly and powerful; once
  per video, at the withheld peak.
- **Rate over total** — a huge lifetime total is abstract, while the same thing happening *during this
  sentence* is not. Prefer the rate whenever the ledger supports both.
- **Trigger against response** — the channel's lever stated as a comparison: the size of the human
  action set directly beside the size of what it moved. Every video owes this one at least once.
- **Physical anchoring** — give a number a body: weight, temperature, how much room it takes up, how far
  it reaches. A magnitude the viewer cannot place is not scale, it is trivia.

**The bar, and it is `dna.md`'s earned-magnitude rule in working form:** the number is sourced in
`research.md`'s fact-ledger (§4), and the comparison is honest at the precision the ledger supports.
Rounding up for effect, picking the flattering unit, choosing the framing that makes a modest system
sound enormous — each of those is scale-inflation, the pitfall this niche is judged on, and it reads as
slop the moment a skeptical viewer checks. If the honest number isn't big, the topic was wrong, not the
number. And a comparison the viewer cannot picture instantly is not a comparison; it is a second number.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

## 3. The structure

**3.1 — Impose a shape; refuse the parts tour.** Design the sequence in the outline before drafting.
The enemy here is not the calendar, it is the inventory: the order a reference article lists a system's
components is never the order that lands. Sequence for where the vertigo hits hardest. The two licensed
shapes are set in `dna.md` — one action → one descent by default, one action → branching systems only
when a single honest chain runs short.

**3.2 — Action-hook, then the first drop.** Open inside the action (§2.1), then go through the surface
inside the opening stretch — that transition is the cutaway-reveal beat (§3.8), and it runs straight into
the layer discipline (§2.7). Nothing is gained by circling the action for a minute before descending;
the promise of the hook is that we are going somewhere.

**3.3 — Cross-cut, but consolidate — don't ping-pong.** Where a video takes the branching shape, weave
the threads but finish one branch cleanly before cutting, with a casual signpost that says plainly where
we are now. One clean cut beats four choppy ones. Each branch returns to the same opening action before
the next one starts, so the viewer never loses the doorway they came in through.

**3.4 — Casual transitions, question turns, and forward-promise seams.** Spoken connective tissue and
the question-answered-casually move are this channel's workhorse seam, because a descent is a chain of
questions each layer raises about the one beneath it. What is dead is the literary connector ("which
brings us to," "little did they know") and the announced category ("here's the strange part," "the
uncomfortable question," "piece one/two/three"): **deliver the moment, don't announce its category**, and
open on the content itself. A plain spoken doorway that opens the next stretch is normal speech, not an
announced transition. A beat exits on an open loop or forward promise the viewer stays to collect, and
escalation is honest ("it gets bigger" is followed by something actually bigger).

**3.5 — Stop the way a friend stops.** The close is casual, brisk, and unceremonious, usually landing
one last laugh or one last plain fact. Banned: the essay conclusion ("here's what it means," "and that's
why"), the moral, the instruction to be amazed, and the crafted profundity written to sound resonant,
because a line reaching for meaning drains everything the story just earned. Nobody is being taught
anything at the end; the insight was woven into the body. The *shape* the close takes on this channel is
§3.9.

**3.6 — Tension without a mystery.** Our titles and thumbnails usually give away the destination — the
object, the number, the punchline — so tension cannot ride on what the answer turns out to be. It rides
on how-exactly and how-much-bigger: the assembly of the mechanism step by step, the widening gap between
what the viewer assumed was happening and what actually is, and the standing sense that we are not at
the bottom yet. Letting the viewer see one more layer coming before we take it is the sincere cousin of
comic dread, and it is the main engine on a pre-spoiled topic.

**3.7 — Non-linear, but followable.** Jumping around is the craft; the bar is that a first-time viewer
can follow the causal through-line. Introduce a system, a place, or an operator before the story leans
on it; establish a connection before a beat depends on it. The line is *withheld* versus
*unestablished*: a question the story clearly raises and answers later is suspense (good); a connection
used as if already understood is a hole. On a descent the specific version of that hole is leaning on a
layer the viewer has not yet stood on. Structural confusion gets a structural fix — resequence — never
an explaining sentence pasted onto the confusing beat.

**3.8 — The cutaway-reveal beat.** The named beat where the ordinary surface splits open: the viewer's
action is the whole world of the video, everything is completely normal, and then the frame goes
*through* the surface into the machinery that was running underneath the entire time. This is the
doorway the wedge exists to build, it happens once, in the opening stretch, and it is the single most
important transition in the script.

- **The surface is established first.** The mundane thing has to be genuinely mundane for a beat — the
  hand, the object, the ordinary second — or there is nothing to split open.
- **The turn is one sentence, and it is concrete.** Not "but there's more going on than you think"
  (an announced category, §3.4), and not a rhetorical question. The line names the first real thing on
  the other side: the physical object, the place, the number.
- **The reveal moves the frame, not the subject.** After the cutaway we are still inside the same second
  of the viewer's life, looking at what it set off. The depth changed; the topic did not.
- **It fires once at full strength.** Later drops are steps (§2.7). Re-firing the reveal move at every
  layer spends it and turns the descent into a series of small gasps.
- **Write it for the picture.** This is the beat where the visuals earn most, so the line should give
  them one obvious literal move to make. (The voiceover stays pure prose — §1.1 — the visual decision
  lives in the shot plan, not in `script.md`.)
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

**3.9 — The resurface ending.** The close returns to the exact action the video opened on, now
transformed by everything underneath it. The viewer does the same small thing again — same hand, same
object, same second — except now they know what it sets off, and the video stops there. This is what
shuts the vertigo → wonder arc: *the machine was always this big; you just never looked.*

- **Same action, specific.** Resurfacing to a general version of the opening ("next time you use
  technology…") is a moral wearing a callback's clothes. Return to the literal thing the video opened on.
- **The transformation is carried by what the viewer now knows.** Never announce it: "you'll never look
  at this the same way again" is the banned form of this beat. Show the action once more; the difference
  is already in the viewer's head, and saying it out loud takes the credit for it.
- **Land on wonder, not dread.** A close that lands on how fragile it all is, how easily it fails, or
  how exposed the viewer is belongs to a different channel's lever and is refused in `dna.md`.
- **One scale beat may resurface with it,** as a callback with no new setup (§2.4): the number from the
  opening returning at the bottom, or the bottom's number set beside the size of the action.
- **It still stops like a friend stopping** (§3.5): no summary of the descent, no recap of the layers, no
  "and that's why." The last line is the action, or a laugh, or a plain fact — not a thesis.
> *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

## 4. Staging & facts

- **Casual is not loose with facts.** The friendly voice stays leashed to `research.md`. Correct what's
  wrong, and never invent to fill a hole.
- **Every number is ledgered.** This is a scale-claims channel (`dna.md`: `research: deep`), so weight,
  distance, count, speed, and rate all trace to a fact in `research.md`'s ledger — and a comparison built
  on a number (§2.8) inherits that number's precision. A figure that is "about right" is not right. An
  unsourced magnitude is the defect this channel can least afford, because the entire lever rests on the
  audience believing the size.
- **Hedging happens in fact selection, never in the narration.** Deciding which version of a contested
  fact is solid enough to tell is the writer's job, done before the line exists. A claim either goes in
  as the strongest version the ledger supports, stated flat, or it does not go in. "By one account,"
  "sources disagree," "reportedly," "the record doesn't support" and every cousin of theirs are banned
  from the voiceover: the viewer hears a narrator apologising for his own story, and the beat dies.
- **A genuine gap in the record gets transparent speculation, in the narrator's voice.** When nobody
  knows why a system works the way it does and the story needs a why, say plainly that nobody knows,
  float the live possibilities as guesses, and move on inside the same breath.
  > *[example — seeded from first approved HM script]*

  The viewer can hear that this is a guess, which is exactly the point: nothing is passed off as fact,
  and nobody has been made to sit through a confession about sourcing. This is the only sanctioned way
  a hole in the research reaches the audio.
- **Reported speech is the default telling mode** wherever people speak at all. Dramatized beats land
  best as the narrator *characterizing* what was said, with attitude — and a paraphrase may never add a
  detail the source doesn't support.
- **Attribution discipline:** these systems are run by named companies and agencies. Present the
  documented facts and let any conclusion be the viewer's inference, never the channel's accusation;
  where a real failure or dispute is part of the descent, dramatize the *act of checking* before
  anything that reads as a charge, and land the payoff on the *system*, not on a person or a firm.
- **Staging craft:** every layer gets one concrete physical object the viewer can hold in their head —
  abstraction is exactly where a descent goes dry — and the analogy-that-reveals, which explains the
  mechanism *and* makes its size felt in the same line.

## 6. The joke toolbox (reach for these; never force them)

All fact-riding, and used **often** (§1.3 — abundance is the default, flatness is the defect):

- **Modern-comparison analogy that teaches the mechanism** (the workhorse): the unfamiliar process
  explained by the familiar one, with the mismatch between them carrying the laugh. The same move closes
  a beat as a kicker.
- **Deadpan undercut / bathos:** the enormous build-up resolved by the small, true, anticlimactic fact.
- **Ironic re-label:** renaming the thing in plain, unflattering language that happens to be accurate.
- **Comic false precision:** an exact ledgered number delivered for deadpan authority.
- **Deflate the mystique** (never the scale): the awesome-sounding system turns out to be a wire, a
  spreadsheet, a person with a clipboard — the *machinery* is mundane while the *size* stays enormous.
- **Viewer-solidarity aside:** the narrator admits he had no idea either, and assumed the same wrong
  thing the viewer did — the discovering-with stance turned into a laugh.
- **Playing out the viewer's own inner monologue:** voicing what the viewer *thought* was happening in
  that ordinary second, in their words, right before showing what actually was.
- **Irreverent reaction:** the narrator says out loud what the viewer is thinking at a number.
- **Franchise pull that carries the fact:** a long-lived cultural reference that does explanatory work,
  not decoration (§1.4's universality bar governs it).
- **The knowing build-up before the reveal:** the stock phrase a friend uses to talk something up, spent
  right before the cutaway or the drop.
- **Scale by modern comparison:** the magnitude landed on something the viewer already has a feel for
  (§2.8 is the full toolbox; the funny version is a comparison that is exact *and* absurd).
- **Narrator at his own expense:** the narrator's own ordinary habits set against what they set in motion.
- **Genre re-label:** naming the kind of story this stretch of machinery has turned into.
- **The real fact is the punchline:** the true, sourced, absurd detail delivered flat, with no joke
  around it, because none is needed.

**The humor bar:** a joke ships only if it clears the universality bar (§1.4), the words are plain, the
narrator states the insane fact flat and lets the *viewer* be the one amazed, and the magnitude survives
the laugh (§1.3). It also has to actually land: warm or dry, a swing that misses costs energy and
returns nothing, so cut it rather than keep it for texture. Punch up at power, at scale, and at
absurdity — never at the viewer for not knowing, because the narrator didn't know either until he
looked. Profanity rides along at the "shit" grade and no further.
