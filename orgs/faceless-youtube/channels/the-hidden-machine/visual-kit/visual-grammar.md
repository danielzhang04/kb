# The Hidden Machine — Visual Grammar (staging law)

**What this is:** how The Hidden Machine stages shots — the doc `visual-prompt-writer` reads when
authoring `shots.json`: staging conventions, scale-driven composition, mode routing, and how our
lever bends the universal shot-classes. Register / prompt blocks / seed law live in `style-bible.md`;
the live asset vocabulary is `registry/registry.json`; writing craft is `../storytelling-grammar.md`.

The narration-type → shot-class table, the within-shot motion grammar, the cut-cadence +
stretch-to-fill rule, and the core doctrine live in `universal.md` §13/§13a and are **BINDING** —
this doc does not restate them.

**Procedure:** read the line → identify its narration type → pick the shot class (`universal.md
§13a`) → **route it to a render mode (§4)** → INVENT a fresh, on-register shot. Everything below is
how to stage "on-register" here.

---

## 1. Staging conventions — the system is the character

- **No on-screen narrator, and no ensemble.** One recurring human, **the everyman**
  (`style-bible.md §4`), and the machinery. Everything else on screen is infrastructure or the
  ordinary world above it.
- **The everyman is a SCALE UNIT, not a protagonist.** He exists to establish the ratio the beat is
  about. He acts, then he is dwarfed or absent; he is never named, never speaks, never carries the
  beat on his face. When he reappears at the end, that return *is* the wonder landing.
- **Open inside the human action, never on the machine** (`dna.md §Doctrine` — the wedge). The cold
  open stages an ordinary moment at body or object scale in a warm human environment
  (`refs/env/coffee-shop.png`); the system is unnamed and unseen until the descent begins. A frame
  that reveals the machine before the action has landed breaks the wedge.
- **Machines are staged as actors, not diagrams-of-record.** Two racks facing each other across a
  link, a terminal wrapping something, a route threading a hall — machinery *doing* the verb the
  narration names. This is `universal.md §13a`'s personification row realized without ever giving a
  machine a face.
- **Object and hand macro is native here** — the wedge's own framing is a hand, a card, a terminal at
  contact scale. Unlike a rig-based channel, this register renders hands and objects cleanly at macro
  distance; `refs/env/coffee-shop.png` is itself a hand-on-terminal macro.
- **Human-scale figures inside a system are silhouettes for scale**, not characters. They carry no
  identity, no expression, no costume claim — a figure on a walkway exists to be small.
- **Reuse the locked world before inventing one.** The three environment canonicals cover the wedge
  interior, the inside-the-system hall, and the world-above street. A new location is a deliberate
  build (`style-bible.md §7`), not a per-shot invention.

## 2. Composition — scale contrast is the signature

The channel's one composition, returned to deliberately across every video: **a tiny human-scale
element and a vast system in the same frame, at true relative size.** Reach for it first; everything
else is a variation on it.

- **Scale must be EARNED and honest.** The human element is genuinely small against a system that is
  genuinely large. A ratio faked by lens, crop, or foreshortening is a doctrine failure
  (`style-bible.md §3`) — the same rule that forbids inflating a number to manufacture the lever.
- **THE DESCENT is the recurring staging metaphor.** The vertical axis carries depth-into-the-system:
  an ordinary surface opened in cutaway cross-section onto the layers below, each step down cooler
  and darker, one continuous warm signal thread tracing the path from the human action to the bottom.
  A descent shot can carry a whole act.
- **Vertigo → wonder, never dread.** Composition and palette land the arc: the bottom of a descent is
  vast and quiet, never menacing. No alarm cues, no ruin or abandonment staging, no threat lighting.
  The warm thread persisting to the bottom is what keeps vertigo from becoming fear.
- **Come back up.** A descent that never returns to human scale leaves the viewer at the bottom;
  the resolution frame re-establishes the ordinary world (`refs/env/street-corner.png`), now read
  differently.
- **Escalate by multiplying, not by redrawing** (`universal.md §13a` rule 9): more racks, more halls,
  more cable, the same system seen further out.
- **Dense compositions are also the safe ones.** Wide establishing exteriors with open sky and ground
  on every side are the border-drift path (`style-bible.md §2c`); tight, deep, edge-filling framings
  hold containment *and* read as scale. When a beat genuinely wants a wide, author it and budget the
  retry — don't quietly downgrade it to a medium.
- **Negative space is vertical here.** Where a standard channel gives a single graphic idea air, this
  one gives it depth below and mass above.

## 3. Lever translation — how scale-shock bends each shot-class

Our locked lever (`dna.md §Doctrine`) decides HOW we execute the universal shot-classes:

- **Scale-as-argument is not one row in the table, it is the house move.** Any class can be staged as
  a size relationship, and where a beat carries a magnitude, it must be.
- **Concrete object + startling number is the proven converter** — a scale-fact lands on a tangible
  thing (a data card at the payload beat, a mass at true size), never as decoration on a scene that
  is already doing the work.
- **Abstract force / property → the infrastructure that embodies it**, not a personified creature.
  Latency is a route across a hall; capacity is a rack row receding; reach is a cable leaving a wall
  into deep water.
- **Mechanism / "how it really works" → mechanism mode (§4).** This is `universal.md §13a`'s
  register-shift row realized: the cleaner register **is** the signal that you are now seeing the
  real machinery.
- **Grim / failure beats are out of register, not just softened.** Fragility, alarmism, and
  disaster-adjacent staging are a *different channel's lever* and are rejected outright
  (`dna.md §Doctrine` — named refusals), not toned down into aftermath shots.
- **No comedic deflation and no competence-lecture staging.** A punchline cutaway that undercuts the
  scale, or a frame that admires the engineers rather than the size, is off-lever even when it is a
  good image.
- **Ironic counterpoint is available but rare** — this channel's contrast is *scale vs. ordinariness*
  (a thumb's movement against a continent of machinery), not image-contradicts-words.

## 4. Mode routing — the channel-specific decision every shot makes

Every shot declares one of three render modes before its prompt is written (blocks and anchors in
`style-bible.md §2/§2b/§2d`):

| The beat is… | Mode |
| --- | --- |
| the wedge, a human moment, an environment, mood, arrival, the walk-away | **full-texture R1** |
| how-it-works, a mechanism acting, a cutaway, a labelled part, machines exchanging | **mechanism mode** |
| a stat, a count, a duration, a distance — the scale payload | **data card** |

- **Mode switches carry meaning; don't churn them.** Dropping into mechanism mode announces "here is
  the real machinery," and coming back out announces the return to the world. A video that alternates
  every shot spends the signal.
- **Never ask a full-texture frame to carry data.** A number goes on a data card or a diegetic
  surface — never floated over a textured scene.
- **Data cards are `universal.md §13`'s number-glue rule satisfied, not an exception to it:** the card
  is a designed, baked, full-bleed surface in the register — the same-inks paper-print artifact the
  number lives *on* — and never a render-time caption or overlay. Reserve them for the payload beats;
  three cards in a video is a lot.
- **Mechanism-mode frames are still scenes**, composed with the same depth and density discipline as
  §2. Drafting clarity is a render mode, not permission for a floating diagram on empty ground.

## 5. Motion direction — our dial

- **Every still moves.** An eased Ken Burns push or pan runs for the still's full on-screen duration —
  data cards and mechanism frames included. This channel has **no true statics**, overriding
  `universal.md §13a-iii` rule 1's dead-static text/diagram default.
- **Generated clips are reserved and budgeted:** 15–20 motion-worthy beats per 8–10 minute video
  (`style-bible.md §9`). A beat earns a clip by carrying real motion — the ordinary action itself, a
  mechanism acting, a scale reveal — never to animate a static idea.
- **Camera stays locked or barely drifting inside a clip**; the descent is staged in the frame, not
  driven by a camera move.
- **Hard cuts only.** No transitions, no fades, no cards.
- **No burned captions in long-form.** All text is a data card, baked at generation time.
- **Cadence follows the beat, not a number.** Enumeration and scale-fact beats run fast; descent,
  mechanism, and resolution beats hold longer with a within-shot move rather than being cut
  mid-sentence. Values and the >8s progressive-reveal rule: `universal.md §13a-ii/§13a-iii`.
