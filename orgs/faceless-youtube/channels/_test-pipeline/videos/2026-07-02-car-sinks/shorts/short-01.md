## Payload (self-contained, closes in-short)
A sinking car floats for up to two minutes on trapped air, but its electrics only last about sixty
seconds in the water — so the float is a countdown on your power windows. Drop the window while the
dash is still lit. Closes in-short; no long-form needed.

## VO + cues

[B-ROLL: frame one already mid-sink — first-person POV, nose tipped down, dash lit, water climbing the
windshield. Motion on frame one.]
A sinking car floats. [BEAT] For about a minute — and that minute is the part that gets people.

[B-ROLL: exterior — the car bobbing, headlights glowing under the surface, air pockets outlined in the
doors.]
Trapped air in the cabin and the doors holds it up. Thirty seconds. Sometimes two whole minutes. Long
enough to feel safe. [PAUSE] It isn't.

[B-ROLL: interior — dashboard still glowing, water creeping up the outside of the door.]
Watch the dash, not the water. While those lights are on, your windows still work — and a car's
electrics get about sixty seconds under before they short out and quit.

[B-ROLL: a hand drops the power window while the glass is still above the waterline.]
So drop the window while the switch still answers. Not the door. The door won't budge, and the ocean
is very insistent about that.

[B-ROLL: the dash flickers dead; a finger presses a switch that does nothing; cut to black.]
Once the lights go, that switch is just a button. One minute of float. Spend it on the glass.
