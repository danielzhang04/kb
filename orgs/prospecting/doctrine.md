# Affinity copy doctrine

This is a directional copy doctrine for informational networking, not a claim
that B2B sales benchmarks transfer unchanged to finance-professional outreach.
The 50–125-word body band comes from Overloop's 2026 aggregation and a roughly
75-word Boomerang peak; the 36–50-character subject band comes from
Backlinko/Pitchbox (2019). Those sources study B2B sales and link-building
datasets, not this use case. Their direction informs a bounded experiment; their
percentages do not.

P3's body-word count excludes the first and final body lines, so the greeting
and signature are already outside the measured band.

## Ranked signals

| Rank | Signal | Copy treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong | Shared school, prior employer, or coherent career path | Use when evidence-backed and specific |
| Medium | Writing, board/portfolio work, firm thesis, role family, or level | Use as a supporting fact when evidence-backed |
| Weak | Hometown, interest, or activity | Do not use in copy |

Never imply a mutual relationship or consent that is not present in evidence.
Use one verifiable reason, make the learning objective clear, and ask one
specific question only.

## Structures by goal

| Goal | Structure |
| --- | --- |
| Startup operations | Career-path observation → operating question → short call ask |
| Investing | Thesis or portfolio observation → perspective question → short call ask |
| Recruiting conversation | Relevant transition → role-learning question → short call ask |
| Alumni or curiosity | Shared institutional/context signal → focused question → short call ask |

## Cadence

| Touch | Timing | Required new value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial | Day 0 | One evidence-backed affinity signal |
| Follow-up | Day 7–10 | One new fact or a graceful reduction in ask |

The default is two touches. A small, reputation-sensitive list should not receive
an automated third touch without a separate human decision.

## Commonality-to-sentence patterns

1. A shared school made a career transition worth understanding.
2. A prior-employer connection made a role choice concrete.
3. A path from one employer kind to another suggests a useful transition lesson.
4. A role-family overlap creates a focused operating question.
5. A level transition can frame a question about scope and judgment.
6. A published piece can support a question about the reasoning behind it.
7. A board role can support a question about the operating perspective it adds.
8. A portfolio item can support a question about selection criteria.
9. A firm thesis can support a question about its practical implications.
10. A current role can support a question about day-to-day work.
11. A prior role can support a question about how a transition was evaluated.
12. A follow-up can add one new fact before offering call or email questions.

These are structures, not fill-in claims: each rendered sentence still needs
stored, copy-allowed evidence and must pass the P3 validators.
