# DRAFT — Top-eight automated income projects: commercial deep dive

_Research date: 2026-07-22. Prepared from the 2026-07-21 project-portfolio handoff plus bounded current-market research. This is a planning artifact, not a forecast or investment recommendation._

_Inputs: [asset-first automated income dossier](asset-first-income-projects-2026-07-21.md) and [solo automation-first portfolio](solo-automated-project-portfolio-2026-07-21.md)._

## Executive verdict

The prior ranking is directionally right, but it double-counts one opportunity. Models 1, 2, 4, and 6 are best understood as one **decision-data property**:

- calculators provide the immediate user value;
- a tracker supplies fresh prices, availability, or status;
- a verified directory supplies canonical entities and possible actions;
- alerts create repeat use and direct distribution.

The newsletter is the property's retention and distribution layer, not a separate first build. The app is a later surface for a workflow already proven on the web. Acquisition is a way to enter or accelerate a model, not a business model by itself.

The practical portfolio is therefore:

1. **Primary bet: one calculator-led decision-data property**, selected through a one-week niche tournament.
2. **Parallel diversifier: a commercially specific footage/motion/SFX licensing catalog.**
3. **Gated extension: one cross-platform utility app**, only after the web property proves repeat use.
4. **Later accelerator: acquire an adjacent utility/data asset**, only after the operator understands the niche and can absorb a total loss.

Do not launch eight production systems. Launch two. Treat the remaining models as modules, channels, gates, or later capital allocation decisions.

## 1. High-intent decision utility + alerts

### Best formulation

Build one narrow property around an expensive or repeated decision. It should combine a user-specific calculation, fresh source-grounded data, stored history, a saved scenario or alert, and at least two transactional partners.

The strongest niche shapes from the handoff remain:

- trade-equipment rent/buy and used-equipment total cost;
- RV connectivity and power planning;
- shipping/3PL dimensional cost and break-even;
- home-energy upgrade, rebate, and operating-cost decisions;
- cloud storage/GPU/backup total cost.

Choose through evidence, not intuition. Each niche must clear lawful data access, two monetization rails, a costly user decision, repeat/change frequency, and an accuracy definition.

### Money sources

1. Referral or affiliate action from a completed result.
2. Qualified lead routing after consent and attribution are proven.
3. Clearly separated sponsorship or featured placement.
4. Display advertising after repeat traffic exists.
5. Later data/API licensing or an asset sale.

Do not let one merchant produce more than 35% of revenue. Amazon-style commissions can supplement the model but should not carry it; program terms and economics can change.

### Crowding and differentiation

Generic comparison sites are crowded. A constraint-aware decision engine is less so. The moat is not prose or an attractive front end; it is canonical entities, normalized history, source provenance, model assumptions, user-specific calculations, saved state, and trusted correction handling.

This category is not dying. Generic informational pages are deteriorating: Pew found traditional-result clicks in 8% of Google visits with an AI summary versus 15% without one, and Google now explicitly warns against scaled low-value generated pages. Interactive decisions and fresh alerts are harder to replace than summaries. [Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), [Google Search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)

### Automation and human floor

Automate ingestion, normalization, diffing, calculations, alert queues, stale-data suppression, QA sampling, analytics, and publishing. Human work remains in source-rights review, entity-match exceptions, high-impact change verification, model assumptions, partner replacement, and reported errors.

Reject a niche if exception handling grows linearly with its record count. A stabilized asset should fall below two owner-hours a week only after at least four clean operating weeks.

### 90-day development and push-out plan

- Days 1–7: run the niche tournament; document two sources, two monetization rails, 30 real user queries, and a measurable error cost for each finalist.
- Days 8–30: create 50–150 canonical entities, provenance/freshness records, one calculation, analytics, and a basic result page.
- Days 31–60: ship three connected tools, saved scenarios, an alert, and ten real-user tests.
- Days 61–90: publish only useful entity/decision pages; distribute through permitted niche communities, supplier/educator relationships, demonstrations, and opt-in email.

Initial cash: roughly $150–800 before paid data. Continue at day 90 with any of: 1,000 qualified monthly users, 100 returning users, 100 email subscribers with 25 saved alerts, or five verified monetizable actions. Kill if freshness is below 98%, match precision below 97%, corrections exceed 48 hours, or manual exceptions exceed one hour per week per 100 active entities.

## 2. Price/deal/status tracker

### Best formulation

Do not build a general consumer price tracker. Google Shopping and Chrome already expose price history, target-price tracking, drop notifications, and shopping insights. The generic lane is packed and being absorbed into search/browser agents. [Google Shopping price tracking](https://support.google.com/googleshopping/answer/13971184), [Chrome Shopping Insights](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/11625545)

Track a state that broad shopping systems model poorly:

- refurbished condition, warranty, and compatible alternatives;
- used industrial/trade equipment and delivered total cost;
- scarce regional inventory;
- auction lots with fees, freight, and condition;
- specialist software plan or entitlement changes;
- permit, appointment, or application status.

The alert should mean “this now fits your decision,” not merely “the number changed.”

### Money sources and earning logic

Use merchant-approved referral feeds, high-value lead routing, relevant sponsors, ads, and eventually aggregate market reports where source terms permit. The base case depends on high-value actions and repeat alerts, not coupon-site traffic.

The lane can still earn, but the plain version is commoditizing. Its durable value comes from condition/warranty normalization, compatibility, local availability, total delivered cost, provenance, history, and a very low false-alert rate.

### Automation and launch

Polling/webhooks, deduplication, thresholds, alert delivery, anomaly detection, and stale suppression automate well. Humans must resolve variants, condition errors, misleading deals, source changes, and policy compliance.

Start with 100 entities and five test alerts. Expand to 500–1,000 only after 97% match precision. By day 90 require 50 saved alerts, at least 15 repeat alert users, and five verified commercial actions; alternatively require 1,000 qualified sessions with at least 10% saving/tracking behavior. Treat email opens as diagnostic only because privacy proxies distort them. Kill if false alerts exceed 2%, freshness drops below 98%, an essential source prohibits collection, or reconciliation exceeds two hours a week.

**Decision:** this is a feature of project #1, not a second codebase, domain, or audience.

## 3. Multi-marketplace footage, motion, and SFX catalog

### Best formulation

Create commercially specific asset families that save an editor real time:

- hard-to-source regional or procedural B-roll;
- clean mechanical and ambient sound effects;
- motion/data-visualization components and overlays;
- distinct shot families mapped to buyer use cases.

Avoid generic AI people, decorative loops, cosmetic prompt variations, imitation styles, trademarks, and weakly documented rights.

### Money sources

Adobe currently pays 33% on non-video assets and 35% on video. Pond5 pays 30% for non-exclusive video and other media, or 40% for exclusive video; its dataset licenses pay 20%. Keep dataset licensing as optional upside, not the base case. [Adobe Stock royalties](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/payments-earnings/royalties-pricing/royalty-rates-assets.html), [Pond5 payout overview](https://contributor.pond5.com/getting-started/payout-overview/)

Start non-exclusive on Adobe and Pond5. Add a third marketplace only after metadata and upload syndication work. Preserve masters, releases, provenance, keywords, and platform-specific IDs.

### Crowding, durability, and automation

Generic stock is heavily saturated. Rights-clean procedural footage, functional SFX, and production-ready components remain differentiated because their value is a specific saved production step. The industry is changing, not dying; platform pricing, generative supply, and discovery concentration are real risks.

Automate batch rendering/transcoding, loudness and technical checks, metadata drafts, duplicate detection, manifests, uploads, and reporting. Human work remains in capture, creative direction, releases/rights, final selection, and interpreting sales. Adobe explicitly warns against excessive similar generative submissions, reinforcing usefulness over volume. [Adobe distinctness guidance](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/submit-your-content/submit-generative-ai-content/distinct-generative-ai-submission-best-practices.html)

### 90-day development and push-out plan

- Week 1: choose three commercial use cases and define rights, release, and asset-registry rules.
- Weeks 2–4: produce 30–50 assets in two formats; learn rejection reasons.
- Weeks 5–8: reach 100 approved, distinct assets; automate manifests and metadata QA.
- Weeks 9–13: reach 150–250 approved assets and double down only on families with licenses or strong acceptance/search signals.

Initial cash: roughly $100–1,500 using existing equipment. Do not buy specialized gear before a demand signal. At day 90, 100 approved assets, 70% approval, and metadata/upload below 20% of production time are quality and operability gates—not proof of buyer demand. Kill a family after 25 assets and 120 days with zero licenses. Stop net-new production at month six unless 100 assets have produced at least 15 paid licenses or $50 plus an improving three-month trend; paid demand is the decisive commercial gate.

## 4. Verified niche directory + change alerts

### Best formulation

The asset is a trusted change-history database, not a list of businesses. Strong cases use authoritative records whose changes lead to action: licenses, recalls, compatibility, procurement, grants, permits, inspections, or regulated-provider status.

### Money sources

Use high-intent referrals, transparent sponsored placements separated from ranking, ads after repeat traffic, consented lead routing, and later data/API licensing. Do not depend on Google Places as the source of record: Google restricts storing and caching Places content beyond defined exceptions. [Google Places policies](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/policies)

### Crowding and stability

“Best X near me” is a packed and fragile lane. Canonical identifiers, primary-source provenance, timestamps, transparent eligibility, change history, corrections, and saved alerts create the differentiation.

Durability is medium-high with primary/open data and direct/email return traffic; it is low with scraped listings, geography-template SEO pages, or a single commercial API. Human correction disputes and ambiguous entity merges keep practical automation closer to 75–85%.

### 90-day plan and gates

Tournament three verticals. Each needs lawful terms, two money rails, at least 500 candidate entities or meaningful changes, and a useful weekly change rate. Launch 150–300 verified entities, one alert type, and ten decision/action pages by day 60. Recruit through niche communities and source-adjacent partners, not mass SEO.

Continue at day 90 with 750 qualified monthly users, 75 alert subscribers at 35% opens, 25 repeat users, or five monetizable actions. Kill on unclear rights, freshness below 98%, matches below 97%, corrections slower than 48 hours, or more than one owner-hour per week per 100 entities.

**Decision:** add this layer to project #1 only after the calculator and source economics pass.

## 5. One ad-supported cross-platform utility app

### Best formulation

Build one account-free, privacy-light, offline-capable app around a recurring field workflow: trade measurement/cut/yield, equipment maintenance, shipping/package optimization, RV power/connectivity, or creator production utilities.

Do not build cloned calculators. Google requires meaningful functionality, and Apple expects an app to offer more than a repackaged website. Store fees on paid digital transactions also constrain paid models; Apple offers qualifying small developers a 15% commission rate, while Google Play's fee structure varies by program and region. [Google Play functionality policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898783), [Apple Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/), [Google Play service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622)

### Money sources and economics

Use unobtrusive ads, transparent contextual referrals inside completed workflows, optional one-time paid utility only if demand proves exceptional, and eventual asset resale. Ads alone make the conservative and base cases small.

This is a crowded but not dying category. Distribution is the hard part. A winning app replaces a workflow, works natively/offline, prevents errors, saves jobs, respects local unit conventions, and exports useful results.

### Automation and launch gates

CI, crash monitoring, release notes, analytics, support triage, and store-listing experiments automate well. Native-device QA, privacy/SDK review, store rejections, crash diagnosis, and product judgment do not.

Do not start until the web property identifies a repeat job. Then budget 60–80 focused hours and about $200–600 cash, including Apple's $99 annual developer membership. Closed-test with 25–50 real users before store submission.

At day 90 post-release require at least three of: 500 non-incentivized installs, 100 MAU, 10% D30 retention, and 99.5% crash-free users. At day 180 require $50 monthly owner revenue or at least 20% three-month MAU growth. Kill if privacy, SDK, review, or support work exceeds two hours weekly for four weeks.

## 6. Specialized calculator/tool suite

### Best formulation

Build three to five connected tools around one expensive recurring decision. A strong suite shares inputs and data, exposes assumptions and source dates, supports saved scenarios and export, and produces a clear next action.

Generic mortgage, BMI, conversion, and one-formula calculator sites are saturated and easily reproduced. Durable tools encode a real workflow: zone and dimensional shipping economics, material cut/yield/waste, equipment total cost and maintenance, RV power/connectivity, or another domain with meaningful edge cases.

### Money sources and differentiation

Attach referral actions, contextual sponsors, ads after engagement, consented leads, later app reuse, and possible API licensing. A tool preventing a $500 error or guiding a $5k–$50k purchase has better economics than a summary article.

Deterministic formulas, golden test cases, source refreshes, regression tests, explanations, analytics, and alert triggers can reach 85–95% automation. Domain validation, assumption changes, safety/legal boundaries, edge cases, and partner quality remain human responsibilities.

### 90-day plan and gates

Specify one calculation notebook, versioned assumptions, and 30–50 golden test cases. Have a domain practitioner review it. Ship the first calculator by day 30 and two adjacent tools by day 60. Add saved scenarios, export/share, source dates, and email only after correctness.

Ongoing cash is roughly $30–200 monthly before paid data, overlapping project #1's $150–800 initial 90-day setup budget rather than adding to it automatically. Continue at day 90 with 1,000 qualified users, 100 repeat users, 100 email subscribers plus 25 saved scenarios, or five monetizable actions. Kill if formulas cannot be independently validated, disputes become material, update work scales linearly, or no tool reaches 10% return use within 60 days.

**Decision:** this should be the first user-facing layer of project #1.

## 7. Data-led local or industry newsletter

### Best formulation

Use email to distribute material changes from a proprietary maintained dataset. A generic AI-written roundup has no moat. The email should send readers into tools, saved alerts, or decision pages and should make the underlying asset more valuable.

### Money sources and stability

Use relevant referral actions, ad-network slots, paid recommendations, self-serve sponsors, and eventually paid data access if the data genuinely supports decisions. beehiiv reports 66 median days to first dollar for publications launched in 2025, 41% average opens, and paid-subscription revenue growing from $8M to $19M in 2025. These show functioning rails, not likely individual earnings. [beehiiv 2026 report](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/beehiiv-the-state-of-newsletters-2026)

Email is not a dying channel; the generic newsletter lane is packed. Durability comes from opt-in direct reach plus data with recurring operational consequences. The human voice, materiality judgment, verification, reputation, and sponsor quality control keep automation around 60–75%, with roughly 5–10 owner-hours a week in year one.

### 90-day plan and gates

Choose one geography/vertical, document source rights and cadence, and seed 100–300 entities. Launch searchable data, a weekly digest, and event alerts. Test one referral rail before ads. Acquire readers from relevant communities, partnerships, and the utility itself—not bulk lists or paid growth.

Do not use subscriber count or opens as a sole hard gate; opens are distorted by privacy proxies, and a large inactive list can mislead. Continue only if the attached property has at least one behavioral signal—3% unique clicks into useful destinations, 50 saved-alert users, 50 return sessions from email, or five monetizable actions—plus one of those signals again or 250 qualified opt-ins. Use 35% opens across four sends only as a diagnostic. Kill if the product requires constant original reporting or correction work exceeds one hour weekly per 100 entities.

**Decision:** build only as the owned retention channel for project #1 or #4.

## 8. Acquire a proven utility/data asset

### Best formulation

Acquire a narrow asset similar to the models above: 12–18 months of verified profit, recurring direct/email/app users, two monetization rails, documented data rights, transferable code/domain/analytics/contracts, and low support.

Reject display-ad content sites, AI/SEO archives, single-affiliate dependencies, scraped datasets with unclear rights, and sellers who will not reconcile analytics with payments.

### Economics, automation, and stability

Acquire.com's January 2026 report says profitable SaaS businesses sold at a median 3.9× annual profit in both 2024 and 2025. Empire Flippers' 2026 report places typical-business average listings around 27.8× monthly earnings, while emphasizing that moat, quality, and risk drive the range. These are marketplace anchors, not fair prices for a fragile target. [Acquire.com multiples](https://blog.acquire.com/acquire-com-biannual-acquisition-multiples-report-jan-2026/), [Empire Flippers report](https://info.empireflippers.com/hubfs/2026%20Lead%20Magnets/2026%20State%20of%20the%20Industry%20Report.pdf)

| Acquisition case | Purchase capital | Stabilization reserve | 12-month operating cash flow | 24-month operating cash flow | Payback implication |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Conservative | $20–60k | 10–25% | $4–14k | $10–28k | Usually beyond 24 months |
| Base | $60–160k | 10–25% | $20–55k | $50–130k | Commonly 2.5–5+ years |
| Upside | $150–350k | 10–25% | $55–135k | $150–340k | Can cross payback inside 24–48 months |

These broad scenarios are deliberately not valuation formulas. Underwrite each target from verified cash flow and downside risk.

Immediate revenue does not mean immediate payback. Underwrite the full purchase price, diligence, transition, repair reserve, founder hours, and a 30–40% traffic/revenue decline. Inherited automation may be high, but the first 90–180 days often require 5–15 owner-hours weekly for transfer, source validation, backup/monitoring, undocumented exceptions, and revenue stabilization.

### 90-day acquisition plan and gates

- Days 1–21: screen 20–30 targets on concentration, rights, direct traffic, support, code, and owner time.
- Days 22–45: reconcile read-only analytics with payment records; inspect cohorts, channels, code, infrastructure, support, source contracts, and downside economics.
- Days 46–60: negotiate escrow, transfer, seller transition, and holdback/earn-out where appropriate; release funds only after transfer conditions are met.
- Days 61–90: freeze large changes, establish monitoring/backups, remove single points of failure, and test one reversible retention or conversion improvement.

Walk unless 12–18 months of analytics and payouts reconcile; no source, merchant, or platform exceeds 35% of revenue; no single search query supplies over 35% of organic sessions or attributable revenue; data/content rights transfer in writing; support is below five hours weekly; and a downside case remains cash-positive after owner labor. Require at least 40% direct/email traffic if paying above 24× monthly profit.

**Decision:** defer until the build-from-zero project validates a niche and a dedicated acquisition reserve exists. Then buy adjacent data or distribution, not an unrelated “passive” site.

## Reconciled 90-day portfolio plan

### Week 1 — choose the decision niche

Score the five candidate niches from 0–5 on:

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Lawful, stable, retainable data | 20% |
| Value of the decision/error avoided | 20% |
| At least two credible money rails | 15% |
| Repeat use or meaningful change frequency | 15% |
| Competition gap beyond generic content | 15% |
| Accuracy and exception work can automate | 15% |

Require direct evidence for every score. Disqualify any niche with unclear rights, only one money rail, regulated personalized advice, or no objective correctness standard.

### Days 8–30 — two bounded builds

- Decision property: one validated calculator, 50–150 canonical entities, provenance/freshness records, analytics, and one result action.
- Licensing catalog: 30–50 rights-clean assets across three buyer use cases and two marketplaces.

Hard first-90-day allocation: at most **60 focused hours and $800** for the decision property, plus **60 focused hours and $1,500** for the licensing catalog using existing gear. No tracker expansion, directory build, standalone newsletter, app work, or acquisition diligence may consume this build budget before the two day-90 reviews.

### Days 31–60 — create repeat use

- Decision property: three connected tools, saved scenarios, one tracker/alert, ten real-user tests, and a correction queue.
- Licensing catalog: 100 approved assets, automated manifests, technical QA, and metadata review.

### Days 61–90 — distribute and measure

- Decision property: useful decision/entity pages, demonstrations, niche-community distribution, opt-in email, and the first monetization experiment.
- Licensing catalog: 150–250 approved assets and family-level licensing/approval analysis.
- No app and no acquisition work beyond keeping a watchlist.

At day 90, promote at most two systems. The default is the decision property plus licensing catalog. Build the app only after repeat web behavior reveals a workflow; add the directory/newsletter only where they improve the same asset's retention; consider acquisition only after domain understanding and cash reserves exist.

## Bottom line

The highest expected-value move is not eight launches. It is one data-and-calculation asset with several reinforcing surfaces, paired with one low-support licensing catalog.

The packed lanes can still earn, but only when the work is commercially specific:

- generic tracker: declining; vertical decision-state tracker: viable;
- generic stock: oversupplied; procedural/functional media: viable;
- generic directory: fragile; verified change history: viable;
- generic calculator: commoditized; workflow suite with current data: viable;
- generic newsletter: crowded; proprietary-data alerts: viable;
- generic app: weak distribution; proven field workflow: viable.

The durable moat across the portfolio is the same: rights-clean source material, structured history, correctness, direct return traffic, and assets that save a user or buyer measurable time or money.
