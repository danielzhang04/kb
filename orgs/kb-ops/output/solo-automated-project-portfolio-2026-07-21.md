# DRAFT — Solo, automation-first project portfolio

_Research date: 2026-07-21. This is a selection artifact, not a forecast. Revenue, cost, time, and automation figures labeled **estimate** are hypotheses to validate with paid pilots._

## Executive recommendation

The strongest current opportunity is not a generic “AI business.” AI has made content, code, and automation cheap, which has increased competition and made distribution, proprietary data, trust, and retention more valuable.

The best next portfolio is:

1. **Municipal pre-RFP intelligence for one high-ticket trade** — best overall and best first launch.
2. **Niche federal-contract capture intelligence** — second launch; it reuses most of the ingestion, matching, scoring, evidence, and delivery machinery.
3. **Industrial catalog/specification-change intelligence** — conditional diversification option; activate only after permissioned data access and matching accuracy are proven.

Do not build a SaaS interface first. Each should begin as a paid report, email, spreadsheet, or private feed. Software is earned only after paying buyers reveal a repeated workflow.

If direct customer work is unacceptable, replace #2 or #3 with a proprietary benchmark publication/database and run only two projects. If short-term cash is the overriding goal, add a tightly scoped creator/media agent-operations service as a temporary cash accelerator, not as the long-term business.

## What the market evidence says

- Solo formation is at an all-time high, but outcomes are increasingly power-law distributed. Stripe reports that 63% of Q2 2026 Atlas C-corps were solo-founded, while median initial six-month revenue for 2025 solo founders fell 23% and top-decile revenue rose 19%. Median solo B2B revenue was more than four times median solo B2C revenue by month 24, and top performers used recurring billing more often. [Stripe Atlas](https://stripe.com/blog/top-solo-founder-traits)
- Influencer claims are an unreliable evidence class. A 2026 study of 377 YouTube videos promoting GenAI monetization found recurring structural problems including unverifiable income claims, content misappropriation, synthetic engagement, and shifting authorship norms. [Research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07036)
- Owned email is useful, but a newsletter is distribution—not automatically a business. beehiiv reports a 66-day median time to first dollar for publications launched in 2025 and paid-subscription revenue rising from $8M to $19M; the wins came from specialized utility, not generic summaries. [beehiiv 2026 report](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/the-state-of-newsletters-2026)
- Commodity informational SEO is materially riskier. Pew observed a traditional-result click in 8% of Google visits with an AI summary versus 15% without one, and only 1% clicked a source inside the summary. Google also treats scaled low-value output as spam regardless of whether AI or humans produced it. [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), [Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- Public procurement is a large, data-rich buyer market. The SBA reports $179B in FY2025 prime contracts and $273B including subcontracts awarded to small businesses. SAM.gov provides an official opportunity API with daily active-notice updates. [SBA](https://www.sba.gov/article/2026/06/25/sba-releases-fy25-scorecard-small-business-contracting), [GSA API](https://open.gsa.gov/api/get-opportunities-public-api/)
- Local-government information is fragmented at a scale that rewards normalization. The Census counted 91,438 U.S. local governments in 2025. [U.S. Census](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2026/econ/govtorg2225.html)

## 1. Municipal pre-RFP intelligence

### Offer

Pick one state and one high-ticket vendor category—examples to test include commercial HVAC/building controls, water/wastewater contractors, energy-service companies, fleet/equipment vendors, or specialized school-facility contractors.

Monitor council and school-board agendas, capital plans, budgets, bond documents, permits, meeting minutes, and solicitations. Deliver only scored opportunities:

- buyer and jurisdiction;
- project and evidence;
- stage and likely timing;
- estimated fit;
- next action;
- source links and confidence;
- change history.

Do not sell raw permits or keyword matches. Sell “what is likely to become a buying event, why, and what the contractor should do next.”

### Why it ranks first

- The fragmented source surface creates real normalization work and a growing historical moat.
- Buyers are identifiable before the product exists.
- One won job can justify a year of fees.
- Delivery can start manually and become highly automated without a consumer app.
- Current market pricing validates willingness to pay: Obedio lists $100/month for one national keyword, $500/month for all keywords in one state, and $1,000/month for full single-state access. [Obedio](https://www.getobedio.com/pricing)

### Estimated pilot economics

- Offer: $199 setup + $399/month; richer or exclusive territory $699–$999/month.
- Eight customers: approximately $3.2K–$8K MRR.
- Cash tools/hosting: $150–$400/month at a narrow initial scope.
- Stabilized owner QA: 2–4 hours/week.

### 30-day validation gate

Build no dashboard. Select one vertical/state, map 20–50 official sources, produce three sample briefs, and contact 30 qualified prospects.

Continue only if:

- at least two customers prepay;
- the feed produces at least five genuinely actionable signals per week;
- at least 70% of delivered signals pass your own QA without material correction;
- projected steady-state QA is under four hours/week for the first five customers.

Kill or change vertical if fewer than five prospects request a sample, no one prepays, or the sources do not yield enough early signals.

## 2. Niche federal-contract capture intelligence

### Offer

Serve already-registered small contractors in one NAICS/PSC cluster. Deliver:

- opportunity and amendment matching;
- incumbent and award-history context;
- bid/no-bid score;
- requirement/compliance matrix;
- teaming or competitor signals;
- optional source-grounded capture dossier.

The raw SAM.gov search is free and mature competitors exist, so the product must be client-specific judgment and memory—not another alert screen.

### Why it ranks second

- Large, explicit spend and official machine-readable data.
- High-value recurring decisions with clear buyer identities.
- The same core engine as project #1 can ingest, normalize, match, score, verify, and deliver.
- Market pricing demonstrates budget: GovTribe lists $1,900/year for an individual plan and $6,000/year for its Growth tier. [GovTribe](https://govtribe.com/plans)

### Estimated pilot economics

- $399–$699/month monitoring.
- $750–$1,500 per capture dossier.
- Six $499 clients plus two $750 dossiers in a month: approximately $4.5K.
- Stabilized owner QA: 10–15 hours/month.

### 30-day validation gate

Run a wedge tournament across three NAICS/PSC clusters. Score each on weekly opportunity count, award size, incumbent fragmentation, number of reachable prospects, document complexity, and obvious competitor saturation.

Continue the winner only if:

- two contractors prepay;
- at least 60% of surfaced opportunities are judged relevant by prospects;
- one capture dossier can be produced in under two owner-hours after automation;
- customers say the bid/no-bid context is worth paying for beyond the free alert.

Kill if feedback consistently reduces the offer to “send me SAM.gov matches.”

## 3. Industrial catalog/specification-change intelligence

### Offer

Target one industrial distribution category where specification changes, discontinuations, substitutions, availability, or pricing anomalies can invalidate quotes or create costly procurement mistakes.

Deliver a source-grounded change feed:

- normalized manufacturer/SKU identity;
- what changed and when;
- affected compatible products, quotes, or customer records;
- approved substitute candidates;
- confidence and source;
- operational next action.

Do not compete with generic ecommerce price trackers. The paid value is difficult vertical entity matching plus business impact: “this safety-rated part changed specification; these open quotes may now be wrong.”

### Why it is the diversification option

- Very high recurring utility when errors are expensive.
- Different market and data sources from government intelligence.
- Change detection, matching, evidence, and alerting are highly automatable.
- Census reports 382,081 U.S. wholesale-trade employer establishments and roughly $11.7T in 2023 sector revenue, while commodity price-monitoring vendors already charge from roughly $99 to hundreds per month—evidence of both market size and the need for a sharper wedge. [Census wholesale profile](https://data.census.gov/profile/42_-_Wholesale_trade?codeset=naics~42), [Prisync](https://prisync.com/alternative/price2spy/)

### Estimated pilot economics

- $750–$2,000/month per distributor or manufacturer.
- Three $1,000/month customers: $3K MRR.
- Mature owner QA: 2–5 hours/week if the source set is stable and matching is reliable.

### Permission and accuracy gate

Before building, interview five qualified distributors. Continue only if they will provide target catalogs, source permissions or customer-supplied feeds, historical examples, and a specific costly change workflow.

After a shadow pilot, continue only if:

- at least three customers will pay $750/month or more;
- entity-matching precision is at least 98%;
- source maintenance stays below 20% of revenue;
- every monitored source is public and permitted, licensed, customer-supplied, or accessed through a permissioned API.

Kill before launch if it requires prohibited scraping, if buyers only want generic price alerts, or if false matches can create unacceptable safety or contractual risk.

## 4. Proprietary benchmark publication/database

### Offer

Build a measured database around an expensive, frequently changing decision. The most natural first hypothesis is a creator-production stack benchmark, but the pattern is portable.

For creator production, measure rather than summarize:

- cost per accepted output or finished minute;
- generation and revision time;
- failure/retry rate;
- visual/voice consistency;
- commercial-use and disclosure constraints;
- API and automation support;
- quality by use case;
- policy or price changes;
- reproducible test inputs and dates.

Publish a free weekly signal digest. Monetize later with aligned sponsorships, affiliates, premium comparisons/exports, and a paid tier. Do not start with generic AI news or a directory of logos and rewritten descriptions.

### Why it belongs in the portfolio

- It is an owned audience/data asset rather than another client service.
- It compounds with every measurement and update.
- It can supply acquisition to other businesses without depending on Google.
- Current operator examples show the ceiling, though they are selected success stories: AI Central reports a six-figure annual business and more than $150K from the beehiiv Ad Network after hundreds of issues and a multi-year publishing streak. The lesson is the streak plus differentiated utility, not “AI newsletter.” [AI Central case study](https://www.beehiiv.com/case-studies/ai-central)

### Estimated validation economics

- Free digest first; pre-sell premium at roughly $15/month or $150/year only after interviews.
- Early B2B sponsor test: $250–$1,000 per placement **estimate**, after demonstrating a qualified audience.
- Mature owner QA/research: 3–6 hours/week if tests are standardized.

### 60-day validation gate

Create 50 verified records and five original comparison tests. Interview 20 intended users and acquire 250 opt-in subscribers without buying a large list.

Continue only if:

- at least 10 readers prepay or two relevant sponsors commit;
- open rate exceeds 35% across four sends;
- at least 20% of subscribers click a benchmark/data page in a month;
- original measurements—not news summaries—drive the strongest engagement.

Kill or pivot if growth depends on generic AI-news curation, affiliate clicks alone, or mass-generated SEO pages.

## Optional cash accelerator: creator/media agent operations

This is the highest-probability route to larger checks quickly, but it is less aligned with the goal of a mostly automated owner-light asset.

Sell a fixed outcome such as a research-to-publication workflow, approval/QA system, evidence ledger, observability setup, or content-compliance pipeline. Avoid “we automate anything.” Upwork reported AI-related work above a $300M annualized GSV run rate in Q4 2025 and AI Integration & Automation growth above 90% year over year; Fiverr reported an 18,347% surge in searches for AI-agent expertise. These are demand signals, not expected earnings. [Upwork FY2025](https://investors.upwork.com/node/12716/pdf), [Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/news/spring-bti-2025)

Use three fixed-scope pilots to learn. Productize repeated components. Stop accepting custom work that cannot be reused. Kill the service if delivery remains below 60% automated after three clients or if each client requires a materially different stack.

## Recommended simultaneous portfolio design

### Phase 1 — two active validations plus one access test (days 1–30)

| Workstream | Purpose | Maximum build before payment |
|---|---|---|
| Municipal pre-RFP | Fast recurring cash hypothesis | Source map + three briefs |
| GovCon capture | Adjacent-market challenger | Three cluster scans + two sample dossiers |
| Catalog/spec changes | Diversification option | Five interviews + permissioned sample feeds; no platform |

At day 30, promote at most two to full projects. The default promotion is municipal intelligence plus whichever of GovCon or catalog intelligence gets stronger prepaid demand. Keep all three only if each has a paying signal and combined projected owner time is below 20 hours/week. If catalog data rights or matching fail, use the proprietary benchmark publication as the third-slot fallback.

### Why this mix

- Projects #1 and #2 reuse technical machinery, shortening build time.
- Project #3 diversifies market and source risk; the benchmark fallback diversifies distribution and business-model risk.
- None requires employees, inventory, fundraising, or a finished SaaS product.
- Each can begin with a human approval gate and increase automation from actual history.

| Popular 2025–2026 pitch | What is real | What is usually omitted | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-person AI company | AI increases solo leverage | Stripe’s 61× top-decile/median gap | Use AI leverage, ignore inevitability claims |
| AI automation agency | Demand is strong | Custom delivery, sales, support, no moat | Cash bridge only; verticalize hard |
| Automated newsletter | Production can be automated | Distribution, churn, sponsor sales, trust | Add proprietary utility or skip |
| Programmatic SEO empire | Structured data pages can work | AI Overviews and scaled-content enforcement | Only with real data and transactional intent |
| AI templates/courses | Fulfillment margins are excellent | Earnings are highly concentrated; audience is the moat | Sell only tested artifacts |
| Affiliate media | Useful extra monetization | Common Amazon categories pay roughly 3% | Layer, not core model |
| Local rank-and-rent | Businesses value leads | Fake/virtual profiles violate Google rules | Work only with authorized real businesses |
| KDP/Etsy/POD/stock factory | Platforms permit some disclosed AI work | Near-zero entry barriers and originality pressure | Reject as a core project |
| Synthetic influencer | Attention can be automated | Monetization proof and platform originality enforcement | Reject |
| Buy a boring online business | Existing cash flow is valuable | Capital, diligence, traffic concentration, owner workload | Revisit after cash flow, not now |

## Projects to reject now

- Generic AI news newsletter.
- Generic prompt packs or “10,000 templates.”
- Mass-generated KDP books, Etsy printables, POD designs, or stock images.
- Programmatic informational SEO without proprietary data.
- A general AI automation agency.
- An AI wrapper SaaS built before paid discovery.
- Local lead generation using fake locations or unowned Google Business Profiles.
- Automated trading, betting, or speculative arbitrage presented as reliable income.
- Synthetic influencer networks whose business case is follower count.
- Any model whose visible operators primarily earn by teaching the model.

## Decision rule

Choose the project where the following sentence is strongest:

> “A specific reachable buyer will prepay for a recurring, source-grounded decision advantage before I build software, and each delivery makes the next one cheaper and more accurate.”

By that rule, municipal pre-RFP intelligence is the current #1. The second slot is a paid market test between GovCon capture and the proprietary benchmark publication, not a debate to settle with more brainstorming.
