## 4. Video state of play

There is no established, broadly trusted hosted video API in this pass with an unambiguous public permission for explicit fictional-adult output. ZenCreator’s docs explicitly advertise trusted-account, uncensored NSFW video modes; that is a vendor claim with the SaaS risks noted above. Eromify also advertises 18+ motion capability without a retrievable policy clause.

The credible open-weight route is self-hosted I2V: a locked persona reference rendered through **Wan I2V** or **HunyuanVideo-I2V** in a ComfyUI workflow. It is real, available technology, but still materially less reliable than still-image generation for face persistence, anatomy over motion, temporal coherence, and repeatability. Wan’s licence is the cleaner published commercial starting point; Hunyuan’s territorial/AUP restrictions are a blocker for a global paid use unless separately cleared. [Wan paper/repo](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.1) · [Hunyuan I2V](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/HunyuanVideo-I2V)

## 6. Legal and payment guardrails — factual flags, not legal advice

- **Identity/age/consent.** Keep personas fictional and unequivocally adult in appearance; do not use a real person’s face, voice, or other likeness without documented permission. Fanvue, Civitai, fal, Replicate, and the major model licences separately prohibit or restrict real-person sexualized/deceptive output and all prohibit sexualized minors. A stated prompt age is not a sufficient visual-age safeguard on Fanvue. [Fanvue](https://legal.fanvue.com/community-guidelines) · [Civitai](https://civitai.tech/articles/13632/policy-and-content-adjustments) · [fal](https://fal.ai/legal/acceptable-use-policy)
- **Distribution age assurance.** UK services in scope that publish/allow pornography must use “highly effective age assurance”; Ofcom’s child-protection requirements took effect in 2025. In the US, state rules vary: Texas, for example, requires a commercially reasonable age-verification method for covered services, and similar laws have been enacted in multiple states. These are distribution-platform obligations and need jurisdiction-specific review before a direct-to-consumer launch. [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/age-assurance) · [Texas statute](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.509.htm)
- **Payments are a core architecture constraint.** Visa treats adult content as a legal but higher-risk industry subject to acquirer/network oversight. Mastercard’s current rules for non-face-to-face adult-content merchants include specialty registration and operational requirements such as an accessible complaint/reporting process with review/resolution within seven business days. This is why a platform’s adult permission does not by itself make card monetisation durable. [Visa](https://corporate.visa.com/en/about-visa/visa-network-integrity.html) · [Mastercard rules](https://www.mastercard.com/content/dam/mccom/shared/business/support/rules-pdfs/SPME-Manual.pdf)

## Recommendation (derived from the published constraints)

Use two deliberately separate lanes, not one shared SaaS workflow:

1. **IG-register lane:** use a mainstream, terms-compatible image/video generator and publish only mainstream-safe assets; disclose the fictional AI persona as required by the destination platform. Keep its asset store, prompts, automation, and posting account separate from the paid lane.
2. **Paid explicit lane:** use self-operated ComfyUI with an SDXL-compatible photoreal checkpoint plus a persona LoRA, on infrastructure whose contract explicitly permits the workload (or controlled hardware). Treat source licences, checkpoint licence, adult-age/right-of-publicity gate, platform disclosure, age assurance, takedown/complaint handling, and payment onboarding as release gates. Do **not** make fal, Replicate, RunPod, or Modal the production API for this lane under their currently published terms. Fanvue is the documented platform fit; the cited OnlyFans material does not establish a fit for a wholly fictional persona.

**Single fact to verify hands-on first:** whether the selected paid distribution platform will approve the exact disclosed fictional-AI creator account and its intended monetisation/payment configuration **in writing**. That test resolves the highest-impact uncertainty—platform/payment continuity—before time is spent training a proprietary persona LoRA.
